# Analytic Gap Ledger — v0.3

*v0.17 note.*  Gaps A, B and C below are open in the library proper.  For consumers that accept a
classical result without proof, the opt-in module `CompactLieHeatKernel/Literature/HeatKernel.lean`
imports the whole `HeatSemigroupKernelBridge` from the literature as one axiom; see
`LITERATURE_AXIOMS.md`.

## A. `Δ_G/2` to a self-adjoint Markov semigroup

This is now separated conceptually from the compact-manifold heat-kernel theorem.  The target is
`LaplacianGeneratorBridge` plus `SelfAdjointMarkovGenerator`; the generic Friedrichs extension work
is being developed in the independent `FriedrichsExtension` project.

Remaining group-specific inputs after generic Friedrichs are integration by parts, symmetry and
nonpositivity of `Δ_G/2` on the smooth core, and the identification of the Friedrichs generator with
the basis-free group Laplacian.

## B. Compact-manifold regularisation — the sole large point-4 gap

Target output: `CompactManifoldRegularisationData`.

The v0.3 project proves everything downstream of this target.  In particular the following are **no
longer external assumptions**:

* the Feller semigroup law (`FellerLift.op_add`);
* preservation of constants (`FellerLift.op_one`);
* nonnegativity (from strict positivity);
* symmetry of the density (from L² self-adjointness, Fubini and full-support uniqueness);
* mass one of the density;
* Chapman--Kolmogorov, both real-valued and `ENNReal`-valued;
* the Markov-kernel composition law;
* compatibility of the old `HeatSemigroupKernelBridge` representation field.

The genuinely missing theorem is now:

> Let `M` be a compact connected smooth Riemannian manifold, let `μ` be its full-support normalised
> volume measure, and let `P_t` be the self-adjoint Markov semigroup generated by `Δ_M/2`.  Then
> `P_t` admits an L²-compatible Feller lift and, for every `t>0`, a jointly smooth density
> `k_t(x,y)` which is everywhere strictly positive and represents the Feller operator.

A standard proof can be organised spectrally:

1. compact resolvent / discrete spectrum for the Friedrichs Laplacian;
2. elliptic regularity for eigenfunctions and sufficiently strong eigenfunction estimates;
3. locally uniform convergence with derivatives of
   `Σ_n exp(-t λ_n) φ_n(x) φ_n(y)` for `t>0`;
4. identify the series with the L² semigroup;
5. positivity preservation + strong maximum principle / positivity improving to obtain strict
   positivity;
6. identify the regularising kernel with the Feller/L² semigroup (symmetry is then derived in Lean).

Alternatively, a parametrix/parabolic-regularity proof would need comparable new PDE infrastructure.
Current mathlib provides neither route as a ready theorem package.

## C. Lie-group invariance after the compact-manifold theorem

Once the compact-manifold regularisation datum exists, one still proves that the group Laplacian
commutes with left and right translations.  Uniqueness of the heat evolution then gives the two
translation-invariance fields of `GroupRegularisationData`.  From there all centrality statements
are already formalised in `GroupHeatKernel.lean`.

## Closed local gap from v0.2

The former bookkeeping gap

`transitionKernel t ∘ₖ transitionKernel s = transitionKernel (s+t)`

is now proved in `KernelSemigroup.lean` from density-level Chapman--Kolmogorov by Tonelli.

## D. Totalisation-Safety Boundary (v0.9)

The supplied `TotalisationSafety_v05` source bundle now supplies the shared manifold-derivative audit and
proof-carrying wrappers; the HeatKernel-specific audit records only local branch conditions.  In particular, Mathlib's `mfderiv` is zero when
manifold differentiability fails.  The first derivative on explicitly `ContMDiff` scalar functions and the
conjugation derivative are guarded formally.  The second derivative in `leftHessian` remains tied
to the open second-order manifold-calculus theorem through the explicit proposition
`LeftHessianDifferentiabilityAt`; it is not silently treated as proved.

The broad `kernelAction` definition is also recorded as an API-tightening boundary: actual bridge
use is on continuous functions over a compact probability space, but a future revision should carry
integrability structurally in the input type.
