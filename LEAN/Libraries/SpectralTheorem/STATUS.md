## Deviations from Reed–Simon

* Normal instead of self-adjoint bounded operators throughout (Reed–Simon's Problems VII.3–5);
  mathlib's continuous functional calculus covers normal operators directly.
* No separability in the main results: cyclic decompositions over arbitrary index sets, and one
  measure space `Σ_ψ σ(T)` instead of `⊕ₙ L²(ℝ, dμₙ)`.  The measure is then not finite in
  general; for separable `H` the `_finite` versions give Reed–Simon's finite measure.
* Proposition VIII.3.1 is proved for any measure (Reed–Simon take it finite), without the
  statement that the spectrum is the essential range.
* Theorem VII.2 is stated for bounded Borel functions on `σ(T)` rather than on `ℝ`: Reed–Simon's
  (c) `φ̂(x) = A` uses the unbounded function `x`, which only makes sense through its values on
  `σ(A)`.  Uniqueness is among `⋆`-homomorphisms with (b)–(d), as in Reed–Simon.
* Theorem VII.2 (g) for normal `T` assumes that `B` commutes with `T` and `T†`; without Fuglede's
  theorem (not formalised) the second condition does not follow from the first.  For self-adjoint
  `T` it is Reed–Simon's statement.
* Theorem VIII.4: `f` is real everywhere, not only a.e., and Reed–Simon's last step ("`f` is
  real since `M_f` is self-adjoint") is replaced by `σ(R) ⊆ {|z|² + Im z = 0}`, proved from the
  resolvent identity and the injectivity of the continuous functional calculus.
* Theorem VIII.5 is stated as in Reed–Simon, for bounded Borel functions on `ℝ`.  Reed–Simon
  leave the uniqueness to the reader; the proof here shows `Φ((x + i)⁻¹) = R` from (c) and (d),
  then uses the uniqueness of the continuous functional calculus of `R` on the compact circle and
  the monotone class theorem.

* Theorem VII.8 is proved for normal operators and p.v.m.'s on `ℂ` (`spectralPVMEquiv`), and
  Reed–Simon's self-adjoint version on `ℝ` (`selfAdjointPVMEquiv`) is derived from it.  A bounded
  p.v.m. has `P(closedBall 0 r) = 1` for some `r` (Reed–Simon: `P_(-a,a) = I`).  `∫ λ dP_λ` is
  `∫ λ 1_{|λ| ≤ r} dP_λ`, independent of `r`.
* The p.v.m. structure has one extra field setting `P_Ω = 0` for non-measurable `Ω`, so that
  p.v.m.'s agreeing on Borel sets are equal (needed for the bijections to be `Equiv`s).
* `g(A)` for unbounded `g` (pp. 263–264) is defined twice and the two agree
  (`integralPMap_pvm`): from the multiplication form (`borelPMap`, `U⁻¹ T_{g∘f} U`), and from
  the p.v.m. as the limit of the truncations `g 1_{|g| ≤ n}` (`integralPMap`).  Reed–Simon define
  it by its quadratic form on `D_g`; `eq_borelPMap_of_inner` shows that the form determines it.
* Real Hilbert spaces are not treated by Reed–Simon.  The real functional calculus is the descent
  of the complex one on the complexification; its uniqueness is among `⋆`-homomorphisms from real
  bounded Borel functions (trivial `⋆`) to `𝓛(E)` (adjoint), i.e. with self-adjoint values.
  The real multiplication operator form is obtained from the complex one with real cyclic
  vectors, rather than by a separate real construction.

## Deviations, M11

* The general theory is stated for a unital `⋆`-representation `π : C(X, ℂ) →⋆ₐ[ℂ] 𝓛(H)` of a
  compact Hausdorff `X`; the single-operator case is `X = σ(T)`, `π = cfcHom`.  The modules
  duplicate the single-operator development (`SpectralMeasure`, `Cyclic`, `Decomposition`,
  `MultiplicationForm`) rather than replacing it; a later refactor could derive the latter from
  the former.
* Uniqueness of the p.v.m. holds among p.v.m.'s whose measures are regular (any compact
  Hausdorff `X`), and outright when `X` is metrizable.  The p.v.m. constructed here is always
  regular.
* Commutativity of the subalgebra is a hypothesis of the structure `CommOperatorAlgebra`, and
  star-commutation (`Commute (T i) (star (T j))`) is a hypothesis for families: Fuglede's theorem
  is not formalised.
* The joint p.v.m. lives on `ι → ℂ` with the product `σ`-algebra, which is the Borel `σ`-algebra
  exactly for countable `ι`; the support and uniqueness statements assume `ι` countable, and
  uniqueness is among p.v.m.'s carried by the joint spectrum.
* Rudin's *Functional Analysis* is cited by chapter only: the book is not available locally, so
  theorem and page numbers could not be checked against the page.

## Deviations, M12

* A normal operator is defined by `D(N†) = D(N)` and `‖N ψ‖ = ‖N† ψ‖` (`IsNormalPMap`), the usual
  textbook definition for closed densely defined operators; closedness is proved, not assumed.
* Reed–Simon I treat only self-adjoint operators; the normal case follows Rudin, Ch. 13, and
  von Neumann's theorem is Reed–Simon II, Theorem X.25, p. 180 (page checked), proved here by
  von Neumann's own graph argument rather than Reed–Simon's quadratic forms.
* `T†† = T` for closed densely defined `T` and the adjoint's antitonicity were missing from
  mathlib and are proved in `NormalGeneral.lean`.
* The p.v.m. of a normal operator is obtained as an existence statement; its uniqueness is not
  proved, and neither is uniqueness in the resolvent case of `NormalSpectral.lean`.
* `‖T Q‖ ≤ 1` rather than the classical `≤ 1/2`, which would need a division.

## Known quirk

In this mathlib, `rw`/`simp` with generic scalar lemmas (`smul_zero`, `smul_add`) fail on
`V →L[ℂ] V` for concrete `V` (checked for `ℂ`, `ℂ × ℂ`, `EuclideanSpace ℂ (Fin 2)` and
`Complexification E`), although they work for a variable `V`; the `module` tactic, or passing
the type explicitly (`smul_zero (A := V →L[ℂ] V) c`), works.  `RealCalculus` uses `module`.
