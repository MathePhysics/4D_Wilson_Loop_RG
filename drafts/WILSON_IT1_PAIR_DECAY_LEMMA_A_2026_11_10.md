# Lemma A: Weighted Covariance Bounds for Uniformly Convex Measures (10 November 2026)

Lemma A of the decay plan (WILSON_IT1_PAIR_DECAY_PLAN_2026_11_10.md, #DP.4), part (i). The source is J. Johnsen, *On the spectral properties of Witten-Laplacians, their range projections and Brascamp–Lieb's inequality*, Integral Equations Operator Theory 36 (2000) 288–324, cited as Johnsen with page and equation.

## Setting

- {#DA.1} Φ ∈ C²(ℝ^n) with ∫e^{−Φ}dx = 1 (Johnsen (1.5), p.290), μ := e^{−Φ}dx, (·|·)_μ the inner product of L²(μ) := L²(ℝ^n, μ, ℂ) and of L²(μ; ℂ^n) ((4.1), p.301), and H¹(μ) := {u ∈ L²(μ): ∂_ju ∈ L²(μ) for all j} (p.290). Φ″(x) denotes the Hessian. Assume c₀ ≤ Φ″(x) ≤ c₁ as matrices for every x, with 0 < c₀ ≤ c₁ < ∞.
  {#DA.1-1} A₁ is Johnsen's operator on 1-forms, defined by the triple (H₁, V₁, a₁) of (4.2)–(4.5) (p.301): H₁ = L²(μ; ℂ^n), V₁ = D(d₁) ∩ D(d₀*) with the norm (4.6), D(A₁) = {u ∈ V₁: there is f ∈ H₁ with a₁(u, w) = (f | w)_μ for all w ∈ V₁}, and A₁u = f. C₀^∞ is dense in V₁ and in D(d₀) = H¹(μ) (Lemma 4.1, p.302).
  {#DA.1-2} *The form on H¹.* On C₀^∞(ℝ^n, ℂ^n), a₁(v, v) = Σ_{j,k}‖∂_jv_k‖²_μ + (Φ″v | v)_μ ((4.39), p.307), so by polarisation a₁(v, w) = Σ_{j,k}(∂_jv_k | ∂_jw_k)_μ + (Φ″v | w)_μ there. With c₀ ≤ Φ″ ≤ c₁, the V₁-norm² ‖v‖²_μ + a₁(v, v) lies between ‖v‖²_{H¹} := ‖v‖²_μ + Σ_{j,k}‖∂_jv_k‖²_μ and (1 + c₁)‖v‖²_{H¹} on C₀^∞. Both V₁ and H¹(μ)^n embed continuously in H₁ and are the closures of C₀^∞ in these equivalent norms (#DA.1-1), so V₁ = H¹(μ)^n.
  {#DA.1-2.1} Both sides of the polarised identity of #DA.1-2 are continuous on V₁ ((4.7), p.301; Φ″ ≤ c₁), so a₁(v, w) = Σ_{j,k}(∂_jv_k | ∂_jw_k)_μ + (Φ″v | w)_μ on V₁. Hence a₁(v, v) ≥ c₀‖v‖²_μ on V₁, and the self-adjoint A₁ (p.302, after (4.8)) satisfies A₁ ≥ c₀ and maps D(A₁) bijectively onto H₁.
  {#DA.1-3} *The identity.* For g₁, g₂ ∈ H¹(μ), (g₁ − ⟨g₁⟩ | g₂ − ⟨g₂⟩)_μ = (A₁^{−1}dg₁ | dg₂)_μ (Johnsen Proposition 4.5, (4.34), p.306). Its hypothesis (4.33), closed range of A₀, holds when Φ″ ≥ c₀ > 0 (p.307, the sentence after (4.39)). For real g₁, g₂ the left side is Cov_μ(g₁, g₂).
- {#DA.2} For φ: {1, …, n} → ℝ let D_φ be the diagonal matrix with entries e^{φ(a)}, acting on ℂ^n-valued functions pointwise. For a real n × n matrix M let M_s := (M + M^T)/2.

## The Weighted Bound

- {#DA.3} *Lemma A1.* Under #DA.1, let φ be such that m_φ := inf_x λ_min((D_φΦ″(x)D_φ^{−1})_s) > 0. Then for real g₁, g₂ ∈ H¹(μ), |Cov_μ(g₁, g₂)| ≤ m_φ^{−1}‖D_φdg₁‖_{L²(μ)}‖D_φ^{−1}dg₂‖_{L²(μ)}.
  {#DA.3-1} *D_φ preserves D(A₁).* D_φ multiplies each component by a constant, so it preserves V₁ = H¹(μ)^n (#DA.1-2). For v ∈ D(A₁) and w ∈ V₁, the form of #DA.1-2.1 gives a₁(D_φv, w) = a₁(v, D_φw) + ((Φ″D_φ − D_φΦ″)v | w)_μ = (D_φA₁v + [Φ″, D_φ]v | w)_μ, since D_φ is real, diagonal and constant. As [Φ″, D_φ]v ∈ H₁, (4.4)–(4.5) give D_φv ∈ D(A₁) with A₁D_φv = D_φA₁v + [Φ″, D_φ]v. The same holds with −φ.
  {#DA.3-2} *Accretivity.* So T := D_φA₁D_φ^{−1} maps D(A₁) bijectively onto H₁, with T^{−1} = D_φA₁^{−1}D_φ^{−1} (#DA.1-2.1), and by #DA.3-1 with −φ, Tu = A₁u + D_φ[Φ″, D_φ^{−1}]u = A₁u + (D_φΦ″D_φ^{−1} − Φ″)u. For u ∈ D(A₁), (A₁u | u)_μ = a₁(u, u), so by #DA.1-2.1 Re(Tu | u)_μ = Σ_{j,k}‖∂_ju_k‖²_μ + Re(D_φΦ″D_φ^{−1}u | u)_μ ≥ m_φ‖u‖²_μ, since for a real matrix M and u ∈ ℂ^n, Re⟨u, Mu⟩ = ⟨Re u, M_sRe u⟩ + ⟨Im u, M_sIm u⟩.
  {#DA.3-3} Hence for y ∈ H₁ and w := T^{−1}y, m_φ‖w‖² ≤ Re(Tw | w) = Re(y | w) ≤ ‖y‖‖w‖, so ‖T^{−1}‖ ≤ m_φ^{−1}. Since A₁^{−1} = D_φ^{−1}T^{−1}D_φ and D_φ^{−1} is real and diagonal, #DA.1-3 gives Cov_μ(g₁, g₂) = (A₁^{−1}dg₁ | dg₂)_μ = (T^{−1}D_φdg₁ | D_φ^{−1}dg₂)_μ, which gives the bound.
- {#DA.4} *Lemma A2 (local Hessians).* Under #DA.1, let d be a pseudometric on {1, …, n}, and suppose Φ″ ≥ m and |Φ″_{ab}(x)| ≤ Ke^{−κd(a,b)} for all x, a, b. Let 0 < λ < κ and S_λ := sup_aΣ_bd(a, b)e^{−(κ−λ)d(a,b)}. If |φ(a) − φ(b)| ≤ λd(a, b) for all a, b and KλS_λ ≤ m/2, then m_φ ≥ m/2.
  {#DA.4-1} *Proof.* D_φΦ″D_φ^{−1} − Φ″ =: R has entries R_{ab} = Φ″_{ab}(e^{φ(a)−φ(b)} − 1). By |e^t − 1| ≤ |t|e^{|t|}, |R_{ab}| ≤ Kλd(a, b)e^{−(κ−λ)d(a,b)}, so the row and column sums of |R| are ≤ KλS_λ, and ‖R‖ ≤ KλS_λ ≤ m/2 by the Schur test. So ⟨u, (D_φΦ″D_φ^{−1})_su⟩ ≥ ⟨u, Φ″u⟩ − ‖R‖|u|² ≥ (m/2)|u|².
- {#DA.5} *Corollary A3 (separated gradients).* Under #DA.4, let S₁, S₂ ⊂ {1, …, n} with d(S₁, S₂) ≥ D, and real g₁, g₂ ∈ H¹(μ) with ∂_ag_i = 0 off S_i. Then |Cov_μ(g₁, g₂)| ≤ (2/m)e^{−λD}‖dg₁‖_{L²(μ)}‖dg₂‖_{L²(μ)}.
  {#DA.5-1} *Proof.* φ(a) := λmin(d(a, S₁), D) is λ-Lipschitz for d. It vanishes on S₁ and equals λD on S₂. So ‖D_φdg₁‖ = ‖dg₁‖ and ‖D_φ^{−1}dg₂‖ = e^{−λD}‖dg₂‖, and #DA.3 with #DA.4 gives the bound.
- {#DA.6} *Corollary A5 (kernel form).* Under #DA.4, for real g₁, g₂ ∈ H¹(μ), |Cov_μ(g₁, g₂)| ≤ (2/m)Σ_{a,b}e^{−λd(a,b)}‖∂_ag₁‖_{L²(μ)}‖∂_bg₂‖_{L²(μ)}.
  {#DA.6-1} *Proof.* Let e_a be the a-th unit vector of ℂ^n, u_a := (∂_ag₁)e_a and w_b := (∂_bg₂)e_b, both in H₁. By #DA.1-3 and sesquilinearity, Cov_μ(g₁, g₂) = Σ_{a,b}(A₁^{−1}u_a | w_b)_μ. Fix a, b and let φ(c) := λmin(d(c, a), d(a, b)), which is λ-Lipschitz for d, with φ(a) = 0 and φ(b) = λd(a, b). By #DA.4, m_φ ≥ m/2. By #DA.3-2, A₁^{−1} = D_φ^{−1}T^{−1}D_φ, so (A₁^{−1}u_a | w_b)_μ = (T^{−1}D_φu_a | D_φ^{−1}w_b)_μ, and #DA.3-3 gives |(A₁^{−1}u_a | w_b)_μ| ≤ (2/m)‖D_φu_a‖‖D_φ^{−1}w_b‖ = (2/m)e^{−λd(a,b)}‖∂_ag₁‖‖∂_bg₂‖.
