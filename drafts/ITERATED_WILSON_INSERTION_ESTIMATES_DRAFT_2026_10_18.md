# Draft updates for repeated blocking of the retained Wilson insertion

This is a proposal for strengthening the first-step output so it can serve as input to an induction. The active task is the first-step construction and its estimates, not a second RG integration. It starts from the first-step quantities in WILSON_FIRST_RG_STEP_V2.tex: rho_1, N_1, kappa_C, the closed flux word L_xi, and the conditional source correction H_xi. It does not replace the current manuscript estimates or assume that their hypotheses hold at later steps.

## 1. The object that must be retained

After the first extraction the numerator is kappa_C^{-1}N_1 and the vacuum density is rho_1. The exact representation retains, under the history operation, exp(E_xi^circ)L_xi and kappa_C^{-1}H_xi. The function H_xi is defined by the original conditional integral, including its denominator. It is not discarded after estimating its integrated size.

A later integration must act on these source terms with its own vacuum density, histories, inherited fields, gauge factors and boundary factors. It may create several correlated attachments. The geometric class should therefore allow finite closed Wilson networks near the original contour, including merged attachments and terms joining distinct sides. A factor Y_c or an open transport is not a separately estimated or propagated activity.

## 2. Update the scalar extraction at every step

The next scalar extraction must be applied to the connected source terms produced by that integration, including terms descending from H_xi and the earlier flux insertions. Reusing the first bare-Wilson coefficient at every step would omit scalar terms generated by those descendants.

The proposed procedure is to isolate the local identity contribution along each straight segment and the local identity contribution at each corner, using the flat evaluation of that step's field-dependent source interaction. Subtract those contributions within the same closed contractions. Keep the remaining source terms. As in the first step, changes of regional kernel, domain or periodic geometry require explicit comparison errors. A normalised reference-loop ratio alone does not identify the subtraction inside the inherited source.

The actual local activity index set and this projection must be constructed from the second-step integrals before an activity norm or recurrence can be asserted. In particular, no unnamed source kernel, independence of inherited fields, or division by an individual activity is an input to this proposal.

## 3. Conjectural geometric scaling of the retained marks

Let a_k=L^k epsilon be the lattice spacing after k blockings, and let C_k be the blocked image of the fixed physical square. In the regular logarithm chart, for an oriented plaquette p based at a contour vertex x define

$$
F_{k,p}=\frac{1}{i a_k^2}\log U_k(\partial p)\;.
$$

Here U_k is the bond field on that lattice and the logarithm is the same principal group chart as in the manuscript. Let h_{k,x} be the holonomy of the whole closed C_k based at x. A model for a retained curvature mark is the scalar

$$
a_k^2\left.\frac{d}{dt}\chi(e^{itF_{k,p}}h_{k,x})\right|_{t=0}\;.
$$

Under a gauge transformation, F_{k,p} and h_{k,x} are simultaneously conjugated at x, so this is a gauge-invariant closed contraction. The field F_{k,p} is used only inside that contraction; no separate norm of a Lie-algebra component or open segment is proposed. The formula illustrates a possible local source structure. It is not a claimed expansion of H_xi, and the source class cannot be restricted to these linear marks without checking its closure.

Straight-edge power counting predicts a factor L^{1-Delta} for a local mark of canonical dimension Delta: L fine attachment positions merge into one coarse position, while a_k^Delta=(L a_k)^Delta L^{-Delta}. For a curvature mark Delta=2, the predicted factor is L^{-1}. A point attachment at a corner instead has predicted factor L^{-Delta}. These are scaling conjectures for the projected source terms, not probabilistic estimates. The cancellation of all terms with the scaling of an edge or corner identity must be proved by the scalar extraction, not inferred from this count.

This suggests that the useful induction should separate local identity coefficients from the other marks rather than estimate their sum by an undifferentiated loop-length exponential. It does not justify replacing typical plaquette curvature by a deterministic small continuum curvature.

## 4. Quantitative changes needed from the first-step bound

The present whole-loop bound contains

$$
\exp\left(C_\chi n_C\rho
 (\varepsilon_1+e^{-cb/(LM)})\right)-1\;.
$$

It supplies no contraction for the inherited source activities. For repeated blocking the proposed replacements are:

- A signed local estimate after the relevant young-field integrations, with its exact conditional denominator. It must retain the cancellations within a connected source term and all companion terms that are needed for them. Absolute values should be taken only after that specified signed integral, not on its Dirac tensors or open matrix factors.
- A first-step support-weighted bound that retains the scalar subtraction, curvature factor and local background derivatives. These are the proposed inputs to a later blocking estimate; the predicted L^{-1} gain is not to be proved by performing a second step in this task. The first-step source correction must be resolved into its existing regular and boundary terms rather than replaced by a fresh Wilson loop or a flat toy source.
- A treatment of several attachments and of networks joining different sides, with multiplicities and extent costs retained. Noncommutative ordering is part of the source, and products of child characters cannot replace it.
- A boundary estimate with enough exponential reserve left to sum the evolving collar dependence over histories. Regional scalar errors and boundary-response terms must stay together where their signed cancellation is used. The estimate for a single chosen regular region is not by itself a history bound.
- A joint source/vacuum normalisation estimate for the inherited density. Each stage must use its own original physical denominator; a product of one-root ratios or a freshly normalised Gaussian source is not a substitute.

These are proposed requirements. A formula for a numerical activity norm is deliberately not supplied before its local source operands and their label set have been constructed. The present full-loop correction H_xi is defined, but its decomposition into a class stable under a second step is still to be made.

## 5. Active task: the renormalised first-step output

Use the first-step group functions c_W(U;h), flat coefficients a_W, joint defects R_xi(B), and restricted reference sums of Stage1 prop:joint-extraction and Stage4 Section6. These are already defined; their expansion algebra is not to be repeated. The subtraction is

$$
d_W(U;h)=c_W(U;h)-a_W\chi(h)\;.
$$

It satisfies d_W(1;h)=0. The closed joint defect R_xi(B) uses these differences in the parent contraction, with its ordering retained. After scalar extraction, the regular conditional source is the existing exact expression

$$
\frac{F_\xi(0)}{S^\circ_{\xi,C}}
 =f_\xi(0)+
 \sum_{\varnothing\ne\mathfrak B\ \mathrm{compatible}}
 \frac{S^{\circ,[\mathfrak B]}_{\xi,C}}{S^\circ_{\xi,C}}
 R_\xi(\mathfrak B)\;.
$$

The denominator here is the nonzero regional reference scalar, not the physical denominator. This formula is used inside Stage1 eq:c-one-step-branch-identity: the factor exp(c_xi) I_{xi;1}, the boundary term B_xi[f_xi], every history weight and the final rho_1 and physical Z_0 remain present. The leading source f_xi(0) is the reconstructed closed flux word, not an undecorated coarse loop. Uncovered branches keep their original conditional source and weights.

The improvement sought is a support-weighted analytic and curvature estimate for these subtracted closed insertions, including the joint reference ratio, together with a localised boundary estimate. The unsubtracted bound on c_W is an ingredient rather than the target. In particular the constant Taylor coefficient of d_W must be absent before taking a norm; the local background derivatives and their support costs must be bounded on the same first-step recipe. The remaining closed flux insertions are retained separately and must not be assigned a quantum rho factor they do not possess.

No second blocking integral is to be performed in this task. The scaling count in Section3 describes why this first-step structure is a candidate for iteration; it is not a contraction estimate to be claimed at the first step.

The first-step calculation is written in SECOND_RG_SOURCE_ANALYTIC_ATTEMPT_2026_10_18.md, Sections12--17. It defines a Taylor norm for d_W beginning at order one, allocates the joint reference ratio by its connected cluster occurrences, states the reassembly constraints and factorials, and attempts the weighted activity payment. It also represents the good-box arguments explicitly by logarithms of based fundamental-loop holonomies and substitutes the result into the numerator with E_xi^circ and the boundary source retained. The proof remains subject to the holomorphic selected-integral and pin inputs and to checking the new grouping/payment. Boundary and wrapping sectors retain their separate estimates. These qualifications are not replaced by a claim that a second step has been performed.

## 6. A later contraction must separate scaling from the analytic constants

The attempted complex-source estimate is in SECOND_RG_SOURCE_ANALYTIC_ATTEMPT_2026_10_18.md. Even if established, its Cauchy derivative constant contains inverse powers of a fixed complex radius delta, which can depend on L and M. Combining such a generic constant with the geometric L^{-1} count would give C_{L,M}/L, not automatically a number below one. That would not establish an inductive contraction.

The projected second-step estimate must therefore identify the linear blocking action on the retained local source structures, or use an explicitly adapted norm that controls it, and then estimate the interacting corrections. The scalar extraction must remove every identity contribution generated in this operation. A usable inequality would need its linear coefficient strictly below one; small coupling can absorb a genuinely coupling-small correction, but cannot fix an uncontrolled linear constant. No such inequality is asserted in this proposal.

For the first application, CRE (3.12) is insufficient to supply the curvature gain because its displayed prefactor L^2 cancels the putative L^{-2}. The stronger regularity of U_{k+1} quoted after CRE (3.17), after the next coarse-field cutoff, is the relevant potential input. Its use must preserve the stated regular-history support and keep the complementary history contribution rather than applying it to all histories.

## 7. Boundary template from CRE for the first-step output

CRE, in the paragraph preceding (2.24), distinguishes the renormalised regular interaction from the boundary interaction and states that the latter needs no additional renormalisation. In (2.41)--(2.42) it localises boundary terms on domains meeting the enlarged large-field region. For fixed exterior fields and history labels the local terms have gauge-invariant analytic extensions in the specified background/force variables (U,J), with exponential domain decay. The paragraph following (2.42) permits resumming domains meeting one large-field component, retaining decay in the complement of that component.

CRE (3.28)--(3.29) retains both the interaction derivatives and the derivative of the characteristic function produced by integration by parts, under the normalised interacting conditional law. For the Wilson source, Stage3's B_xi[f_xi] already retains these contributions in its response estimate. The further first-step task is their local closed-insertion representation, not a second RG integration and not a declaration that the entire history-dependent function is holomorphic. The present U-only analytic norm for the regular coefficients is not by itself the independent (U,J) extension of CRE. Neither that extension nor the boundary activity bound is assumed in the new regular-component calculation.
