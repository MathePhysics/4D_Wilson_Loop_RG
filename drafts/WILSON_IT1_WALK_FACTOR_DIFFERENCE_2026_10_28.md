## 2. What the Two Runs Share (Derived)

- *Shared.* The data of every layer of the R-operation's sets lie at shared scales j ≥ k/2 − 1 ≥ 1 (LF note §4(d), *The lifts and the chain rule.*). The big blocks of 𝒟_j are cells of ML^jηℤ^d, and ML^jε = ML^{j+1}(ε/L), so the partitions 𝒟, the cubes □, □̃ⁿ, the functions h_□ and ζ_{□̃} as functions of the point of space, the localisation domains X, the walks ω, the cubes σ₀ of RG II, and the s-weights are the same in both runs. The partition functions are defined at the end of PRT2 Sect. A as functions on the lattice; that they are restrictions of one function of the continuum to the two lattices is not checked here.
- *Not shared.* The factors act on, or take values in, fields and sources on each run's own fine lattice, T_ε ∩ Ω₀^A(□) or T_{ε/L} ∩ Ω₀^B(□): G̃ and every factor K(h)G_□h act between fine sources and fine fields, and H and H₀ map data to fine fields (§1(a), (d)).
- *The two local sequences.* For □ at the shared scale j, run A's sequence is {Ω_n^A(□)}_{n=0,…,j+1} with η = ε, and run B's is {Ω_{n′}^B(□)}_{n′=0,…,j+2} with η = ε/L. By PBG p.408 l.32, dist(Ω_n^c, □̃⁴) = 2R₀M₀L^nη(L^{j−n} − 1)/(L − 1). For run B at n′ = n + 1 this is 2R₀M₀L^nε(L^{j−n} − 1)/(L − 1), run A's value at n. So Ω_{n+1}^B(□) = Ω_n^A(□) for n = 0, …, j + 1, the shells of the two runs at a common spacing L^nε coincide, and run B has one extra outer shell Ω₀^B ∖ Ω₁^B of width 2R₀M₀ε/L at the spacing ε/L, where run A's operators have their Dirichlet exterior. On run A's shell 0 the two runs' spacings agree only after one averaging step: run A's field lives there on T_ε itself, and run B's field on T_{ε/L} is averaged once.

## 3. (W-δ) as Printed, and the Form the Consumer Needs (Derived)

(a) *As printed.* (W-δ) asks that "the two runs' factors R_α(X_i) … differ by at most δ_w(X_i) times their bound in (1.7)" (LF note l.351). By §2 the two runs' factors act between different spaces, except those from data to data (the factors h_□C_□h_□ of (3.98)), so their difference is not defined. By §1(d), (1.7) bounds whole walk terms, and PBG states no precise bound per factor. The LF note's sentence that the walks are the same in both runs (l.349) holds (§2); the fluctuation variable B on the data space is shared, but the fields in which the walk terms of H, H₀, and G̃ take values are not.

(b) *The averaged form.* Let W^X_ω denote the term of the walk ω in run X's expansion of H, H₀, or G̃, at run X's background. Let Q₁ := Q̄₁(U^B) be the linear part of the one-step double bar at run B's background, as in the Cauchy note's §10.4, and ℒ a right inverse of Q₁. The averaged form is, per walk term,

  |(Q₁W^B_ω − W^A_ω𝒥)λ(x)| ≤ δ_w(ω)·O(1)(L^jη)^pO(M^{−1/2})^{|ω|}M^{−½|ω|}e^{−(δ₀/6)d(ω,y,y′)}|λ|,

  with 𝒥 the identity on data (H and H₀) and 𝒥 = ℒ* on sources (G̃), (L^jη)^p the power of (3.108) for the norm in question, and the same for the norms of (3.42)–(3.47). By the Cauchy note's §10.4, Q₁ maps run B's constraint set {Q^B𝒜 = B} into run A's, so (b) is the comparison through the chain rule.

(c) *The fine form.* With ℒ_ι := dι_Y(Ũ), the data derivative of the localised lift ι_Y of V(Y) (LF note (i); the Cauchy note's *The lift.* before its §1, §9.7, and §9.8) at the aligned background, the fine form is, per walk term,

  |(W^B_ω − ℒ_ιW^A_ω𝒥′)λ(x)| ≤ δ_w(ω) times the bound of (b) in run B's norms,

  with 𝒥′ the identity on data and 𝒥′ = Q₁* on sources. Since Q₁ℒ_ι = id, (c) implies (b) with δ_w multiplied by the norm of Q₁.

(d) *Which form the consumer needs.* The consumer is Σ_YV(Y), whose terms are numbers. Each is built from a function 𝐄(□₀, 𝐔, 𝐀) of RG II (1.8), analytic in the fine field 𝐀 on the domain (I.3.31), at 𝐀^X(s) := (tζ̃_□ + t_□ζ_□)𝐇^X_k(s), which satisfies (I.3.31) with ½α₂ on the t_□ circle (RG II p.7 l.8–11). Here 𝐄^X and 𝐀^X(s) are run X's own, on its own fine lattice. Each localised term is a Cauchy integral in s on |s(Δ)| = e^{κ₁} (RG II (1.23), p.7 l.17–23), so the run difference must be bounded on the whole polydisc, not only at s = 1. The difference of values splits as

  𝐄^B(𝐔^B, 𝐀^B(s)) − 𝐄^A(𝐔^A, 𝐀^A(s)) = [𝐄^B(𝐔^B, 𝐀^B(s)) − 𝐄^B(𝐔^B, ℒ_ι𝐀^A(s))] + [𝐄^B(𝐔^B, ℒ_ι𝐀^A(s)) − 𝐄^A(𝐔^A, 𝐀^A(s))].

  The second bracket is a frozen difference of the kind of the LF note's (i), at a run-A argument, with the fine side carried by ι and ℒ_ι. The first bracket is run B's function at two run-B fluctuation fields. Cauchy's estimate in 𝐀 on (I.3.31) bounds it by a multiple of the size of 𝐀^B(s) − ℒ_ι𝐀^A(s) relative to ½α₂, which is the form (c) together with (Wδ-3). The form (b) controls only Q₁𝐀^B(s) − 𝐀^A(s): it leaves the part of 𝐀^B(s) − ℒ_ι𝐀^A(s) in ker Q₁ uncontrolled. At s = 1 that part is controlled by the chain's alignment, since the decoupled field there is the minimiser's and ι(MU^B) = U^B. At complex s the decoupled field is not a minimiser's (LF note l.348). So (b) is well posed but does not suffice, and the consumer needs (c). This is (Wδ-2).

## 4. The Intertwined Galerkin Identities (Derived, Given (S))

Fix □ at the shared scale j and one of the local operators of §1(c): 𝒪^X := Ω₀Δ_aΩ₀ (for G_□) or Ω₀Δ′_aΩ₀ (for G′_□), on the space 𝔉^X of fields on the bonds (or sites) of T^X ∩ Ω₀^X(□), with run B's background U^B and run A's aligned background Ũ := MU^B. The pairings ⟨·,·⟩_X carry the weights η_X^d of PBG p.393 l.46 and are bilinear, so 𝒪^X is symmetric on the complex domain (3.37) as the analytic extension of a symmetric form. G^X := (𝒪^X)^{−1} on 𝔉^X. Let χ_A be the characteristic function of Ω₀^A(□), and let ℒ be a right inverse of Q₁ with

  (S) ℒ maps 𝔉^A into 𝔉^B.

The local bilinear defect is β_□(ℱ, 𝒜) := ⟨χ_AQ₁ℱ, 𝒪^Aχ_AQ₁𝒜⟩_A − ⟨ℱ, 𝒪^B𝒜⟩_B for ℱ, 𝒜 ∈ 𝔉^B, and β_□(·, 𝒜)^♯ is the source on T_{ε/L} ∩ Ω₀^B(□) that represents ℱ ↦ β_□(ℱ, 𝒜) on 𝔉^B.

(a) *The coarse side.* For every source g on T_{ε/L} ∩ Ω₀^B(□),

  χ_AQ₁G^Bg − G^Aχ_Aℒ*g = G^Aχ_Aℒ*β_□(·, G^Bg)^♯.

  Proof. Put u_B := G^Bg and u_A := G^Aχ_Aℒ*g. For X ∈ 𝔉^A, ℒX ∈ 𝔉^B by (S), and χ_AQ₁ℒX = X. So ⟨X, 𝒪^Au_A⟩_A = ⟨ℒX, g⟩_B = ⟨ℒX, 𝒪^Bu_B⟩_B and ⟨X, 𝒪^Aχ_AQ₁u_B⟩_A = ⟨χ_AQ₁ℒX, 𝒪^Aχ_AQ₁u_B⟩_A. Their difference is β_□(ℒX, u_B) = ⟨X, ℒ*β_□(·, u_B)^♯⟩_A. Both χ_AQ₁u_B − u_A and the right side lie in 𝔉^A, X is arbitrary there, and 𝒪^A is invertible on 𝔉^A.

(b) *The fine side.* For every source f on T_ε ∩ Ω₀^A(□),

  ℒG^Af − G^BQ₁*χ_Af = −G^Bβ_□(·, ℒG^Af)^♯.

  Proof. Put u_A := G^Af and u_B := G^BQ₁*χ_Af. For Y ∈ 𝔉^B, ⟨Y, 𝒪^Bu_B⟩_B = ⟨χ_AQ₁Y, f⟩_A = ⟨χ_AQ₁Y, 𝒪^Au_A⟩_A = ⟨χ_AQ₁Y, 𝒪^Aχ_AQ₁ℒu_A⟩_A, since χ_AQ₁ℒu_A = u_A by (S). So ⟨Y, 𝒪^B(ℒu_A − u_B)⟩_B = −β_□(Y, ℒu_A), and ℒu_A − u_B ∈ 𝔉^B.

  With ℒ = ℒ_ι this is the identity of the form (c). The Cauchy note's representation E = G₁^Qℒ*β(·, 𝒜^B) of §10.4 is the analogue of (a) for the data lift H₁ on the whole lattice.

(c) *Why the kernel route fails.* Step 2 of the suggested route asks for a kernel bound on δ𝒪, for instance Q₁*𝒪^AQ₁ − 𝒪^B on 𝔉^B. For a field 𝒜 that alternates at the spacing ε/L inside one block, with Q₁𝒜 = 0, ⟨𝒜, (Q₁*𝒪^AQ₁ − 𝒪^B)𝒜⟩_B = −⟨𝒜, 𝒪^B𝒜⟩_B, which is of order (ε/L)^{−2}‖𝒜‖². So the difference of the two runs' operators has no kernel bound with a small factor. It is small only as a bilinear form between fields regular at the scale of their shell, through the oscillation, and the identities of (a) and (b) use it only there: β_□ is paired with G^Bg or ℒG^Af, which are C^{1,β} by (3.43). The functional form (Wδ-1) is what turns this into kernel bounds (§5(c)).

(d) *The condition (S).* For the scalar operators (G′_□), (S) holds with the transported block-constant lift ℒ′f(x) := R(U^B(Γ_{y,x}))^{−1}f(y) for x ∈ B(y): by (3.19), Q′(U^B)ℒ′f(y) = Σ_{x∈B(y)}L^{−d}f(y) = f(y), so ℒ′ is an exact right inverse of finite range one block. Ω₀^A(□) is a union of big blocks, and the margin 2R₀M₀ε/L of §2 exceeds one block of T_ε when 2R₀M₀ ≥ L, so ℒ′ maps 𝔉^A into 𝔉^B. ℒ′ is also an isometry, ⟨ℒ′f, ℒ′f′⟩_B = ⟨f, f′⟩_A, since the bilinear Killing form is invariant under Ad of the complexified group, so the Gram defect of the lift in Q′G′²Q′* vanishes for this ℒ′. For one-forms, Q₁ = Q̄₁(U^B) is the main term of CMP 98 (124), the straight-path average of the tube, plus terms that vanish at U = 1 but not at a regular U^B. The tube-constant lift inverts the main term exactly, but its right inverse for Q̄₁(U^B) obtained by a Neumann series has exponentially decaying tails, as do the Cauchy note's ℒ and dι. A right inverse of Q̄₁(U^B) of finite range is not derived here, so (S) for one-forms is part of (Wδ-1).

## 5. The Shells of the Local Problems

Shells are indexed by run A's n: run A's shell n and run B's shell n + 1 coincide (§2), and run B's extra shell is indexed n = −1. For a block y of the shell n, ‖𝒜‖_{1,β;y} := sup((L^nη)^{−1}|𝒜| + |∇𝒜|) + (L^nη)^β‖∇𝒜‖_β, taken over the blocks within weighted distance 2 of y, with the Hölder quotient of PBG (3.40); this is the norm of (Wδ-1).

(a) *The averaging terms (derived).* The a-terms are where the chain rule acts.
- Q*aQ of (3.16) carries the plain a and the weight (L^nη)^{d−2} on the bonds of Λ_n. Run B's layer Λ^B_{n+1} is run A's Λ^A_n, with the weight (L^{n+1}ε/L)^{d−2} = (L^nε)^{d−2}, and Q^B_{n+1}(U^B) = Q^A_n(Ũ)Q₁ by (B4), exactly (the Cauchy note's §10.4), with Q^A₀ the identity on run A's shell 0 (the 0-fold composition of (3.15)). For n ≥ 1, Q^A_n averages inside Ω^A₁(□), and for n = 0 the factor χ_A is 1 on Λ^A₀, so χ_A changes nothing. Hence the a-part of β_□ is exactly −aΣ_{b∈Λ^B₀}(ε/L)^{d−2}⟨ℱ(b), 𝒜(b)⟩, supported on run B's extra shell, with s₀ of size ≤ a(ε/L)^{−1}‖𝒜‖_{1,β;y} there in the normalisation of (Wδ-1).
- Q′*aQ′ of (3.24) carries a_n, with a_{n+1} = aa_n/(aL^{−2} + a_n) and a₁ = a₀ = a (p.394 l.29), so 1/a_n = (1/a)Σ_{i=0}^{n−1}L^{−2i} for n ≥ 1. The chain rule Q′^B_{n+1} = Q′^A_nQ′(U^B) is the composition (3.19), with the averaged configurations of (3.19) identified with the aligned ones as in (B1) and (B4). Run A's shell n carries a_n and run B's the same shell a_{n+1}. They agree at n = 0, and for n ≥ 1, 1/a_{n+1} − 1/a_n = L^{−2n}/a, so |a_{n+1} − a_n|/a_n ≤ L^{−2n}/(1 − L^{−2}). So the scalar a-part has the form of (Wδ-1) with ϖ_n replaced by L^{−2n}/(1 − L^{−2}) ≤ 2L^{−βn} for β ≤ 2, plus the extra-shell term as above.

(b) *The other terms (open, (Wδ-1)).* The chain rule does not act on D*D (Δ_U for scalars), on the curvature term Δ′ of (3.10), or on DRD* = DD* − DPD* (p.414 l.3). On run A's shells n ≥ 1 their defect is the oscillation of the Cauchy note's §10.4 at the ratio ε/(L^nε) = L^{−n}, which the Cauchy note derives for the global single-scale G₁ at H₁ fields and not for the local multiscale operators. DPD* is nonlocal through P = G′Q′*(Q′G′²Q′*)^{−1}Q′G′, so its defect needs the scalar comparisons first, then P, then G. On run A's shell 0 and on run B's extra shell nothing relates the two runs' operators: there run A's D*D on T_ε meets run B's averaged once, and run B's extra shell meets run A's Dirichlet exterior. So, shell by shell, the chain rule reduces the averaging terms of every shell, the scale-0 shell included, to the shared lattice, and it does not reduce the Laplacian, curvature, and gauge-fixing parts of the scale-0 shells. (c) shows that these parts need no reduction: with ϖ = 1 there, the decay through the shells makes their contribution of relative order L^{−j} = ε/a_j.

(c) *The reduction (derived, given (Wδ-1) and (S)).* Let ℒ satisfy (S) with range one block of T_ε and with Σ_{z∈Δ(y)}η_B^d(|∇_Bℒv(z)| + (L^nη)^{−1}|ℒv(z)|) ≤ C_ℒΣ_{y‴}Σ_{w∈Δ(y‴)}η_A^d(|∇_Av(w)| + (L^nη)^{−1}|v(w)|), the sum over the 3^d blocks y‴ next to y. Let y, y′ be blocks of □ (layers j and j + 1 of the local sequence), x ∈ Δ(y), and supp g ⊂ Δ(y′). The conditions on PBG's R₀ and M₀ are

  (W-c) (2.59) at α = ⅓ for the local sequences, with the shell width 2R₀M₀ in place of RM, (1/6)δ₀R₀M₀ > 2d log c₀(1/6) + 1; and 4δ₀R₀M₀ ≥ 3(1 + β)log L; and 2R₀M₀ ≥ L (for §4(d)).

## 6. The Background Part (Derived)

A walk term W^A_ω depends on the configuration on ∪_iX̃_i⁵ (Theorem 3.10, p.416 l.7–8). By the LF note's (ii) (l.344), U^A = e^{iεA}Ũ with a_{j(x)}|A(x)| + a_{j(x)}²|∇A(x)| ≤ d̄(x) := C_U(M)(ε/a_{min(j(x),n)})^{(1−ε₀)/3}ε₁. Put d̄(ω) := sup_{x∈∪X̃_i⁵}d̄(x) and U(t) := e^{itεA}Ũ.
- *The domain.* For |t| ≤ α₁/(2d̄(ω)), U(t) satisfies (3.37) layer by layer for the global layers (LF note §4(d), *The multipliers.*, l.207). For the local sequence of a cube, (3.37) asks |A| < α₁(L^nη)^{−1} on the shell n, which is weaker on the finer shells since L^nη ≤ a_{j(x)}.
- *Analyticity with the bounds.* The local propagators extend analytically to this domain with the inequalities of Theorems 3.1–3.3 (Theorem 3.4, p.400 l.8–9, whose proof (3.82)–(3.86) uses only Theorems 3.1–3.3 at U, p.407 l.40, and these hold for the local sequences by Corollary 3.6). The explicit local operators are analytic in U by their formulas (3.88), (3.101)–(3.103). PBG's proof of (3.108) uses only the constituent bounds (p.410 l.13–30), so W^A_ω(U(t)) is analytic for |t| < α₁/(2d̄(ω)), with the bound (3.108) there.
- *Cauchy's estimate.* With r := α₁/(2d̄(ω)) ≥ 2, |W^A_ω(U^A) − W^A_ω(Ũ)| ≤ sup_{|t|≤1}|∂_tW^A_ω(U(t))| ≤ sup_{|t|=r}|W^A_ω(U(t))|/(r − 1) ≤ (4d̄(ω)/α₁) times the bound (3.108), in each norm of (3.108).
- *The current.* The operators of PBG Sect. D (G₁ and H₁, and with them VAR's H) carry the current. For them the path is the LF note's pair path, with 𝐉(s) = J(U^A) − sδ𝐉 and a_m³|𝐉(s) − J(U^A)| ≤ c_J|s|d̄(x) (l.355), and the analyticity holds on the pair class 𝒟̄ (l.192). Under 2B̄_Jε₁ ≤ α_J the same estimate holds with α_*′ := min(α₁, α_J/c_J) in place of α₁.
The background part is therefore ≤ 4d̄(ω)/α_*′ ≤ 4C_U(M)ε₁(ε/a_{m(ω)})^{(1−ε₀)/3}/α_*′ times the bound of the walk term, with m(ω) the finest global layer that ∪X̃_i⁵ meets, under 4d̄(ω) ≤ α_*′. If the consumer carries the change of background separately, as the LF note's (iii) does through r(Y), only the lattice part of §5 is needed.

## 7. The Walk Term, the Telescoping, and (1.11) (Derived, Given (Wδ-1)–(Wδ-3))

- *Constituents.* A walk term is a product of factors, and each factor is a product of at most N_c constituents. N_c is an absolute number: "several" operators, at worst three attached to one (PBG p.412 l.3–6, l.34–35, p.415 l.28–30). The constituents are the local propagators of §1(c), the multiplications by h_□, ζ_{□̃}, and characteristic functions, the explicit local operators K(h), P₁(∂h), S_j(∂h), D, D*, Q′, and Q′*, and the differences of (3.97).
- *Intertwining of the explicit constituents.* Q and Q′ intertwine exactly by (B4) and (3.19), and D by the covariance Q̄₁D_B = D_AQ′₁, PBG (3.114)–(3.115) at n = 1. For a multiplication by a function h of the point, Q₁(hf)(b) − h(b)(Q₁f)(b) is a sum over the fine bonds b′ of the tube of b of (h(b′) − h(b))f(b′) with the weights of Q₁. By the gradient bound |∂h| ≤ c_h(ML^jη)^{−1} (p.414 l.1–2, with c_h the constant of the O there), this is of relative order c_hε/(ML^jη) = c_h(ε/a_j)/M, and the same holds, with the factor C_ℒ, for ℒ* when ℒ has range one block. K(h), P₁(∂h), and S_j(∂h) are built from the operators with one factor ∂h (PBG (3.88), (3.101)–(3.104)), so their defects are of the type of β_□ and belong to (Wδ-1).
- *Telescoping.* Q₁W^B_ω − W^A_ω𝒥 is a sum, over the constituents c of the walk term, of the product with run A's constituents to the left of c, the intertwining defect of c, and run B's constituents to the right, with Q₁, ℒ*, or the identity between constituents according to the slot (fine field, fine source, or data). There are at most N_c(|ω| + 1) such terms. Suppose that each defect satisfies the bounds of its constituent times δ_c, at the rate δ₀/3 of §5(c). Then PBG's proof of (3.94) and (3.108) applies to each term: it inserts the localisations Δ(y_i), bounds each factor, and sums over the y_i with half of each exponential (p.410 l.13–30). So each term is ≤ δ_c times the bound (3.108) with the rate δ₀/6 in place of ½δ₀, and the walk term's difference is ≤ N_c(|ω| + 1)max_cδ_c times that bound. This is the per-walk-term form (b), and the same argument with ℒ_ι gives (c).
- *The absorption.* (|ω| + 1) ≤ 2^{|ω|}, so the factor O(M₁^{−1/2})^{|ω|} of (1.7) becomes (2O(M₁^{−1/2}))^{|ω|}. These factors "are used to control the sum over ω, and they determine the constant B₀" (RG II p.3 l.23–24). So the sum converges for M₁ four times Bałaban's lower bound, with B₀′, the constant B₀ with the doubled factor, in place of B₀.
- *The bound (1.11).* Bałaban's argument (p.5 l.13–16) uses δ₀d(ω) ≥ δ₁mM for m > 2⁴, with δ₁ depending on δ₀ only, and δ₁M ≥ κ₁. At the rate δ₀/6 this needs (W-M) δ₁(δ₀/6)M ≥ κ₁, a condition on M. Then on |s(Δ)| ≤ e^{κ₁}, |(Q₁H^B(s) − H^A(s))X| ≤ N_cδ_w(Y)B₀′e^{16κ₁}|X|, with δ_w(Y) the supremum of max_cδ_c over the walks in Y. The same holds for G̃(s), H₀(s), and the fine form (c). This is the LF note's l.349 inequality with N_c and B₀′ in place of 2 and B₀. A walk in Y meets only the layers of Y, so m(ω) ≥ m(Y) and (ε/a_{m(ω)})^{γ} ≤ (ε/a_{m(Y)})^{γ}.

## 8. What (W-δ) Would Be, and Its Status

Given (Wδ-1), (Wδ-2), and (Wδ-3), §§4–7 give the fine per-walk-term form (c), and with it (b), with

  δ_w(ω) ≤ C_w(ε/a_{m(ω)})^{γ_w}, γ_w := min(β, (1 − ε₀)/3), C_w := N_c[C_□(β) + c_h(1 + C_ℒ)/M] + 4C_U(M)ε₁/α_*′,

where β < 1 is the Hölder exponent at which (Wδ-1) holds, C_□(β) is that of §5(c), and m(ω) is the finest global layer that ∪X̃_i⁵ meets. C_w is a constant of M, β, L, d, and Bałaban's constants. It does not depend on k, K, ε, or the volume, since every constant above is local. The conditions are (S), (W-c), (W-M), 4d̄(ω) ≤ α_*′ with 2B̄_Jε₁ ≤ α_J, and the smallness conditions of the LF note's alignment that d̄ uses. γ_w > 1/4 holds if β > 1/4 and ε₀ < 1/4. If the background change is carried separately through r(Y), γ_w = β. The telescoping of the LF note's l.349 holds as checked in §7, with N_c and B₀′ in place of 2 and B₀ and with the rate δ₀/6 in (1.11), under (W-M).

This is not a proof of (W-δ). The lattice part rests on (Wδ-1), the consumer's fine form on (Wδ-2), and the passage from the propagators to 𝐇_k(s) on (Wδ-3), all open. The conditions (W-c) and (W-M) are lower bounds on PBG's R₀M₀ and on M. PBG takes R₀M₀ as the smallest pair satisfying the conditions of [4] (p.408 l.26–28), which are lower bounds, and B₀ and δ₀ depend on d and L only (Theorem 3.1, p.397 l.37). That a larger R₀M₀ leaves every step of PBG valid is a reading of p.408 l.26–28, not checked against PRT2.

## 9. The Suggested Route, Step by Step

1. *The factors.* Quoted and checked (§1). Each R_α(X) is a product of local propagators of PBG Sect. C on the sequence {Ω_n(□)}_{n=0,…,j+1} inside □̃⁵, with Dirichlet conditions on Ω₀(□)^c and the multiscale averagings Q and Q′ of the local layers down to the scale 0, at the run's background restricted to X̃⁵, together with partition functions and explicit local operators.
2. *The resolvent identity with a kernel bound on δ𝒪.* Fails as stated (§4(c)): the two runs' operators act on different lattices, and no common-space difference of them has a small kernel. It is replaced by the exact intertwined identities of §4(a)–(b), derived given (S), with the defect β_□ paired only against fields regular at their shell.
3. *The two parts.* The identities of §10.2 and §10.4 hold for the local operators with PBG's Dirichlet conditions in the intertwined form of §4, given (S). (S) is derived for the scalar operators and open for one-forms. The bounds (3.42)–(3.44) for the local operators are supplied by PBG (Corollary 3.6). The local defect bounds, the analogues of (10.6a)–(10.6c), are not supplied: (Wδ-1), open. The chain rule cancels the averaging terms of every shell (§5(a)), derived. The background part is derived (§6), on PBG's domain (3.37) and the pair class, not on Bałaban's old-term class.
4. *Multiscale domains.* Derived (§5(d)): the coarser layers contribute (ε/a_j)^β ≤ (ε/a_m)^β, and PBG's finer local shells cost only c_β.
5. *The non-inverse factors.* Derived for the averagings, the gauge directions, and the multiplications (§7, relative order ε/(ML^jη) for the multiplications). Open for K(h), P₁(∂h), and S_j(∂h), within (Wδ-1).
6. *The result.* §8: γ_w = min(β, (1 − ε₀)/3), C_w as stated, conditional on the open items. The telescoping of l.349 is checked against (1.7) and (1.11) in §7.
