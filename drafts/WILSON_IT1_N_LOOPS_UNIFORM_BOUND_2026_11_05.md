## 1. Setting (Definition and Derived Bounds) {#NLS1}

{#NL1.1} C₁, …, C_{N_C} are coarse translates of counterclockwise squares, each in a plane parallel to a coordinate plane of its own (the planes may differ), with V(C_i) ∩ V(C_j) = ∅ for i ≠ j, and χ_i, π_i as in #332. C := ⊔_iC_i and ℓ_Σ := Σ_iℓ_i. The step-k traversals of C are the 4ℓ_ΣL^{K′−k} traversals of the C_i, ordered C₁, …, C_{N_C}, each loop cyclically from its own base point.
{#NL1.1-1} *The planes.* #332.1 holds for each loop, with "every loop" for "both squares". So V4's choice of e₁, e₂, made through the axis permutation, costs nothing loop by loop, and the cross arguments use only distances between the sets V(C_i). Where §9 cites V4's pair lemmas, read "the square geometry of C_i" in C_i's own plane.
{#NL1.1-2} *The cross inputs, for any planes.* The stopping rule's q_{ii′} is the least q with the displacement between the corners of C_i and C_{i′} in L^{−q}uℤ⁴ (#MD2.3.4.15-11), for any orientations. #334's D ≥ 1 needs only that the loops are unions of step-K′ lattice bonds without a common site. #749's torus distances hold per loop. Every cross bound uses δ_n^{(k)} ≥ DL^{K′−k} for all traversals (#NL1.3), the worst case. That case is attained by congruent squares in parallel planes, stacked at distance D.
  {#NL1.1-2-1} For two squares in planes with no common direction (e₁e₂ and e₃e₄), the coordinates of C_i that vary along C_{i′} are fixed along C_i, so δ_n ≥ max(DL^{K′−k}, r_n), with r_n the ℓ^∞ distance of n's (e₁, e₂)-coordinates from the fixed (e₁, e₂)-coordinates of C_{i′}. A square's boundary has O(r) traversals with r_n ≤ r, so Δ_× ≤ O(DL^{K′−k} + M/ν_×)e^{−ν_×DL^{K′−k}/M}, with D in place of ℓ_Σ in the prefactor. The bounds hold as stated.
{#NL1.2} *Stopping.* K′ = K − j, with j the least j ≥ max(q_i, q_{ii′} : i ≠ i′) + q̄(j), the rule of #307 with the q₁₂ clause for every pair of loops (a common index for the family, as #ND.6.7-3 requires).
{#NL1.3} *The cross distance.* For a step-k traversal n of C_i, δ_n^{(k)} is the step-k ℓ^∞ torus distance from n to ∪_{i′≠i}C_{i′}. D := min_{i≠i′}dist(C_i, C_{i′}) in step-K′ units. #334 for each pair gives D ≥ 1 and δ_n^{(k)} ≥ DL^{K′−k}; D ≤ N_s/2.
{#NL1.4} Δ_×^{(k)} := Σ_ne^{−ν_×δ_n^{(k)}/M} over the step-k traversals of C, as in #335. So Δ_×^{(k)} ≤ 4ℓ_ΣL^{K′−k}e^{−ν_×DL^{K′−k}/M}.
{#NL1.5} *Constants.* Those of #337–#351, with C_χ := max_iC_{χ_i}, and with ν_× := min(ν_e, 2η/A′_E) and A′_E := (8N_C + 1)(2R_e + 1)⁴, from the count of #NL9.6-1 (17(2R_e + 1)⁴ at N_C = 2).
{#NL1.6} *Norms.* #352–#354 with the normalised trace on ⊗_iV_{π_i}: since tr(⊗_iA_i) = Π_itr A_i, a product of N_C normalised traces, one per loop, is the normalised trace of the tensor product, and |dim^{−1}tr(M₁⋯M_r)| ≤ Π_s‖M_s‖ for operators on ⊗_iV_{π_i}, as for two factors (V4 lem:two-loop-source, first sentence of the proof).

The squares are those of #NL1.1, each with V4's square/torus geometry. f = Π_iχ_i(Hol_UC_i), ℐ_C = ⊔_iℐ_{C_i}, and V4's traversal count is n_C = Σ_in_{C_i}. For a ∈ ℐ_{C_i}, δ_a is the periodic ℓ^∞ fine distance from a's initial site to ∪_{i′≠i}V(C_{i′}), and Δ_× := Σ_{a∈ℐ_C}e^{−c_×δ_a/(LM)}. Covered means covered for every square, the intersection of the single-square events, and far means far for every square.
{#NL9.2} *Groups* (def:two-loop-groups, lem:two-loop-grouping). H^i_γ is defined for every i ≤ N_C. Two components overlap when H^i_γ ∩ H^i_{γ′} ≠ ∅ for some i, and (A2) is imposed for each square. A C_i-group has ℋ_{i′}(W) = ∅ for i′ ≠ i, and a cross group has at least two nonempty ℋ_i(W). Compatibility asks disjoint intervals on every square. The proof of lem:two-loop-grouping(a)–(d) is per square, with "every other square" in place of "C_{3−i}". In (c), a C_i-group of the N_C squares is a single-square group of C_i none of whose roots has a contact on another square.
{#NL9.3} *Schur identity and joint extraction* (lem:two-loop-schur). Part (a) is per group, with χ_i for a C_i-group, and part (b) is verbatim, with the grouping lemma for N_C squares. In (c) the reference Wilson function at e_{𝒜(𝒮)}a is Π_iχ_i(Π_{W C_i-group}P°_W(a)), and each group is integrated by Fubini with the characters of the other squares as constant factors. So A°_ξ(𝒮) = Π_{W∈𝒮}a_W and R°_ξ(𝔅) = 0 for every nonempty family without cross groups.
{#NL9.4} *Sources* (lem:two-loop-source). f = Π_idim(π_i)^{−1}tr Y_i is the normalised trace of ⊗_iY_i, so #NL1.6 gives the inequality the proof uses. The bond sets of the squares are pairwise disjoint, so each bond carries at most one traversal of C and the row count is unchanged. The constant becomes C^src_{χ₁⋯χ_{N_C}}.
{#NL9.5} *Defects* (lem:two-loop-defect). The proof forms one word Y_i per square and uses the product of the N_C normalised traces; each X_W enters exactly one of them. So (two-loop-defect) holds, with K_χ := max_iK_{χ_i}.
{#NL9.6} *Pins* (lem:two-loop-pins).
- {#NL9.6-1} (a), count: ℋ(W) has at most |W| ≤ n(W) cyclic intervals on each square, each in at most 8 + t/(LM) cubes for t traversals. So |π₁(ℋ(W))| ≤ 8N_Cn(W) + |ℋ(W)|/(LM), |X(W) ∪ π₁(ℋ(W))| ≤ (8N_C + 1)𝔰(W), which gives #NL1.5's A′_E.
- {#NL9.6-2} (a), connectivity of a group W long on exactly the squares of a set T ≠ ∅. Each overlap class in W has a contact on a square of T. Otherwise splitting the class off W is admissible, as in V4's proof: the remainder is nonempty and keeps every component with a contact on a square of T, and the class, with no contact on a square of T and its hulls on every other square inside those of W, is long on no square. Let Γ be the graph on T joining i and i′ when a class of W has contacts on both.
  {#NL9.6-2-1} If Γ were disconnected, with a component T₁, the classes with a contact on a square of T₁ and the others would form an admissible splitting of W: both parts are nonempty, W being long on the squares of T₁ and of T ∖ T₁, neither part has a contact on a square of the other's part of T, each contains every component with a contact on its own squares of T, and neither is long on a square outside T. So Γ is connected.
  {#NL9.6-2-2} Each class meets π₁(ℐ_{C_i}) ⊆ π₁(ℋ(W)), a connected set, for a square C_i of T on which it has a contact, and a class with contacts on C_i and C_{i′} meets both π₁(ℐ_{C_i}) and π₁(ℐ_{C_{i′}}). Γ being connected, X(W) ∪ π₁(ℋ(W)) is connected. Groups long on no square are overlap classes, as in V4.
- {#NL9.6-3} (a), cross groups: π₁(ℋ(W)) contains a cube containing a site of V(C_i) for each square C_i with ℋ_i(W) ≠ ∅, at least two of them.
- {#NL9.6-4} (b): the hull pins are per square, and in the footprint pin at most CLM traversals of each square have stencils meeting a given cube, so at most N_CCLM in all.
- {#NL9.6-4a} (b), part (c) of lem:c4-8-2: 𝔰(γ) = n_γ + Σ_i|H^i_γ|/(LM) sums the hulls over the squares, so 𝔰(γ) ≤ (1 + N_CA_h)n_γ ≤ N_CA_sn_γ (#NL.3, from V4's 1 + 2A_h ≤ 2A_s). Then 100η𝔰(γ) < ϖn_γ/40 needs 1000ηA_s < ϖ/(4N_C), which is V4's 1000ηA_s < ϖ/8 at N_C = 2. η is chosen under this condition, before the coupling, and the rest of (b) is unchanged.
- {#NL9.6-5} (c): let W be long on exactly the squares of T, with a class K₀ long on C_{i₀}, i₀ ∈ T, which exists as in V4's proof. Since Σ_{γ∈W}Σ_{i∈T∖{i₀}}|H^i_γ| ≥ Σ_{i∈T∖{i₀}}n_{C_i}/2 and Σ_i|H^i_γ| ≤ LM𝔰(γ), one factor 1 ≤ e^{−15ηΣ_{i∈T∖{i₀}}n_{C_i}/(LM)}Π_{γ∈W}e^{30η𝔰(γ)} covers all of T∖{i₀}.
  {#NL9.6-5-1} The classes other than K₀ have contacts on squares of T (#NL9.6-2) and are anchored at traversals of these squares, so V4's computation for a group long on both squares, with Σ_{i∈T}n_{C_i} for n_C, gives at most Cρn_{C_{i₀}}e^{−10ηn_{C_{i₀}}/(LM)}. There are at most N_C2^{N_C−1} pairs (T, i₀), so the long groups have total weighted mass at most 2^{N_C−1}Σ_iCρn_{C_i}e^{−10ηn_{C_i}/(LM)} ≤ C′ρ, C′ := N_C2^{N_C−1}CLM/(10eη), which is V4's 2CLM/(5eη) at N_C = 2.
- {#NL9.6-6} (d): the proof applies with these constants, fixed with η before the coupling.
{#NL9.7} *Cross terms* (lem:two-loop-cross).
- {#NL9.7-1} (a): for a cross group W and a ∈ ℋ_i(W), 𝔈(W) contains a cube containing a site of some V(C_{i′}), i′ ≠ i (#NL9.6-3), so 𝔰(W) > δ_a/(A′_ELM), and the sum over cross groups is ≤ C_×ρΔ_× as in V4.
- {#NL9.7-2} (b): E^×_ξ := 𝓛 − Σ_i𝓛_i. A tuple of the N_C-square gas whose members are C_i-groups for one i is a tuple of the gas of C_i none of whose members touches another square, with the same Ψ, and conversely. So E^×_ξ is the sum over the tuples 𝒳₀ of the N_C-square gas with members on two or more squares, minus the sums over the tuples 𝒳_i of the gas of C_i with a member touching another square.
- {#NL9.7-3} The extent argument of V4's proof applies with V(C_{i′}), i′ ≠ i, for V(C_{3−i}). Pinning 𝒳₀ over ℐ_C and each 𝒳_i over ℐ_{C_i} gives |E^×_ξ| ≤ 2CρΔ_×, as for the pair, and |S°_{ξ,C}| ≤ Π_i|S°_{ξ,C_i}|e^{|E^×_ξ|} ≤ e^{C_×ρΔ_×}.
- {#NL9.7-4} (c): the single-square sums add to at most Σ_iC_{χ_i}n_{C_i}ρ(ε₁ + e^{−c_ib/(LM)}) ≤ C_χn_Cρ(ε₁ + e^{−cb/(LM)}), c := min_ic_i. The corollary cor:two-loop-scalar reads S°_{ξ,C} = Π_iκ_{C_i}e^{Σ_iE°_{ξ,i} + E^×_ξ}.
{#NL9.8} *The retreated carrier* (lem:two-loop-retreated). Its proof, (i)–(vi), uses the lemmas above at D(Λ) and the single-square inputs for each square. Verbatim with N_C squares.
{#NL9.9} *Inputs and the theorem* (lem:two-loop-inputs, thm:two-loop-theorem). By the product rule and |χ_{i′}(Y_{i′})| ≤ 1, h(∇_bf) ≤ Σ_iC_{resp,χ_i}g₀1_{I_{C_i}}(b), so (a) holds with Σ_iC_{resp,χ_i}.   {#NL9.9-1} In the proof of the theorem, part (iii), on A_cov, e^{Σ_i|E°_{ξ,i}|} ≤ e^{N_CCτ²(2 + 1/c)}exp(Cτ²Σ_{j∈J_C}e^{−cd_j}) replaces the factor e^{2Cτ²(2 + 1/c)}.
