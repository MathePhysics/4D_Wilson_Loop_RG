## 1. Setting (Definition and Derived Bounds) {#NLS1}

{#NL1.1} C₁, …, C_{N_C} are coarse translates of counterclockwise squares, each in a plane parallel to a coordinate plane of its own (the planes may differ), with V(C_i) ∩ V(C_j) = ∅ for i ≠ j, and χ_i, π_i as in #332. C := ⊔_iC_i and ℓ_Σ := Σ_iℓ_i. The step-k traversals of C are the 4ℓ_ΣL^{K′−k} traversals of the C_i, ordered C₁, …, C_{N_C}, each loop cyclically from its own base point.
{#NL1.1-1} *The planes.* #332.1 holds for each loop, with "every loop" for "both squares". So V4's choice of e₁, e₂, made through the axis permutation, costs nothing loop by loop, and the cross arguments use only distances between the sets V(C_i). Where §9 cites V4's pair lemmas, read "the square geometry of C_i" in C_i's own plane.
{#NL1.1-2} *The cross inputs, for any planes.* The stopping rule's q_{ii′} is the least q with the displacement between the corners of C_i and C_{i′} in L^{−q}uℤ⁴ (#MD2.3.4.15-11), for any orientations. #334's D ≥ 1 needs only that the loops are unions of step-K′ lattice bonds without a common site. #749's torus distances hold per loop. Every cross bound uses δ_n^{(k)} ≥ DL^{K′−k} for all traversals (#NL1.3).
{#NL1.2} *Stopping.* K′ = K − j, with j the least j ≥ max(q_i, q_{ii′} : i ≠ i′) + q̄(j), the rule of #307 with the q₁₂ clause for every pair of loops (a common index for the family, as #ND.6.7-3 requires).
{#NL1.3} *The cross distance.* For a step-k traversal n of C_i, δ_n^{(k)} is the step-k ℓ^∞ torus distance from n to ∪_{i′≠i}C_{i′}. D := min_{i≠i′}dist(C_i, C_{i′}) in step-K′ units. #334 for each pair gives D ≥ 1 and δ_n^{(k)} ≥ DL^{K′−k}; D ≤ N_s/2.
{#NL1.3-1} *Separation.* Let Q := max(q_i, q_{ii′} : i ≠ i′) and h := L^{j−Q}. Then D ≥ h ≥ L^{q̄(j)}.
  {#NL1.3-1.1} *Proof.* In step-K′ units put a corner of C₁ at the origin. The corners of C_i differ from it by vectors of L^{j−q_{1i}}ℤ⁴ (#MD2.3.4.15-11) and from each other by multiples of the side p_iL^{j−q_i}, so they lie in hℤ⁴, and C_i, having axis-parallel sides, is a union of edges of the lattice hℤ⁴. N_s/h = (T_ph/u)L^Q is an integer (#ND.1.1, #RP.1), so this holds on the torus.
  {#NL1.3-1.2} Let x ∈ C_i and y ∈ C_{i′}, i ≠ i′, with ‖x − y‖_∞ < h, on edges e ⊆ C_i parallel to e_μ and e′ ⊆ C_{i′} parallel to e_ν. The coordinates α ∉ {μ, ν} of x and y lie in hℤ and differ by less than h, so they agree. If μ = ν, e and e′ lie on one line parallel to e_μ, and |x_μ − y_μ| < h makes them share an endpoint.
  {#NL1.3-1.3} If μ ≠ ν, y_μ ∈ hℤ lies within h of x_μ, so it is an endpoint coordinate of e; likewise x_ν is one of e′. The point z with z_μ := y_μ, z_ν := x_ν and z_α := x_α otherwise lies on e and on e′. Either way C_i and C_{i′} share a point of hℤ⁴, a step-K′ site, against V(C_i) ∩ V(C_{i′}) = ∅. So D ≥ h, and h ≥ L^{q̄(j)} by #NL1.2.
{#NL1.3-2} *Separation at every step.* If γ²β′ ≤ 1 and β₀ ≤ 1 (the smallness of γ, and (T2), #CP.26), then δ_n^{(k)} ≥ DL^{K′−k} ≥ L²MR_k for 1 ≤ k ≤ K′. So a set of step-k ℓ^∞ diameter less than L²MR_k meets at most one loop. At the fine scale, D is at least L^{K′+3}M fine units, as L^{q̄(j)} ≥ L³M (#MOMGD.30), so a set of fine diameter less than L^{K′+3}M meets at most one loop.
  {#NL1.3-2.1} *Proof.* R_k ≤ L(log g_k^{−2})^r (CRE (2.5)), and (log g_k^{−2})^r ≤ (1 + g_K²β′(K − k))^{β₀}(log g_K^{−2})^r (CRE (2.7) with n = K, m = k, as in #MD2.3.4.15-5). Put x := K′ − k, so K − k = j + x, and 1 + a(j + x) ≤ (1 + a(j + 1))(1 + ax) for a ≥ 0. With g_K ≤ γ and g_K ≥ g_min, MR_k ≤ LM(1 + γ²β′(j + 1))^{β₀}(log g_min^{−2})^r(1 + γ²β′x)^{β₀}.
  {#NL1.3-2.2} By the definition of q̄(j) (#MOMGD.30) the first factors are at most L^{q̄(j)−2}, and (1 + γ²β′x)^{β₀} ≤ 1 + x ≤ 2^x ≤ L^x for integers x ≥ 0. So L²MR_k ≤ L^{q̄(j)+x} ≤ DL^{K′−k} by #NL1.3-1.
{#NL1.3-5} *One loop per block.* For 1 ≤ k ≤ K′, a closed cube of step-k side s < L²MR_k meets at most one loop (#NL1.3-2) and at most 4(s + 1) of its bonds, s + 1 on each side (#715-1). A π_k-cube meets at most 8M bonds of ⊔_iC_i, all on one loop.
  {#NL1.3-5.1} *Proof of the last claim.* Its side M is below L²MR_k, so it meets one loop C_i. C_i lies in a 2-plane, which meets the cube's sites, if at all, in an M × M grid. Each of the M grid lines parallel to one side of C_i holds at most two sites of C_i unless it contains a side, and at most two lines do, with at most M sites each. So the cube holds at most 4M sites of C_i, each the end of two bonds of C_i, and a bond meets the cube when an end does (#628-1).
{#NL1.3-6} *Tube hulls.* Fix a step k ≥ 1 and δ := L²MR_k in step-k units, or L^{K′+3}M in fine units, so that points of distinct loops are at ℓ^∞ distance ≥ δ (#NL1.3-2), and let N_i be the open ℓ^∞ δ/4-neighbourhood of C_i, so distinct N_i are at distance ≥ δ/2. An *admissible piece* is a closed connected set meeting at most one N_i: for instance a set of diameter below δ/2, or a set within δ/2 of one loop, which is at distance ≥ δ/2 from every other loop. Let S′ be the union of a finite family 𝒫 of admissible pieces, connected, Σ(𝒫) := Σ_{p∈𝒫}diam_ℓ∞p, and A ⊆ S′ a finite set of bonds of ⊔_iC_i.
  {#NL1.3-6.1} The *clusters* on C_i are the components of the intersection graph of the pieces meeting N_i. Each piece lies in at most one cluster. A bond b ∈ A on C_i lies in S′, the pieces meeting b meet N_i, and they form a connected intersection graph, b being connected and the pieces closed. So b lies in ∪𝒦 for exactly one cluster 𝒦, and meets no piece of another. A_i(𝒦) is the set of the bonds of A on C_i in ∪𝒦, and for A_i(𝒦) ≠ ∅, H_𝒦 is the smallest arc of C_i containing A_i(𝒦). These arcs are the *tube hulls* 𝒯(𝒫, A).
  {#NL1.3-6.2} (a) |H_𝒦| ≤ 4diam(∪𝒦) (#MD2.4.12.22-2) ≤ 4Σ_{p∈𝒦}diam p (#516), so Σ_{H∈𝒯}|H| ≤ 4Σ(𝒫). (b) The end bonds of H_𝒦 lie in A_i(𝒦), and each point of H_𝒦 is within arc length |H_𝒦|/2 of one of them. (c) If A meets two loops, every cluster 𝒦 with A_i(𝒦) ≠ ∅ has diam(∪𝒦) ≥ δ/4, so #𝒯 ≤ 4Σ(𝒫)/δ.
  {#NL1.3-6.3} *Proof of (c).* A piece holding a bond of A on another loop does not meet N_i, so it is not in 𝒦. S′ is connected, so a piece q ∉ 𝒦 meets ∪𝒦 at a point x. If q met N_i it would be in 𝒦, so x ∉ N_i, and ∪𝒦 holds x and a bond of C_i, at distance ≥ δ/4. Summing diam(∪𝒦) ≥ δ/4 over the clusters with (a)'s bound gives #𝒯 ≤ 4Σ(𝒫)/δ.
  {#NL1.3-6.4} (d) If A contains every bond of an arc J ⊆ C_i, then J lies in one tube hull: J is connected and lies in S′, so its bonds lie in ∪𝒦 for one cluster 𝒦 (#NL1.3-6.1), and H_𝒦 ⊇ A_i(𝒦) ⊇ J.
{#NL1.4} Δ_×^{(k)} := Σ_ne^{−ν_×δ_n^{(k)}/M} over the step-k traversals of C, as in #335. So Δ_×^{(k)} ≤ 4ℓ_ΣL^{K′−k}e^{−ν_×DL^{K′−k}/M}.
{#NL1.5} *Constants.* Those of #337–#351, with C_χ := max_iC_{χ_i}, and with ν_× := min(ν_e, 2η/A′_E) and the pair's A′_E := 17(2R_e + 1)⁴, which the count of #NL9.6-1 gives for every N_C.
{#NL1.6} *Norms.* #352–#354 with the normalised trace on ⊗_iV_{π_i}: since tr(⊗_iA_i) = Π_itr A_i, a product of N_C normalised traces, one per loop, is the normalised trace of the tensor product, and |dim^{−1}tr(M₁⋯M_r)| ≤ Π_s‖M_s‖ for operators on ⊗_iV_{π_i}, as for two factors (V4 lem:two-loop-source, first sentence of the proof).
