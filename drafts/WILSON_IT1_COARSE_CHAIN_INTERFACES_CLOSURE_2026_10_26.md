## What the Objects Are

{#CC.0.5} *The coarse contour and the tube.* Fix k ≤ j. C^{(k)} is the set of scale-k bonds of C. The sides of C are multiples of L^K fine bonds with a corner on the scale-K lattice, so C^{(k)} is a closed square contour on the scale-k lattice. B^k(y) is the k-th order block of y (RG1 (0.3), p.252), the lattice points of the cube of side ℓ_k centred at y. For c ∈ C^{(k)}, T(c) is the set of fine bonds with both ends in B^k(c_−) ∪ B^k(c_+); it contains the L^k fine bonds of c. The tube of a set I of scale-k bonds is T(I) := ∪_{c∈I}T(c).

{#CC.0.6} *The composed bond matrices.* For a fine field 𝐔 put U^{[k]}(𝐔)(c) := (M^k𝐔)(c) on the inner bonds c of C^{(k)}, those whose two scale-k blocks lay in Ω_k at step k, and U^{[k]}(𝐔)(c) := T_c(𝐔), the transport along the fine bonds of c, on the others (background note, What the Objects Are). Here M^k is the k-fold iterate of RG1 (0.4), p.253. Every contour in (0.4)'s average lies in the blocks of the two ends of the bond, so U^{[k]}(𝐔)(c) depends on 𝐔 only on T(c) (background note §4.1(1)).

{#CC.0.7} *The deformed background.* 𝐔 := e^{iη𝐇_j(s,B′)}e^{iηa′}U_{j+1} bondwise, the product form of #MD2.1.4.50-1. Here 𝐇_j(s,B′) is RG2's localised shift (D2.1(4) *Setting*, (a)–(c)), s are the parameters of the π_j-cubes, and a′ is the complex datum of D(j+1)(iii). The bounds below hold equally for e^{iη(𝐇_j + a′)}U_{j+1}, the form of the background note §4.1(3).

{#CC.0.8} *The expansion.* Put Φ^g(s) := W_χ(C^{(k)}; U^{[k]}(𝐔)) = d_π^{−1}tr∏_{c∈C^{(k)}}π(U^{[k]}(𝐔)(c)) in loop order, negatively traversed bonds inverted (background note §1.3). D2.1(4)'s *Expansion* run on Φ^g gives Φ^g(1) = Σ_σ∏_{Δ∈σ}∫₀¹ds(Δ)∂_{s(Δ)}Φ^g(s)|_{s(σ^c)=0}. Since 𝐇(0,B′) = 0 (D2.1(4)(a)), the term σ = ∅ is W_χ(C^{(k)}; U^{[k]}(e^{iηa′}U_{j+1})), the leading word at the complex datum; at a′ = 0 it is g_k^{(j+1)}.

{#CC.0.9} *Where a component acts.* At s(σ^c) = 0, 𝐇(s,B′) vanishes on a neighbourhood of Y(σ)^c, and on a component Y it depends on s|_Y and B′|_Y only (D2.1(4)(b)). If no block of a bond of C^{(k)} meets Y, then Φ^g does not depend on s|_Y by #CC.0.6, and the term of σ vanishes. For a component Y put S^{(k)}(Y) := {c ∈ C^{(k)} : (B^k(c_−) ∪ B^k(c_+)) ∩ Y ≠ ∅}. I^{(k)}(Y) is the smallest arc of C^{(k)} containing S^{(k)}(Y), chosen by #MD2.1.4.10's rule: remove the longest maximal run of bonds not in S^{(k)}(Y), ties going to the run whose first bond comes first from x₀, and I^{(k)}(Y) = C^{(k)} if S^{(k)}(Y) = C^{(k)}.

{#CC.0.10} *Groups and words.* Components whose arcs share a bond form groups G, as at #MD2.1.4.11, and I^{(k)}(G) := ∪_{Y∈G}I^{(k)}(Y); distinct groups have disjoint arcs. The composed word of G is
  Y^g_G := [∏_{Y∈G}∏_{Δ⊂Y}∫₀¹ds(Δ)∂_{s(Δ)}]∏_{c∈I^{(k)}(G)}π(U^{[k]}(𝐔)(c)), at s(Δ) = 0 for every Δ outside the components of G,
a linear map between the fibres at the two ends of the arc.

{#CC.0.11} *The result.* Φ^g(1) = W_χ(C^{(k)}; U^{[k]}(e^{iηa′}U_{j+1})) + Σ_{𝔊≠∅}d_π^{−1}tr(Y^g_{G₁}U^{(k)}_{(1)}⋯Y^g_{G_n}U^{(k)}_{(n)}), with the compatibility of D2.1(4) *Result*. Here U^{(k)}_{(i)} is the U^{[k]}(e^{iηa′}U_{j+1})-transport along C^{(k)} between consecutive arcs. At a′ = 0, Φ^g(1) = g_k^{(j)}(e^{iB′_j}V^{(j)}) (background note §1.3). This is the slot family 𝔖_k(j+1) of the background note §4.3, with its slots X_c := π(U^{[k]}(𝐔)(c)U^{[k]}(U_{j+1})(c)^{−1}) − 1 grouped into the words Y^g_G.

{#CC.0.12} *Other groups do not act on Y^g_G.* Suppose a block of a bond c ∈ I^{(k)}(G) meets a component Y′ of another group G′. Then c ∈ S^{(k)}(Y′) ⊆ I^{(k)}(G′), which is disjoint from I^{(k)}(G), a contradiction. So the blocks of the bonds of I^{(k)}(G) meet only components of G, and the factors on I^{(k)}(G) depend only on the parameters of G. On a gap bond c ∈ I^{(k)}(G) ∖ ∪_{Y∈G}S^{(k)}(Y), T(c) meets no component, and U^{[k]}(𝐔)(c) = U^{[k]}(e^{iηa′}U_{j+1})(c).

{#CC.0.13} *What this forces.* Y^g_G depends on B only through 𝐇_Y(s|_Y, B′|_Y), Y ∈ G (D2.1(4)(b)). So D2.4(11)(a)'s B-dependence domain D, m(M), c_D, and the whole of D2.4(10) are those of the fine chain. Three things change: the per-bond factor on the arc (§1.3); the arc I^{(k)}(G), whose composed bond set reaches 3ℓ_k/2 beyond the components (§1.2); and the background-dependence domain, the tube of the arc (§4.1).

{#CC.0.14} *The outcome.* The result is (S-w) for the coarse gas with the amplitude 𝔞^g_{j+1} = c_Xβ_k²ρ_{j+1}·pl from a trivial base at step k (§4), and the class closure's complex-coupling supremum with ε_H^{(k)} := ε_H/β_k and ε′^{(k)} (§6). Every constant is uniform in j at fixed k.

## §1 Hypotheses, Geometry, and the Per-Bond Bound

{#CC.1.1} *1.1 The hypotheses (background note §4.1, §4.3).* They hold for the composed words at step j+1 on the polydisc |s(Δ)| ≤ e^{κ_s}, |B′|_Y ≤ ε_H^{(k)}, with a′ in D(j+1)(iii)'s domain 𝒟_{j+1} or its sub-domain 𝒟^{sub}_{j+1} (SW closure §3(b1)).
- {#CC.1.1-1} (D1) *Locality.* Y^g_G depends on B through B′ on the components of G, and on the background (U_{j+1}, a′) only on T(I^{(k)}(G)).
- {#CC.1.1-2} (D2) *Vanishing and covariance.* If 𝐇_Y = 0 for some Y ∈ G, the arc product does not depend on s|_Y, its s|_Y-derivatives vanish, and Y^g_G = 0. Under a gauge transformation u of U_{j+1}, U^{[k]}(𝐔)^u(c) = u(c_−)U^{[k]}(𝐔)(c)u(c_+)^{−1}. For M^k this follows from RG1 (0.4), p.253: the loop of each average is based at c_−, its holonomy is conjugated by u(c_−), and the principal logarithm is conjugation-equivariant. With the covariance of 𝐇 (D2.2(6)(b)) the word is conjugated by π(u) at the two ends of its arc. At U_{j+1} = 1 the words are invariant under constant gauge transformations (Schur, D2.3(2)).
- {#CC.1.1-3} (D3) *Analyticity.* Under (C2′) and (C4) every level-i holonomy and bond product entering (0.4), i < k, lies in the chart domain {‖W − 1‖ ≤ 1/10}, and Y^g_G is analytic in (s, B′, a′) jointly on the polydisc (background note §4.1(3)). (C4) is the background note's condition 1.06(d+2)β_{k−1}c_h(O(1)·4B₀e^{16κ_s}ε_H + (1+β₀)α_{1,K}) ≤ 1/22, with RG2 p.6's O(1) ≥ 1.
- {#CC.1.1-4} (D4) *Size.* Put Δ_0(c) := sup_{b∈T(c)}‖𝐔(b)U_{j+1}(b)^{−1} − 1‖ and Δ_k(c) := ‖U^{[k]}(𝐔)(c) − U^{[k]}(U_{j+1})(c)‖. On inner bonds the Lipschitz recursion of the background note §4.2 gives Δ_k ≤ (b_d^cL)^kΔ_0 under (C4).
- {#CC.1.1-5} (D5) *Transports.* The transports between slots on an arc and between arcs are U^{[k]}-transports, covariant by (D2), analytic by (D3), and bounded per bond by #CC.1.3.

{#CC.1.2} *1.2 Geometry.*
- {#CC.1.2-1} (i) *Reach of the composed bond set.* Let c ∈ S^{(k)}(Y), with B^k(c_±) meeting Y. The block lies in the ℓ∞-ball of radius ℓ_k/2 about c_±, so dist_∞(c_±, Y) ≤ ℓ_k/2. Every point of c is within ℓ_k of c_±, so c lies in Y^{+3ℓ_k/2}, the closed ℓ∞-neighbourhood of Y of radius 3ℓ_k/2 ≤ 3/2.
- {#CC.1.2-2} (ii) *Length of C in a cube.* C is a square with axis-parallel sides, so its intersection with any cube of side a has length at most 4a: each side meets the cube in a segment of length at most a. For a π_j-cube this is c_CM = 4M (#MD2.0.5.30, c_C = 4). Y^{+3ℓ_k/2} is covered by the #(Y) cubes of side M + 3ℓ_k concentric with the cubes of Y. With length(S^{(k)}(Y)) := ℓ_k·#S^{(k)}(Y),
  length(S^{(k)}(Y)) ≤ length(C ∩ Y^{+3ℓ_k/2}) ≤ 4(M + 3ℓ_k)#(Y) ≤ 1.03c_CM#(Y), since 3ℓ_k ≤ 0.03M.
- {#CC.1.2-3} (iii) *Arc length.* Y is a connected union of #(Y) cubes of side M, so its ℓ∞-diameter is at most M#(Y); distances are on the torus, whose periods are at least twice the side of C. By (i) the points of the bonds of S^{(k)}(Y) have ℓ∞-diameter D ≤ M#(Y) + 3ℓ_k. The argument of #MD2.1.4.51 (one side, two adjacent sides, or the whole contour) holds for the square contour C^{(k)} and gives length(I^{(k)}(Y)) ≤ 4D. Hence
  length(I^{(k)}(G)) ≤ Σ_{Y∈G}4(M#(Y) + 3) ≤ 4.12M#(G), since 12 ≤ 0.12M#(Y).
- {#CC.1.2-4} (iv) *Reach of the tube.* T(I) lies within ℓ∞-distance ℓ_k/2 of the sites of I, since each block lies in the ℓ∞-ball of radius ℓ_k/2 about its centre. In step-(j+1) units the reach of S^{(k)}(Y) beyond Y is at most 3/(2L), and that of T(I) beyond I at most 1/(2L).

{#CC.1.3} *1.3 The per-bond bound.* For c ∈ C^{(k)} put h(c) := sup_{b∈T(c)}(|𝐇(b)| + |a′(b)|), where |a′(b)| := (phase of a′ on b)/η. Under (C4),
  ‖π(U^{[k]}(𝐔)(c))‖ ≤ exp(c_π′β_kℓ_kh(c)), with c_π′ := 2c_πmax(1, c_h) (#MD2.1.4.27).
- {#CC.1.3-1} *A fine bond.* For b ∈ T(c), ‖𝐔(b)U_{j+1}(b)^{−1} − 1‖ = ‖e^{iη𝐇(b)}e^{iηa′(b)} − 1‖ ≤ e^{c_hη(|𝐇(b)| + |a′(b)|)} − 1, by ‖Z‖ ≤ c_h|Z| (#MOMST.9-2). The exponent is at most 1/10. Indeed η|𝐇(b)| ≤ O(1)·4B₀e^{16κ_s}ε_H (background note §4.1(3)), the phase of a′ per fine bond is below α_{1,n}L^{−n} ≤ (1+β₀)α_{1,K} on every layer (SW closure §3(b1)), and (C4) gives c_h(O(1)·4B₀e^{16κ_s}ε_H + (1+β₀)α_{1,K}) ≤ 1/(23.32(d+2)) < 1/10. Since e^x − 1 ≤ 1.06x for 0 ≤ x ≤ 1/10, Δ_0(c) ≤ 1.06c_hηh(c).
- {#CC.1.3-2} *An inner bond.* Put V := U^{[k]}(𝐔)(c)U^{[k]}(U_{j+1})(c)^{−1}. Since U^{[k]}(U_{j+1})(c) is unitary, ‖V − 1‖ = Δ_k(c). By (D4) and (C4), Δ_k ≤ (b_d^cL)^kΔ_0 ≤ b_d^c/(22(d+2)) ≤ 0.075 for d ≥ 2 (background note §4.2). Hence log V ∈ 𝔤^c with ‖log V‖ ≤ −log(1 − ‖V − 1‖) ≤ 1.04Δ_k, and ‖π(V)‖ ≤ exp(c_π‖log V‖) by the Hermitian-part bound of the background note §4.2. Since π(U^{[k]}(U_{j+1})(c)) is unitary and L^kη = ℓ_k,
  log‖π(U^{[k]}(𝐔)(c))‖ ≤ 1.04c_π(b_d^cL)^k·1.06c_hηh(c) = 1.1024c_πc_hβ_kℓ_kh(c) ≤ c_π′β_kℓ_kh(c), as c_π′ ≥ 2c_πc_h.
- {#CC.1.3-3} *A non-inner bond.* T_c(𝐔) is the product of the L^k factors e^{iη𝐇(b)}e^{iηa′(b)}U_{j+1}(b) over the fine bonds of c, and each has ‖π(·)‖ ≤ e^{c_π′η(|𝐇(b)| + |a′(b)|)} by #MD2.1.4.27, U_{j+1}(b) being unitary. So ‖π(T_c(𝐔))‖ ≤ exp(c_π′L^kηh(c)) = exp(c_π′ℓ_kh(c)) ≤ exp(c_π′β_kℓ_kh(c)).
- {#CC.1.3-4} For 𝐔 = e^{iη(𝐇+a′)}U_{j+1} the same proof applies, with |𝐇(b) + a′(b)| ≤ |𝐇(b)| + |a′(b)|.

## §2 The Composed Claim

{#CC.2.1} *2.1 Statement.* Let G be a group of the composed family with every component in Ω_j; this is case (A) of #MD2.1.4.23, and the groups with a component in Ω_jᶜ are kind (γ), D2.6's, as for the fine chain. Let |B′|_Y ≤ ε_H^{(k)} for every Y ∈ G, let a′ be 𝔤^c-valued with 𝐔 in the domain of (D3), and let ‖a′‖_∞ be the supremum of |a′| over T(I^{(k)}(G)). Under (C4) and (C7),
  ‖Y^g_G‖ ≤ ∏_{Y∈G}(|B′|_Y/ε_H^{(k)})e^{−(κ_s − 2 − 4.12c_π′β_kM‖a′‖_∞)#(Y)}.
Under 4.12c_π′β_kM‖a′‖_∞ ≤ 1, which (C8) implies, the exponent is −(κ_s−3)#(Y). This is #MD2.1.4.43–44 with ε_H → ε_H^{(k)}, I(G) → I^{(k)}(G), and the a′-constant 4c_π′ → 4.12c_π′β_k.

{#CC.2.2} *2.2 Proof (#MD2.1.4.47–52 run for the composed word).*
- {#CC.2.2-1} *Polydisc.* Replace B′|_Y by t_YB′|_Y for each Y ∈ G. By D2.1(4)(c), 𝐇_Y(s, t_YB′) is analytic for |s(Δ)| ≤ e^{κ_s} and |t_YB′|_Y < ε₃^{RG2}, with |𝐇_Y(s, t_YB′)| ≤ 4B₀e^{16κ_s}|t_YB′|_Y in case (A) (#MD2.1.4.4-1; RG2 (1.18), p.6). On |t_Y| ≤ T_Y^{(k)} := ε_H^{(k)}/|B′|_Y ≥ 1 one has |t_YB′|_Y ≤ ε_H^{(k)} ≤ ε_H < ε₃^{RG2}, and Y^g_G is analytic in (s, t) by (D3).
- {#CC.2.2-2} *Schwarz in t_Y.* At t_Y = 0, 𝐇_Y = 0 and Y^g_G = 0 by (D2). This holds for each Y separately, so the Schwarz lemma in each t_Y gives the factor ∏_Y(T_Y^{(k)})^{−1} = ∏_Y|B′|_Y/ε_H^{(k)} times the polydisc supremum.
- {#CC.2.2-3} *Cauchy in s(Δ).* Circles of radius e^{κ_s} − 1 ≥ e^{κ_s−1} about s(Δ) ∈ [0,1] (κ_s ≥ 1) give e^{−(κ_s−1)} per cube of the components.
- {#CC.2.2-4} *The arc product.* 𝐇 vanishes on T(c) unless c ∈ ∪_{Y∈G}S^{(k)}(Y) (#CC.0.12), and on a fine bond |𝐇| = |𝐇_Y| for at most one Y, the components being disjoint. So by #CC.1.3
  sup_{polydisc}∏_{c∈I^{(k)}(G)}‖π(U^{[k]}(𝐔)(c))‖ ≤ exp(c_π′β_k·4B₀e^{16κ_s}ε_H^{(k)}Σ_{Y∈G}length(S^{(k)}(Y)))·exp(c_π′β_k‖a′‖_∞·length(I^{(k)}(G))).
- {#CC.2.2-5} *The 𝐇-part.* By #CC.1.2-2 and β_kε_H^{(k)} = ε_H, the first exponent is at most 1.03c_π′c_CM·4B₀e^{16κ_s}ε_H·#(G) ≤ #(G) under
  (C7) 1.03c_π′c_CM·4B₀e^{16κ_s}ε_H ≤ 1.
  (C7) does not depend on k. The background note's (Cε), 1.12c_π′c_CM·O(1)·4B₀e^{16κ_s}ε_H ≤ 1 with O(1) ≥ 1, implies it. The factor β_k of #CC.1.3 is cancelled by the slot amplitude ε_H^{(k)}.
- {#CC.2.2-6} *The a′-part.* By #CC.1.2-3 the second exponent is at most 4.12c_π′β_kM‖a′‖_∞#(G).
- {#CC.2.2-7} *Assembly.* The product ∏_Y(|B′|_Y/ε_H^{(k)})·e^{−(κ_s−1)#(G)}·e^{#(G)}·e^{4.12c_π′β_kM‖a′‖_∞#(G)} is the claim. ∎

{#CC.2.3} *2.3 Locality, first order, and the real-point hypothesis.*
- {#CC.2.3-1} *Locality.* Y^g_G depends on B′ on the components of G and on the background on T(I^{(k)}(G)) (D1). This is D2.1(4)'s *Locality* with the tube in place of the arc.
- {#CC.2.3-2} *First order.* A group with two or more components has no part linear in B′, since each component's factor vanishes at t_Y = 0. For G = {Y} the linear part is the s|_Y-derivative of the first-order term of ∏_cπ(U^{[k]}(e^{iη𝐇_Y(s,B′)}e^{iηa′}U_{j+1})(c)) in 𝐇_Y. This term is linear in 𝐇_Y, and the first-order part H₀(s)B′ of 𝐇_Y is odd in B′ (D2.1(4) *First order*). So the linear part is odd in B′, which is the parity input of D2.4(11)(f).
- {#CC.2.3-3} *The real-point hypothesis.* At real configurations |B′| < O(1)ε_j on B(Γ_{j+1}) (CRE (3.14); D2.1(4) *The hypothesis*), so O(1)ε_j ≤ ε_H^{(k)} suffices. On D2.4's support |B′|_Y ≤ b_j := C₁^{RG2}δ_j (D2.4(11)(b); RG2 (1.20), p.6), and §3 uses β_kb_j ≤ ε_H/2. Both are smallness conditions on g_K at fixed k, since ε_j and δ_j tend to 0 with g_j ≤ (1+β₀)g_K.

## §3 D2.4(10)–(11) for the Composed Slots

{#CC.3.1} *3.1 D2.4(10), the H̃ bound.* D2.4(10)(b) bounds |H̃(M)| by ‖𝒳_M‖_sup times factors from RG2 (2.14)–(2.26) and Lemma 3 applied to the B-integral. The slot factor enters only as a bounded function of B on the bonds of D ((10)(c)). The composed 𝒳^g_M = ⊗_iY^g_{G_i} depends on B through B′ on the components (D1), so D, m(M), D′, and (10)(b)–(e) are the fine chain's, and
  |H̃^g(M)| ≤ ‖𝒳^g_M‖_sup·e^{81A#_{j+1}(D′)}·e^{(5+3λ_L)κ(m(M)−1)}·exp(−r·d_{j+1}(M;D′)),
with (10)'s constants, uniformly in j. The per-term bounds rest on CRE pp.276–279 and the clauses of #CC.0.4, as for the fine chain.

{#CC.3.2} *3.2 D2.4(11)(a)–(b).* (a): a composed group G_i is a set of s-components together with the coarse chain's touched old insertions W, the coarse words made at the steps i with k < i ≤ j. D_i, c_B, c_D ≤ 81, m(M) ≤ c_D#(G_M), and n(M) are as printed, since they count the B-dependence. (b): the support threshold g_j|B| < δ_j, |B′|_Y ≤ b_j, x_j = δ_j/ε₁^{RG2} ≤ 1/2 is that of the cutoff and the measure, the same for both chains.

{#CC.3.3} *3.3 D2.4(11)(c), the per-slot bounds at complex data.* Let the hull of G with its allowance and the tube of I^{(k)}(G) lie in Ω_j; otherwise G is kind (γ), D2.6's, as for the fine chain. The phase of a′ per fine bond is below α_{1,j}L^{−j} on Ω_j∖Ω_{j+1} and below α_{1,j+1}L^{−(j+1)} on Ω_{j+1} (SW closure §3(b1)).
