# General Lattice Loops: The Multi-Loop Bound, the Cauchy Bound, and the Limit

{#GL.0} Status: derived, not reviewed (2026-11-10). Owned by the BETA session; other sessions read it only.

{#GL.1} **The K-uniform bound, the Cauchy bound, and the limit along the full sequence hold for every finite family of pairwise vertex-disjoint simple closed loops on the L-adic lattices, read with general loops in place of squares, section by section, as NL reads the pair with N_C loops.** No new analytic input enters. For two loops the pair factorisation bound holds with ℓ̄_i := P_{K′}(C_i)/4 in place of the sides (§8). Reflection positivity (#RP.7, #RP.9) and nonvanishing (#NV.1–#NV.6) hold for loops in a half-torus of diameter below T_ph/3 (§9).
{#GL.1-1} Each change is one of four kinds: a geometric count of §2, at most 2Λ̄(C_i) times the square's on each loop (#GL6.1-2); a perimeter 4ℓ_C replaced by P_{K′}(C); the four corners replaced by the corner sum over 𝒦(C), with the corner isolation r_k(C) in place of s_k/2 (#GL2.7); or a condition fixed before the coupling, or a smallness condition on the coupling, that now depends on Λ̄(𝒞) and on the ratios P(C_i)/r(C_i) (#GL7.1). Since Λ̄(C) ≤ max(2, P(C)/(2r(C))) and abs(𝒦(C)) ≤ P(C)/(2r(C)) (#GL2.7-2), every shape constant is bounded in terms of P(C)/r(C).

## 1 The Setting {#GLS1}

{#GL1.1} *Loops.* A loop C is a simple closed path of lattice bonds of uL^{−q}ℤ⁴ for some q ≥ 0, in any planes (non-planar loops included); simple means that no site is visited twice. Its *segments* are its maximal straight subpaths, and its *corners* 𝒦(C) are the sites where consecutive segments meet.
{#GL1.1-1} At c ∈ 𝒦(C) the incoming and outgoing orientations (v_c, w_c) lie in 𝒱 := {±e_μ : μ ≤ 4} and are perpendicular: they differ by maximality, and w_c = −v_c would visit a site twice. A loop has at least four segments.
{#GL1.1-2} q(C) is the least q such that the displacement between any two corners lies in uL^{−q}ℤ⁴. A *coarse bond* of C is a bond of spacing uL^{−q(C)} on the lattice through its corners; every segment is a union of coarse bonds. P^{co}(C) is the number of coarse bonds of C. It is even, since each coordinate returns to its initial value.

{#GL1.2} *Families.* 𝒞 = (C₁, …, C_{N_C}), N_C ≥ 1, pairwise vertex-disjoint loops, with χ_i, π_i as in #332. Where V4, V3, IT-1, the map, the closures, the two-loops note, the pair note, the pair factorisation note, or NL say "square", read "loop of 𝒞". At N_C = 1 this is the single-loop chain (V4, the map, the closures, the Cauchy note, the continuum note).

{#GL1.3} *Stopping.* K′ = K − j, with j the least j ≥ max(q(C_i), q_{ii′} : i ≠ i′) + q̄(j), q_{ii′} the least q with the displacement between corners of C_i and C_{i′} in uL^{−q}ℤ⁴ (#NL1.2, #MD2.3.4.15-11).
{#GL1.3-1} #MD2.3.4.13–#MD2.3.4.16 use only that the corners and their displacements lie on uL^{−q}ℤ⁴. At step k ≤ K′ a coarse bond of C_i is L^{j−q(C_i)+K′−k} step-k bonds, a multiple of Π_{k+1}: L^{j−q(C_i)} ≥ L^{q̄(j)} ≥ L^{q*(K′)} (#MD2.3.4.15-6) and Π_{k+1} divides L^{q*}L^{K′−k} (#MD2.3.4.12-2).
{#GL1.3-2} With one corner at the origin, every corner of every loop is a period point at every step, every segment is a union of whole period cells starting at a period point, and every step is commensurate (#MD2.3.4.15-9). #MD2.3.4.16-8 holds with P_{K′}(C) = P^{co}(C)L^{j−q(C)} ≤ P^{co}(C)L⁴M(1+γ²β′(j+1))^{β₀}(log g_min^{−2})^r in place of ℓ_C.

{#GL1.4} *Lengths at step k*, in step-k bonds: P_k(C) the perimeter (the map's notation), s_k(C) the shortest segment, δ_k(C) the least ℓ^∞-distance between two non-adjacent segments (as point sets), and diam_k(C) the ℓ^∞-diameter. Each is its step-K′ value times L^{K′−k}.
{#GL1.4-1} *δ is at least one coarse bond.* Non-adjacent segments of a simple loop are disjoint, since they could meet only at a site visited twice. Let A, B be disjoint axis-parallel segments with endpoints on a lattice aℤ⁴. If a coordinate constant on both differs, it differs by at least a. Otherwise A and B are collinear, and disjoint intervals with endpoints in aℤ are at least a apart; or they are perpendicular, along e_μ and e_ν, with A's e_ν-coordinate a_ν and B's e_μ-coordinate b_μ, and disjointness forces b_μ outside A's e_μ-range or a_ν outside B's e_ν-range, each at distance at least a.
{#GL1.4-2} So s_k(C), δ_k(C) ≥ one coarse bond ≥ Π_{k+1} ≥ max(MR_k, LMR_{k+1}) step-k bonds (#GL1.3-1; #MD2.3.4.1, #MD2.3.4.3: the scale-k domain cubes are MR_k and the scale-(k+1) ones LMR_{k+1} step-k bonds). A coarse bond is L^{j−q(C)+K′−k} ≥ L^{q̄(j)+K′−k} step-k bonds, so s_k(C), δ_k(C) ≥ L²MR_k for 1 ≤ k ≤ K′ (#NL1.3-2.2), and s_0(C), δ_0(C) ≥ L^{K′+3}M fine bonds (L^{q̄(j)} ≥ L³M, #MOMGD.30). In step-(k+1) bonds, s_{k+1}(C) ≥ MR_{k+1} ≥ LM (R_{k+1} ≥ L for small g, #MD2.3.4.3), also at k + 1 = K′. And s_0(C) ≥ LMR_1 ≥ L²M fine bonds.
{#GL1.4-3} For a square of side s_k: s_k(C) = δ_k(C) = diam_k(C) = s_k, P_k(C) = 4s_k, and |𝒦(C)| = 4.

{#GL1.5} *Torus.* N_s ≥ 2max_idiam_{K′}(C_i) replaces N_s ≥ 2max_iℓ_i (#290, #PC2.3, #NL10.2), so N_min^{(k)} ≥ 2diam_k(C_i) at every step. Two points of C_i differ by at most diam_k(C_i) in each coordinate, so their torus ℓ^∞-distance is the lifted one (#749).
{#GL1.5-1} At step 1, V4's K_μ ≥ 3(n + 2) reads K_μ ≥ 3(diam_0(C)/L + 2), diam_0(C)/L being an integer since the corners are coarse sites (#GL1.4-2). Then LK_μ ≥ 3diam_0(C) + 6L, and the periodic coordinate distances between points of C equal the lifted ones, as V4 has for the square in the paragraph fixing C_n before eq:marginal-hull-extent.

## 2 Geometric Lemmas {#GLS2}

{#GL2.1} *The chord–arc constant.* Let |C| be the union of the segments as point sets and d_C(x, y) the length of the shorter of the two arcs of |C| between x and y. Put Λ(C) := sup_{x≠y∈|C|}d_C(x, y)/|x − y|_∞, Λ̄(C) := max(2, Λ(C)), and Λ̄(𝒞) := max_iΛ̄(C_i). Λ(C) is always written with its argument; it is not V4's region Λ.
{#GL2.1-1} (a) Λ(C) ≤ max(2, P_k(C)/(2δ_k(C))) at every k. If x, y lie on one segment, the segment is an arc of length |x − y|_∞. If they lie on adjacent segments meeting at c, perpendicular, at distances a, b from c, then |x − y|_∞ = max(a, b) and the arc through c has length a + b ≤ 2max(a, b). Otherwise they lie on non-adjacent segments, |x − y|_∞ ≥ δ_k(C), and d_C(x, y) ≤ P_k(C)/2.
{#GL2.1-2} (b) Λ(C) is scale-free: all lengths scale by L^{K′−k}. For positions a, b of C at any step (a traversal's position is its initial site), the shorter arc between them has d_C(a, b) ≤ Λ(C)|a − b|_∞ traversals.
{#GL2.1-3} (c) A square has Λ = 2: on one side the ratio is 1, on adjacent sides at most 2 by (a), and on opposite sides |x − y|_∞ ≥ s and d_C ≤ 2s. A bent path (two perpendicular rays) has ratio at most 2, by the adjacent-segments case (V4, the paragraph before eq:corner-bent-tail).

{#GL2.2} *Containment.* Let S be a nonempty set of positions of C at step k, of ℓ^∞-diameter D (torus distance, equal to the lifted one by #GL1.5), x₀ ∈ S, and A(x₀) the arc of the positions within d_C ≤ Λ(C)D of x₀.
{#GL2.2-1} (a) If 2Λ(C)D < P_k(C)/2, then S ⊂ A(x₀), an arc of length at most 2Λ(C)D. The largest gap of S (a maximal arc of C without points of S) is unique and contains C ∖ A(x₀), so the smallest arc containing S is unique and lies in A(x₀), and each of its points is within ℓ^∞-distance Λ(C)D of x₀.
{#GL2.2-2} Proof: d_C(x₀, x) ≤ Λ(C)|x − x₀|_∞ ≤ Λ(C)D for x ∈ S (#GL2.1-2). The gap containing C ∖ A(x₀) has length at least P_k − 2Λ(C)D > P_k/2, and every other gap lies in A(x₀), so it is shorter than P_k/2. ℓ^∞-distance is at most arc distance.
{#GL2.2-3} (b) Otherwise P_k(C) ≤ 4Λ(C)D, and every point of C is within diam_k(C) ≤ P_k(C)/2 ≤ 2Λ(C)D of every point of S (each coordinate's range is traversed twice, so diam_k(C) ≤ P_k(C)/2).
{#GL2.2-4} Hence the smallest arc containing S has length at most 2Λ̄(C)D in case (a), and is at most the whole loop, of length at most 4Λ̄(C)D, in case (b); its points are within 2Λ̄(C)D of S. V4's hull of S (its shortest cyclic interval if that has fewer than P_k(C)/2 traversals, the whole contour otherwise) has at most 4Λ̄(C)D + 2 traversals: at most 2Λ(C)D + 1 if short, and if not, P_k(C) ≤ 4Λ(C)D + 2 in either case. For a square, case (a) is D < s/2, and these are V4's 4D + 1 and 8D (the paragraph before eq:marginal-hull-extent).
{#GL2.2-5} (c) Each point of the shorter arc between positions a, b is within ℓ^∞-distance Λ(C)|a − b|_∞ of a and of b.
{#GL2.2-6} (d) The positions of the smallest arc containing S, and those of V4's hull of S, have ℓ^∞-diameter at most 2Λ̄(C)D. In case (b) every set of positions has diameter at most diam_k(C) ≤ 2Λ(C)D (#GL2.2-3). In case (a) the smallest arc, and the hull when short, lie within Λ(C)D of x₀ (#GL2.2-1).
{#GL2.2-6-1} In case (a) the hull is the whole contour only if the smallest arc, of ℓ_a ≤ 2Λ(C)D bonds, has ℓ_a + 1 ≥ P_k(C)/2 traversals, and then diam_k(C) ≤ P_k(C)/2 − s_k(C) ≤ ℓ_a: the coordinate of largest range varies by at most P_k(C) − 2s_k(C) in total, since another coordinate, along which some segment runs, varies by at least 2s_k(C).

{#GL2.3} *Counts in a box.* The positions of C in a set of ℓ^∞-diameter D number at most 2Λ(C)D + 1: with x₀ one of them, all lie in A(x₀) (#GL2.2-2), which has at most 2Λ(C)D + 1 positions. No case split is needed.
{#GL2.3-1} So a closed box of side t holds the initial sites of at most 2Λ(C)t + 1 ≤ (Λ̄(C)/2)·4(t + 1) traversals of C, and an ℓ^∞-ball of radius s at most 4Λ(C)s + 1 ≤ (Λ̄(C)/2)·4(2s + 1). The square's counts are 4(t + 1) and 4(2s + 1) (four sides; the proof of V4 lem:c5-7-expmoment), so the general counts are at most Λ̄(C)/2 times the square's.
{#GL2.3-2} *Small cubes.* A closed cube of side t < δ_k(C) with lattice corners meets C, if at all, in one arc of at most 2t + 1 positions, and meets at most 2t + 2 bonds of C. For a square the bond counts are 4(t + 1), and 8M for a π_k-cube (#NL1.3-5).
{#GL2.3-3} Proof: two segments meeting the cube are within t of each other, so adjacent, and as C has at least four segments (#GL1.1-1) at most two meet it. If two meet it, at x and y, it contains the coordinate box of x and y, which holds their common corner, so its intersection with C is one arc through that corner. Each segment meets it in an interval of at most t + 1 sites, and a bond meets it only if an end lies in it, at most m + 1 bonds for an arc of m sites.

{#GL2.4} *Runs and cubes.* An arc of n traversals at step k has at most n/s_k(C) + 2 straight pieces, its interior pieces being whole segments. A straight piece of n_i bonds meets at most n_i/M′ + 2 cubes of a partition into cubes of side M′ along it and at most 2 in each transverse coordinate, so at most 8(n_i/M′ + 2) cubes (#MD2.4.12.19-3's count per piece).
{#GL2.4-1} So the arc meets at most 8n/M′ + 16n/s_k(C) + 32 cubes, and at most (8 + 16/L)n/M′ + 32 when s_k(C) ≥ LM′. With M′ = M this holds at step k + 1 for the π_{k+1}-cubes and at step k for the π_k-cubes (#GL1.4-2). It replaces the square's "at most 5 straight pieces, 8n/M + 80" (#MD2.4.12.19-3).
{#GL2.4-2} A run along one segment lies in one transverse cell of a partition (V4's cubes Δ_z partition the sites), so V4's count per run, ⌊t/(LM)⌋ + 2 cubes (lem:c4-6-env(a)), gives at most |ℋ|/(LM) + 2|ℋ|/s_0(C) + 4 ≤ (1 + 2/L)|ℋ|/(LM) + 4 cubes for a cyclic interval ℋ of fine traversals, by s_0(C) ≥ L²M (#GL1.4-2).

{#GL2.5} *Long arcs.* An arc of at least P_k(C)/2 consecutive traversals contains two positions at d_C = P_k(C)/2 (P_k(C) is even, #GL1.1-2), so by #GL2.1-2 its ℓ^∞-diameter is at least P_k(C)/(2Λ̄(C)). For a square this is P_k/4 = s_k (#MD2.3.4.8-1, #448).

{#GL2.6} *Step sums.* For x, c > 0 and L ≥ 2, Σ_{m≥0}xL^me^{−cxL^m} ≤ xe^{−cx}(1 + 2/(cx)) and Σ_{m≥0}e^{−cxL^m} ≤ e^{−cx}(1 + 2/(cx)).
{#GL2.6-1} Proof: put y_m := xL^m. For m ≥ 1, y_m − y_{m−1} ≥ y_m/2, so y_me^{−cy_m} ≤ 2∫_{y_{m−1}}^{y_m}e^{−ct}dt, and the terms m ≥ 1 sum to at most 2∫_x^∞e^{−ct}dt = (2/c)e^{−cx}. The second sum is at most the first divided by x, since y_m ≥ x.

{#GL2.7} *Corner isolation.* At step k give each corner c the last ⌊ℓ/2⌋ traversals of its incoming segment and the first ℓ′ − ⌊ℓ′/2⌋ of its outgoing one, ℓ, ℓ′ their lengths (the half-sides of the proof of V4 eq:corner-square-extraction-bound and of #MD2.3.6.6). The half-segments partition the traversals of C.
{#GL2.7-1} Let S_in(c), S_out(c) be c's two segments and B_c the bent path of eq:corner-bent-path at c with rays along them. For a traversal b given to c, let r(b) be the least of P_k(C)/8 and the ℓ^∞-distance from b's initial site to the initial sites of the traversals of C and of B_c outside S_in(c) ∪ S_out(c).
{#GL2.7-2} (a) r_k(C) ≤ r(b) ≤ diam_k(C), with r_k(C) := min(s_k(C)/2, δ_k(C), P_k(C)/8). The ratio P(C)/r(C) := P_k(C)/r_k(C) is independent of k (#GL1.4). Every segment has at least s_k(C) ≥ 2r_k(C) bonds and δ_k(C) ≥ r_k(C), so abs(𝒦(C)) ≤ P_k(C)/s_k(C) ≤ P(C)/(2r(C)) and Λ̄(C) ≤ max(2, P(C)/(2r(C))) (#GL2.1-1). For a square, r_k(C) = s_k/2 and P(C)/r(C) = 8.
{#GL2.7-3} Proof of (a), for b in the last half of S_in(c), its initial site at distance a ≥ ⌈ℓ/2⌉ from the initial corner c′ of S_in(c): the previous segment is perpendicular to S_in(c) at c′, so all its sites have c′'s coordinate along v_c, at distance a; the segments not adjacent to S_in(c), among them the one after S_out(c), are at least δ_k(C) away; B_c's incoming ray beyond c′ is at distance more than a; its outgoing ray beyond S_out(c) is at distance more than ℓ′ ≥ s_k(C) in the e-coordinate of w_c.
{#GL2.7-4} For b in the first half of S_out(c), at distance i ≤ ⌈ℓ′/2⌉ − 1 from c, the next segment and B_c's outgoing ray beyond it are at distance at least ℓ′ − i ≥ ⌊ℓ′/2⌋ + 1 > ℓ′/2, the segments not adjacent to S_out(c) at least δ_k(C), and B_c's incoming ray beyond S_in(c) more than ℓ ≥ s_k(C). The upper bound: the far end of b's segment is within its length ≤ diam_k(C).
{#GL2.7-5} (b) Let Γ be a cluster of C's gas rooted at b (b ∈ J_Γ) whose network (the supports, arc hulls and merged domains of its groups, which contain the initial sites of its arcs' traversals, #MD2.3.4.8-1) has ℓ^∞-diameter less than r(b), on a torus with N_min ≥ 2diam_k(C). Then its arcs lie in S_in(c) ∪ S_out(c), in a subarc of length less than 4r(b) ≤ P_k(C)/2, and it does not wrap the torus.
{#GL2.7-6} Its smallest arcs, shorter arcs and linked hulls on C are then the ordinary intervals of B_c (#GL2.2-1: a set in an arc shorter than P_k/2 has its unique largest gap outside that arc), no whole-contour rule applies, and by contour locality (#MD2.3.5.10) Γ is a cluster of B_c's gas with the same weight. Conversely a cluster of B_c's gas rooted at b with network diameter less than r(b) has its arcs in S_in(c) ∪ S_out(c) and is a cluster of C's gas.
{#GL2.7-7} Proof of (b): the network lies within ℓ^∞-distance less than r(b) of b's initial site, so no traversal of C or B_c outside S_in(c) ∪ S_out(c) has its initial site in it. On B_c, points within ℓ^∞-distance r(b) of b are within path distance 2r(b) (#GL2.1-3). No wrap: r(b) ≤ diam_k(C) ≤ N_min/2.

{#GL2.8} *The remainder.* At a step k + 1 ≤ K′, with (T′) (#MD2.3.5.2) and (Z) for C's gas (§4),
{#GL2.8-1} F°_{k+1}(C; T) = Σ_σ(ℓ_σ/Π_{k+1})κ^{(k+1)}_{v_σ} + Σ_{c∈𝒦(C)}ζ^{(k+1)}_{v_c,w_c} + E°_{k+1}(C, T), over the segments σ, of length ℓ_σ and orientation v_σ, and
{#GL2.8-2} abs(E°_{k+1}) ≤ C^𝒦_E𝔞_{k+1}(P_k(C) + |𝒦(C)|)e^{−c_dr_k(C)} + 3C̄_Z𝔞_{k+1}P_k(C)e^{−c^Zι_{T,k+1}}, with C^𝒦_E := 2C_S′ + 2C_ζ, not the map's C_E (#T4.37's C^T_E).
{#GL2.8-3} Proof: allocate F° = Σ_ba_C(b; T) (#MD2.3.6.5). For b given to c, the clusters at b with network diameter less than r(b) are those of B_c with the same weight (#GL2.7-6), and the others, in either gas, cost at most C_S′𝔞_{k+1}e^{−c_dr(b)} by (T′) at R = r(b). With (Z) for B_c (#MD2.3.6.8), abs(a_C(b; T) − a_{v_c,w_c}(b)) ≤ 2C_S′𝔞_{k+1}e^{−c_dr_k(C)} + 3C_Z^{(k+1)}𝔞_{k+1}e^{−c^Zι_{T,k+1}}.
{#GL2.8-4} Over c's half-segments, Σa_{v_c,w_c}(b) = Σk_{v_c,w_c}(b) + ζ^{(k+1)}_{v_c,w_c} minus the part of ζ from the b outside them. Numbering b from c, abs(a_{v,w}(b) − k_{v,w}(b)) ≤ 2C_S′𝔞_{k+1}e^{−c_d|b|} (#MD2.3.5.11-1), so that part is at most 2C_S′𝔞_{k+1}(Σ_{b≥⌈ℓ′/2⌉} + Σ_{b≥⌊ℓ/2⌋+1})e^{−c_db}. With x := e^{−c_d/2}, ⌈ℓ′/2⌉ ≥ s_k(C)/2 and ⌊ℓ/2⌋ + 1 ≥ (s_k(C) + 1)/2, this is at most 2C_S′𝔞_{k+1}e^{−c_ds_k(C)/2}(1 + x)/(1 − x²) ≤ 2C_ζ𝔞_{k+1}e^{−c_ds_k(C)/2}, as C_ζ = 2C_S′(1 + x²)/(1 − x²) (#MD2.3.5.11-2) and 1 + x ≤ 2(1 + x²).
{#GL2.8-5} The half-segments make up the segments, each a union of whole period cells from a period point (#GL1.3-2), so the k-sum over σ is (ℓ_σ/Π_{k+1})κ^{(k+1)}_{v_σ} exactly (#MD2.3.6.10-1). Adding, abs(E°) ≤ P_k(C)(2C_S′𝔞e^{−c_dr_k} + 3C_Z𝔞e^{−c^Zι}) + 2|𝒦(C)|C_ζ𝔞e^{−c_ds_k/2}, and s_k(C)/2 ≥ r_k(C).
{#GL2.8-6} For a square (ℓ = ℓ′ = s_k, P_k = 4s_k, |𝒦| = 4, r_k = s_k/2) the two tails start at s_k/2 and s_k/2 + 1, or both at (s_k + 1)/2, and sum exactly to at most C_ζ𝔞e^{−c_ds_k/2} (#MD2.3.6.9-1). The bound is then #MD2.3.6.11's, with the map's C_E = 8C_S′ + 4C_ζ (#MD2.3.6.11-1). The factor 2 on C_ζ is needed only when the incoming and outgoing segments at a corner have different parities.

{#GL2.9} *Coefficients.* κ^{(k+1)}_v (#MD2.3.5.5) is defined on the line ℓ_v for each v ∈ 𝒱, and ζ^{(k+1)}_{v,w} (#MD2.3.5.9) for each of the 48 perpendicular ordered pairs (v, w) ∈ 𝒱², by the same formulas.
{#GL2.9-1} (T′)'s auxiliary contour (#MD2.3.5.1, #MD2.3.5.3-2) is a square in a coordinate plane containing v, or in span(v, w) with the corner (v, w). {#GL9.1} *Level.* Fix q ∈ ℕ and a family 𝒞 all of whose corners lie in uL^{−q}ℤ⁴, so that q ≥ q(C_i), q_{ii′}. §§1–8 hold at level q: with q for q(C_i), q_{ii′} and Q, coarse bonds of spacing uL^{−q}, P^{co}_q(C) := L^{q−q(C)}P^{co}(C) for P^{co}(C), and K′ = K − j_q, j_q the least j ≥ q + q̄(j).
