## 1. Setting

Run A: lattice spacing ε = uL^{−K}, bare coupling g^A_0 = g_0(ε, ḡ), k := K − j(C) steps to the stopping lattice T_a, a = uL^{−j(C)}, with N_s⁴ points. Run B: spacing ε/L, g^B_0 = g_0(ε/L, ḡ), k + 1 steps to the same T_a. Both are pinned at the unit scale, g^A_K = g^B_{K+1} = ḡ (RG I Theorem 2; ONE_LOOP_PROOFS Corollary 8.2). Run B's step j + 1 acts on the same pair of lattices as run A's step j, j = 1, …, k; the shared scale index is j, and run B's step 1 is the extra step. Couplings at the shared scale j: g^A_j and g^B_{j+1}, with δ_j := (g^B_{j+1})^{−2} − (g^A_j)^{−2}, δ_K = 0.

F_K(C) = N_K/(κ_K Z_K) with κ_K = κ^{(k)} the run's own product of per-step perimeter and corner coefficients (map l.264), so that at the stopping scale F_K is the expectation of the normalised dressed loop 𝒲^A_k = W(C^{(k)}; V) + (dev insertions), leading coefficient 1, against the stopping-scale density ρ^A_k, plus the remainder r_K (continuum note (3.2), (6.3)). The same for run B with 𝒲^B_{k+1} and ρ^B_{k+1}. The comparison is made at T_a with each run's κ divided out; no product of per-step coefficients is compared across runs.

*Why not on the common fine lattice.* After run B's first step both runs are densities on T_ε, and F_{K+1} − F_K can be written exactly through ρ^B_1/ρ^A_0 and the leading word W_ε(C; V). That identity splits into a covariance term and a normalisation term (κ^B_1κ_K/κ_{K+1} − 1)F_K, and the latter carries the two runs' perimeter coefficients at every scale, each multiplied by the perimeter P_{j−1}(C) = 4ℓ_C L^{k−j+1} at its scale; the two terms cancel a factor exp(±O(g⁴L^k)) between them. The stopping-scale comparison has no such cancellation to control.

## 2. The Matched Decomposition (Derived)

Both stopping-scale densities have the form CRE (2.18): ρ = Σ_{{Ω_j},{Λ_j}} χ(Ω_last) 𝐓 exp A, summed over admissible sequences of domains, one domain pair per step. Run B's sequences have k + 1 entries, run A's k. The R-operation acts on a component Z′ of the large-field region by replacing ρ(Z, V) with ρ(Z″, V) times a quotient of integrals (LFR I (0.3)), ρ(Z″, V) being the term of the same sum with Z′ removed from the sequence, the quotient entering as an R-term (LFR I (0.5)–(0.6)); a component is renormalised once it satisfies LFR I p.177's conditions (i) (contained in a cube of size 100MR_k) and (ii) (no new large field inside during the preceding N steps). So every term of run B's stopping-scale representation is one of two kinds:

- (M) its step-1 pair is (T, T) (no large-field region alive from run B's extra step; its renormalised step-1 regions survive only as R-terms); dropping the step-1 entry gives an admissible run-A sequence, and this is a bijection between (M) and run A's terms, since the admissibility conditions (CRE pp.255–256) are the same at the shared scales;
- (U) its step-1 pair is not (T, T): a component born at run B's step 1 is alive at the stopping scale.

So ρ^B_{k+1} − ρ^A_k = Σ_{τ} (ρ^B[τ⁺] − ρ^A[τ]) + Σ_{(U)} ρ^B[·], and the same for the numerators with the dressed loops.

### 8.3 The Recursion (Derived, with §8.5–8.6)

With (8.1), (8.2), the coupling and shell parts of §8.2(b), and the marked-cluster bound of §8.5, the generated difference per scale-(k+1) point in the class norm is

(8.3) d_{k+1} ≤ ε_k^*(D_k + S_k) + C_gε_k^*g_k²|δ_k| + e_{k+1},

with ε_k^* and C_g of (8.2) and §8.2(b), e_{k+1} the depth terms (classical: (9.10) at the fluctuated block field, §8.1; Gaussian and configuration: §10.5), and |Δβ_{k+1}| ≤ C_βd_{k+1} by Cauchy's estimate (the quadratic coefficient Π^Δ_{22,11} of (3.61)–(3.64)), so that the coupling difference is δ_j = Σ_{i=j+1}^{K}Δβ_i from the pinned value δ_K = 0 and |δ_j| ≤ C_βΣ_{i>j}d_i (the per-step coefficient β_i is the marginal part of the step's output, whose two-run difference is the marginal part of the generated difference, and its own dependence on the coupling is inside d_i through §8.2(b)). ### 8.4 The R-Sector (Derived, with §8.6)

The R-terms have no age decay beyond the volume factor (CRE p.283 l.35–39: O(1)(L^jL^{−n})⁴g_j^{κ₀}exp(−κd_j(X)) per scale-j point), so their differences enter (8.3) as S_k = Σ_{j≤k}d^R_j without the factor L^{−β′(k+1−j)}. ### 8.6 The Renormalisation Quotient (Derived)

*Lemma (real backgrounds).* Let f^A, f^B be positive functions on a compact group power with |log(f^B/f^A)| ≤ φ_* pointwise. Then ∫f^B/∫f^A ∈ [e^{−φ_*}, e^{φ_*}]. For quotients q^{A/B} := ∫f^{A/B}_1/∫f^{A/B}_2 of two such pairs, |log q^B − log q^A| ≤ 2φ_* and |q^B − q^A| ≤ (e^{2φ_*} − 1)q^A ≤ 4φ_*q^A for φ_* ≤ 1/2. Proof: ∫f^B = ∫f^Ae^{log(f^B/f^A)} and the pointwise bound; divide; exponentiate. This is the statement at real backgrounds, where the densities are positive (LFR I p.176 l.19–20); the class norm needs the complex version below, which uses absolute integration in place of positivity.

*Application (the complex class).* Bałaban bounds the renormalised terms by uniform bounds on the integrands followed by integration over the real variables: LFR I p.191 l.37–40, "They are easily obtained estimating the integral (1.60), after the renormalization and the localization. All the expressions in the exponential in this integral can be uniformly bounded in the usual way", on the complex spaces Ũ^{(n)c}_k of pp.190–191 built for this purpose (p.190 l.2–4, "to gain a larger domain of analyticity for the effective action, in order to perform the basic step in the R-operation"), with the result (1.70) (p.192), |ℂ^{(n)}_k(X, (𝕌, 𝕁))| ≤ C₀exp(−(1+3β)κd_m(X)), analytic on the space. The renormalisation of a component Z′ at step j replaces ρ(Z, V) by ρ(Z″, V) times the quotient q(V) := ∫dV|_{Z′}ρ(Z, V)/∫dV|_{Z′}ρ(Z″, V) (LFR I (0.3)–(0.5), p.177 l.1–5), whose numerator n is the large-field integral over the component and whose denominator d is the small-field expression localised near Z′, the logarithm of which is given by the convergent small-field expansion, the same expansion that produces the step's small-field output, analytic on the class. At the same complex V, the two runs' numerators have integrands I^B = I^Ae^{D} at the same real integration point, with D the two-run exponent difference on the component and |D| ≤ φ on the class, so |n^B − n^A| ≤ (e^φ − 1)∫|I^A|, and ∫|I^A|/|d^A| is the quantity Bałaban's uniform bound controls, ≤ R₁g_j^{κ₀}exp(−κd_j(X)) (the bound (2.31) is obtained this way); the denominators' logarithms differ by ψ := |log d^B − log d^A|, the generated difference of the localised small-field integral, bounded by (8.3) summed over the component's neighbourhood, so |d^B| ≥ e^{−ψ}|d^A| and |1/d^B − 1/d^A| ≤ (e^ψ − 1)/|d^A|. Hence |q^B − q^A| ≤ |n^B − n^A|/|d^B| + |n^A||1/d^B − 1/d^A| ≤ [e^ψ(e^φ − 1) + (e^ψ − 1)]R₁g_j^{κ₀}exp(−κd_j(X)) ≤ 2(e+1)(φ + ψ)R₁g_j^{κ₀}exp(−κd_j(X)) for φ, ψ ≤ 1, and ≤ 2R₁g_j^{κ₀}exp(−κd_j(X)) ≤ 2(φ + ψ)R₁g_j^{κ₀}exp(−κd_j(X)) by the triangle inequality otherwise. The exponent differences φ, ψ on the component: its points lie in the layers Ω_m ∖ Ω_{m+1}, n ≤ m ≤ j, with n the birth scale of the component (§6.2), the layer m holds at most (100MR_j)^dL^{4(j−m)} scale-m points (the component fits a cube of side 100MR_j in scale-j units, LFR I p.177 (i)), and on each of them the exponent difference is ≤ 𝒟_m of §8.4 in the class norm (the frozen terms of the layer have age m − i for the generation i and are evaluated at U_m-type configurations, CRE p.280 l.15–17, so R-Δ^c gives D_m; the carried R-differences S_{m−1}; the coupling, the depth terms, and the threshold shells h_m of the characteristic functions on the layer's cubes, §8.2(b)); so φ + ψ ≤ 2(100MR_j)^dL^{4(j−n)}(j − n + 1)𝒟_{[j]}. The memory j − n is bounded by the size of the component: a component of n_c cubes of side MR_n at its birth has diameter ≤ n_cMR_nL^{−(j−n)} in scale-j units and is renormalised once this is ≤ 100MR_j, so L^{4(j−n)} ≤ (n_cR_n/(100R_j))⁴L⁴ ≤ (n_c)⁴L⁴ (R_n ≤ 2R_j for j − n ≤ log g_j^{−2} by CRE (2.5) and Lemma 2.1) and j − n + 1 ≤ log_L(n_c) + 2. The factor (n_c)⁴L⁴(log_L(n_c) + 2) ≤ L⁴exp(c₁n_c) is paid by the component's weight: the component carries exp(−κ_j(Z) − 2p₀(g_{j(Z)})) with κ_j(Z) ≥ κ̂_j(Z) + ½G_j(Z) by (11.4) of §11(d), κ̂_j(Z) ≥ 0 the half-rate weight that pays Bałaban's entropies in the sum over components, and ½G_j(Z) ≥ ⅛γ₀A₁²(1 + β₀)^{−2}p₀²(g_a)n_c/(3·2^{d−1}) for the n_c cubes at birth of the regions merged into Z (LFR II p.384 l.31–32, CRE (2.7)), so exp(−½G_j(Z)) pays L⁴exp(c₁n_c) once ⅛γ₀A₁²(1 + β₀)^{−2}p₀²(g_a) ≥ 3·2^{d−1}(c₁ + 4 log L), a threshold of Bałaban's kind folded into (T2). Hence, for a component renormalised at step j,

### 8.7 The Solution of the Recursion (Derived)

### 8.8 What §8 Gives

With R-Δ (§5), R-mark (§8.5), and (8.4), the recursion (8.3) is derived in the class norm with the constant ε_k^* of (8.2), the R-sector is bounded by (8.5), and the coupled system is solved by (8.6): the generated differences of one step are a fixed small multiple, ε^*, of the age-weighted sum of the input differences, plus the coupling term, plus the extra generation, plus the depth terms, and the carried differences at the stopping scale are geometric in the number of steps up to the term (j(C) + 2)μ_K from the R-sector's fine-scale memory. *The localised lift.* For a localisation domain X the frozen difference Δ_j of (3.1) uses ι_X, the one-step minimiser of A_{ε/L} on X^∼ with block data U on X^∼ ∩ T_ε and the fixed exterior field, so that Δ_j depends on U on X^∼ only (CRE p.259 (i)). ### 10.5 What the Gaussian Depth Feeds (And What It Needs)

In (8.1) the quadratic insertion −½⟨A, C*δΔ^{(k)}CA⟩ on a localisation domain Y is a marked activity of §8.5's kind: its Gaussian mean is ≤ |Y|·sup_yΣ_{y′}|δΔ_k(y,y′)|·⟨|A|²⟩ ≤ |Y|c_δr_kB₀·4C_G(c_k + d_k(Y)) in scale-ℓ units, and its bound on Bałaban's fluctuation domain (3.43) is |Y|c_δr_kB₀(C₁p₁(g_k))², so by the two-part bound of §8.5 its contribution to the generated difference per scale-(k+1) point is ≤ C_KPC_Xc_δB₀[4C_Gc_k + a_k(C₁p₁(g_k))²]r_k ≤ C_e′r_k, uniformly in k (a_kp₁(g_k)² = g_kP(log g_k^{−2}) → 0 and c_k ≤ ℓ_K), geometric in k by (10.6): this is the Gaussian part of the depth source e_{k+1} of (8.3), which together with the classical part gives e_{k+1} ≤ C_eθ̃^{k+1}, and Σ_k of it is finite. The classical part of e_{k+1} is (9.10) at the fluctuated block field (§8.1). The configuration part, the variation group of (3.1) and its fluctuation-dependent version in §8.2, is the change of a frozen term under the scale-a smooth background change of §9.7, |𝒜| ≤ σ_k/a, |∇𝒜| ≤ σ_k/a²; the Cauchy estimate of 10.3 with the term's inductive bound (I.1.18) alone gives E₀(σ_k/α₁)L^{−2(k−j)} per scale-(j+1) point, i.e. E₀(σ_k/α₁)L^{2(k−j)}N_s⁴ at the stopping scale, whose sum over j is (a/ε)^{1+2ε₀}ε₁N_s⁴, not small: without the age factor the variation group is not controlled. The Gaussian depth itself needs no age factor.

## 11. The Relative Weight of the Large-Field Terms (Derived; Replaces (Q); Unreviewed)

The continuum note's (7.1)–(7.3) reduce the relative weight of the large-field terms to the quotient (Q) of two integrals over the stopping-scale variables on the neighbourhood Z^∼ of a large-field region with the exterior variables fixed, and (Q) is not derived there. The stopping lattice T_a is fixed and finite, with N_s⁴ points, so the two integrals are compared as wholes instead: the whole all-small-field integral is bounded below by the Haar volume of a ball of configurations, and every large-field term's integral is bounded above by the supremum of its small-field exponent, each at the price of a constant exponential in N_s⁴ and independent of K, which the patterns' factors exp(−(3/2)p₀(g_a)) absorb for ḡ small at the fixed torus, the setting of the continuum limit (the infinite-volume limit is not part of D2).
