\subsection{Cubes, localisation domains and tree lengths}
\label{app:domain-geometry}

The partitions $\pi_0$ (side $M$) and $\pi_1$ (side $LM$) of Section~\ref{sec:fixed-first-kernel} have cubes $Q_z=Mz+[-M/2,M/2]^4$ and $\Delta_z=LMz+[-LM/2,LM/2]^4$, reduced modulo the periods, with $M_1$ and $M$ odd. Cube faces therefore lie at half-integer fine coordinates: every lattice site lies in the interior of exactly one cube of each partition, a fine bond meets at most two cubes of each partition, and a fine bond lies in the interior of a union of cubes exactly when both its endpoints lie in cubes of that union. A cube has $M^4$ sites and meets at most $8M^4$ positive fine bonds. Two cubes of the same partition are wall adjacent when they share a three-dimensional face; a localisation domain of $\pi_i$ is a finite nonempty union of cubes of $\pi_i$ that is connected under wall adjacency (RG1 p.257). A localisation domain $Y$ of $\pi_i$ has $N_i(Y)$ cubes and, following RG1 p.257, tree length
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c2-tree-length}
    d_i(Y)=\inf\{\,|\tau|\, | \,\tau\subset Y\text{ a connected finite union of segments meeting every cube of }Y\,\},
\end{equation}
the Euclidean length $|\tau|$ being measured in units of the cube side. Put $\ell_i(Y)=d_i(Y)+5$. Two domains are compatible when they share neither a cube nor a wall (RG2 (2.11)); a domain is incompatible with itself.

\begin{lemma}[geometry of localisation domains]\label{lem:c2-domain-geometry}
Let $Y,Y_1,\ldots,Y_m$ be localisation domains of one partition and $C_{\mathrm g}=162$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item $N_i(Y)\leq C_{\mathrm g}\ell_i(Y)$ and $\ell_i(Y)\leq N_i(Y)+4$.
\item If $\bigcup_jY_j$ is a localisation domain, then $\ell_i(\bigcup_jY_j)\leq\sum_j\ell_i(Y_j)$.
\item If $Z$ is a localisation domain containing $Y_1,\ldots,Y_k$ ($k\geq0$, pairwise disjoint) and $Z\setminus\bigcup_jY_j$ consists of $n$ cubes, then $\ell_i(Z)\leq\sum_j\ell_i(Y_j)+5n$.
\item The number of localisation domains with $n$ cubes containing a given cube is at most $64^{n-1}$, and for $s>C_{\mathrm g}\log64$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:native-source-animal-sum}
    \sum_{Y\ni Q}e^{-s\ell_i(Y)}\leq B(s)=\frac{e^{-s/C_{\mathrm g}}}{1-64e^{-s/C_{\mathrm g}}}
\end{equation}
for every cube $Q$, on every torus. For $s\geq C_{\mathrm g}(\log64+\log2)$, $B(s)\leq2e^{-s/C_{\mathrm g}}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(i) Let $\tau$ realise \eqref{eq:c2-tree-length} up to $\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)$. A closed traversal of $\tau$ (each segment twice) has length at most $2(d_i(Y)+\varepsilon)$; place sample points along it at arclength spacing one, at most $2d_i(Y)+2$ of them. Every cube of $Y$ contains a point of $\tau$, which is within arclength $1/2$, hence Euclidean and $\ell^\infty$ distance $1/2$, of a sample; the centre of that cube is then within $\ell^\infty$ distance one of the sample. Cube centres form a unit lattice, so at most $3^4=81$ cubes are assigned to each sample, and $N_i(Y)\leq81(2d_i(Y)+2)\leq162\,\ell_i(Y)$. For the second inequality join the centres of wall-adjacent cubes along a spanning tree of the wall-adjacency graph; each of the $N_i(Y)-1$ edges has length one and lies in $Y$, so $d_i(Y)\leq N_i(Y)-1$.

(ii) Take trees $\tau_j\subset Y_j$ with $|\tau_j|\leq d_i(Y_j)+\varepsilon$. The graph on $\{Y_j\}$ with an edge when $Y_j\cap Y_k\ne\varnothing$ is connected: a wall-adjacent chain of cubes of the union passes from $Y_j$ to $Y_k$ through consecutive cubes that share a wall, hence through domains that intersect. For each edge of a spanning tree pick $x\in Y_j\cap Y_k$, a cube $Q_j\ni x$ of $Y_j$ and a point $p_j\in\tau_j\cap Q_j$, and add the segment $[p_j,x]\subset Q_j$ (length at most the diameter $2$ of a unit cube in $\mathbb R^4$), and likewise for $k$. The result is a connected union of segments inside $\bigcup Y_j$ meeting every cube, of length at most $\sum_j(d_i(Y_j)+\varepsilon)+4(m-1)$. Letting $\varepsilon\to0$, $d_i(\bigcup Y_j)+5\leq\sum_jd_i(Y_j)+4m+1\leq\sum_j(d_i(Y_j)+5)$ for $m\geq1$.

(iii) Apply (ii) to the family consisting of $Y_1,\ldots,Y_k$ and the $n$ single cubes, each of which has $\ell_i=5$.

(iv) A depth-first traversal of a spanning tree of the wall-adjacency graph of a domain with $n$ cubes, started at the given cube, is a word of length $2(n-1)$ over the eight wall directions, and determines the domain; this gives $64^{n-1}$. By (i), $e^{-s\ell_i(Y)}\leq e^{-sN_i(Y)/C_{\mathrm g}}$, so the rooted sum is at most $\sum_{n\geq1}64^{n-1}e^{-sn/C_{\mathrm g}}=B(s)$. The last claim is $64e^{-s/C_{\mathrm g}}\leq1/2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[scale comparison]\label{lem:c2-scale}
Let $Z$ be a localisation domain of $\pi_0$, $\widetilde Z$ the union of the $M$-cubes meeting $Z$, and $Z'$ the union of the $LM$-cubes meeting $\operatorname{int}\widetilde Z$. This is the enlargement rule of Section~\ref{sec:fixed-first-kernel}, which takes the $LM$-cubes meeting $\operatorname{int}Q^\sim$ for some cube $Q$ of $Z$: indeed $\widetilde Z=\bigcup_{Q\subseteq Z}Q^\sim$, an $LM$-cube meeting $\operatorname{int}Q^\sim$ meets $\operatorname{int}\widetilde Z$, and conversely an $LM$-cube $\Delta$ meeting $\operatorname{int}\widetilde Z$ contains a nonempty open subset of $\widetilde Z$, a finite union of the closed sets $Q^\sim$, one of which therefore contains a nonempty open subset of $\Delta$ (a finite union of closed sets with empty interiors has empty interior), so $\Delta$ meets $\operatorname{int}Q^\sim$ for that $Q$. The same argument shows, for a union $Z_0$ of $M$-cubes with wall components $Z_{0,j}$, that $Z_0'=\bigcup_jZ_{0,j}'$. Then $Z\subset\operatorname{int}\widetilde Z$, $\widetilde Z\subset Z'$, $Z'$ is a localisation domain of $\pi_1$, every point of $\widetilde Z$ is within $\ell^\infty$ distance $2M$ of every tree realising $d_0(Z)$ up to any $\varepsilon>0$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c2-scale-inequality}
    \ell_1(Z')\leq a_L\ell_0(Z)+b_L,\qquad a_L=\frac{2049}{L},\quad b_L=5+\frac{1024}{L}\;.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Every $M$-cube meeting a point of $Z$ belongs to $\widetilde Z$, so $Z\subset\operatorname{int}\widetilde Z$. An $M$-cube $\widetilde Q$ of $\widetilde Z$ lies in a single $LM$-cube, which meets $\operatorname{int}\widetilde Q\subset\operatorname{int}\widetilde Z$; hence $\widetilde Z\subset Z'$. If $\widetilde Q$ meets $Q\subset Z$ only at a lower-dimensional face, the cubes between them sharing walls also meet $Q$, so $\widetilde Z$ is wall connected, $\operatorname{int}\widetilde Z$ is connected, and the $LM$-cubes meeting a connected open set are wall connected (a path avoiding lower-dimensional faces passes between consecutive cubes through a common wall). Let $\tau\subset Z$ meet every $M$-cube of $Z$ with $|\tau|\leq M(d_0(Z)+\varepsilon)$ in fine units. For $x\in\widetilde Z$ pick $\widetilde Q\ni x$, a cube $Q\subset Z$ meeting $\widetilde Q$, $y\in\widetilde Q\cap Q$ and $p\in\tau\cap Q$; then $|x-p|_\infty\leq|x-y|_\infty+|y-p|_\infty\leq2M$.

For \eqref{eq:c2-scale-inequality}, place samples along a closed traversal of $\tau$ at arclength spacing $LM$; there are at most $2(d_0(Z)+\varepsilon)/L+1$ of them, and every point of $\tau$ is within Euclidean distance $LM/2$ of a sample. For $\Delta\subset Z'$ pick $x_\Delta\in\Delta\cap\operatorname{int}\widetilde Z$; by the preceding paragraph it is within $\ell^\infty$ distance $2M+LM/2\leq7LM/6$ of a sample ($L\geq3$), so the centre of $\Delta$ is within $\ell^\infty$ distance $5LM/3<2LM$ of that sample. The centres of $LM$-cubes form a lattice of spacing $LM$, and an open $\ell^\infty$ ball of radius $2LM$ contains at most $4^4=256$ of its points. Hence $N_1(Z')\leq256(2(d_0(Z)+\varepsilon)/L+1)$. Now build a connected union of segments in $Z'$ meeting every $LM$-cube of $Z'$: start from $\tau\subset Z\subset Z'$ and, for each $\Delta\subset Z'$ not met by $\tau$, add the segments $[x_\Delta,y]\subset\widetilde Q\subset Z'$ and $[y,p]\subset Q\subset Z'$ chosen above, each of length at most $2M$. Its length is at most $M(d_0(Z)+\varepsilon)+4MN_1(Z')$, so
\begin{equation*}
    d_1(Z')\leq\frac{d_0(Z)+\varepsilon}{L}+\frac{4}{L}\cdot256\Bigl(\frac{2(d_0(Z)+\varepsilon)}{L}+1\Bigr)\leq\frac{2049}{L}(d_0(Z)+\varepsilon)+\frac{1024}{L}\;.
\end{equation*}
Let $\varepsilon\to0$, add $5$ to both sides and use $d_0(Z)\leq\ell_0(Z)$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The regular-background bounds}
\label{app:regular-covariance}

\begin{lemma}[entry range and stencils]\label{lem:c2-entry-range}
For free bonds $b,b'$ let $z(b)$ be the coarse label of the block containing $b_-$ and $f_{bb'}(U)$ the entry function \eqref{eq:c-entry-function} with its entry stencil $\mathcal S_{bb'}$ (the union of the background sets of the terms of \eqref{eq:regular-stencil-quadratic-decomposition} whose free-input set contains both $b$ and $b'$). If $|z(b)-z(b')|_1>2$ then $\mathcal T_{bb'}=\varnothing$ and $f_{bb'}=0$. The stencil of a nonzero entry consists of bonds $e$ with $|z(e)-z(b)|_1\leq2$; in fine units all bonds and sites of $\mathcal S_{bb'}$, and both endpoints of $b$ and $b'$, lie within $\ell^\infty$ distance $3L$ of $b_-$, so $\rho_L=4L$ is an admissible entry range in Lemma~\ref{lem:hessian-gauge-locality}. Each row has at most $164L^4$ nonzero entries.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:regular-stencil-quadratic-decomposition} and its support sets, a term containing the free input $b$ is $\mathfrak a_p$ with $b\in\mathcal F_r$ for a boundary bond $r$ of $p$, $\mathfrak g_y$ with $b\in\mathcal E_y$, or $\mathfrak t_c$ with $b\in\mathcal E_c\cap E_*$. The free inputs of a bond $r$ have block labels among the endpoint blocks of $r$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:two-block}): $z(r)$ for a free bond, $\{c_-,c_+\}$ for $r=b_0(c)$. The four vertices of a fine plaquette in directions $\mu,\nu$ have block labels differing only in the $\mu$ and $\nu$ coordinates, by at most one each, so these labels, and the endpoint blocks of its boundary bonds, lie in a set of $\ell^1$ diameter at most two. The free inputs of $\mathfrak g_y$ lie in one block, those of $\mathfrak t_c$ in the two blocks of $c$. Hence two free inputs of one term have $|z(b)-z(b')|_1\leq2$, and the background bonds of that term (plaquette bonds, the sets $\mathcal E_c$, and for $\mathfrak t_c$ the plaquettes meeting $b_0(c)$, whose vertices lie in the two blocks of $c$) have block labels within $\ell^1$ distance two of $z(b)$. A site of a block with label within $\ell^1$ distance two of $z(b)$ is within $\ell^\infty$ distance $2L+(L-1)<3L$ of $b_-$; the endpoints of $b$ and $b'$ are such sites or within one of them. The $\ell^1$ ball of radius two in $\mathbb Z^4$ has $41$ points and a block has at most $4L^4$ positive bonds with initial site in it.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[plaquette deviation on the collar]\label{lem:c2-plaquette-deviation}
Let $B_3$, $\delta_{\mathrm c}$ and the $O(1)$ be the constants of the comparison following CRE (1.19), as recorded in (B3)(iii): for every $L^2M_2R_1$-cube $\square'\subseteq\Omega_1^{\sim-1}\setminus R_1$ (CRE's $\square'\subset R_1^c$, p.250 L38--39, for which $\square'^{\sim4}\subseteq\Omega_1$, p.250 L46), $|U_1(e)U_{1,\square'}(e)^{-1}-1|<O(1)B_3e^{-\delta_{\mathrm c}LM_2R_1}\varepsilon_1$ on the bonds $e$ of $\square'^{\sim2}$ (p.250 L46--50). CRE states no dependence for these constants: it reuses the symbols $B_3$ and $\delta$ at every step (p.268 L40--45, the regularity bound $|\partial U_{k+1}-1|<2B_3\varepsilon_{k+1}(L^{-1}\eta)^2$ and the comparison with $B_3\exp(-\delta LM_2R_{k+1})44d^2B_3\varepsilon_{k+1}$) and derives the comparison (3.19) from these facts and the bounds (106)--(108), (159)--(163) of [12] (p.268 L50). This paper uses one property of them only, the reading recorded in (B3)(iii): $B_3$, $\delta_{\mathrm c}$ and the $O(1)$ do not depend on the coupling $g_0$ or on the branch, so that at fixed $L$ and $M_2$ the factor $e^{-\delta_{\mathrm c}LM_2R_1}$ tends to zero as $g_0\to0$. CRE's $\delta$ is written $\delta_{\mathrm c}$ here to distinguish it from the Mayer constant $\delta$ of Section~\ref{app:mayer-constants}. Reduce the coupling threshold until $c_{\mathrm h}\,O(1)B_3e^{-\delta_{\mathrm c}LM_2R_1}\leq1/4$, which holds for small $g_0$ because the constants do not depend on $g_0$ and $R_1\geq(\log g_1^{-2})^{\mathfrak r}\to\infty$ as $g_0\to0$ (CRE (2.5) and (2.6), p.255: $g_1\leq(1+\beta_0)g_0$ and $g_0\leq(1+\beta_0)g_1$, so $g_1\to0$ with $g_0$), and let $R_{\mathrm c}$ satisfy Lemma~\ref{lem:collar-coverage}. Here $\varepsilon_1=g_1p_0(g_1)$ is CRE's constant of the same name (p.246 L60, CRE (2.4)) and $g_1$ the first-step coupling. On every branch, every fine plaquette $p$ having a bond within distance $3R_{\mathrm c}$ of $I$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c2-plaquette-deviation}
    \|U_1(\partial p)-\id\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq2c_{\mathrm h}\varepsilon_1\;.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $b$ be such a bond and $\square'$ the cube of the partition of CRE (1.18) into cubes of side $L^2M_2R_1$ that contains $b_-$ (a partition, so the cube is unique). By Lemma~\ref{lem:collar-coverage}(i) (stated for $d(b,I)\leq3R_{\mathrm c}$; the standing condition $LMR_1>4R_{\mathrm c}+4L^2M_2R_1+8$ gives $3R_{\mathrm c}+1+L^2M_2R_1<LMR_1-1$), $b_-\in\Omega_1^{\sim-1}$ and $\square'$ is a good cube of CRE (1.18), $\square'\subset R_1^c\subset\Omega_1^{\sim-1}$; since $M\geq8LM_2$, the layer of $LMR_1$-cubes around $\Omega_1^{\sim-1}$ inside $\Omega_1$ contains $\square'^{\sim4}$, as CRE states after (1.19). The outer weight \eqref{eq:native-source-explicit-outer-operation} contains the background test $\chi_1(\Omega_1)$ of CRE (2.17) at $k=1$, a function of $V$, and it is nonzero on a branch: the pair is admissible, so $D^{(11)}(V,V_0)\ne\varnothing$ (Section~\ref{sec:branch-data}), and $\chi_1(\Omega_1)\ne0$ at every point of $D^{(11)}$. The plaquette $p$ contains $b$, hence lies in $\square'^{\sim}$. Writing the plaquette holonomy as an ordered product of four unitaries and telescoping, $\|U_1(\partial p)-U_{1,\square'}(\partial p)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\sum_{e\subset\partial p}\|U_1(e)-U_{1,\square'}(e)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\varepsilon_1$, and $c_{\mathrm h}\varepsilon_1L^{-2}+\varepsilon_1\leq2c_{\mathrm h}\varepsilon_1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[local gauge on a rectangle]\label{lem:c2-local-gauge}
Let $\mathfrak P$ be a product of fine integer intervals of side at most $s_{\mathfrak P}$ (a lifted rectangle, injective modulo the periods), and let $U$ be a configuration whose plaquettes inside $\mathfrak P$ satisfy $\|U(\partial p)-\id\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq2c_{\mathrm h}\varepsilon_1$ with $6c_{\mathrm h}s_{\mathfrak P}\varepsilon_1\leq r_G$. There is a site gauge $u$ on $\mathfrak P$ such that every bond $b\subset\mathfrak P$ has $U^u(b)=e^{iA_b}$ with $A_b\in\mathfrak g$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c2-local-gauge-bound}
    h(A_b)\leq\widehat\mu_{\mathfrak P}=6c_Gc_{\mathrm h}s_{\mathfrak P}\varepsilon_1\;.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix the corner $x_0$ of $\mathfrak P$ with minimal coordinates. For $x\in\mathfrak P$ let $P(x)$ be the lattice path from $x_0$ to $x$ that runs first in direction $e_1$, then $e_2,e_3,e_4$; it stays in $\mathfrak P$. These paths form a spanning tree; define $u(x_0)=\id$ and $u(x')=u(x)U(x,x')$ along tree bonds oriented away from the root. Then $U^u(x,x')=u(x)U(x,x')u(x')^{-1}=\id$ on tree bonds, and for any bond $(x,x')\subset\mathfrak P$, $U^u(x,x')=U(P(x))U(x,x')U(P(x'))^{-1}$ is the holonomy of the closed loop $P(x)\cdot(x,x')\cdot P(x')^{-1}$ based at $x_0$. Let $(x,x')$ have direction $e_\mu$, so $x'=x+e_\mu$. The two tree paths share their runs in directions $\nu<\mu$ and their first $x_\mu-x_{0,\mu}$ steps in direction $\mu$; after conjugation by the holonomy of that common initial segment, which preserves $\|\cdot-\id\|_{\mathrm{op}}$, the loop becomes $B\cdot(x,x')\cdot B'^{-1}\cdot(y+e_\mu,y)$, where $B=(b_1,\ldots,b_k)$ is the staircase from $y=(x_1,\ldots,x_\mu,x_{0,\mu+1},\ldots,x_{0,4})$ to $x$ in directions $\nu>\mu$, $B'$ its translate by $e_\mu$, and $k\leq3s_{\mathfrak P}$. Writing $\mathcal L_j$ for the loop built from $(b_1,\ldots,b_j)$, one checks $\mathcal L_j=(b_1\cdots b_{j-1})\,\partial p_j\,(b_1\cdots b_{j-1})^{-1}\cdot\mathcal L_{j-1}$, where $\partial p_j=b_je_\mu b_j'^{-1}e_\mu^{-1}$ is the boundary of the plaquette spanned by $b_j$ and $e_\mu$, which lies in $\mathfrak P$. Hence the holonomy of the loop is an ordered product of $k$ conjugates of plaquette holonomies, and telescoping gives $\|U^u(x,x')-\id\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq2kc_{\mathrm h}\varepsilon_1\leq6c_{\mathrm h}s_{\mathfrak P}\varepsilon_1\leq r_G$. The element $g=U^u(x,x')\in G$ therefore lies in $N_G$, and $A_b=\log_Gg\in\mathfrak g$ is the unique $X$ in the ball $h(X)<\varrho_G$ with $e^{iX}=g$ (the group chart of the conventions), with $h(A_b)\leq c_G\|g-\id\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq6c_Gc_{\mathrm h}s_{\mathfrak P}\varepsilon_1$.
\end{proof}

Here are the fixed geometric choices. Reduce $\tau_L$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:two-block} so that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c2-tau-final}
    \tau_L\leq\min\Bigl(1,\ 2c_Gr_G,\ \frac{r_G}{2(L-1)c},\ \frac{r_M}{4(L-1)c\,c_G}\Bigr)\;,
\end{equation}
with $c$, $r_G$, $c_G$ the group constants of the preamble (depending on the group only) and $r_M$ the radius of Lemma~\ref{lem:c2-uniform-chart}(iv) (depending on $L$ and the group); this is the final value of $\tau_L$, used in every statement. Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:regular-covariance-radius-replay}
    c_{1,L}=320000\,m_L,\qquad
    R_{\mathrm p}=\max\Bigl(2,\Bigl\lceil2\sqrt{c_{1,L}/\gamma_L}\Bigr\rceil\Bigr),\qquad
    s_{\mathfrak P}=L(2R_{\mathrm p}+5)+1,\qquad
    C_{L,R_{\mathrm c}}=6c_Gc_{\mathrm h}s_{\mathfrak P}\;,
\end{equation}
and choose the collar range $R_{\mathrm c}\geq\max(2R_{\mathrm{loc}}+1,\ s_{\mathfrak P}+2)$. Require every coarse period to exceed $2R_{\mathrm p}+8$, so that the coarse $\ell^\infty$ ball of radius $R_{\mathrm p}+3$ about any coarse label lifts injectively, and impose on $\varepsilon_1$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:convex-background-thresholds}
    \widehat\mu=C_{L,R_{\mathrm c}}\varepsilon_1<\frac{\tau_L}{2},\qquad
    \max(1,c_L)\widehat\mu<\frac{\gamma_L}{4}\;.
\end{equation}
Since $\tau_L\leq2c_Gr_G$, the first condition gives $6c_{\mathrm h}s_{\mathfrak P}\varepsilon_1=\widehat\mu/c_G<r_G$, the hypothesis of Lemma~\ref{lem:c2-local-gauge}.

\begin{lemma}[regional entries]\label{lem:c2-regional-entry}
Let $\Omega$ be a region and $U$ a background on which the regional form $\mathsf H^\Omega_U$ is defined (Section~\ref{sec:regular-hessian-stencils}: $\mathsf M(U)$ defined and $\alpha_c(U)$ invertible on the rows of $\Omega^{[1]}$, the local group means $\mathcal U_U(y,x)$ defined for $y\in\Omega^{(1)}$). For all $(b,b')\in\Omega^*\times\Omega^*$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:convex-regional-entry}
    (\mathsf H^\Omega_U)_{bb'}=f_{bb'}(U|_{\mathcal S_{bb'}})\;,
\end{equation}
the entry function \eqref{eq:c-entry-function} evaluated on the background restricted to the entry stencil $\mathcal S_{bb'}$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:c2-entry-range}, and $f_{bb'}=0$ when $|z(b)-z(b')|_1>2$. In particular, on every branch, $(H_\xi)_{bb'}=f_{bb'}(U_1|_{\mathcal S_{bb'}})$ for all $b,b'\in\Omega_1^*$, and for $b,b'\in I$ the stencil $\mathcal S_{bb'}$ lies inside $\Omega_1$ (within $3L<R_{\mathrm c}$ of $I$, Lemma~\ref{lem:collar-coverage}(i)).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
This is the compression identity \eqref{eq:c-regional-compression} of Section~\ref{sec:regular-hessian-stencils}, whose proof is recalled: the regional form is the sum of the terms of \eqref{eq:regular-stencil-quadratic-decomposition} with index in $\Omega$ (plaquettes meeting $\Omega$, centres in $\Omega$, rows in $\Omega^{[1]}$), its entry at $(b,b')$ is the sum of the polarised entries of those of these terms whose free-input set contains $b$ and $b'$, and every term whose free-input set contains a bond $b$ of $\Omega^*$ has its index in $\Omega$: a plaquette term $\mathfrak a_p$ with $b\in\mathcal F_r$, $r\subset\partial p$, has $r=b$ or $r=b_0(c)$ with $b\in\mathcal E_c$, and in either case $p$ contains a bond with an endpoint in the block of the endpoint of $b$ that lies in $\Omega$ ($b$ has such an endpoint, being a bond of $\Omega$; the bonds of $\mathcal E_c$ have both endpoints in the two blocks of $c$, and $b_0(c)$ has one endpoint in each), and that block lies in $\Omega$, a union of blocks; a gauge term $\mathfrak g_y$ with $b\in\mathcal E_y$ has $y\in\Omega$; a row term $\mathfrak t_c$ with $b\in\mathcal E_c\cap E_*$ has an endpoint block of $c$ containing an endpoint of $b$, so $c\in\Omega^{[1]}$. Hence the set of terms contributing to the regional entry is exactly $\mathcal T_{bb'}$, and the entry is $f_{bb'}(U|_{\mathcal S_{bb'}})$, a finite formula in the background on the stencil in which the rowwise elimination \eqref{eq:regular-stencil-elimination} uses only the rows of the terms in $\mathcal T_{bb'}$, which are rows of $\Omega^{[1]}$, where $\alpha_c(U)$ is invertible: the rows of $\mathfrak g_y$ and $\mathfrak t_c$ are rows with a centre in $\Omega$ by the above, and a row $c'$ of $\mathfrak a_p$ has $b_0(c')\subset\partial p$, whose endpoints are the two sites of the segment between the centres of $c'$ next to the common face of its blocks; the other two vertices of $p$ are their translates by one unit in a direction perpendicular to $c'$, which stay in the same two blocks as $L\geq3$; so all four vertices of $p$ lie in the two blocks of $c'$, one of which meets $\Omega$ since $p$ does, hence lies in $\Omega$, and $c'\in\Omega^{[1]}$. The vanishing for $|z(b)-z(b')|_1>2$ is Lemma~\ref{lem:c2-entry-range}. For the last claim, by Lemma~\ref{lem:c2-entry-range} every site of $\mathcal S_{bb'}$ is within $3L$ of $b_-\in I$, hence within $3R_{\mathrm c}$ of $I$ and in $\Omega_1^{\sim-1}$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:collar-coverage}(i).
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:regular-bounds}]
Extend $B\in\mathfrak g^I$ by zero. By Lemma~\ref{lem:c2-regional-entry}, $\langle B,H_\xi B\rangle=\sum_{b,b'\in I}\langle B_b,f_{bb'}(U_1|_{\mathcal S_{bb'}})B_{b'}\rangle$ with the local entries, whose stencils lie inside $\Omega_1$; the vanishing $H_{\xi,II}(b,b')=0$ for $d(b,b')>\rho_L$ is Lemma~\ref{lem:c2-entry-range}. The quantities $m_L$, $c_L$ of \eqref{eq:regular-covariance-entry-constants} bound the row sums $\sum_{b'}$ of $\|f_{bb'}(A)\|$ and $\|Df_{bb'}(A)\|$ over $\|A\|_\infty\leq\tau_L$; since $f_{b'b}(A)=f_{bb'}(A)^*$ (the form is symmetric) and hence $Df_{b'b}(A)[X]=(Df_{bb'}(A)[X])^*$, the column sums obey the same bounds.

\emph{Partition of unity.} Let $\mathcal Z_T$ be the coarse grid and $\psi(u)=\prod_{\mu=1}^4(1-|u_\mu|/R_{\mathrm p})_+$ on the lifted coarse ball of radius $R_{\mathrm p}$, extended by zero and periodised (injectively, by the period condition). For $w\in\mathcal Z_T$ put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:regular-covariance-partition}
    \chi_w(b)=\frac{\psi(z(b)-w)}{S^{1/2}},\qquad S=\sum_{u}\psi(u)^2=\Bigl(\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}(1-|k|/R_{\mathrm p})_+^2\Bigr)^4\geq\Bigl(\frac{R_{\mathrm p}}{4}\Bigr)^4\;,
\end{equation}
the last inequality because at least $R_{\mathrm p}$ integers $k$ satisfy $|k|\leq R_{\mathrm p}/2$ and each contributes at least $1/4$. Then $\sum_w\chi_w(b)^2=1$ for every $b$. A one-step difference $|\psi(u)-\psi(u+e_\nu)|$ is at most $1/R_{\mathrm p}$, since only one factor changes and the others are at most one, and it vanishes unless $u$ or $u+e_\nu$ lies in the support, a set of at most $(2R_{\mathrm p}+2)^4\leq(5R_{\mathrm p})^4$ points. Telescoping along a coarse path of $n=|z(b)-z(b')|_1$ steps and using $(\sum_{i=1}^na_i)^2\leq n\sum a_i^2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:regular-covariance-cutoff-error}
    \sum_w(\chi_w(b)-\chi_w(b'))^2\leq\frac{1}{S}\,n\sum_{i=1}^n\sum_w(\text{one-step difference})^2\leq\frac{256}{R_{\mathrm p}^4}\,n^2\,\frac{(5R_{\mathrm p})^4}{R_{\mathrm p}^2}=\frac{160000}{R_{\mathrm p}^2}\,|z(b)-z(b')|_1^2\;.
\end{equation}
Since $\sum_w\chi_w(b)\chi_w(b')-1=-\frac12\sum_w(\chi_w(b)-\chi_w(b'))^2$, Lemma~\ref{lem:c2-entry-range} ($n\leq2$ on nonzero entries) and the Schur bound $\sum_{b,b'}\|f_{bb'}\|h(B_b)h(B_{b'})\leq m_L\|B\|^2$, valid because row and column sums of $\|f_{bb'}\|$ are at most $m_L$ (shown below), give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:regular-covariance-localisation-error}
    \Bigl|\sum_w\langle\chi_wB,H_\xi\chi_wB\rangle-\langle B,H_\xi B\rangle\Bigr|\leq\frac12\cdot\frac{160000\cdot4}{R_{\mathrm p}^2}\,m_L\|B\|^2=\frac{c_{1,L}}{R_{\mathrm p}^2}\|B\|^2\leq\frac{\gamma_L}{4}\|B\|^2\;.
\end{equation}

\emph{Local comparison.} Fix $w$ with $\chi_wB\ne0$; since $B$ is supported on $I$, some $b\in I$ has $\chi_w(b)\neq0$, so the patch $\{b\, | \,|z(b)-w|_\infty\leq R_{\mathrm p}\}$ meets $I$. Let $\mathfrak P_w$ be the lifted rectangle of fine sites in blocks with $|z-w|_\infty\leq R_{\mathrm p}+2$, of side at most $L(2R_{\mathrm p}+5)+1=s_{\mathfrak P}$; it lies within fine distance $s_{\mathfrak P}+1\leq2R_{\mathrm c}-1$ of $I$, so Lemma~\ref{lem:c2-plaquette-deviation} applies to all its plaquettes and Lemma~\ref{lem:c2-local-gauge} gives a gauge $u_w$ on $\mathfrak P_w$ with phases $h(A_e)\leq\widehat\mu$. Extend $u_w$ by the identity. By Lemma~\ref{lem:c2-entry-range} the stencils of all entries with both labels in the patch lie in $\mathfrak P_w$. With $T_w=R_{u_w,*}$ the orthogonal action of Lemma~\ref{lem:hessian-gauge-locality},
\begin{equation*}
    \langle\chi_wB,H_\xi\chi_wB\rangle=\sum_{b,b'}\langle(T_w\chi_wB)_b,f_{bb'}(A)(T_w\chi_wB)_{b'}\rangle
    =\langle T_w\chi_wB,\mathsf H_{\id}T_w\chi_wB\rangle+\sum_{b,b'}\langle(T_w\chi_wB)_b,(f_{bb'}(A)-f_{bb'}(0))(T_w\chi_wB)_{b'}\rangle\;,
\end{equation*}
where $f_{bb'}(A)$ is the entry at the gauge-transformed background $U_1^{u_w}=e^{iA}$ on the stencil; the first equality is the entrywise gauge covariance \eqref{eq:c-entry-gauge-covariance}, $f_{bb'}(U^u)=\operatorname{Ad}_{u(b_-)}f_{bb'}(U)\operatorname{Ad}_{u(b'_-)}^{-1}$, together with $(T_w\chi_wB)_b=\operatorname{Ad}_{u_w(b_-)}\chi_w(b)B_b$ and the invariance of the inner product.
The map $A\mapsto f_{bb'}(A)$ is analytic on $\|A\|_\infty\leq\tau_L$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:hessian-gauge-locality}) and $\|A\|_\infty\leq\widehat\mu<\tau_L/2$, so $\|f_{bb'}(A)-f_{bb'}(0)\|\leq\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\|Df_{bb'}(tA)\|\,\widehat\mu$, and the row and column sums of these bounds are at most $c_L\widehat\mu$ by \eqref{eq:regular-covariance-entry-constants}; the Schur test gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:regular-covariance-local-error}
    \Bigl|\langle\chi_wB,H_\xi\chi_wB\rangle-\langle T_w\chi_wB,\mathsf H_{\id}T_w\chi_wB\rangle\Bigr|\leq c_L\widehat\mu\,\|\chi_wB\|^2\;.
\end{equation}
The operator $\mathsf H_{\id}$ is the flat Hessian $\mathsf H$ of \eqref{eq:flat-covariance}: at the identity $J_{\id}=0$, every relative mean $\mathcal U_{\id}(y,x)$ is the identity, so $\mathscr G_{\id}$ is the gauge functional of \eqref{eq:action-primitive}, and $\mathsf C_{\id}$ is \eqref{eq:constraint-extension}. By \eqref{eq:covariance-uniform-gap} on the branch's torus, $\langle T_w\chi_wB,\mathsf H_{\id}T_w\chi_wB\rangle\geq\gamma_L\|\chi_wB\|^2$. Summing over $w$ and using $\sum_w\|\chi_wB\|^2=\|B\|^2$ and \eqref{eq:convex-background-thresholds},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:regular-covariance-lower-bound}
    \langle B,H_\xi B\rangle\geq\Bigl(\gamma_L-\frac{c_{1,L}}{R_{\mathrm p}^2}-c_L\widehat\mu\Bigr)\|B\|^2\geq\frac{\gamma_L}{2}\|B\|^2\;.
\end{equation}

\emph{Upper bound.} For a row $b\in I$ all entries $f_{bb'}$ have stencils in $\mathfrak P_{z(b)}$ (the patch of $w=z(b)$ meets $I$ at $b$); in the gauge $u_{z(b)}$ of that patch their phases are at most $\widehat\mu\leq\tau_L$, and $\|f_{bb'}\|$ is unchanged by the fibre rotations of \eqref{eq:c-entry-gauge-covariance}, which are orthogonal. Hence $\sum_{b'}\|f_{bb'}(U_1)\|\leq m_L$ by \eqref{eq:regular-covariance-entry-constants}, and by the symmetry noted at the start the column sums obey the same bound. The Schur test gives $\|H_{\xi,II}\|\leq m_L$. Both bounds pass to principal restrictions: the lower bound by compression, the upper by the Schur test on fewer entries. This proves \eqref{eq:c-regular-two-sided} and the range statement.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Uniform charts and primitive bounds}
\label{app:charts}

Primitives are evaluated on the input $A=g_0\mathsf C_UB$ with the free vector $B=a+tA_{\mathrm e}$, $t\in[0,1]$ (the vacuum potentials of Section~\ref{sec:selected-integrals} have $t=0$). For a coarse row $c$ the relevant input is $A|_{\mathcal E_c}$, with the Hilbert norm on $\mathfrak g^{\mathcal E_c}$ (written $\|A|_{\mathcal E_c}\|$ or $\|A\|$). Lemma~\ref{lem:charts} asks, for every row $c$ within $2R_{\mathrm c}+2L$ of $I$ (every bond of $\mathcal E_c$ within $2R_{\mathrm c}+2L$ of $I$), for the existence and uniqueness of the small solution ($h(d_c)<\rho_{\mathrm d}=2K_*\vartheta_*^2$) of the row equation for every real phase with $\|A|_{\mathcal E_c}\|\leq\vartheta_*/2$, given by a function $\varphi_c$ analytic on the complex ball of radius $\vartheta_*$ and real on real phases, the positivity of the determinant, and the analyticity in $B|_{\mathcal E_c\cap E_*}$ on the row polydisc $\mathcal U_c=\{B\, | \,|B(b)|<2+4r\ \forall b\in\mathcal E_c\cap E_*\}$ (which $g_0\mathsf C_U$ maps into that phase ball under threshold item 3) of the row solution, the phase, the two logarithms and the plaquette terms of the plaquettes with a bond in $\mathcal E_c$; for every centre $y$ within $2R_{\mathrm c}+2L$ of $I$, for the existence of the relative means and the analyticity of the gauge term on the block polydisc $\mathcal U_y=\{|B(b)|<2+4r\ \forall b\in\mathcal E_y\}$; in each case as compositions of phase functions with derivatives up to order five bounded on the closed phase ball $\|A\|\leq\vartheta_*/2$ by constants depending on $L$ and the group only, with the primitive sizes \eqref{eq:c2-primitive-sizes}; Lemmas~\ref{lem:c2-uniform-chart} and~\ref{lem:c2-primitive-bounds} supply this.

\begin{lemma}[uniform implicit chart]\label{lem:c2-uniform-chart}
There are constants $\varrho_*,\vartheta_*>0$, $H_*,K_*\geq1$, $C_{\mathsf C}\geq\max(1,(4L^3(L-1))^{1/2})$, $C_{\mathsf D}$, $r_M,r'_M>0$ with $r'_M\leq\varrho_G$, and derivative constants $C^{\mathsf D}_k$ for $0\leq k\leq6$ and $C^{\mathrm a}_k$ for $0\leq k\leq5$, depending on $L$ and the group only, with $2\vartheta_*<\varrho_*$, $\vartheta_*\leq\tau_L/2$ and $4K_*H_*\vartheta_*<1$, such that for every background $U$ admitting a site gauge $u$ in which the phases $\phi$, $U^u=e^{i\phi}$, satisfy $\|\phi\|_\infty\leq\tau_L/2$ on $\mathcal E_c$, the following statements hold. The operator and derivative bounds in (i)--(iii) transfer under the orthogonal fibre gauge action. In (iv), the near-identity assertions for open paths and the mean itself are made in the chosen gauge $u$; only the relative-mean and gauge-functional conclusions are transferred to $U$. All formulas depending on $\vartheta_*$, including $\rho_{\mathrm d}$ and $C^{\mathsf D}_k$, use its final reduced value. Norms of phases on $\mathcal E_c$ are the Hilbert norms of $\mathfrak g^{\mathcal E_c}$ and of its complexification (so $\|d\|=h(d)$ for a single $d\in\mathfrak g$).
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\|h_U(c)\|\leq H_*$; $\|\mathsf C_UB\|_{\mathcal E_c}\leq C_{\mathsf C}\max_{b\in\mathcal E_c\cap E_*}|B_b|$ and $\|\mathsf C_UB\|_{\mathcal E_c}\leq C_{\mathsf C}\|B|_{\mathcal E_c\cap E_*}\|$ (Hilbert norms); and $\mathcal N_{U,c}$ is analytic on the open complex ball $\|A\|<2\varrho_*$ of $\mathfrak g^{\mathcal E_c}_{\mathbb C}$ with $\mathcal N_{U,c}(0)=0$, $D\mathcal N_{U,c}(0)=0$ and $\|D^2\mathcal N_{U,c}\|\leq K_*$ on the closed ball $\|A\|\leq\varrho_*$; $\mathcal N_{U,c}$ is real on real $A$.
\item On the open complex ball $\|A\|<\vartheta_*$ there is an analytic function $\varphi_c=\widetilde D_{U,c}$, real on real $A$, with $\varphi_c(0)=0$, $D\varphi_c(0)=0$, such that for every $A$ in that ball $\varphi_c(A)$ is the unique solution $d$ of the row equation \eqref{eq:native-source-local-implicit} with $\|d\|\leq2K_*\vartheta_*^2=\rho_{\mathrm d}$ (in particular, for real $A$ the unique small solution in the sense of Section~\ref{sec:source-phase-primitives}); it agrees with the germ of Lemma~\ref{lem:row-locality}, and satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:convex-uniform-implicit-chart}
    \|\widetilde D_{U,c}(A)\|\leq2K_*\|A\|^2,\qquad
    \|D_A\widetilde D_{U,c}(A)\|\leq C_{\mathsf D}\|A\|\;,\qquad C_{\mathsf D}=4K_*\;.
\end{equation}
\item On the closed real ball $\|A\|\leq\vartheta_*/2$ the determinant $\det_{\mathfrak g}(\id-h_U(c)\partial_{A(b_0(c))}\widetilde D_{U,c}(A))$ is positive. On a complex neighbourhood of the closed complex ball $\|A\|\leq\vartheta_*/2$ the functions $\widetilde D_{U,c}$, the phase $q_U(A)|_{\mathcal E_c}=A-h_U\widetilde D_U(A)$, the normalised Haar logarithm $\log\bigl(\sigma(q_U(A)(b))/\sigma(0)\bigr)$, $b\in\mathcal E_c$, and the logarithm of that determinant are analytic (the two logarithms being the real logarithms on real $A$ and their analytic continuations on the complex neighbourhood), and on the closed ball their derivatives of order $k\leq5$ are bounded in operator norm by $C^{\mathrm a}_k$, uniformly in the background; for $\widetilde D_{U,c}$ this holds for $k\leq6$ with $C^{\mathsf D}_k=e^kk!\,2K_*\vartheta_*^2(2/\vartheta_*)^k$. The two logarithms vanish at $A=0$, and the first derivative of the Haar logarithm vanishes there ($\sigma(0)>0$ is the value at the identity of the Haar density $\sigma$, which \eqref{eq:native-source-haar-primitive} subtracts); no statement is made about the first derivative of the determinant logarithm at $A=0$.
\item For a block centre $y$ whose block $\mathcal B_y$ lies in the gauge patch of $u$ (phases $\|\phi\|_\infty\leq\tau_L/2$ on $\mathcal E_y$), put $q^u=R_uq$, where $(R_uq)(b)=\operatorname{Ad}_{u(b_-)}q(b)$, so $\|q^u|_{\mathcal E_y}\|=\|q|_{\mathcal E_y}\|$. For every real $q$ with $\|q|_{\mathcal E_y}\|\leq\vartheta_*$, the path transports $(e^{iq^u}U^u)(\gamma)$, $\gamma\in\mathcal G(y,x)$, $x\in\mathcal B_y$, are within $r_G$ of $\id$ in operator norm, their logarithms $\log_G$ have $h<r_M$, and the local group mean $\mathcal U_{e^{iq^u}U^u}(y,x)$ of \eqref{eq:action-primitive} (RG1 (0.10)--(0.11)), the unique solution near the diagonal value, exists, of the form $e^{iX}$ with $h(X)<r'_M\leq\varrho_G$; the relative mean $\mathcal U_{e^{iq}U}(y,x)\mathcal U_U(y,x)^{-1}$ lies within $r_G$ of $\id$ in operator norm (the domain of $\log_G$), and it and the gauge term $\mathscr G_{y,U}(q)$ are restrictions to real $q$ of functions analytic on the open complex ball $\|q|_{\mathcal E_y}\|<2\vartheta_*$, with derivatives up to order five bounded by $C^{\mathrm a}_k$ on the closed complex ball of radius $\vartheta_*$, uniformly in the background, and $\mathscr G_{y,U}(0)=0$, $D\mathscr G_{y,U}(0)=0$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
There are finitely many row types modulo $L\mathbb Z^4$. For each type the maps $U\mapsto h_U(c)$, $\mathsf C_U$ and $(U,A)\mapsto\mathcal N_{U,c}(A)$ are compositions of exponentials, relative block averages, the fibre inverse $\alpha_c(U)^{-1}$ and the chart logarithm, all analytic for phases $\|\phi\|_\infty\leq\tau_L$ and $A$ near zero (Lemma~\ref{lem:two-block} gives the singular-value bound $L^{-4}/2$ on $\alpha_c$), and real for real arguments. Under the simultaneous gauge transformation of Lemma~\ref{lem:hessian-gauge-locality}, $Q_U,h_U,\mathsf C_U$ and $\mathcal N_U$ intertwine with the orthogonal fibre action $R_{u,*}$ (proof of that lemma), which preserves the Hilbert norms and the maximum norm $\max_b|B_b|$ ($|\cdot|$ is $\operatorname{Ad}$-invariant); so the bounds in (i)--(iii) may be proved in the gauge $u$ and transferred to $U$. Part (iv) distinguishes open path transports in that gauge from the relative means and the gauge functional. The set of phase configurations with $\|\phi\|_\infty\leq\tau_L/2$ on $\mathcal E_c$ is compact, so a common complex radius $2\varrho_*$ of analyticity and bounds $H_*,K_*,C_{\mathsf C}$ exist (the two bounds on $\mathsf C_U$ are two operator norms of the same linear map, each bounded on the compact set; $C_{\mathsf C}$ is the larger, increased if necessary to $\max(1,(4L^3(L-1))^{1/2})$, which only weakens the inequalities); $\mathcal N_{U,c}(0)=0$ because the zero fluctuation reproduces the background averages, and $D\mathcal N_{U,c}(0)=0$ as in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:row-locality}.

(ii) Fix $A$ in the complex ball with $\|A\|\leq\vartheta\leq\vartheta_*$ and consider $\Phi(d)=\mathcal N_{U,c}(A-h_Ud)$ on the closed complex ball $\|d\|\leq2K_*\vartheta^2$. Then $\|A-h_Ud\|\leq\vartheta+2H_*K_*\vartheta^2\leq2\vartheta\leq\varrho_*$; Taylor's formula with $\mathcal N(0)=0$, $D\mathcal N(0)=0$ gives $\|\Phi(d)\|\leq\frac12K_*(2\vartheta)^2=2K_*\vartheta^2$, and $\|D\Phi(d)\|\leq\|D\mathcal N(A-h_Ud)\|H_*\leq K_*\cdot2\vartheta H_*<\frac12$. So $\Phi$ is a contraction of the closed ball into itself, with a unique fixed point; taking $\vartheta=\vartheta_*$ gives the uniqueness in the ball $\|d\|\leq\rho_{\mathrm d}$ for every $A$ with $\|A\|<\vartheta_*$, and taking $\vartheta=\|A\|$ gives the first bound of \eqref{eq:convex-uniform-implicit-chart}; the fixed point $\varphi_c(A)$ is analytic in $A$ on the open ball as the uniform limit of the analytic iterates $\Phi^n(0)$ (each iterate is analytic in $A$, and the convergence is geometric, uniformly in $A$), and it is real for real $A$ because the iterates are. Differentiating the fixed-point equation, $D_A\widetilde D=(\id+D\mathcal N(x)h_U)^{-1}D\mathcal N(x)$ with $x=A-h_U\widetilde D$, $\|D\mathcal N(x)\|\leq K_*\|x\|\leq2K_*\|A\|$ and $\|(\id+D\mathcal N(x)h_U)^{-1}\|\leq2$; this is the second bound, and $D\varphi_c(0)=0$. The germ of Lemma~\ref{lem:row-locality} is the solution near zero, and uniqueness in the ball identifies the two.

(iii) Cauchy's estimate on the ball of radius $\vartheta_*$, where $\|\widetilde D_{U,c}\|\leq2K_*\vartheta_*^2$, bounds the $k$-th derivative at a point of the closed ball of radius $\vartheta_*/2$ in a fixed direction $v$, $\|v\|\leq1$, by $k!\,2K_*\vartheta_*^2(\vartheta_*/2)^{-k}$ for every $k$, and the polarisation identity for symmetric $k$-linear maps bounds the operator norm of $D^k\widetilde D_{U,c}$ by $e^k$ times the diagonal bound; this gives $C^{\mathsf D}_k$ for $0\leq k\leq6$. The constants $C^{\mathrm a}_k$ bounding the other functions are chosen separately after (iv). The phase $q_U(A)|_{\mathcal E_c}=A-h_U\widetilde D_U(A)$ is a composition of $\widetilde D_{U,c}$ with the fixed linear map $h_U(c)$ of norm at most $H_*$, so it is analytic on the ball of radius $\vartheta_*$ and its derivatives are bounded by the chain rule. The Haar density: $\sigma(q)\,\dd q$ is the pullback of Haar measure under $q\mapsto e^{iq}$ on the chart ball $h(q)<\varrho_G$, so $\sigma(q)=\sigma(0)|\det D(q\mapsto e^{iq})(q)|$, the determinant of the differential taken with respect to left-invariant frames; the determinant is analytic in $q$ and nonvanishing on the chart ball (the chart has invertible differential, conventions of Section~\ref{sec:sourced-effective-interaction}), so it has constant sign on the connected ball, positive since it equals one at $q=0$; thus $\sigma$ is analytic and positive there, and even, $\sigma(-q)=\sigma(q)$, because Haar measure is invariant under $g\mapsto g^{-1}$ and $e^{-iq}=(e^{iq})^{-1}$. Hence $\log(\sigma/\sigma(0))$, the real logarithm, extends analytically to a complex neighbourhood of the real chart ball, vanishes at $0$ with vanishing first derivative (evenness), and its composition with $q_U(A)(b)$ (a phase with $h(q_U(A)(b))\leq\|A\|+H_*2K_*\|A\|^2\leq\vartheta_*$ on the ball) is analytic on a complex neighbourhood of the closed ball $\|A\|\leq\vartheta_*/2$ after reducing $\vartheta_*$ so that the compositions stay in the domain of analyticity, with derivatives bounded on the closed ball by the chain rule, and its first derivative at $A=0$ is $D\log\sigma(0)\circ Dq_U(0)=0$. The determinant argument $\id-h_U(c)\partial_{A(b_0(c))}\widetilde D_{U,c}(A)$ equals $\id$ at $A=0$ since $D\widetilde D(0)=0$, and is within $H_*C_{\mathsf D}\vartheta_*/2<1/2$ of $\id$ in operator norm on the ball; for real $A$ it is a real endomorphism of $\mathfrak g$ within $1/2$ of $\id$, whose eigenvalues have positive real part, so its determinant is positive, and on a complex neighbourhood of the closed ball $\|A\|\leq\vartheta_*/2$ the determinant stays in the disc of radius $1/2$ about $1$ (after reducing $\vartheta_*$ once more if needed, by continuity and compactness), where $\log$ is analytic; so the real logarithm of the determinant extends analytically to that neighbourhood and vanishes at $A=0$, where the determinant is one. Its derivatives of order $k\leq5$ are bounded on the closed ball by the chain rule, since the argument contains $\partial_{A(b_0(c))}\widetilde D_{U,c}$ and so involves the derivatives of $\widetilde D_{U,c}$ up to order six, bounded above. Its first derivative at $A=0$, $-\operatorname{tr}_{\mathfrak g}\bigl(h_U(c)\partial_{A(b_0(c))}D\widetilde D_{U,c}(0)[\cdot]\bigr)$, involves the second derivative $D^2\widetilde D_{U,c}(0)=D^2\mathcal N_{U,c}(0)$, which does not vanish in general (RG1 p.267: the expansion of $\widetilde D$ begins with quadratic terms), and nothing is claimed about it. The bounds are uniform in the background by the compactness of the set of phase configurations. All reductions of $\vartheta_*$ are by constants depending on $L$ and the group only, over the finitely many row types.

(iv) Set $q^u=R_uq$; the fibre action is orthogonal on real phases and unitary on their complexifications, so it preserves the phase balls and operator norms of multilinear derivatives. In the gauge $u$ the links of $\mathcal B_y$ are $e^{i\phi}$ with $\|\phi\|_\infty\leq\tau_L/2$, and $h(q_b)\leq\|q|_{\mathcal E_y}\|\leq\vartheta_*\leq\tau_L/2$ for every $b\in\mathcal E_y$. A path of $\mathcal G(y,x)$ has at most $2(L-1)$ bonds (four coordinate runs of length at most $(L-1)/2$), and for unitaries $\|U_1\cdots U_n-\id\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\sum_j\|U_j-\id\|_{\mathrm{op}}$, so every path transport $g_\gamma=(e^{iq^u}U^u)(\gamma)$ lies within $2(L-1)c(\tau_L/2+\vartheta_*)\leq2(L-1)c\,\tau_L\leq r_G$ of $\id$ in operator norm, by \eqref{eq:c2-tau-final}; hence $A_\gamma=\log_Gg_\gamma$ is defined with $h(A_\gamma)\leq c_G\cdot2(L-1)c\,\tau_L\leq r_M/2$, again by \eqref{eq:c2-tau-final}. The local group mean of a family $(g_\gamma)_{\gamma\in\mathcal G(y,x)}$ is the solution $\mathcal U$ near the diagonal value of $\sum_\gamma\log_G(g_\gamma\mathcal U^{-1})=0$; write $g_\gamma=e^{iA_\gamma}$, $\mathcal U=e^{iX}$ and $F(X,(A_\gamma))=\sum_\gamma\log_G(e^{iA_\gamma}e^{-iX})$, an analytic map on a neighbourhood of $(0,0)$ in $\mathfrak g\times\mathfrak g^{\mathcal G(y,x)}$ with $F(0,0)=0$ and $D_XF(0,0)=-n\,\id$, $n=|\mathcal G(y,x)|$ (as $D\log_G(\id)=\id$). Choose the product neighbourhood in the complexified Lie-algebra variables so that every $e^{iA_\gamma}e^{-iX}$ lies in the analytic logarithm domain there. The analytic implicit function theorem on that neighbourhood gives radii $r_M,r'_M>0$, with $r'_M\leq\varrho_G$ (any smaller solution radius is admissible together with a correspondingly smaller $r_M$), and an analytic map $X=X((A_\gamma))$ on $\max_\gamma h(A_\gamma)<r_M$, with $h(X)<r'_M$, such that $X((A_\gamma))$ is the unique solution of $F=0$ with $h(X)<r'_M$; there are finitely many values of $n$ (one per $x\in\mathcal B_y$ modulo translations), so $r_M$, $r'_M$ and the derivative bounds of $X$ are constants of the group and $L$. This solution is identified with the fixed mean used in Stage 1 as follows. RG1 (0.8) places that specified analytic mean near the identity for input families near the identity, and (0.10) says it solves this equation. Reduce $r_M$, before the final choice of $\tau_L$, so that all families $\{e^{iA_\gamma}\}$ with $\max_\gamma h(A_\gamma)<r_M$ lie in the small-diameter domain of the specified mean, that this mean has logarithm of norm below $r'_M$, and that all relative logarithms use the common chart. Use the operator-norm diameter $\max_{\gamma,\gamma'}\|g_\gamma-g_{\gamma'}\|_{\mathrm{op}}$; it is unchanged by the two-sided multiplication $g_\gamma\mapsto u(y)g_\gamma u(x)^{-1}$. Thus the original-gauge families lie in the same small-diameter domain. Their relative products with the mean transform by conjugation at $y$, which preserves the $\operatorname{Ad}$-invariant logarithm chart, so the original-gauge relative logarithms are defined as well. Local uniqueness from the implicit function theorem then identifies it with $e^{iX}$. Its left-right covariance is the stated property RG1 (0.6), not a consequence of uniqueness on an unspecified chart domain. Thus for any admissible gauge $u$ its value in the original gauge is $u(y)^{-1}\mathcal U_{U^u}(y,x)u(x)$; the value is independent of the choice of admissible gauge because both expressions are values of the same specified mean. Since $h(A_\gamma)\leq r_M/2<r_M$ for the transport families above, the mean $\mathcal U=e^{iX((A_\gamma))}$ exists, lies in the chart domain, and is the unique solution near the diagonal value in the sense of \eqref{eq:action-primitive}. Write $A_\gamma(q^u)=\log_G((e^{iq^u}U^u)(\gamma))$ and $X(q^u)=X((A_\gamma(q^u))_\gamma)$. Define $M_x(q)=\mathcal U_{e^{iq}U}(y,x)\mathcal U_U(y,x)^{-1}$ and $M_x^u(q^u)=\mathcal U_{e^{iq^u}U^u}(y,x)\mathcal U_{U^u}(y,x)^{-1}$. Gauge covariance gives $M_x^u(q^u)=u(y)M_x(q)u(y)^{-1}=e^{iX(q^u)}e^{-iX(0)}$, with inverse $M_x^u(q^u)^{-1}=e^{iX(0)}e^{-iX(q^u)}$; both are compositions of the analytic map $X$ with $\log_G$, the exponentials of the phases and the fixed links, so they extend analytically to a complex ball $\|q|_{\mathcal E_y}\|<2\vartheta_*$ (after reducing $\vartheta_*$ by a constant of the group and $L$). For real $q$, $M_x(q)$ is unitary and $\|M_x(q)-\id\|_{\mathrm{op}}=\|e^{iX(q^u)}-e^{iX(0)}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq C\|q|_{\mathcal E_y}\|$ with a constant $C$ of $L$ and the group (the derivatives of $X$ on the ball $\max_\gamma h(A_\gamma)\leq r_M/2$, of $\log_G$ on the $r_G$-ball and of the products of exponentials are bounded), so after reducing $\vartheta_*$ once more $M_x(q)$ lies within $r_G$ of $\id$ for $\|q|_{\mathcal E_y}\|\leq\vartheta_*$. The gauge term is $\mathscr G_{y,U}(q)=\sum_x(1-\operatorname{Re}\tr_NM_x(q))$; for real $q$, $\operatorname{Re}\tr_NM_x(q)=\frac12(\tr_NM_x(q)+\tr_NM_x(q)^{-1})$ because $M_x(q)$ is unitary, and the right side is analytic in $q$ on the complex ball: this is the analytic continuation meant. The derivatives up to order five of $M_x$ and $\mathscr G_{y,U}$ are bounded on the closed ball of radius $\vartheta_*$ by constants $C^{\mathrm a}_k$ uniform in the background, because they are analytic jointly in $q$ and in the phases $\phi$ of the links of $\mathcal B_y$ in the gauge $u$, and the set $\|\phi\|_\infty\leq\tau_L/2$ is compact. $\mathscr G_{y,U}(0)=0$ because $M_x(0)=\id$, and $D\mathscr G_{y,U}(0)=0$ because each summand is nonnegative on real $q$ and vanishes at $q=0$. The gauge $u$ multiplies every transport from $y$ to $x$ on the left by $u(y)$ and on the right by $u(x)^{-1}$, and the mean equation is covariant under this ($\mathcal U\mapsto u(y)\mathcal Uu(x)^{-1}$, so $M_x\mapsto u(y)M_xu(y)^{-1}$, which preserves $\|M_x-\id\|_{\mathrm{op}}$ and $\tr_NM_x$), so the relative-mean and gauge-functional bounds hold for $U$ itself. The near-identity path and unnormalised-mean assertions remain in the gauge $u$. For each $0\leq k\leq5$, choose $C^{\mathrm a}_k$ to be at least one and the maximum of $C^{\mathsf D}_k$ and the uniform derivative bounds for the phase, the two logarithms, the relative means and the gauge functional obtained in (iii)--(iv). Their finiteness and uniformity follow from the compact phase sets, the finitely many templates and the stated analytic neighbourhoods. No explicit value of $C^{\mathsf D}_k$ is asserted for these other functions.
\end{proof}

Let $c$ be a row within $2R_{\mathrm c}+2L$ of $I$ (a near row); by Section~\ref{sec:source-phase-primitives} every row of a near primitive (a primitive whose free-input set meets $I$) is within $2R_{\mathrm c}$ of $I$, since its bonds are within $4L+3\leq2R_{\mathrm c}$ of a free-input bond in $I$, and every row of a plaquette with a bond in $\mathcal E_c$ for such a row $c$ is within $2R_{\mathrm c}+2L$ of $I$ (its bonds are within $2L-1+1$ of a bond of $\mathcal E_c$). The two-block set $\mathcal E_c$ lies inside a lifted rectangle of side $2L+1\leq s_{\mathfrak P}$ all of whose bonds are within $2R_{\mathrm c}+2L+2L+1\leq3R_{\mathrm c}$ of $I$ (as $R_{\mathrm c}\geq2R_{\mathrm{loc}}+1=4L+5$); Lemmas~\ref{lem:c2-plaquette-deviation} and~\ref{lem:c2-local-gauge} give a gauge on it with phases at most $\widehat\mu<\tau_L/2$ (the bound of Lemma~\ref{lem:c2-local-gauge} is monotone in the side), and Lemma~\ref{lem:collar-coverage}(iii) (range $3R_{\mathrm c}$) bounds the retained inputs $A_{\mathrm e}(b)$, $b\in\mathcal E_c\cap J$, by $r_{\mathrm e,\xi}$. Likewise a block $\mathcal B_y$ with centre $y$ within $2R_{\mathrm c}+2L$ of $I$ is a lifted rectangle of side $L\leq s_{\mathfrak P}$ whose bonds are within $3R_{\mathrm c}$ of $I$, so Lemmas~\ref{lem:c2-plaquette-deviation} and~\ref{lem:c2-local-gauge} apply to it directly. For real $B\in\mathcal U_y$, the block input satisfies $\|g_0B|_{\mathcal E_y}\|\leq2C_{\mathsf C}g_0(1+2r)\leq\vartheta_*/4$ by threshold item 3 and $C_{\mathsf C}\geq|\mathcal E_y|^{1/2}$. Hence the real relative-mean conclusions of (iv) apply on the caller's entire block polydisc; its analytic continuation is supplied by the same lemma. So Lemma~\ref{lem:c2-uniform-chart} applies to every row and every centre within $2R_{\mathrm c}+2L$ of $I$, in particular to every row of every near primitive and of every plaquette with a bond in the two-block set of such a row. Put $s_\xi=1+r+r_{\mathrm e,\xi}\leq1+2r$.

\begin{lemma}[primitive bounds]\label{lem:c2-primitive-bounds}
Let $g_0$ satisfy threshold item 3 of Section~\ref{app:thresholds}, $4C_{\mathsf C}g_0(1+2r)\leq\vartheta_*/2$ (hence $4C_{\mathsf C}g_0s_\xi\leq\vartheta_*/2$, and $g_0s_\xi\leq\vartheta_*/(8C_{\mathsf C})\leq1$, since $C_{\mathsf C}\geq1$ and $\vartheta_*\leq\tau_L/2\leq1/2$ by \eqref{eq:c2-tau-final}). Then on the enlarged box $\{|a_b|\leq2r\ \forall b\in I\}$ and for $t\in[0,1]$, every primitive $p_\alpha$ with free-input set $S_\alpha$ meeting $I$ is, at the input $a+tA_{\mathrm e}$, the restriction to real arguments of a function analytic in $(a,t)$ on a complex neighbourhood of the enlarged box times $[0,1]$, and with a constant $C_{\mathrm p}$ depending on $L$ and the group only,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c2-primitive-sizes}
    |p_{\xi,\alpha}(a+tA_{\mathrm e})|\leq C_{\mathrm p}g_0s_\xi^3,\qquad
    |p^{\mathrm H}_{\xi,b}(a+tA_{\mathrm e})|\leq C_{\mathrm p}g_0^2s_\xi^2,\qquad
    |p^{\mathrm J}_{\xi,c}(a+tA_{\mathrm e})|\leq C_{\mathrm p}g_0s_\xi\;,
\end{equation}
and for each of the three kinds, in the Hilbert norms on $\mathfrak g^{S_\alpha}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:convex-primitive-derivatives}
    \|D_a^2p_\alpha\|\leq C_{\mathrm p}g_0s_\xi,\qquad
    \|D_ap_\alpha\|\leq C_{\mathrm p}g_0s_\xi^2,\qquad
    |p_\alpha(a+tA_{\mathrm e})-p_\alpha(tA_{\mathrm e})|\leq C_{\mathrm p}g_0s_\xi^3\;,
\end{equation}
and $\|D_a^3p_\alpha\|\leq C_{\mathrm p}g_0$, $\|D_a^4p_\alpha\|\leq C_{\mathrm p}g_0^2$. Moreover, for every row $c$ within $2R_{\mathrm c}+2L$ of $I$ and every real phase $A$ with $\|A|_{\mathcal E_c}\|\leq\vartheta_*/2$, the row equation at $A$ has exactly one small solution $\widetilde D_{U,c}(A)=\varphi_c(A|_{\mathcal E_c})$ ($h(d_c)<\rho_{\mathrm d}=2K_*\vartheta_*^2$), with $\varphi_c$ analytic on the complex ball of radius $\vartheta_*$, real on real phases, $\varphi_c(0)=0$, $D\varphi_c(0)=0$; $g_0\mathsf C_U\mathcal U_c$ lies in that phase ball; the determinant $\det_{\mathfrak g}(\id-h_U(c)\partial_{A(b_0(c))}\widetilde D_{U,c}(A))$ is positive there; and the row solution, the phase $q_U|_{\mathcal E_c}$, the normalised Haar logarithm and the real determinant logarithm are analytic in $B|_{\mathcal E_c\cap E_*}$ on $\mathcal U_c$, as are, when $c$ is within $2R_{\mathrm c}$ of $I$, the plaquette terms $s_{p,U}(q_U(\cdot))$ of the plaquettes $p$ with a bond in $\mathcal E_c$ (on the intersection of the polydiscs of the rows of $p$, all of which are rows within $2R_{\mathrm c}+2L$ of $I$); for every centre $y$ within $2R_{\mathrm c}+2L$ of $I$ and every $B\in\mathcal U_y$ the relative means exist, lie within $r_G$ of $\id$ in operator norm, and $\mathscr G_{y,U}(g_0B|_{\mathcal E_y})$ is analytic in $B|_{\mathcal E_y}$; all are compositions, with the linear map $B\mapsto g_0\mathsf C_UB$ (of norm at most $C_{\mathsf C}$ for the Hilbert norms, by Lemma~\ref{lem:c2-uniform-chart}(i)), of functions of the phase analytic on a complex neighbourhood of the closed ball $\|A\|\leq\vartheta_*/2$ (on the complex ball of radius $\vartheta_*$ for the row solution and the phase) with derivatives up to order five bounded on that closed ball by constants depending on $L$ and the group only, so their derivatives in $B$ up to order five are bounded on the polydiscs by such constants. Where $(A_{\mathrm e},a)\in\mathcal D_\xi$ these objects are CRE's, by (B4)(i) and the uniqueness of the small solution: for every row within $2R_{\mathrm c}+2L$ of $I$ the phase $g_0\mathsf C_U\Psi_\xi(a+A_{\mathrm e})$ lies in the ball $\|A|_{\mathcal E_c}\|\leq\vartheta_*/4$, because $a$ is in the open box and $\Psi_\xi(a+A_{\mathrm e})=a+A_{\mathrm e}$ with $|A_{\mathrm e}(b)|<r$ on the two-block set (Lemma~\ref{lem:collar-coverage}(i),(iii),(iv)), so $\Psi_\xi(a+A_{\mathrm e})\in\mathcal U_c$ and the bound on $g_0\mathsf C_U\mathcal U_c$ applies. This is Lemma~\ref{lem:charts}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\emph{The input of a primitive.} On the enlarged box, $|a_b+tA_{\mathrm e}(b)|\leq2r+r_{\mathrm e,\xi}\leq2s_\xi$ for every bond $b$ within $3R_{\mathrm c}$ of $I$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:collar-coverage}(iii) bounds the retained inputs by $r_{\mathrm e,\xi}$), in particular for the free bonds of the two-block sets of the near rows and for the free bonds of the plaquettes and blocks of the near primitives. The rows of a near primitive are within $2R_{\mathrm c}$ of $I$ (applicability paragraph). The \emph{input} $\mathfrak u$ of a primitive is the vector $g_0\mathsf C_UB$ restricted to the following bond set $S$: for a plaquette primitive $E_{\xi,p}$, $p_{\xi,p}$ the union of the sets $\mathcal E_c$ over the rows $c$ of $p$ (at most four) together with the free bonds of $\partial p$ not in those sets; for the Haar primitive $p^{\mathrm H}_{\xi,b}$ the set $\mathcal E_c$ when $b=b_0(c)$ and $\{b\}$ when $b$ is free; for the Jacobian primitive $p^{\mathrm J}_{\xi,c}$ the set $\mathcal E_c$; for a gauge primitive $E_{\xi,y}$, $p_{\xi,y}$ the set $\mathcal E_y$ (on which $\mathsf C_U$ is the identity, all its bonds being free). The primitive is a function of the phase $q_U(B)$ on its bonds, and that phase is a function of the input: on $\mathcal E_c$ it is $\mathfrak u|_{\mathcal E_c}-h_U\widetilde D_{U,c}(\mathfrak u|_{\mathcal E_c})$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:c2-uniform-chart}(ii)), and on a free bond outside the row sets it is $\mathfrak u_b=g_0B(b)$. With $B=a+tA_{\mathrm e}$, by Lemma~\ref{lem:c2-uniform-chart}(i) each row component satisfies $\|\mathfrak u|_{\mathcal E_c}\|\leq C_{\mathsf C}g_0\cdot2s_\xi\leq\vartheta_*/4$ and each free-bond component outside the row sets $h(\mathfrak u_b)\leq|\mathfrak u_b|\leq2g_0s_\xi\leq\vartheta_*/4$ (threshold item 3 and $C_{\mathsf C}\geq1$), so for the plaquette, Haar and Jacobian primitives (at most four rows and four further free bonds)
\begin{equation*}
    \|\mathfrak u\|\leq16C_{\mathsf C}g_0s_\xi,\qquad\|D_a\mathfrak u\|\leq5C_{\mathsf C}g_0,\qquad\|\partial_t\mathfrak u\|\leq16C_{\mathsf C}g_0s_\xi\;,
\end{equation*}
the second in the operator norm from the Hilbert norm of $\mathfrak g^{S_\alpha}$ (the Hilbert bound of Lemma~\ref{lem:c2-uniform-chart}(i) on each of at most four rows, plus the identity on the free bonds); for the gauge primitive, whose input $g_0B|_{\mathcal E_y}$ has $|\mathcal E_y|=4L^3(L-1)$ free bonds, $\|\mathfrak u\|\leq g_0|\mathcal E_y|^{1/2}\cdot2s_\xi\leq2C_{\mathsf C}g_0s_\xi\leq\vartheta_*/4$ (as $C_{\mathsf C}\geq(4L^3(L-1))^{1/2}$ and $h\leq|\cdot|$), $\|D_a\mathfrak u\|\leq g_0$ and $\|\partial_t\mathfrak u\|\leq2C_{\mathsf C}g_0s_\xi$, so the three displayed bounds hold for all four kinds. The input is linear in $B$, hence affine in $(a,t)$ and linear in $a$ at fixed $t$. Each primitive is $\varphi(\mathfrak u)$ for a function $\varphi$ of the input analytic on a complex neighbourhood of the closed set $\mathcal P=\{\mathfrak u\in\mathfrak g^S_{\mathbb C}:\|\mathfrak u|_{\mathcal E_c}\|\leq\vartheta_*/2\text{ for each row }c\text{ of the primitive},\ h(\mathfrak u_b)\leq\vartheta_*/2\text{ for each free bond }b\text{ of }S\text{ outside the row sets}\}$ (for the gauge primitive, the closed ball $\|\mathfrak u\|\leq\vartheta_*$ of $\mathfrak g^{\mathcal E_y}_{\mathbb C}$), with derivatives up to order five bounded on $\mathcal P$ by constants of $L$ and the group: Lemma~\ref{lem:c2-uniform-chart}(iii) for the row solution, the phase and the two logarithms, each a function of one row component (the Haar logarithm at a free bond outside the row sets is the function of (iii) at a free bond of a two-block set, where $q_U(A)(b)=A(b)$, a function of the single component $\mathfrak u_b$); for the plaquette term, with $W(q)=(e^{iq}U)(\partial p)$, $s_{p,U}(q)=1-\operatorname{Re}\tr_NW(q)$ equals $1-\frac12(\tr_NW(q)+\tr_NW(q)^{-1})$ for real $q$, $W(q)$ being unitary, and $W(q)$ and $W(q)^{-1}$ are ordered products of the fixed links and of $e^{\pm iq(b)}$, entire functions of the phases $q(b)$, which are analytic functions of one row component (with $h(q_U(A)(b))\leq\|A\|+H_*2K_*\|A\|^2\leq\vartheta_*$ on the ball) or are free-bond components of $\mathfrak u$; this is the analytic function meant by $s_{p,U}(q_U(\cdot))$, with derivatives bounded on $\mathcal P$ by the chain rule; for the gauge term, Lemma~\ref{lem:c2-uniform-chart}(iv). The segment from $0$ to $\mathfrak u$ lies in $\mathcal P$, each component of $\mathfrak u$ being within $\vartheta_*/4$ of zero. Hence every primitive at the input $a+tA_{\mathrm e}$ is the restriction to real $(a,t)$ of a function analytic in $(a,t)$ on a complex neighbourhood of the enlarged box times $[0,1]$ (the composition of $\varphi$ with the affine map), and the bounds below are uniform there.

\emph{Energy residual.} $E_{\xi,\alpha}(a+tA_{\mathrm e})=g_0^{-2}\varphi(\mathfrak u)$ with $\varphi(\mathfrak u)=-[s_{\alpha,U}(q_U(\mathfrak u))-s_{\alpha,U}(0)]$ for a plaquette and $\varphi(\mathfrak u)=-\mathscr G_{y,U}(\mathfrak u)$ for a centre. Since $\mathfrak u$ is linear in $B$, the Taylor polynomial of degree two in $B$ at $B=0$ subtracted in \eqref{eq:native-source-energy-residual} is that of $\varphi$ at $0$ composed with the linear map, so $p_{\xi,\alpha}=g_0^{-2}[\varphi(\mathfrak u)-D\varphi(0)\mathfrak u-\frac12D^2\varphi(0)[\mathfrak u,\mathfrak u]]$, the second-order Taylor residual at the input, bounded by $g_0^{-2}\frac16\|D^3\varphi\|\|\mathfrak u\|^3\leq Cg_0s_\xi^3$ (the segment from $0$ to $\mathfrak u$ lies in $\mathcal P$). Its first derivative in $a$ is $g_0^{-2}(D\varphi(\mathfrak u)-D\varphi(0)-D^2\varphi(0)\mathfrak u)D_a\mathfrak u$, bounded by $g_0^{-2}\frac12\|D^3\varphi\|\|\mathfrak u\|^2\cdot5C_{\mathsf C}g_0\leq Cg_0s_\xi^2$; its second derivative is $g_0^{-2}(D^2\varphi(\mathfrak u)-D^2\varphi(0))[D_a\mathfrak u,D_a\mathfrak u]$, bounded by $g_0^{-2}\|D^3\varphi\|\|\mathfrak u\|(5C_{\mathsf C}g_0)^2\leq Cg_0s_\xi$; its third and fourth derivatives are $g_0^{-2}D^3\varphi(\mathfrak u)[D_a\mathfrak u]^3$ and $g_0^{-2}D^4\varphi(\mathfrak u)[D_a\mathfrak u]^4$, bounded by $Cg_0$ and $Cg_0^2$. The Taylor polynomial subtracted is that of the germ (Section~\ref{sec:source-phase-primitives}), which is the Taylor polynomial of $\varphi$ at $0$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:c2-uniform-chart}(ii).

\emph{Haar logarithm.} $\psi(\mathfrak u)=\log\sigma(q_U(\mathfrak u)(b))-\log\sigma(0)$, the primitive \eqref{eq:native-source-haar-primitive}, has $\psi(0)=0$ and $D\psi(0)=0$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:c2-uniform-chart}(iii): $\sigma$ is even and $Dq_U(0)$ is linear). Hence $|\psi(\mathfrak u)|\leq\frac12\|D^2\psi\|\|\mathfrak u\|^2\leq Cg_0^2s_\xi^2$, $\|D_a\psi\|\leq\|D^2\psi\|\|\mathfrak u\|\cdot5C_{\mathsf C}g_0\leq Cg_0^2s_\xi\leq Cg_0s_\xi^2$, $\|D_a^2\psi\|\leq\|D^2\psi\|(5C_{\mathsf C}g_0)^2\leq Cg_0s_\xi$, and the third and fourth derivatives are bounded by $Cg_0^3\leq Cg_0$ and $Cg_0^4\leq Cg_0^2$.

\emph{Constraint logarithm.} $\lambda(\mathfrak u)=\log|\det_{\mathfrak g}(\id-h_U(c)\partial_{A(b_0(c))}\widetilde D_{U,c}(\mathfrak u))|=\log\det_{\mathfrak g}(\cdots)$ (the determinant is positive, Lemma~\ref{lem:c2-uniform-chart}(iii)) has $\lambda(0)=0$ and derivatives up to order five bounded on $\mathcal P$; its first derivative at $0$ need not vanish. Hence $|\lambda(\mathfrak u)|\leq\|D\lambda\|\|\mathfrak u\|\leq Cg_0s_\xi$, $\|D_a\lambda\|\leq\|D\lambda\|\cdot5C_{\mathsf C}g_0\leq Cg_0\leq Cg_0s_\xi^2$, $\|D_a^2\lambda\|\leq\|D^2\lambda\|(5C_{\mathsf C}g_0)^2\leq Cg_0^2\leq Cg_0s_\xi$ (as $g_0\leq1$ and $s_\xi\geq1$), and the third and fourth derivatives are bounded by $Cg_0^3\leq Cg_0$ and $Cg_0^4\leq Cg_0^2$. Let $C'_{\mathrm p}$ be the maximum over the finitely many templates of the constants $C$ above and put $C_{\mathrm p}=2C'_{\mathrm p}$. Then \eqref{eq:c2-primitive-sizes}, the first two bounds of \eqref{eq:convex-primitive-derivatives} and the third- and fourth-order bounds hold with room to spare, and the last bound of \eqref{eq:convex-primitive-derivatives} is the difference of two values each bounded by $C'_{\mathrm p}g_0s_\xi^3$ (for all three kinds, since $s_\xi\geq1$ and $g_0\leq1$), hence by $2C'_{\mathrm p}g_0s_\xi^3=C_{\mathrm p}g_0s_\xi^3$.

\emph{The chart statements.} Let $c$ be a row within $2R_{\mathrm c}+2L$ of $I$; by the applicability paragraph Lemma~\ref{lem:c2-uniform-chart} applies to it, and (ii)--(iii) give, for every real $A$ with $\|A|_{\mathcal E_c}\|\leq\vartheta_*/2$, the unique small solution $\varphi_c(A|_{\mathcal E_c})$ with $\varphi_c$ analytic on the complex ball of radius $\vartheta_*$ and real on real phases, $\varphi_c(0)=0$, $D\varphi_c(0)=0$, the positivity of the determinant, and the analyticity of the row solution, the phase and the two logarithms on a complex neighbourhood of the closed ball of radius $\vartheta_*/2$, with derivatives up to order five bounded on that ball. For $B\in\mathcal U_c$, $\max_{b\in\mathcal E_c\cap E_*}|B(b)|<2+4r=2(1+2r)$ and $\|g_0\mathsf C_UB\|_{\mathcal E_c}\leq2C_{\mathsf C}g_0(1+2r)\leq\vartheta_*/4$ by threshold item 3, so $g_0\mathsf C_U\mathcal U_c$ lies in the phase ball; $B\mapsto g_0\mathsf C_UB$ is linear with norm at most $g_0C_{\mathsf C}\leq C_{\mathsf C}$ for the Hilbert norms (Lemma~\ref{lem:c2-uniform-chart}(i)), so the derivatives in $B$ are bounded by the same constants times $C_{\mathsf C}^{\,k}$. For $c$ within $2R_{\mathrm c}$ of $I$, the plaquette term of a plaquette $p$ with a bond in $\mathcal E_c$ is analytic in $B$ on the intersection of the polydiscs of the rows of $p$, as above, and these rows are within $2R_{\mathrm c}+2L$ of $I$ (applicability paragraph), so the chart applies to each of them. For a centre $y$ within $2R_{\mathrm c}+2L$ of $I$, Lemma~\ref{lem:c2-local-gauge} applied to the block $\mathcal B_y$ (applicability paragraph) gives a gauge with phases at most $\widehat\mu<\tau_L/2$ on $\mathcal E_y$; every bond of $\mathcal E_y$ is free, so $q_U(B)=g_0B$ there and $\|g_0B|_{\mathcal E_y}\|\leq g_0|\mathcal E_y|^{1/2}\max_b|B(b)|\leq g_0(4L^3(L-1))^{1/2}\cdot2(1+2r)\leq2C_{\mathsf C}g_0(1+2r)\leq\vartheta_*/4$ under threshold item 3 (using $h\leq|\cdot|$, $|\mathcal E_y|=4L^3(L-1)$ and $C_{\mathsf C}\geq(4L^3(L-1))^{1/2}$); Lemma~\ref{lem:c2-uniform-chart}(iv) then gives the existence of the relative means within $r_G$ of $\id$ and the analyticity and derivative bounds of $\mathscr G_{y,U}(g_0B|_{\mathcal E_y})$ on $\mathcal U_y$. The values at $q=0$ of the plaquette and gauge terms are subtracted in the primitives. Finally, where $(A_{\mathrm e},a)\in\mathcal D_\xi$ and $c$ is within $2R_{\mathrm c}+2L$ of $I$, the phase $A=g_0\mathsf C_U\Psi_\xi(a+A_{\mathrm e})$ has $\|A|_{\mathcal E_c}\|\leq\vartheta_*/4$ (statement of the lemma), CRE's row solution $\widetilde D_c(A)$ is a small solution by (B4)(i), hence equals $\varphi_c(A|_{\mathcal E_c})$ by the uniqueness in (ii), and the phase, the Haar density, the Jacobian and the plaquette and gauge terms of CRE (1.14)--(1.15) are the same functions of it.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[host multiplicity and potential size]\label{lem:c2-potential-size}
Let $\mathfrak T$ be the set of primitive templates (kind, label and free-input shape, including the shapes truncated by $\Omega_1$) modulo $L\mathbb Z^4$, anchor each template at one of its input bonds, and let $D_{\mathrm h}$ be one plus the maximum $\ell^\infty$ diameter of a lifted host \eqref{eq:part1-potential-host} and $B_{\mathrm h}$ the maximum number of $M$-cubes in one, both over $\mathfrak T$. With
\begin{equation}\label{eq:part1-host-multiplicity}
    N_{\mathrm h}=|\mathfrak T|(2\lceil D_{\mathrm h}\rceil+3)^4,\qquad
    C_0=N_{\mathrm h}C_{\mathrm p}e^{\kappa(B_{\mathrm h}+4)},\qquad
    \omega(g_0)=C_0g_0(1+r)^3\;,
\end{equation}
at most $N_{\mathrm h}$ primitives are assigned to any host $Y$, $d_0(Y)\leq B_{\mathrm h}+4$, and on the enlarged box, for every host,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:native-source-interaction-size}
    \sup|\mathbf V_\xi(Y,a)|\leq\omega(g_0)e^{-\kappa d_0(Y)}\;.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix a vertex of $Y$. Every primitive assigned to $Y$ has its stencil, hence its anchor, in the lifted host, within $\ell^\infty$ distance $D_{\mathrm h}$ of that vertex; there are at most $(2\lceil D_{\mathrm h}\rceil+1)^4$ such anchor positions, and a template with an anchor position determines the primitive. Projection to the torus only identifies positions. By Lemma~\ref{lem:c2-domain-geometry}(i), $d_0(Y)\leq N_0(Y)-1\leq B_{\mathrm h}$. At $t=0$, $s_\xi$ may be replaced by $1+r$ in Lemma~\ref{lem:c2-primitive-bounds}, since no retained input enters; each of the three kinds is then bounded by $C_{\mathrm p}g_0(1+r)^3$, and $|\mathbf V_\xi(Y,a)|\leq N_{\mathrm h}C_{\mathrm p}g_0(1+r)^3\leq N_{\mathrm h}C_{\mathrm p}g_0(1+r)^3e^{\kappa(B_{\mathrm h}+4)}e^{-\kappa d_0(Y)}$.
\end{proof}

The definition gives $C_0\geq C_{\mathrm p}$, since $N_{\mathrm h}\geq1$ and the exponential factor is at least one. The constant $C_0$ depends on $L$, the group, $M$ and $\kappa$ (through $B_{\mathrm h}$, $D_{\mathrm h}$), not on the volume, the branch or $g_0$. The hypothesis of Lemma~\ref{lem:ibp} holds for $f=f_\xi$ on the enlarged box, which is a neighbourhood of the closed cutoff box: the exponent $\mathsf Q_{\xi,t}$ is a quadratic polynomial plus $P_{\xi,t}$, a finite sum of near primitives, each analytic there by Lemma~\ref{lem:c2-primitive-bounds}; and $f_\xi(a)=W_\chi(C;U_\xi(a))$ is a character of a product of the reconstructed links \eqref{eq:c-reconstructed-field} over the contour bonds, each of which depends on $a$ through the rows of a free-input set meeting $I$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:collar}(i) puts $\Psi_\xi(a+A_{\mathrm e})=a+A_{\mathrm e}$ on those rows, so the phase is \eqref{eq:native-collar-source-phase}), where $q_U$ is analytic in $a$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:c2-uniform-chart}(ii)--(iii); contour bonds whose rows do not meet $I$ do not depend on $a$. Both factors are therefore analytic on the enlarged box, uniformly in $t\in[0,1]$.

\subsection{Proof of the Gaussian payment}
\label{app:fixed-kernel-payment}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:gaussian-payment}]
\emph{Tail of the walk series.} Throughout, $E_0$, $C_{E_0}$, $\Gamma_{E_0}(\sigma)$ are as in \eqref{eq:fixed-kernel-C-Gamma} and the sentence preceding it (their block decomposition is \eqref{eq:fixed-kernel-exact-blocks}), and $S_{Z_0}(1)=\mathsf G^{1/2}$. Let $\mathsf N$ be the set of bonds $b'\in I$ with $d_{\infty,T}(b'_-,Z_0)\leq R+1$. Since $\iota_{E_0}^*H$ has nonzero entries $H(b,b')$ only for $d(b_-,b'_-)\leq R$ and $b\in E_0$ meets $Z_0$, $\Gamma_{E_0}(\sigma)-\Gamma_{E_0}(1)=\iota_{E_0}^*H\Pi_{I\setminus E_0}\Pi_{\mathsf N}(S_{Z_0}(\sigma)-\mathsf G^{1/2})$. A walk $\omega=(r;b_0,\ldots,b_{m+1})$ with $b_0\in\mathsf N$ has $|w_\omega(\sigma)-1|\ne0$ only if $D(\omega)$ meets a cube $\Delta\not\subseteq Z_0'$. The open $M$-neighbourhood of $Z_0$ is $\bigcup_{Q\subseteq Z_0}\operatorname{int}Q^\sim$, and by definition every $LM$-cube meeting it lies in $Z_0'$ (Section~\ref{sec:fixed-first-kernel}). Hence $D(\omega)$ contains a point at distance at least $M$ from $Z_0$, and at least $M-R-1$ from $b_{0,-}$. Each block of $\mathsf A_H^r$ ($r<n_*$) or $\mathsf P_H$ vanishes beyond distance $R_*$, and $D_b$ lies within $\rho_{\mathrm w}$ of $b_-$, so $D(\omega)$ lies within $(m+1)R_*+\rho_{\mathrm w}$ of $b_{0,-}$. By \eqref{eq:fixed-kernel-collar-size}, $M/4\geq\rho_{\mathrm w}+R+R_*+2$, so $(m+1)R_*\geq M-R-1-\rho_{\mathrm w}\geq\frac34M+R_*+1$ and $m>M/(2R_*)\geq N_M$. The absolute block row sum of the terms with $m\geq N_M$ is, by the bounds in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:walk-series} and $|w_\omega-1|\leq2a_*^{m+1}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed-kernel-rooted-tail}
    s_H^{-1/2}\sum_{r<n_*}\sum_{m\geq N_M}a_{n_*m+r}\,2a_*^{m+1}V_*\beta^m
    \leq2s_H^{-1/2}\frac{n_*V_*a_*}{1-a_*\beta}4^{-N_M}=\delta_M\;,
\end{equation}
using $a_*\beta\leq1/4$. The absolute block column sums of these terms obey the same bound, because every walk in the tail has $m\geq N_M$ whatever its first bond. Schur's test gives $\|\Pi_{\mathsf N}(S_{Z_0}(\sigma)-\mathsf G^{1/2})\|\leq\delta_M$. With $\|H\|\leq h_+$ this is the first bound of (i); with $\|C_{E_0}\|\leq\gamma^{-1}$, since $\iota_{E_0}^*H\iota_{E_0}\geq\gamma$, the second follows.

\emph{Reserve identity.} Write $\Gamma=\Gamma_{E_0}(\sigma)=\Gamma_1+i\Gamma_2$ with real $\Gamma_1,\Gamma_2$; $\Gamma_{E_0}(1)$ is real, so $\Gamma_2=\operatorname{Im}(\Gamma_{E_0}(\sigma)-\Gamma_{E_0}(1))$. The quadratic form in \eqref{eq:fixed-kernel-density} is bilinear, so for real $X$, $\operatorname{Re}\langle\Gamma X,C_{E_0}\Gamma X\rangle=\langle\Gamma_1X,C_{E_0}\Gamma_1X\rangle-\langle\Gamma_2X,C_{E_0}\Gamma_2X\rangle$ and $|e^{-\langle B,\Gamma X\rangle}|=e^{-\langle B,\Gamma_1X\rangle}$ for real $B$. For $0\leq\alpha_G<\gamma$ put $C_{E_0,\alpha_G}=(\iota_{E_0}^*H\iota_{E_0}-\alpha_G)^{-1}$. The Gaussian integral over $B$ with density $(2\pi)^{-d_G|E_0|/2}\det(C_{E_0})^{-1/2}e^{-\frac12\langle B,(C_{E_0}^{-1}-\alpha_G)B\rangle-\langle B,\Gamma_1X\rangle}$ equals $\det_{E_0}(\id-\alpha_GC_{E_0})^{-1/2}e^{\frac12\langle\Gamma_1X,C_{E_0,\alpha_G}\Gamma_1X\rangle}$. Collecting the $X$-exponent,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed-kernel-actual-reserve-form}
    \int\Bigl|e^{-\frac12\langle\Gamma X,C_{E_0}\Gamma X\rangle}e^{-\langle B,\Gamma X\rangle}\Bigr|e^{\frac{\alpha_G}{2}\|B\|^2}\dd\mu_{C_{E_0}}(B)
    =\det_{E_0}(\id-\alpha_GC_{E_0})^{-1/2}e^{\frac12\langle X,T_{\alpha_G}X\rangle},\quad
    T_{\alpha_G}=\Gamma_1^{\mathsf T}(C_{E_0,\alpha_G}-C_{E_0})\Gamma_1+\Gamma_2^{\mathsf T}C_{E_0}\Gamma_2\;,
\end{equation}
a real symmetric positive semidefinite matrix on $\mathfrak g^I$ ($C_{E_0,\alpha_G}\geq C_{E_0}$ for $\alpha_G\geq0$). By Lemma~\ref{lem:component-blocks} the columns of $\Gamma$ outside $J_Z$ vanish, so $T_{\alpha_G}$ is supported on $J_Z$, and $\int e^{\frac12\langle X,T_{\alpha_G}X\rangle}\dd\mu_0(X)=\det_{J_Z}(\id-T_{\alpha_G})^{-1/2}$ when $\|T_{\alpha_G}\|<1$. Integrating \eqref{eq:fixed-kernel-actual-reserve-form} against $\mu_0$ therefore gives the integral of (ii) in closed form, $\det_{E_0}(\id-\alpha_GC_{E_0})^{-1/2}\det_{J_Z}(\id-T_{\alpha_G})^{-1/2}$, once $\|T_{\alpha_G}\|<1$ is shown.

\emph{Norm of $T_{\alpha_G}$.} The resolvent identity gives $C_{E_0,\alpha_G}-C_{E_0}=\alpha_GC_{E_0,\alpha_G}C_{E_0}$, of norm at most $\alpha_G/(\gamma(\gamma-\alpha_G))$. By (i) and $\delta_M\leq1$, $\|\Gamma_1\|\leq\|\Gamma_{E_0}(1)\|+h_+\delta_M\leq h_+\gamma^{-1/2}+h_+=A_*$, since $\|\Gamma_{E_0}(1)\|\leq\|H\|\|\mathsf G^{1/2}\|\leq h_+\gamma^{-1/2}$ ($\mathsf G=H^{-1}$ and $H\geq\gamma$); and $\|\Gamma_2\|\leq h_+\delta_M$. Hence, for $\alpha_G\leq\min(\gamma/2,\gamma^2/(16A_*^2))$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed-kernel-reserve-payment}
    \|T_{\alpha_G}\|\leq\frac{\alpha_GA_*^2}{\gamma(\gamma-\alpha_G)}+\frac{h_+^2\delta_M^2}{\gamma}\leq\frac{2\alpha_GA_*^2}{\gamma^2}+\frac18\leq\frac14\;.
\end{equation}

\emph{Determinant bounds.} The eigenvalues of $\alpha_GC_{E_0}$ lie in $[0,1/2]$ and $-\frac12\log(1-t)\leq t$ for $t\leq1/2$, so $\log\det_{E_0}(\id-\alpha_GC_{E_0})^{-1/2}\leq\alpha_G\operatorname{tr}C_{E_0}\leq(\alpha_G/\gamma)d_G|E_0|$. The eigenvalues of $T_{\alpha_G}$ lie in $[0,1/4]$ and $-\frac12\log(1-t)\leq\frac23t$ there, so $\log\det_{J_Z}(\id-T_{\alpha_G})^{-1/2}\leq\frac23\operatorname{tr}T_{\alpha_G}\leq\frac23d_G|J_Z|\|T_{\alpha_G}\|$. With $E_0\subseteq J_Z$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:component-blocks}),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed-kernel-trace-payment}
    \log\bigl[\det_{E_0}(\id-\alpha_GC_{E_0})^{-1/2}\det_{J_Z}(\id-T_{\alpha_G})^{-1/2}\bigr]
    \leq d_G|J_Z|\Bigl(\frac{\alpha_G}{\gamma}+\frac{4\alpha_GA_*^2}{3\gamma^2}+\frac{2h_+^2\delta_M^2}{3\gamma}\Bigr)
    \leq C_{\mathrm d}(\alpha_G+\delta_M^2)|J_Z|,\qquad
    C_{\mathrm d}=d_G\max\Bigl(\frac1\gamma+\frac{4A_*^2}{3\gamma^2},\frac{2h_+^2}{3\gamma}\Bigr)\;.
\end{equation}
A bond of $J_Z$ has its initial site in a cube of $Z$, so $|J_Z|\leq4(LM+1)^4N_1(Z)$. Since $n_*,R_*,C_{\mathrm w}$ are fixed before $M$ and $\delta_M=2C_{\mathrm w}4^{-\lfloor M/(2R_*)\rfloor}$ decays exponentially in $M$, there is $M$ with $4C_{\mathrm d}\delta_M^2(LM+1)^4\leq1/2$; then $\alpha_G\leq(8C_{\mathrm d}(LM+1)^4)^{-1}$ gives the bound $e^{N_1(Z)}$. Both $C_{E_0}$ and $\Gamma_{E_0}$ are block diagonal over the components $D$ of $Z$ by \eqref{eq:fixed-kernel-exact-blocks}, hence so are $T_{\alpha_G}$ and both determinants, which proves the factorisation.

\emph{Bad-bond reserve.} On $\{|B_b|\geq r\}$, $h(B_b)^2\geq c_{\mathrm n}|B_b|^2\geq c_{\mathrm n}r^2$, so $\mathbf 1_{\{|B_b|\geq r\}}\leq e^{\frac{\gamma_2}{2}(h(B_b)^2-c_{\mathrm n}r^2)}$; the product over $\mathsf P$ is (iii).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[cover sum]\label{lem:c2-cover-sum}
For every nonempty union $Y_0$ of $M$-cubes,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:native-source-cover-sum}
    \sum_{\mathcal D\subseteq\mathbf D_0\, | \,\bigcup_{Y\in\mathcal D}Y=Y_0}\ \prod_{Y\in\mathcal D}\alpha_6e^{-\delta\kappa\ell_0(Y)}\leq1\;.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The members of $\mathbf D_0$ are localisation domains of $\pi_0$ and $\mathcal D$ is a set of distinct domains. For a cover, $\sum_{Y\in\mathcal D}\ell_0(Y)\geq\sum_YN_0(Y)/C_{\mathrm g}\geq N_0(Y_0)/C_{\mathrm g}$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:c2-domain-geometry}(i), so $\prod_Ye^{-\delta\kappa\ell_0(Y)/2}\leq e^{-\delta\kappa N_0(Y_0)/(2C_{\mathrm g})}$. Dropping the cover constraint, the remaining sum over sets of domains contained in $Y_0$ is at most $\prod_{Y\subseteq Y_0}(1+\alpha_6e^{-\delta\kappa\ell_0(Y)/2})\leq\exp\bigl(\alpha_6\sum_{Q\subseteq Y_0}\sum_{Y\ni Q}e^{-\delta\kappa\ell_0(Y)/2}\bigr)\leq\exp(\alpha_6B(\delta\kappa/2)N_0(Y_0))$ by \eqref{eq:native-source-animal-sum}. The product of the two bounds is at most one by the choice of $\alpha_6$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[selected majorant]\label{lem:c2-selected-majorant}
On every branch, with $\mathbf W=\mathbf V_\xi$, $\omega(g_0)\leq\omega_*$ and the chart condition, every selected integral of Definition~\ref{def:selection} with $F=1$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed-kernel-selected-majorant}
    |\mathcal A^{\mathrm f}_{(\mathcal D,\mathsf P,Z)}[1]|
    \leq e^{-(\kappa_1-1)N_1(Z\setminus Z_0')+N_1(Z)-a_{\mathrm{bad}}|\mathsf P|}\prod_{Y\in\mathcal D}\omega_*e^{1/2}e^{-\kappa d_0(Y)}\;.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The weakening in \eqref{eq:c-selected-integral} is, for each cube $\Delta$ of $Z\setminus Z_0'$, the operation $G\mapsto\int_0^1\dd s\oint_{|\sigma_\Delta|=e^{\kappa_1}}\frac{G\,\dd\sigma_\Delta}{2\pi i(\sigma_\Delta-s)^2}$ applied to the inner integral (the $t_Y$-, $X$- and $B$-integrals) as a function of $\sigma_\Delta$ on the circle; it needs only the measurability of the inner integral in $\sigma$ on the distinguished boundary (the integrand is continuous in $\sigma$, since $\Gamma_{E_0}(\sigma)$ is the walk series of Lemma~\ref{lem:walk-series}, and Fubini applies to the iterated integral because the integral of the modulus of the inner integrand is bounded uniformly on the distinguished boundary, by the bounds below) and the bound $\sup_{s\in[0,1],|\sigma|=e^{\kappa_1}}|\sigma-s|^{-2}\cdot e^{\kappa_1}\leq e^{\kappa_1}(e^{\kappa_1}-1)^{-2}\leq e^{-(\kappa_1-1)}$, the last step because $e^{\kappa_1}\geq3$. Applying it for each cube of $Z\setminus Z_0'$ bounds \eqref{eq:c-selected-integral} by $e^{-(\kappa_1-1)N_1(Z\setminus Z_0')}$ times the supremum over the distinguished boundary $\{|\sigma_\Delta|=e^{\kappa_1}\ \forall\Delta\in\pi_1(Z)\setminus\pi_1(Z_0')\}$ (with $\sigma_\Delta=0$ off $Z$, $\sigma_\Delta=1$ on $Z_0'$) of the integral of the inner integrand's modulus, which lies in the closed polydisc of Lemma~\ref{lem:gaussian-payment}. There, the Mayer integrals $\int_0^1\dd t_Y$ have mass one; $\chi_b\leq1$; by Lemma~\ref{lem:gaussian-payment}(iii) with $\gamma_2\leq\alpha_G$, $\prod_{b\in\mathsf P}(1-\chi_b)\leq e^{-a_{\mathrm{bad}}|\mathsf P|}e^{\frac{\alpha_G}{2}\|B\|^2}$; on the box $|\mathbf V_\xi(Y,B)e^{t_Y\mathbf V_\xi(Y,B)}|\leq\omega(g_0)e^{-\kappa d_0(Y)}e^{\omega(g_0)}\leq\omega_*e^{1/2}e^{-\kappa d_0(Y)}$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:c2-potential-size} and $\omega_*\leq1/2$ (which follows from $\eta_*\leq1/32$); and the integral of the absolute density against $e^{\alpha_G\|B\|^2/2}$ is at most $e^{N_1(Z)}$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:gaussian-payment}(ii).
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:activity-bounds}]
Fix a connected final domain $Z$. Each selection $(\mathcal D,\mathsf P)$ in \eqref{eq:c-vacuum-activity} determines $Y_0=\bigcup\mathcal D$, the seed $Z_0$ with wall components $Z_{0,1},\ldots,Z_{0,k'}$, the components $Y_i$ of $Y_0$ (each inside one $Z_{0,j}$), $n_j=N_0(Z_{0,j}\setminus Y_0)$ and $n=\sum_jn_j$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:c2-selected-majorant} and $\omega_*e^{1/2}e^{-\kappa d_0(Y)}\leq\eta_*e^{-\kappa\ell_0(Y)}$, the sum over selections is organised as $\sum_{Y'\subseteq Z}\sum_{Z_0\, | \,Z_0'=Y'}\sum_{Y_0\subseteq Z_0}\sum_{\mathcal D\, | \,\bigcup\mathcal D=Y_0}\sum_{\mathsf P}$ over unions of cubes $Y'$ of $\pi_1$ and $Y_0$ of $\pi_0$, and each inner sum is bounded in turn.

\emph{Potential labels.} Split $\eta_*e^{-\kappa\ell_0(Y)}=t_0e^{-(1-\delta)\kappa\ell_0(Y)}\cdot\alpha_6e^{-\delta\kappa\ell_0(Y)}$. Each $Y\in\mathcal D$ lies in one component $Y_i$, and $\sum_{Y\subseteq Y_i}\ell_0(Y)\geq\ell_0(Y_i)$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:c2-domain-geometry}(ii), so with $t_0\leq1$ and at least one label per component, $\prod_{Y\in\mathcal D}t_0e^{-(1-\delta)\kappa\ell_0(Y)}\leq\prod_it_0e^{-(1-\delta)\kappa\ell_0(Y_i)}$. Lemma~\ref{lem:c2-cover-sum} sums the second factors to at most one.

\emph{Bad-bond labels.} Given $Y_0$ and $Z_0$, every $b\in\mathsf P$ has $b\cap Y_0=\varnothing$ and both endpoints in cubes of $Z_0$, hence in $Z_0\setminus Y_0$; there are at most $4M^4n$ such bonds. Every cube of $Z_0\setminus Y_0$ meets a bond of $\mathsf P$ and a bond meets at most two cubes, so $|\mathsf P|\geq n/2$. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:native-source-bad-label-sum}
    \sum_{\mathsf P}e^{-a_{\mathrm{bad}}|\mathsf P|/2}\leq e^{-a_{\mathrm{bad}}n/8}\sum_{\mathsf P}e^{-a_{\mathrm{bad}}|\mathsf P|/4}\leq\exp\Bigl[-\frac{a_{\mathrm{bad}}}{8}n+4M^4e^{-a_{\mathrm{bad}}/4}n\Bigr]\leq e^{-a_{\mathrm{bad}}n/16}\;,
\end{equation}
since $4M^4e^{-a_{\mathrm{bad}}/4}\leq a_{\mathrm{bad}}/16$: by \eqref{eq:native-source-bad-budget}, $a_{\mathrm{bad}}\geq160(\kappa+1)>1$ and $64M^4e^{-a_{\mathrm{bad}}/4}\leq1$ (as $a_{\mathrm{bad}}/4\geq40\log(8M^4+8)\geq\log(64M^4)$). The unused half $e^{-a_{\mathrm{bad}}|\mathsf P|/2}\leq e^{-a_{\mathrm{bad}}n/4}$ leaves in total $e^{-5a_{\mathrm{bad}}n/16}=\prod_je^{-5a_{\mathrm{bad}}n_j/16}$, of which $e^{-a_{\mathrm{bad}}n_j/32}$, $e^{-a_{\mathrm{bad}}n_j/64}$ and $e^{-a_{\mathrm{bad}}n_j/64}$ are used below.

\emph{Component reserve.} For a component $Z_{0,j}$ containing $k_j\geq1$ components $Y_i$ of $Y_0$, Lemma~\ref{lem:c2-domain-geometry}(iii) gives $\ell_0(Z_{0,j})\leq\sum_{i}\ell_0(Y_i)+5n_j$, so with $a_{\mathrm{bad}}/32\geq5\kappa$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:native-source-component-reserve}
    t_0^{k_j}e^{-(1-\delta)\kappa\sum_i\ell_0(Y_i)}e^{-a_{\mathrm{bad}}n_j/32}\leq t_0e^{-(1-\delta)\kappa\ell_0(Z_{0,j})}\;.
\end{equation}
If $k_j=0$ then $n_j\geq1$, $\ell_0(Z_{0,j})\leq5n_j$, and $e^{-a_{\mathrm{bad}}n_j/32}e^{-a_{\mathrm{bad}}n_j/64}\leq e^{-5\kappa n_j}\eta_*\leq t_0e^{-(1-\delta)\kappa\ell_0(Z_{0,j})}$, using $e^{-a_{\mathrm{bad}}/64}\leq\eta_*\leq t_0$.

\emph{Choice of $Y_0$.} With the reserve $e^{-a_{\mathrm{bad}}n/64}$, $\sum_{Y_0\subseteq Z_0}e^{-a_{\mathrm{bad}}N_0(Z_0\setminus Y_0)/64}=(1+e^{-a_{\mathrm{bad}}/64})^{N_0(Z_0)}\leq\exp(e^{-a_{\mathrm{bad}}/64}C_{\mathrm g}\sum_j\ell_0(Z_{0,j}))\leq\prod_je^{\delta\kappa\ell_0(Z_{0,j})}$, since $C_{\mathrm g}e^{-a_{\mathrm{bad}}/64}\leq162e^{-5(\kappa+1)/2}\leq\kappa/40$ for $\kappa\geq3$. Altogether, writing $\mathfrak M(Z_0,Z)$ for the sum of \eqref{eq:fixed-kernel-selected-majorant} over the selections with seed $Z_0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:native-source-initial-domain-sum}
    \mathfrak M(Z_0,Z)\leq e^{-(\kappa_1-1)N_1(Z\setminus Z_0')+N_1(Z)}\prod_{j}t_0e^{-(1-2\delta)\kappa\ell_0(Z_{0,j})}\;.
\end{equation}

\emph{Scale change.} By Lemma~\ref{lem:c2-scale}, the enlargement $Z_0'$ of Section~\ref{sec:fixed-first-kernel} (the $LM$-cubes meeting $\operatorname{int}Q^\sim$ for some cube $Q$ of $Z_0$) equals $\bigcup_jZ_{0,j}'$, where $Z_{0,j}'$ is the enlargement of the component $Z_{0,j}$ in the sense of that lemma, so \eqref{eq:c2-scale-inequality} applies to each $Z_{0,j}$. Each $Z_{0,j}'$ is connected, so every component $Y'_i$ of $Y'=Z_0'$ is the union of the $Z_{0,j}'$ it contains. Split $e^{-(1-2\delta)\kappa\ell_0(Z_{0,j})}=e^{-\delta\kappa\ell_0(Z_{0,j})}e^{-(1-3\delta)\kappa\ell_0(Z_{0,j})}$ and apply \eqref{eq:c2-scale-inequality} in the form $\ell_0(Z_{0,j})\geq\frac{L}{2049}\ell_1(Z_{0,j}')-\frac{5L+1024}{2049}$, with $(5L+1024)/2049\leq L/2$ for $L\geq3$:
\begin{equation*}
    \prod_{j\, | \,Z_{0,j}'\subseteq Y'_i}e^{-(1-3\delta)\kappa\ell_0(Z_{0,j})}\leq e^{L\kappa/2\cdot k'_i}\prod_je^{-(1-3\delta)\frac{L\kappa}{2049}\ell_1(Z_{0,j}')}\leq e^{L\kappa k'_i/2}e^{-(1-3\delta)\frac{L\kappa}{2049}\ell_1(Y'_i)}\;,
\end{equation*}
by Lemma~\ref{lem:c2-domain-geometry}(ii), where $k'_i$ counts the fine components in $Y'_i$. The remaining factors $t_0e^{L\kappa/2}e^{-\delta\kappa\ell_0(Z_{0,j})}$ pay for the sum over the set of fine components inside $Y'_i$: each contains an $M$-cube of $Y'_i$, of which there are $L^4N_1(Y'_i)$, so by \eqref{eq:native-source-animal-sum} the sum over single components is $x_i\leq L^4N_1(Y'_i)t_0e^{L\kappa/2}B(\delta\kappa)\leq\varepsilon_*\frac{\delta L\kappa}{2049}\ell_1(Y'_i)$, using $N_1\leq C_{\mathrm g}\ell_1$ and the definitions of $C_{\mathrm s}$ and $\varepsilon_*=C_{\mathrm s}t_0$, and the sum over nonempty sets is at most $e^{x_i}-1\leq x_ie^{x_i}$. Write $\Lambda_i=\frac{L\kappa}{2049}\ell_1(Y'_i)$ and $y=\delta\Lambda_i$, so $x_i\leq\varepsilon_*y\leq y$. Then $x_ie^{x_i}e^{-(1-3\delta)\Lambda_i}\leq\varepsilon_*ye^{y}e^{-(1-3\delta)\Lambda_i}=\varepsilon_*ye^{-(1-4\delta)\Lambda_i}=\varepsilon_*(ye^{-y})e^{-(1-5\delta)\Lambda_i}\leq\varepsilon_*e^{-\lambda'\ell_1(Y'_i)}$, using $ye^{-y}\leq1$, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:native-source-enlarged-domain-sum}
    \sum_{Z_0\, | \,Z_0'=Y'}\mathfrak M(Z_0,Z)\leq e^{-(\kappa_1-1)N_1(Z\setminus Y')+N_1(Z)}\prod_{Y'_i\in\operatorname{cc}(Y')}\varepsilon_*e^{-\lambda'\ell_1(Y'_i)}\;.
\end{equation}

\emph{Choice of $Y'$.} For nonempty $Y'\subseteq Z$ with $n'=N_1(Z\setminus Y')$ cubes outside, Lemma~\ref{lem:c2-domain-geometry}(iii) gives $\ell_1(Z)\leq\sum_i\ell_1(Y'_i)+5n'$, so $\prod_ie^{-\lambda'\ell_1(Y'_i)}\leq e^{-\lambda'\ell_1(Z)}e^{5\lambda'n'}$, and $\varepsilon_*^{\#\operatorname{cc}(Y')}\leq\varepsilon_*$. With $\kappa_1-1\geq5\lambda'+1$,
\begin{equation*}
    |k^{\mathrm f}_\xi(Z)|\leq\varepsilon_*e^{N_1(Z)}e^{-\lambda'\ell_1(Z)}\sum_{\varnothing\ne Y'\subseteq Z}e^{-N_1(Z\setminus Y')}
    \leq\varepsilon_*e^{(1+e^{-1})N_1(Z)}e^{-\lambda'\ell_1(Z)}\leq\varepsilon_*e^{-(\lambda'-2C_{\mathrm g})\ell_1(Z)}\;,
\end{equation*}
which is the first inequality of \eqref{eq:fixed-kernel-raw-activity-payment}.

\emph{Criterion.} Let $Z$ be any union of $LM$-cubes, connected or not (the lemma states the case of a connected final domain; the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:vacuum-clusters} uses the general case). A domain $Z'$ incompatible with $Z$ contains one of the at most $9N_1(Z)$ cubes of $Z$ or wall adjacent to $Z$. Since $(1+6\theta)N_1(Z')\leq(1+6\theta)C_{\mathrm g}\ell_1(Z')\leq\frac{\lambda_*}{2}\ell_1(Z')$ by $C_{\mathrm g}\leq\lambda_*/8$ and $6\theta C_{\mathrm g}=3\lambda_*/8$, \eqref{eq:native-source-animal-sum} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:native-source-weighted-vacuum-criterion}
    \sum_{Z'\not\sim Z}|k^{\mathrm f}_\xi(Z')|e^{(1+6\theta)N_1(Z')}\leq9N_1(Z)\varepsilon_*B(\lambda_*/2)\leq\frac18N_1(Z)\;.
\end{equation}
The reference data are treated in Section~\ref{app:reference-check}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Proof of the vacuum-cluster bounds}
\label{app:kp}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:vacuum-clusters}]
Species are the connected final domains $D$, with weights $\widetilde z_D=|k^{\mathrm f}_\xi(D)|e^{2\theta N_1(D)}$ and sizes $a(D)=N_1(D)/8$; two species are incompatible when they share a cube or a wall, and each with itself. By \eqref{eq:native-source-weighted-vacuum-criterion} and $2\theta+\frac18\leq1+6\theta$,
\begin{equation*}
    \sum_{D'\not\sim D}\widetilde z_{D'}e^{a(D')}\leq\sum_{D'\not\sim D}|k^{\mathrm f}_\xi(D')|e^{(1+6\theta)N_1(D')}\leq a(D)\;.
\end{equation*}
Tuples of species are written $\mathfrak c=(D_1,\ldots,D_m)$ in this proof ($\gamma$ is the spectral bound $\gamma_L/2$). The argument of Section~\ref{sec:marginal-perimeter} following \eqref{eq:marginal-pinned-bound} applies verbatim to these species, weights and sizes: the absolute Ursell coefficient $|\varphi^T(\mathfrak c)|$ is bounded by the number of spanning trees of the incompatibility graph of $\mathfrak c$, the rooted-tree generating sum with root $D$ is the monotone limit of the recursion \eqref{eq:marginal-pinned-recursion} with $\widetilde z$ in place of $|\widetilde z_p|$, the displayed criterion gives $T^{(k)}_D\leq\widetilde z_De^{a(D)}$ inductively, and the factor $1/m!$ in \eqref{eq:part1-vacuum-cluster} together with the multiplicity $m_D(\mathfrak c)$ of $D$ in the tuple reproduces the rooted sum. Hence, for every connected final domain $D$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c2-pinned-vacuum}
    \sum_{\mathfrak c}m_D(\mathfrak c)|\Phi^{\mathrm v}_\xi(\mathfrak c)|e^{2\theta\sum_jN_1(D_j)}\leq|k^{\mathrm f}_\xi(D)|e^{2\theta N_1(D)+N_1(D)/8}\;.
\end{equation}
Here $\mathcal A$ is any finite set of unions of $LM$-cubes, connected or not; the criterion \eqref{eq:native-source-weighted-vacuum-criterion} was proved for every such union. A tuple hitting $\mathcal A$ contains a member $D'$ incompatible with some $D\in\mathcal A$, so
\begin{equation*}
    \sum_{\mathfrak c\text{ hits }\mathcal A}|\Phi^{\mathrm v}_\xi(\mathfrak c)|e^{2\theta\sum_jN_1(D_j)}
    \leq\sum_{D\in\mathcal A}\sum_{D'\not\sim D}\sum_{\mathfrak c}m_{D'}(\mathfrak c)|\Phi^{\mathrm v}_\xi(\mathfrak c)|e^{2\theta\sum_jN_1(D_j)}
    \leq\sum_{D\in\mathcal A}\sum_{D'\not\sim D}|k^{\mathrm f}_\xi(D')|e^{(2\theta+\frac18)N_1(D')}\leq\frac18\sum_{D\in\mathcal A}N_1(D)\;.
\end{equation*}
For \eqref{eq:part1-joint-vacuum-exponential}, at fixed volume there are finitely many species. Let $\mathcal S_+$ be the set of species with $k^{\mathrm f}_\xi(D)\ne0$; species with $k^{\mathrm f}_\xi(D)=0$ contribute to neither side (a tuple containing one has $\Phi^{\mathrm v}_\xi=0$, and the gas does not see them), so they are discarded. For complex activities $z=(z_D)_{D\in\mathcal S_+}$, the hard-core gas $\Xi(z)=\sum_{\mathcal P\text{ compatible}}\prod_{D\in\mathcal P}z_D$ is a polynomial with $\Xi(0)=1$: the polymer partition function (5.3) of \cite[Chapter~5]{FriedliVelenik} for the polymers $\mathcal S_+$ with the hard-core interaction $\zeta(D,D')=0$ on incompatible and $\zeta(D,D')=1$ on compatible pairs (which satisfies (5.1)--(5.2) there). Let $S(z)=\sum_{\mathfrak c}\frac{\varphi^T(\mathfrak c)}{m!}\prod_jz_{D_j}$, the sum over all ordered tuples $\mathfrak c=(D_1,\ldots,D_m)$, $m\geq1$, of members of $\mathcal S_+$; this is the cluster series of that gas, since the Ursell function of \cite[(5.6)]{FriedliVelenik} is $\frac1{m!}\sum_G\prod_{\{i,j\}\in G}(\zeta(D_i,D_j)-1)=\frac{\varphi^T(\mathfrak c)}{m!}$, the sum over the connected graphs $G$ on $\{1,\ldots,m\}$, a factor $\zeta-1$ being $-1$ on an incompatible pair and $0$ otherwise. \cite[Proposition~5.3]{FriedliVelenik} is the identity $\Xi(z)=\exp(S(z))$ between formal power series in the variables $z_D$ (the book states it as a formal identity; its proof, (5.4)--(5.8) there, rearranges finitely many terms for each monomial). By \eqref{eq:c2-pinned-vacuum} with $z_D$ in place of $k^{\mathrm f}_\xi(D)$ (the bound is monotone in the weights and only uses $|z_D|\leq|k^{\mathrm f}_\xi(D)|$ through the criterion; equivalently \cite[Theorem~5.4]{FriedliVelenik}, whose hypothesis (5.10) is the first display of this proof and whose conclusion (5.9) is this absolute convergence), $S$ converges absolutely at $|z_D|=|k^{\mathrm f}_\xi(D)|$, hence absolutely and uniformly on the closed polydisc $\overline P=\{|z_D|\leq|k^{\mathrm f}_\xi(D)|\ \forall D\in\mathcal S_+\}$ (Weierstrass M-test), so $S$ is continuous on $\overline P$, analytic on the open polydisc $P$, and its Taylor series at $0$ is the formal series $S$ grouped by monomials. The analytic functions $\Xi$ and $\exp\circ S$ on the connected open set $P\ni0$ have the same Taylor series at $0$, by the formal identity, so they coincide on $P$ (identity theorem) and, by continuity, on $\overline P$; in particular at $z_D=k^{\mathrm f}_\xi(D)$, where $\Xi(k^{\mathrm f}_\xi)=\Xi^{\mathrm f}_\xi$ and $S(k^{\mathrm f}_\xi)=\sum_{\mathfrak c}\Phi^{\mathrm v}_\xi(\mathfrak c)$ by \eqref{eq:part1-vacuum-cluster}. The restricted gas $(\Xi^{\mathrm f}_\xi)^{[\cup\mathcal A]}$ is the same gas with $z_D=0$ for the $D$ incompatible with $\bigcup\mathcal A$, which are exactly the $D$ incompatible with some member of $\mathcal A$; its expansion is the sum over the tuples none of whose members hits $\mathcal A$. The quotient is therefore $\exp(-\sum_{\mathfrak c\text{ hits }\mathcal A}\Phi^{\mathrm v}_\xi(\mathfrak c))$.
\end{proof}
