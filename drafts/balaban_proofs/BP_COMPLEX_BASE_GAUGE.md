# Bałaban Proof: CMP98's Gauge Transformation (106) at a Complex Base

Bałaban proof: a bound on the gauge transformation of CMP98 (106) when its base is a complex configuration e^{iηA′}U with U G-valued, as at the complex points of LFR II, where Bałaban introduces (𝐔, 𝐉) "into the localized function 𝐇_k in (1.63), hence into u_{k,Λ}" (LFR II p.374 l.38–39). CMP98 and REG take the base G-valued (REG (1.33), p.82; CMP98 p.34 l.4–9), and REG's bound (1.70) uses that the conjugations by the base's transports are unitary. Here (106) at the complex base is reduced to two gauges at the G-valued base U and a factor fixed by the normalisation. Used by the LF note (WILSON_IT1_LARGE_FIELD_DEPTH_2026_10_27.md, #4.536-2). Tags continue the LF's numbering.

- {#4.536.1} The setting. T_η is the lattice of step k, η = L^{−k}, which is CRE's ξ at step k (RG I p.257 l.3 and l.36). The domains Ω_j and Λ_j are those of REG (1.3)–(1.6) (p.77); for x ∈ B^j(y), y ∈ Λ_j, the chain x = x_0, x_1, …, x_j = y has x_n ∈ B(x_{n+1}) (REG p.79 l.4). Gauge transformations act by (V^g)_b = g(b_−)V_bg(b_+)^{−1} (REG p.78), so (V^g)^h = V^{hg}. The norm |·| on 𝔤^c is the LF's.
  {#4.536.1-1} For a configuration V, V(Γ^{(m)}_{x_m,x}) := \bar V^{m−1}(Γ_{x_m,x_{m−1}})⋯\bar V(Γ_{x_2,x_1})V(Γ_{x_1,x}) is the transport along the axial contours through the averages (CMP98 p.29 l.66). For G-valued V it is unitary.
- {#4.536.2} Three facts of the definitions, for G^c-valued g with |log g| small.
  - {#4.536.2-1} (a) (\overline{V^g}^j)_c = g(c_−)(\bar V^j)_cg(c_+)^{−1} for c ⊂ T^{(j)} (REG p.78, stated for G-valued g). The averages are built from products, principal logarithms, averages, and exponentials, and log(gXg^{−1}) = Ad(g)log X for X near 1, so the identity holds for G^c-valued g near 1.
  - {#4.536.2-2} (b) The axial gauge Ax(B) relative to a base B (REG (1.19)–(1.20), p.79) is the set of the configurations V with \bar V^n(Γ_{x_{n+1},x_n}) = \bar B^n(Γ_{x_{n+1},x_n}) for all chains: the product Π_{b⊂Γ}R(\bar B^n(Γ_{x_{n+1},b_−}))(\bar V^n_b(\bar B^n_b)^{−1}) of (1.19) telescopes to \bar V^n(Γ_{x_{n+1},x_n})\bar B^n(Γ_{x_{n+1},x_n})^{−1}.
  - {#4.536.2-3} (c) The averaged gauge transformation N_B(v) := \overline{R_Bv}^j of CMP98 (78)–(80) (p.30) is N^j, where N^0 := v and N^{n+1}(x_{n+1}) := M[w], w(x_n) := Ad(\bar B^n(Γ_{x_{n+1},x_n}))N^n(x_n) for x_n ∈ B(x_{n+1}), and M[w] := w(y)exp(Σ_{x∈B(y)}L^{−d}log(w(y)^{−1}w(x))) at y = x_{n+1}, the contour from y to y being empty.
  {#4.536.2-4} So Ax(B) and N_B depend on B only through its transports along the axial contours, B(Γ_{x_1,x}) and \bar B^n(Γ_{x_{n+1},x_n}).
- {#4.536.3} The gauge a_B(V). For a base B and a configuration V near B, a_B(V) is the gauge transformation g with V^g ∈ Ax(B) and N_B(g)(y) = 1 for y ∈ Λ_j (REG (1.29), p.81 l.14). At a G-valued base it is CMP98 (106) (REG p.88 l.36). Both conditions are identities of analytic functions of the base and of the configuration, so (106) continued to a complex base satisfies them, by the identity theorem; Bałaban's u_{k,Λ}, u_1, and u″_k at complex points are a_B at their complex bases, up to inversion.
  {#4.536.3-1} a_B(V) is unique. If V^g and V^{g′} are in Ax(B), then h := g′g^{−1} maps V^g ∈ Ax(B) into Ax(B), so by (a) and (b) h(x_{n+1})\bar B^n(Γ_{x_{n+1},x_n})h(x_n)^{−1} = \bar B^n(Γ_{x_{n+1},x_n}) along every chain, that is h(x) = Ad(B(Γ^{(j)}_{y,x})^{−1})h(y).
  {#4.536.3-2} For such h, N_B(hv) = h(y)N_B(v)(y): by induction N^n(hv)(x_n) = h(x_n)N^n(v)(x_n), since Ad(\bar B^n(Γ_{x_{n+1},x_n}))h(x_n) = h(x_{n+1}) and M[h(y)w] = h(y)M[w]. So N_B(g′) = N_B(g) = 1 forces h(y) = 1, and h = 1.
- {#4.536.4} Covariance. For G^c-valued g near 1, N_{B^g}(v) = Ad(g)N_B(Ad(g^{−1})v): in (78) at the base B^g, B^g(Γ_{y,x}) = g(y)B(Γ_{y,x})g(x)^{−1}, and Ad(g(y)) commutes with the logarithm, the average, and the exponential; the higher levels follow by (a). With V^a ∈ Ax(B), (V^g)^{gag^{−1}} = (V^a)^g ∈ Ax(B^g) by (a). Hence a_{B^g}(V^g) = g a_B(V)g^{−1}.
- {#4.536.5} The reduction. Let 𝐔 = e^{iηA′}U with U G-valued and A′ 𝔤^c-valued, W = e^{iηX}𝐔, and put ũ := a_U(𝐔) and û := a_U(W), gauges at the G-valued base U.
  {#4.536.5-1} 𝐔^ũ ∈ Ax(U), so 𝐔^ũ and U have the same transports along the axial contours, and by #4.536.2-4 Ax(𝐔^ũ) = Ax(U) and N_{𝐔^ũ} = N_U, so a_{𝐔^ũ} = a_U. By #4.536.4 with g = ũ^{−1}, a_𝐔(W) = ũ^{−1}a_U(W^ũ)ũ.
  {#4.536.5-2} W^ũ = (W^û)^{ũû^{−1}} with W^û ∈ Ax(U). As in #4.536.3-1, a gauge g with (W^ũ)^g ∈ Ax(U) is g = h′ûũ^{−1} with h′(x) = Ad(U(Γ^{(j)}_{y,x})^{−1})h′(y), and by #4.536.3-2 the normalisation gives h′(y) = N_U(ûũ^{−1})(y)^{−1}. Hence a_𝐔(W) = ũ^{−1}h′û, and |log h′(x)| = |log h′(y)|, U(Γ^{(j)}_{y,x}) being unitary.
- {#4.536.6} The gauges at U. ũ and û are (106) at the G-valued base U for the 𝔤^c-valued perturbations e^{iηA′} and WU^{−1} = e^{iηĤ}, ηĤ := (1/i)log(e^{iηX}e^{iηA′}) bondwise. Let ε_A and ε_X bound A′ and X in the form of REG (1.69) (p.88), |·| < ε(L^jη)^{−1} and |∇^η_U·| < ε(L^jη)^{−2} on Ω_j, X's derivative taken at 𝐔.
  {#4.536.6-1} Ĥ satisfies these bounds at ε_W ≤ (ε_A + ε_X)(1 + O(ε_A + ε_X)), by the Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff formula bondwise; ∇^η_𝐔 and ∇^η_U differ on X by η^{−1}(Ad(e^{iηA′}) − 1), of size O(ε_Aε_X)(L^jη)^{−2}. U satisfies CMP98 (109)'s plaquette condition, as the real part of a configuration of the norm class (CRE (2.34), p.261).
  {#4.536.6-2} The count of the LF's #4.517-1–#4.517-2 then gives the brackets of (106) the bounds |log β_n| ≤ 2t_n with t_n < 4dεL^{n+1−j}, ε := ε_A + ε_W, and |log ũ| ≤ 32dε_A, |log û| ≤ 32dε_W, for 256dε ≤ 1 and ε below REG's threshold of (1.71).
- {#4.536.7} The averages of ũ and û. By CMP98 (107) (p.33), the level-n average of a gauge u of the form (106) is N^n_U(u)(x_n) = Π_{l≥n}Ad(\bar U^n(Γ^{(l+1−n)}_{x_{l+1},x_n}))^{−1}β_l, the factor l = n on the right. Transported by \bar U^n(Γ_{x_{n+1},x_n}), it is P_n·β_n(x_{n+1}, x_n), with P_n the product of the factors l > n at x_{n+1}; at x_{n+1} the contour of the factor l = n is empty, and N^n_U(u)(x_{n+1}) = P_n·N̄_n(x_{n+1})^{−1}, N̄_n the normalisation (105), |log N̄_n| ≤ t_n.
  {#4.536.7-1} So o_n(u) := max|log(Ad(\bar U^n(Γ_{x_{n+1},x_n}))N^n_U(u)(x_n)) − log N^n_U(u)(x_{n+1})| ≤ 4t_n for u = ũ, û and ε small, and N^j_U(ũ)(y) = N^j_U(û)(y) = 1 at y ∈ Λ_j by (1.29).
- {#4.536.8} The step M. Write log w(x) = λ_y + μ_x. Every term of the Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff series of log(e^{−λ_y}e^{λ_y+μ_x}) beyond the first contains μ_x, and so does every term of log(e^{λ_y}e^Z) beyond the first, Z the average of the former; the second-order terms −½avg[λ_y, μ_x] and ½[λ_y, avg μ] cancel. So |log M[w] − avg_x log w(x)| ≤ C₀s·max_x|μ_x|, with s := max_x|log w(x)| ≤ 1/8 and C₀ an absolute constant.
- {#4.536.9} The factor h′. Let v := ûũ^{−1}, Ñ^n := N^n_U(û)N^n_U(ũ)^{−1}, r_n := log N^n_U(v) − log Ñ^n, and ρ_n := sup|r_n|; r_0 = 0. Let s bound the logarithms of N^n_U(û), N^n_U(ũ), and N^n_U(v); by #4.536.7 and #4.536.6-2, s ≤ 64dε + ρ_n.
  {#4.536.9-1} The step M at v gives log N^{n+1}_U(v) = avg Ad(\bar U^n(Γ))log N^n_U(v) + E_v, |E_v| ≤ C₀s((1 + C₀s)(o_n(û) + o_n(ũ)) + 2ρ_n), since the oscillation of Ñ^n is at most (1 + C₀s)(o_n(û) + o_n(ũ)) and that of N^n_U(v) exceeds it by at most 2ρ_n. At û and ũ it gives log N^{n+1}_U(û) = avg â + Ê and log N^{n+1}_U(ũ) = avg ã + Ẽ, â and ã the transported logarithms at level n, |Ê| ≤ C₀s·o_n(û), |Ẽ| ≤ C₀s·o_n(ũ).
  {#4.536.9-2} Write log(e^pe^{−q}) = p − q + B(p, −q). Then r_{n+1} = avg Ad(\bar U^n(Γ))r_n + E_v − Ê + Ẽ + [avg B(â, −ã) − B(avg â + Ê, −avg ã − Ẽ)]. The second-order part of B is bilinear, and avg[â, ã] − [avg â, avg ã] = avg[â − avg â, ã − avg ã]; the higher parts have second derivatives O(s); so the bracket is at most C(o_n(û)o_n(ũ) + s(o_n(û) + o_n(ũ))²) + Cs(|Ê| + |Ẽ|), the deviations from the average being at most 2o_n.
  {#4.536.9-3} With o_n ≤ 4t_n, ρ_{n+1} ≤ (1 + 2C₀s)ρ_n + C₁(s + t_n)t_n, C₁ an absolute constant. Hence ρ_j ≤ C₁Σ_{n<j}(1 + 2C₀s)^{j−1−n}(s + t_n)t_n ≤ 4C₁d(s + 4dε)εΣ_{i≥0}((1 + 2C₀s)/L)^i ≤ 8C₁d(s + 4dε)ε for L ≥ 2(1 + 2C₀s). By induction on n, ρ_n ≤ dε keeps s ≤ 65dε, and the recursion then gives ρ_{n+1} ≤ C₂d²ε² ≤ dε, C₂ absolute, for ε small.
  {#4.536.9-4} At y, Ñ^j(y) = 1, so N^j_U(v)(y) = e^{r_j(y)} and |log h′| = |r_j(y)| ≤ C₂d²ε² ≤ dε for ε small.
- {#4.536.10} The bound. a_𝐔(W) = ũ^{−1}h′û, so |log a_𝐔(W)| ≤ (33/32)(32dε_A + dε + 32dε_W) ≤ 35dε for ε small, by the Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff formula; with ε ≤ (2ε_A + ε_X)(1 + O(ε_A + ε_X)) (#4.536.6-1), |log a_𝐔(W)| ≤ 72d(ε_A + ε_X) for ε_A + ε_X small.
