# Bałaban Proof: Covering the Layers by VAR's Class Cubes

Every point of a layer of Bałaban's determining sets, except the bottom strips of the joins, lies in a cube of the class of VAR Theorem 1 with the parameter M′ or 2LM′, and in a cube of the class of VAR's proof with the parameter M′ or 2M′. The bottom strips are covered by LF #4.7-1-4–#4.7-1-4-3-2. The class, a_j, and R₁M₁ = M′ are those of LF #4.7-1 (WILSON_IT1_LARGE_FIELD_DEPTH_2026_10_27.md), and R′ and the layer widths are those of BP_JOINS_BLOCK_SIZE.md #4.22-0 and #4.24. A cited #4.x not in this file is in the LF note or in another file of this folder.

## Cubes and Distances

- {#4.7-1-1} *Placing a cube.* The big blocks of the L^{−j}-lattice have the side M₁^{VAR}a_j (VAR p.277 l.12), and M₁^{VAR} divides M′ = R₁M₁ (#4.23). So for M a multiple of M′ the cubes of side 2Ma_j on the big-block grid are unions of big blocks, and for every point x and every choice of a side in each coordinate one of them contains x in its last big block on the chosen sides; it extends from x at least (2M − M′)a_j towards the other side in each coordinate.
  {#4.7-1-1-1} Cubes are sets of lattice points, read as half-open cubes. So the concentric cube □̃ of side (2M + 4M′)a_j of a cube □ ∋ x lies within ℓ∞-distance < (2M + 2M′)a_j of x. For x in the layer j, δ(x) and δ⁺(x) are the ℓ∞ distances of x from Ω_j^c and from Ω_{j+1} in the units a_j, with δ⁺(x) := ∞ in the top layer.
  {#4.7-1-1-2} Every layer below the top one is ≥ R′M′ wide in its own units (#4.24), with R′ ≥ 12dL (#4.22-0). Where the width is measured in ℓ¹ (PRT2 p.223 l.15–16), it bounds the ℓ∞ width by its d-th part. So every layer below the top one is ≥ 12LM′ wide in ℓ∞ in its own units. L ≥ 2 throughout.

## The Class of Theorem 1

- {#4.7-1-2} *δ(x) ≥ 4M′: the index j, M = M′.* Take □ ∋ x (#4.7-1-1). □̃ lies within < 4M′ of x, so □̃ ⊂ Ω_j. Ω_{j+2} lies at ≥ 12LM′ from Ω_{j+1}^c in the units a_{j+1}, that is at ≥ 12L²M′ > 4M′ in the units a_j (Ω_{j+2} = ∅ if the layer j + 1 is the top one), and x ∉ Ω_{j+1}, so □̃ misses Ω_{j+2}. So □̃ ⊂ Ω_j ∖ Ω_{j+2}, and □̃ ⊄ B^{j+1}(Λ_{j+1}) since x ∈ □̃ ∖ Ω_{j+1}: □ is a class cube of the index j. The width of the layer j is not used, so this holds on the top layers of 𝐁₂ and of 𝐁_{1.70} (BP_LAYER_WIDTHS #4.14).
- {#4.7-1-3} *δ(x) < 4M′, j ≥ 1: the index j − 1, M = 2LM′.* Let y ∈ Ω_j^c with |x − y|_∞ = δ(x). In the units a_{j−1} = a_j/L, take the cube □ of side 4LM′ that contains x in its last big block on the sides away from y (#4.7-1-1). In each coordinate □ extends from x towards y at least (4L − 1)M′, and □̃ a further 2M′, together (4L + 1)M′ > 4LM′ in the units a_{j−1}, that is > 4M′ > δ(x) in the units a_j. So y ∈ □̃.
  {#4.7-1-3-1} □̃ lies within < (4L + 2)M′ < 12LM′ of x ∈ Ω_j in the units a_{j−1}, so □̃ ⊂ Ω_{j−1} by the width of the layer j − 1. If the layer j is not the top one, x lies at > 12LM′ − 4M′ from Ω_{j+1} in the units a_j, by its width, more than the reach (4 + 2/L)M′ of □̃ in these units, so □̃ misses Ω_{j+1}. So □̃ ⊂ Ω_{j−1} ∖ Ω_{j+1}, and □̃ ⊄ B^j(Λ_j) since y ∈ □̃ ∖ Ω_j: □ is a class cube of the index j − 1, and M = 2LM′ is a multiple of M′. If the layer j is the top one, its width is not used, so this covers the rest of the top layers of 𝐁₂ and of 𝐁_{1.70}.
  {#4.7-1-3-2} So #4.7-1-2 and #4.7-1-3 cover the layers j ≥ 1, and the points of the layer 0 with δ(x) ≥ 4M′. On 𝐁 and 𝐁″_k the bottom domain is the whole lattice, so δ(x) = ∞ in the layer 0 (LF #4.7-1-4). The remaining points are the bottom strips of the joins.

## The Class of the Proof

- {#4.7-1-5} VAR's proof takes the cubes □ of side 2Ma_j, M a multiple of R₁M₁, that meet Ω_j but not Ω_{j+1} (p.300 l.27–29), with □̃ as above (l.35), and states □̃ ⊂ B^{j−1}(Λ_{j−1}) ∪ B^j(Λ_j) ⊂ Ω_{j−1} (l.36); (167) proves (9)–(10) on these cubes (LF #4.528). For the cubes below, □̃ ⊂ Ω_{j−1} ∖ Ω_{j+1} = B^{j−1}(Λ_{j−1}) ∪ B^j(Λ_j) is proved.
  {#4.7-1-5-1} *δ⁺(x) ≥ 4M′: the index j, M = M′.* Take □ ∋ x as in #4.7-1-1. □ meets Ω_j at x, and □̃ lies within < 4M′ of x, so it misses Ω_{j+1}. For j ≥ 1, Ω_{j−1}^c lies at ≥ 12LM′ in the units a_{j−1}, that is ≥ 12M′ > 4M′ in the units a_j, from x ∈ Ω_j, so □̃ ⊂ Ω_{j−1}. For j = 0 put Ω_{−1} := Ω_0; then □̃ ⊂ Ω_0 for δ(x) ≥ 4M′, and the other points are the bottom strips of the joins (#4.7-1-3-2). So □̃ ⊂ Ω_{j−1} ∖ Ω_{j+1}.
  {#4.7-1-5-2} *δ⁺(x) < 4M′: the index j + 1, M = 2M′.* Let y ∈ Ω_{j+1} with |x − y|_∞ = δ⁺(x); in the units a_{j+1} = La_j, |x − y|_∞ < 4M′/L ≤ 2M′. Take the cube □ of side 4M′a_{j+1} that contains x in its last big block on the sides away from y (#4.7-1-1 at the index j + 1). It extends at least 3M′ > 2M′ from x towards y in each coordinate, so y ∈ □ and □ meets Ω_{j+1}. □̃ lies within < 6M′ of x in the units a_{j+1}.
    {#4.7-1-5-2-1} Ω_{j+2} lies at ≥ 12LM′ > 6M′ from x in the units a_{j+1}, by the width of the layer j + 1 and x ∉ Ω_{j+1} (Ω_{j+2} = ∅ if the layer j + 1 is the top one). Ω_j^c lies at > 12LM′ − 4M′ from x in the units a_j, by the width of the layer j, that is at > (12 − 4/L)M′ ≥ 10M′ in the units a_{j+1}. So □̃ ⊂ Ω_j ∖ Ω_{j+2}, □ misses Ω_{j+2}, and □ is a cube of the proof's class of the index j + 1.
    {#4.7-1-5-2-2} M = 2M′ ≤ 2LM′ ≤ M(ε₁) by LF #4.7-3-1. The bounds of (9)–(10) on □ are 2B₃M′ε₁a_{j+1}^{−p} = 2L^{−p}B₃M′ε₁a_j^{−p} with p ≥ 1, and likewise for the Hölder term with p = 2 + β; they are at most the bounds B₃M′ε₁a_j^{−p} of the index j with M′, since L ≥ 2.
