# Bałaban Proof: REG's Landau Gauge for the Chain's Dressed Average

Bałaban proof: a proof that REG's results which VAR uses hold for the dressed average of BETA's §7 Setting (#B7.12-1–#B7.14), built from RG1's average (0.4) or (0.12) and RG1's variables (0.11). These are REG's Lemma 1, (1.65), Propositions 3 and 5–7, and Theorems 2 and 4, together with the results of CMP98 Sects. B, C and F that REG takes from CMP98, and VAR's step off the Landau slice, which VAR takes from REG's sketched Theorem 8 and which is proved here without it.

## Statement

- {#BR.1} *Statement.* Let G be a compact Lie group, U₀ and U′U₀ G-valued as in REG (1.33)–(1.35), and replace CMP98's averages by the chain's, on a sequence {Ω_j} with Ω₀ = T as (P-PBG) requires (#BR.3): the raw average by RG1's (0.4) or (0.12), iterated over the levels as in CMP98 (43), and every contour variable W(Γ_{y,x}) of a level-l configuration W, x ∈ B(y), by RG1's W(y, x) of (0.11), in the definitions #BR.2-1–#BR.2-4.
  {#BR.1-1} Then REG's Lemma 1 holds with a constant C_{L1} in place of 4d², (1.65) with C_{(1.65)} in place of 11d², Proposition 6 with the constants of #BR.13-1, Proposition 7 with (1.145) on the bonds of the Λ_j (#BR.13-3), and Propositions 3 and 5 and Theorems 2 and 4 as REG states them; so do CMP98's Propositions 1, 2 and 8–10 and (213)–(214) for these averages.   {#BR.1-2} VAR's conclusion at p.297 l.9–13 and p.302 l.30 holds without REG's Theorem 8, for CMP98's averages and for the chain's (Step 11). C_{L1}, C_{(1.65)} and all the constants depend on d, L and G only, and so do the thresholds (#BR.15–#BR.15-1).

## Proof

- {#BR.4} *Step 1 (covariance; CMP98 Sect. C).* By RG1 (0.6), W^u(y, x) = u(y)W(y, x)u(x)^{−1}. With CMP98's W′^v_b := v(b₋)W′_bR(W_{0,b})v^{−1}(b₊) ((55)), W′^vW₀ = (W′W₀)^v. Hence (R_{0,y}W′^v)(y, x) = v(y)(R_{0,y}W′)(y, x)(R_{0,y}v)^{−1}(x), which is CMP98's identity before (60) with (y, x) for Γ_{y,x}.
  {#BR.4-1} CMP98's (60)–(108) are derived from (55), (57), this identity, the log-mean form of (78), and the covariance (93) of the raw average, which holds for (0.4) and (0.12) by BETA #B9.40-3f. So they hold for the chain with (y, x) for Γ_{y,x} and W^{(j)} for the composite contours: in particular (77) and (87), which determine u from U₁, (92), (97) and (99), and (105)–(108).
  {#BR.4-2} The same applies to (168)–(170), (178)–(179) and (181)–(182) of Sect. F. It also applies to REG's (1.30)–(1.31), with (y, x) in (1.31), and to the uniqueness of the representative of Ax_k^{ch} in each orbit of the group (1.14). The equations (72)–(77) give u at the level-n points from its values at level n + 1, and u = 1 on 𝔅_k fixes it.
- {#BR.5} *Step 2 (size).* (S1) Let A_d := 3d², and let ℓ be a loop of one of three kinds: Γ ∪ (−Γ₁) with Γ, Γ₁ ∈ 𝐆(y, x); Γ ∪ b ∪ (−Γ′) with b ⊂ B(y), Γ ∈ 𝐆(y, b₋), Γ′ ∈ 𝐆(y, b₊); or a loop of (0.4) at a bond c with c₋ = y. Let P be the smallest box containing y and ℓ; it lies in B(y) for the first two kinds and in the box B(c₋) ∪ B(c₊) for the third. If |W₀(∂p) − 1| ≤ β on the plaquettes of P, then |W₀(ℓ) − 1| ≤ A_dL²β.
  {#BR.5-0} Indeed, with CMP98's axial contours Γ^{ax}_{y,·} from y (p.024 l.22–24), W₀(ℓ) is conjugate to the product over the bonds b of ℓ of the holonomies of Γ^{ax}_{y,b₋} ∪ b ∪ (−Γ^{ax}_{y,b₊}). Each is a product of conjugates of at most (d − 1)r plaquette holonomies of the box spanned by y and b, which lies in P, so it is within (d − 1)rβ of 1, r the ℓ^∞-distance of b from y (p.024 l.52–p.025 l.2). The three kinds have at most d(L − 1) + 1, d(L − 1) + 1 and d(L − 1) + 2L bonds, with r at most L/2, L/2 and 3L/2. So the sum is at most (3/2)(d + 2)(d − 1)L²β ≤ A_dL²β.
  {#BR.5-1} Put g_Γ := W₀(Γ)W₀(y, x)^{−1} for Γ ∈ 𝐆(y, x), and fix Γ₁ ∈ 𝐆(y, x). By (0.6), W₀(y, x) = W₀(Γ₁)M({h_Γ}) with h_Γ := W₀(Γ₁)^{−1}W₀(Γ), conjugate to a loop of the first kind. So g_Γ is conjugate to h_ΓM({h_Γ})^{−1}, and |g_Γ − 1| ≤ (1 + (1 + δ)²)A_dL²β ≤ 3A_dL²β once (1 + δ)A_dL²β ≤ ε, by (I4). Also M({g_Γ}) = 1 by (0.6).
  {#BR.5-2} (S2) Let W′ = e^{iA} on the bonds of B(y), A 𝔤^c-valued with |A| ≤ a, and a_Γ := (W′W₀)(Γ)W₀(Γ)^{−1} = Π_{b⊂Γ}R(W₀(Γ_{y,b₋}))W′_b, Γ_{y,b₋} the part of Γ before b. By (0.6), (R_{0,y}W′)(y, x) = M({a_Γg_Γ}). Let a_Γ(t) be built from e^{itA}, f(t) := (1/i)log M({a_Γ(t)g_Γ}), so f(0) = 0, and R₀ := ε/(dLa). Assume 2dLa ≤ ε and 40A_dL²β ≤ ε.
  {#BR.5-3} For |t| ≤ R₀, a_Γ(t)g_Γ is a product of exponentials with exponents of total norm at most ε/2 + (1 + δ)3A_dL²β ≤ 0.6ε. So by (I4) twice, |f(t)| ≤ (1 + δ)²0.6ε ≤ 0.75ε. By the maximum principle for f(t)/t, |f(1)| ≤ 0.75ε/R₀ ≤ dLa. By Cauchy's estimate, as R₀ ≥ 2, |f(1) − f′(0)| ≤ 2·0.75εR₀^{−2} ≤ 2(dLa)²/ε.
  {#BR.5-4} f′(0) = Σ_Γw_Γ[(R_{0,y}A)(Γ)], with (R_{0,y}A)(Γ) := Σ_{b⊂Γ}R(W₀(Γ_{y,b₋}))A_b of norm at most dLa/2, and w_Γ the partial derivatives of (1/i)log M at {g_Γ}. By (0.8), w_Γ = |𝐆(y, x)|^{−1} at g_Γ = 1, and by Cauchy's estimate in the g_Γ it differs from this by O(L²β), the O depending on d and G.
  {#BR.5-5} Hence |(1/i)log(R_{0,y}W′)(y, x)| ≤ dLa, and (1/i)log(R_{0,y}W′)(y, x) = (R_{0,y}A)(y, x) + O(L³βa + (La)²), with (R_{0,y}A)(y, x) := |𝐆(y, x)|^{−1}Σ_Γ(R_{0,y}A)(Γ) and the O depending on d, L and G, through the number |𝐆(y, x)| of the terms O(L²β)·dLa/2. This is CMP98 (187) for the chain, with the extra error O(L³βa).
  {#BR.5-6} (S3) For a bond c with c₋ = y, the loop factor E of (0.4) or (0.12) at c, defined by W̄(c) = EW(c), satisfies |E − 1| ≤ 8A_dL²β once 7A_dL²β ≤ ε. For (0.4), E is the exponential of a weighted mean of logarithms of loops of the third kind. For (0.12), W(x′, c₊) = W(c₊, x′)^{−1} (BETA #B4.187-b), so its loop is g_Γ^{−1}W(Γ)W([x, x′])W(Γ′)^{−1}g_{Γ′}W(−c), within 7A_dL²β of 1 by #BR.5-1, and E is the exponential of the L^{−d}-weighted sum of their logarithms, so |E − 1| ≤ (1 + δ)7A_dL²β. This replaces CMP98's e^{iO(L²α₀)} on p.047 l.18 and in (194).
