# Bałaban Proof: The Second-Class Components in LFR II's Expansion

Bałaban proof: the derivations, for LFR II's second-class components and the polymers (1.90)–(1.97), that (6C) of the two-loops note (WILSON_IT1_TWO_LOOPS_UNIFORM_BOUND_2026_10_27.md) states at #805, #812, #814-0, #814-4-2, and #819. Pages are LFR II's unless named.

{#805-1} *The distance (#805).* A shortest tree for d_{k,∪Y}(X′) meets every M-cube of X′∖∪_iY_i. X′ is connected, so each Y_i ⊆ X′ is joined to a cube of X′∖∪_iY_i, or to a Y_j already joined, by a chain of adjacent cubes of X′. Add, for each Y_i, the segments of length M joining the centres of adjacent M-cubes along a spanning tree of its cubes, and a path of length at most dM inside two adjacent cubes to the tree or to a Y_j already joined. The result is a connected graph in X′ meeting every M-cube of X′, and a spanning tree of it is no longer.
  {#805-1-1} If X′∖∪_iY_i is empty, X′ is one Y_i, and the spanning tree of its cubes alone gives the bound.

{#812-0} *The q-parts (#812).* Two parts on one X′ overlap, so (1.90) and its cluster expansion are unchanged in sum. (1.92)–(1.95) bound each F_q(X′) by the q-term of (1.95)'s right side, and (1.96) sums these terms over q to (1.97)'s bound, so Σ_q|F_q(X′)| obeys (1.97). A q-part with q ≥ 1 holds a first-class component.
  {#812-1} A domain with a q = 0 part meets ∪_iY_i, since a domain disjoint from ∪_iY_i has q ≥ 1, so it contains some Y_i (p.389 l.27–28: "Either the domain X′ contains one of the domains Y_i, then the sum over q above begins with q=0, … or the domain X′ is disjoint with ⋃_{i=1}^mY_i, and then the sum begins with q=1"), and its c₁ is "α^{1/3} in the remaining cases" (p.390 l.2), not e^{−p₀(g_k)}.

{#814-1} *The root (#814-0).* Fix a π_k-cube r^Y_i off Y_i with a face on Y_i. Z is a union of MR_k-cubes (p.384 l.24, p.380 l.47–49, at j = k), each a union of π_k-cubes (#628-1-1).   {#814-1-0} A V-domain Y ∈ 𝐃 meeting Y_i contains the component of Z^∼ through Y_i (p.377 l.5–6), and with it r^Y_i. A term determines the localisation domain ⋃_{Y∈𝐃}Y ∪ ⋃_jX_j (p.387 l.42), equal to X′ for the terms of F(X′) (p.388 l.14), so a q = 0 domain X′ ⊇ Y_i is a union of V-domains, one of which meets Y_i. {#814-2} *The count (#814-0).* Each Y_j ⊆ X′ likewise has its r^Y_j in X′∖∪_lY_l. If d_{k,∪Y}(X′) ≤ n, (1.67)'s tree meets r^Y_i and every π_k-cube of X′∖∪_jY_j, and the cubes it meets form a connected set S ∋ r^Y_i of at most O(1)(n + 1) cubes. X′ is then fixed by a subset of S and a choice among the at most 2d|S| components Y_j with a face on S.
  {#814-2-0} So at most e^{O(1)(n+1)} such X′ pass through r^Y_i, and with (1.97)'s e^{−(1+β)κd_{k,∪Y}(X′)} and (1 + β)κ, net of the c_F^× uses, above twice this entropy constant of d (κ large, #T3.34) their sum at half that net rate is C_Ψ″ times the amplitude, with no factor N(Y_i).
  {#814-2-1} C_Ψ″ is a constant of d, κ and β, and the count is the same through any π_k-cube c off ∪_jY_j in place of r^Y_i.

{#814-4-2-0} *The Bałaban polymers at an MR_k-cube (#814-4-2).* Q lies in one Y_l or off ∪_jY_j, Z being a union of MR_k-cubes (#814-1).   {#814-4-2-0-1} In the second case (1.67)'s tree, contained in X′, meets c and the cube r^Y_l of every Y_l ⊆ X′ (#814-3), each point of which lies within ∞-distance M of Y_l. So d_{k,∪Y}(X′) ≥ dist_∞(c, r^Y_l)/M ≥ mR_k − 1 ≥ m − 1 for every Y_l ⊆ X′ with dist_∞(Y_l, Q) ≥ mMR_k, and X′ is summed at the root r^Y_l of each Y_l ⊆ X′.

Half of #814-2's net rate, at least 2d for κ large, gives e^{−2d(m−1)_+} on these, and the other half gives C_Ψ″((8α)^{1/3} + e^{−p₀(g_k)}) at r^Y_l.
  {#814-4-2-2} Σ_{m≥0}(2m + 3)^de^{−2d(m−1)_+} ≤ 6^d for d ≥ 2. For m ≥ 2 the term is ((2m + 3)e^{−2(m−1)})^d, whose base divided by 6 is (7/6)e^{−2} < 1/6 at m = 2 and shrinks by the factor ((2m + 5)/(2m + 3))e^{−2} ≤ (9/7)e^{−2} < 1/5 from m to m + 1, so the sum divided by 6^d is at most 2^{−d} + (5/6)^d + (25/24)6^{−d}, which decreases in d and equals (34 + 25/24)/36 < 1 at d = 2.

{#819.1} *The small-domain branch (#819; D2.7 15.8(c)).* On the support of χ_k^c(Y_i^{∼−6}) = 1 − χ_k(Y_i^{∼−6}) (p.378 l.6, l.21), some cube □ ⊂ Y_i^{∼−6} has a plaquette p ⊂ □̃ with |U_{k,□}(V_k, ∂p) − 1| ≥ ε_kη² (CRE (2.17), p.257 l.39).
  {#819.1-0} If every plaquette of V_k on the neighbourhood of □ that determines U_{k,□}, inside Y_i by the margin ∼−6, were < (O(1)B₃M²)^{−1}ε_k, VAR Theorem 1 (p.279 l.8–29) would give |∂U_{k,□} − 1| < ε_kη², as Bałaban uses it for U_{k,Z} at LFR I p.193 l.20 and l.28–29.
