# Bałaban Proof: The Number of Cubes of a Localisation Domain

RG2 bounds the number of cubes of a localisation domain by 3·2³ times its linear size ((1.28), p.8 l.19; (2.30), p.18 l.25; p.9 l.23), which fails for a single cube. The bound #BK.1-1 holds for every localisation domain, it gives RG2's (1.26) with an absolute constant, and RG2's Lemmas 1–3 and (2.41) hold with it in place of RG2's count, with larger absolute constants. RG1 = CMP 109 and RG2 = CMP 116; pins are to Balaban\tex\<paper>\parts\pNNN_corrected.tex.

## Statement

- {#BK.1} *Statement.* Let X ∈ 𝐃_j, a union of a connected finite family of closed cubes of π_j of side M (RG1 p.257 l.2–11), and let N(X) := M^{−4}|X| be the number of these cubes, |X| the volume of X. RG1 defines d_j(X) as the length, divided by M, of a shortest tree graph in the continuous space that is contained in X and meets all the cubes of X (p.257 l.14–16), without naming the norm on ℝ⁴ in which the length is measured. For every norm |·|_p, p ∈ [1, ∞],
  {#BK.1-1} N(X) ≤ 2⁸(2 + d_j(X)) ≤ 2⁹(1 + d_j(X)) and diam X ≤ M(2d_j(X) + 4),
  with d_j(X) measured in |·|_p and diam X in the Euclidean norm.
  {#BK.1-2} RG2's (1.28) and p.9 l.23 state N(Y) ≤ 3·2³d_k(Y), and (2.30) states (3·2³)^{−1}N(Y) ≤ d_k(Y). These fail when d_k(Y) = 0, as for a single cube, where N(Y) = 1. RG2 restricts the sum (1.26) to "X with d_j(X) ≠ 0, as it follows from our inductive construction" (p.8 l.8); #BK.1 needs no such restriction.

## Proof

- {#BK.2} *A tour of a tree.* Let T be a tree graph of RG1's class for X. The proof uses only that T is a connected finite union of rectifiable arcs between finitely many points, without cycles. Write ℓ_p(T) for the sum of the |·|_p-lengths of its arcs and ℓ := ℓ_∞(T). As |v|_∞ ≤ |v|_p for v ∈ ℝ⁴, ℓ ≤ ℓ_p(T).
  {#BK.2-1} Doubling every arc of T gives a connected multigraph with all degrees even, so it has a closed walk through every doubled arc once (Euler). Traversed by |·|_∞-arclength, this walk is a map γ: [0, 2ℓ] → T onto T with |γ(s) − γ(s′)|_∞ ≤ |s − s′|. If T is one point, ℓ = 0 and γ is constant.
- {#BK.3} *Covering points.* Let n := ⌈ℓ/M⌉ and s_i := min(2iM, 2ℓ) for i = 0, …, n, so s_n = 2ℓ. Every s ∈ [0, 2ℓ] has |s − s_i| ≤ M for some i. Indeed s = 2ℓ is s_n; otherwise take the i < n with 2iM ≤ s < 2(i + 1)M, and use s_i = 2iM if s ≤ 2iM + M, and s_{i+1} otherwise, as then s ≤ s_{i+1} ≤ 2(i + 1)M < s + M. So every point of T lies within |·|_∞-distance M of one of the n + 1 ≤ ℓ/M + 2 points p_i := γ(s_i).
- {#BK.4} *The count.* The corners of the cubes of π_j form a translate c + Mℤ⁴, since the cubes tile the space with centres on T_M^{(j+m)} (p.257 l.3–4). A cube ∏_ν[a_ν, a_ν + M] with a ∈ c + Mℤ⁴ meets the box ∏_ν[p_ν − M, p_ν + M] iff a_ν ∈ [p_ν − 2M, p_ν + M] for every ν, an interval of length 3M holding at most 4 points of c_ν + Mℤ. So at most 4⁴ = 2⁸ cubes meet the box. Every cube of X contains a point of T, which lies in the box around some p_i (#BK.3), so N(X) ≤ 2⁸(n + 1) ≤ 2⁸(2 + ℓ/M) ≤ 2⁸(2 + ℓ_p(T)/M). The infimum over T gives N(X) ≤ 2⁸(2 + d_j(X)).
- {#BK.5} *The diameter.* Two points x, x′ of X lie in cubes □, □′ of X, which contain points y, y′ of T. A cube of side M has Euclidean diameter 2M in d = 4. The arc of T from y to y′ has |·|_∞-length at most ℓ, so |y − y′| ≤ 2|y − y′|_∞ ≤ 2ℓ ≤ 2ℓ_p(T). So |x − x′| ≤ 2M + 2ℓ_p(T) + 2M, and the infimum over T gives diam X ≤ M(2d_j(X) + 4). ∎
- {#BK.5-1} *RG2's (1.26).* For □ ∈ π_j and κ ≥ 2⁹(6 log 2 + 1), Σ_{X∈𝐃_j, X⊃□}exp(−κd_j(X)) ≤ 2^{6·2¹⁰} + 1. A family of N cubes connected through common walls and containing □ is the set of cubes visited by a closed walk from □ of 2(N − 1) steps between cubes with a common wall, a depth-first tour of a spanning tree; each step is one of 2·4 = 8, so there are at most 8^{2(N−1)} such families.
  {#BK.5-2} By #BK.1-1, d_j(X) ≥ max(0, N/2⁸ − 2), and N/2⁸ − 2 ≥ N/2⁹ for N ≥ 2¹⁰. So the sum is at most Σ_{N<2¹⁰}2^{6(N−1)} + Σ_{N≥2¹⁰}2^{6N}e^{−κN/2⁹}; the first sum is < 2^{6·2¹⁰}, and the second is ≤ Σ_{N≥1}e^{−N} < 1, as κ/2⁹ ≥ 6 log 2 + 1.

## RG2's Uses

- {#BK.6} With #BK.1 in place of RG2's count, each use in RG2 holds with larger absolute constants, for κ large depending on δ (RG2 assumes "δκ sufficiently large", p.9 l.20). The bounds below are for d := d_k(Y), and δκ ≥ 1 gives 1 + d ≤ e^{δκd}.
  - {#BK.6-1} p.8 l.17–19 and p.9 l.23 bound the number of cubes □₀ ⊂ Y by an exponential of d. #BK.1 gives N(Y) ≤ 2⁹exp((1/16)(κ₁ − 2)d) for κ₁ ≥ 18, and N(Y) ≤ 2⁹exp(δκd). The factor 2⁹ enters the absolute constants O(M^q) of (1.29) and C₁ of (1.36) (p.9 l.48–50).
  - {#BK.6-2} (2.20) (p.16 l.26–34) follows without the count. The sum Σ_{Y∈𝐃}α₄exp(−δκd_k(Y)) runs over distinct Y ⊂ Y₀, so it is at most Σ_{□⊂Y₀}Σ_{Y∋□}α₄exp(−δκd_k(Y)) ≤ O(1)α₄M^{−4}|Y₀| by (1.26) (#BK.5-1) with δκ for κ. The quadratic part follows by Schur's test, as Σ_{b′}M^{−4}exp(−(1/16)(κ₁ − 1)M^{−1}|b_− − b′_−|) ≤ O(1).
  - {#BK.6-3} p.19 l.19 bounds the right side exp(εM^{−4}|Z₀|) of (2.34), times exp(−δκd_k(Z₀)), by 1, where ε := exp(−γ₂ε₁²/(20γ²)) ≤ ε₂ ≤ 1 (p.19 l.10–13). (2.35) records the bound component by component, Z₀ = ∪Z_i. By #BK.1, for each component exp(εN(Z_i))exp(−δκd_k(Z_i)) ≤ e^{2⁹ε}exp((2⁹ε − δκ)d_k(Z_i)) ≤ e^{2⁹} for δκ ≥ 2⁹. This factor multiplies ε₂ in (2.35), and it is absorbed by the O(1) in the conditions on ε₂ of p.20 l.4 and l.6 (BETA #B2.1.26) and in C₃ (p.20 l.25).
  - {#BK.6-4} p.19 l.40 counts cubes by (1.28) with δκ for κ. #BK.1 adds the factor 2⁹ to (L + 2)⁴O(1)ε₂ (p.20 l.2).
  - {#BK.6-5} p.20 l.24 bounds exp(c(LM)^{−4}|Z|), with c below an absolute constant c₀ (p.20 l.23–24), times exp(−δ½Lκd_{k+1}(Z)), by 1. By #BK.1 at the index k + 1 it is at most e^{2⁹c₀} for δ½Lκ ≥ 2⁹c₀, and this factor joins the O(1) of C₃.
  - {#BK.6-6} In (2.40) (p.21 l.12–20), Σ_{Z⊂X}exp(−½δLκd_{k+1}(Z) + 2(LM)^{−4}|Z|) ≤ e^{2¹⁰}Σ_{□⊂X}Σ_{Z∋□}exp(−(½δLκ − 2¹⁰)d_{k+1}(Z)) ≤ e^{2¹⁰}O(1)(LM)^{−4}|X| by #BK.1 and (1.26) with ½δLκ − 2¹⁰ for κ. Then (LM)^{−4}|X| ≤ exp((LM)^{−4}|X|), and this times exp(−½δLκd_{k+1}(X)) is at most e^{2⁹} for ½δLκ ≥ 2⁹, where RG2 has 1. Both factors join the O(1) of (2.41).
- {#BK.7} So RG2's Lemmas 1–3 and (2.41) hold as stated, with larger absolute constants O(1), C₁, and C₃, for κ above thresholds that depend on δ.
