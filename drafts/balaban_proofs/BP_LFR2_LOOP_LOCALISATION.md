# Localisation of Words and Insertions over the Class Components

{#BLL.0} Let F be a word or a carried insertion of the dressed loop of D2.7 15.5 (WILSON_IT1_D27_CLOSURE_2026_10_26.md), a function of the pre-R configuration (#BLL.3). Then F(U^{old}) = Σ_𝒦F^𝒦, the sum over the sets 𝒦 of units (the class components far from F, #BLL.4-1), where F^𝒦 depends among the units only on the data of those in 𝒦 and obeys the bounds of #BLL.31 and #BLL.36–#BLL.36-3, and F^∅ is F at the post-R configuration when F has no home (#BLL.34). The construction is Bałaban's localisation expansion (LFR II pp.372–376), applied to F in place of a term of the action.
{#BLL.0-1} Page references without a paper are to LFR II. EP, GC, BFL, and BP-widths are BP_TL_EVAL_POINTS.md, BP_TL_GC_TRANSFORMS.md, BP_FAMILY_LOCALISATION.md, and BP_LAYER_WIDTHS.md in balaban_proofs/. LF is WILSON_IT1_LARGE_FIELD_DEPTH_2026_10_27.md, the map is WILSON_IT1_D2_STEP_MAP_2026_10_22.md, and the table is CONSTANTS_AND_CONVENTIONS_WDELTA.md.

## Setting

{#BLL.1} Fix a step k and a term of (1.72). Z is the union of its class components, of the first and the second class (p.378 l.14–21); the unchanged components lie outside Z (D2.7 15.5(i)). Distances are sup-distances in the units L^kη.
{#BLL.1-0} The M-cubes are the cubes of π_k (RG1 p.257 l.3–11, EP #44-1-3), and |S| is the number of M-cubes of a union S of them. d_□ is the ℓ^∞ distance of the cube indices; two unions of M-cubes meet when d_□ ≤ 1, that is, when the closed sets intersect. S^{[n]} is the set of the M-cubes at d_□ ≤ n from S. A set of cubes is connected when any two of its cubes are joined by a chain of its cubes, consecutive ones meeting; its components are its maximal connected subsets.
{#BLL.1-1} Z is a union of components of the large-field region Λ_k^c (LFR I p.177 l.17–18, l.33–41), which consists of the closed MR_k-cubes of one partition not in Λ_k (CRE p.256 l.21–22), its components being those of #BLL.1-0 for these cubes. Two disjoint closed unions of cubes of a partition into cubes of side a lie at sup-distance ≥ a, so distinct components of Z lie at sup-distance ≥ MR_k, hence at d_□ ≥ R_k + 1. Each lies in a cube of side 100MR_k (table #T6.10-1), so K^{[1]} has at most N_K := (100R_k + 3)^d cubes, and K^{[n]} at most N_K(2n + 1)^d.
{#BLL.2} Z^{(2)} := the points of Z at sup-distance ≥ 2MR_k from Z^c. Λ ⊂ Z^{(2)}, as Λ is at sup-distance ≥ 8MR_k from Z^c (LF #4.44-0). Ω_k^c ∩ Z ⊂ Z^{(2)}, as the points of Z within 2MR_k of Z^c lie in Ω_k (EP #44-1-1, BP-widths #4.18-2-1-1–#4.18-2-1-2). A point of a component K in Z^{(2)} is at sup-distance ≥ 2MR_k from K^c: a segment from it to another component is not contained in Z, its end-points lying in different components, so it contains a point of Z^c.
{#BLL.3} F is either a letter π(T_i(U^{old})) of a far bond i of C, one lying in no set K⁺ of #BLL.4 (D2.7 15.5(i)), with Dep(F) := {i}, or a carried insertion D of D2.7 15.5(i-0), with Dep(F) := Dep(D) of D2.7 15.5(i-0). U^{old} is the configuration of EP #39-3: Bałaban's (1.61) with exp iη𝐇_k″(g_kB) and the N preliminary deformations exp iηℍ^{(n)}_k(g_jCB_j − hD̃(g_jCB_j)) as left factors (LFR I p.191 l.39–41), where N = O(log log g_k^{−2}) (table #T12.20).
{#BLL.3-0} ‖F‖ is F's class bound at the evaluation points of D2.7's #7 block. For a letter, ‖F‖ := τ_i := e^{c_π′λ_wα_{1,k}}, with c_π′ of map #MD2.1.4.27 and λ_w of table #T4.47, which bounds a scale-k bond's word on 𝒟^λ_k (EP #170–#171); τ_i ≤ 2 for g small.
{#BLL.3-1} F may also be F_h := ⊗_iπ(T_i(U^{old})), the tensor product over the bonds i of C in ⋃_{K∈h}K⁺, h a home (D2.7 15.5(i): a class of components of Z, of either class, under the overlap of the sets K⁺), with Dep(F_h) := the set of these bonds. It takes values in ⊗_iEnd(V_π) with the projective norm. A contraction in loop order (D2.7 15.3) has norm at most 1 for this norm, since an ordered product of operators has norm at most the product of their norms. ‖F_h‖ := Π_iτ_i.
{#BLL.4} h(F) is F's home (D2.7 15.5(i)), and h(F_h) := h. It is closed under the overlap of the sets K⁺, and it contains every component K with i ⊂ K⁺ for a letter, and with K⁺ ∩ Dep(D) ≠ ∅ for an insertion D. K⁺ = K^{∼2} lies within sup-distance 2MR_k of K, as each ∼ adds one layer of MR_k-cubes (CRE p.245 l.41–43), so K⁺ lies in a cube of side 104MR_k (#BLL.1-1). h₀(F) := the components K ∈ h(F) with K⁺ ∩ Dep(F) ≠ ∅.
{#BLL.4-00} X(F) is the union of the M-cubes meeting Dep(F). Y₁(F) is the set D′ of EP #39-5-1 for X = X(F). It is the union of X(F), the components of Z meeting X(F), and the cubes around each face of dimension < d − 1 at which a cube of X(F) outside a component touches a cube of it, so Y₁(F) ⊂ X(F)^{[1]} ∪ (the components meeting X(F)). Y₁⁺(F) is the set D′ for X = X(F)^{[2]}, so Y₁⁺(F) ⊂ X(F)^{[3]} ∪ (the components meeting X(F)^{[2]}).
{#BLL.4-1} T̂₀ := Y₁(F) ∪ ⋃_{K∈h₀(F)}K⁺. A component K of Z is near F when d_□(K, T̂₀) ≤ R_k − 3. The other components are the units, and 𝒰(F) is their set. T̂_Z := ⋃_{K∈h₀(F)}K⁺ ∪ ⋃_{K near F}K and T̂ := Y₁(F) ∪ T̂_Z. A unit lies at d_□ ≥ R_k − 2 from T̂: from T̂₀ by definition, and from a near component by #BLL.1-1. A component meeting T̂ is near F, so T̂_Z contains every component that T̂ meets.
{#BLL.5} A component near F lies in h(F). Indeed, let K ∉ h(F). Then K⁺ ∩ K′⁺ = ∅ for K′ ∈ h(F). Also i ⊄ K⁺ for a letter, no bond of F_h meets K⁺, these bonds lying in the sets K′⁺, and K⁺ ∩ Dep(F) = ∅ for an insertion. K⁺ contains the points within sup-distance MR_k of K, so K lies at sup-distance ≥ MR_k from K′⁺ and ≥ MR_k − 1 from Dep(F), a bond having length 1.
{#BLL.5-00} X(F) lies within M of Dep(F), and Y₁(F) within M of X(F) and of the components meeting X(F), which are not K and lie at sup-distance ≥ MR_k from it. So K lies at sup-distance ≥ MR_k − 3M from T̂₀, and d_□(K, T̂₀) ≥ R_k − 2.
{#BLL.5-0} Let y be an M-cube of X(F), and K a component with d_□(y, K) ≤ 3. Then K ∈ h₀(F) and y^{[1]} ⊂ K⁺ ⊂ T̂_Z. Indeed, y lies within sup-distance 3M of K, and Dep(F) has a point in y. For an insertion this point lies in K⁺. For a letter, the bond i lies within 3M + 1 ≤ MR_k of K, so i ⊂ K⁺. For F_h, the bond through this point lies in K′⁺ for a K′ ∈ h and meets K⁺, and h is closed under overlap. So K ∈ h(F) (#BLL.4), and K⁺ ∩ Dep(F) ≠ ∅.
{#BLL.5-0-1} The cubes of y^{[1]} lie within 4M ≤ MR_k of K. A component meeting X(F)^{[2]} lies at d_□ ≤ 3 from a cube of X(F), so Y₁⁺(F) ⊂ X(F)^{[3]} ∪ T̂_Z (#BLL.4-00).
{#BLL.5-1} |T̂| ≤ (1 + 2^dN_K)(3^d + N_K + 5^dN⁺)|X(F)|, with N⁺ := (104R_k + 1)^d. Indeed, |X(F)^{[1]}| ≤ 3^d|X(F)|. An M-cube meets at most one component, its diameter M being less than MR_k (#BLL.1-1), and a component has at most N_K cubes. A K ∈ h₀(F) has K⁺ meeting a cube y of X(F), so K is within sup-distance 2MR_k of y (#BLL.4). The points of these components nearest to y are pairwise ≥ MR_k apart in a cube of side 4MR_k + M, so they number at most 5^d; and |K⁺| ≤ N⁺. This bounds |T̂₀|.
{#BLL.5-2} A component near F has a cube at d_□ ≤ R_k − 3 from a cube y of T̂₀. These cubes, one per component, lie in the box of 2R_k − 5 cubes per side about y, pairwise at d_□ ≥ R_k + 1. Halving the box along each axis gives 2^d boxes of at most R_k − 2 cubes per side, each holding at most one of them. So at most 2^d|T̂₀| components are near F, each with at most N_K cubes, and |T̂| ≤ (1 + 2^dN_K)|T̂₀|.
{#BLL.6} *The sources.* The arguments of the functions of the operations (#BLL.8) are the following. The left factors take g_kB, which is localised in Λ (p.372 l.14–15), and g_jCB_j − hD̃(g_jCB_j), j < k, the fluctuation field on the bonds of (Ω^c_{j+1} ∖ Z″_{j+1}) ∩ Z integrated at the preliminary step j, which C parametrises by the remaining variables B_j (LFR I p.188 l.52–53; RG1 p.268 l.2–3; LF #4.249). The intersection with Z is LFR I's convention (p.177 l.33–35), the 𝐑-operation integrating in the components of Z, in which the integrations factorise (p.378 l.24–26).
{#BLL.6-00} 𝐇_k″ takes the arguments of (1.54); they lie in Λ except 𝐆″_kJ_{1,2}, whose sources lie in Z″_{h+2} ⊂ Λ (LF #4.174–#4.174-1, #4.177). 𝐇_{𝐁₁} takes −i log M^·(U_{k,Λ}) on Z″_k (p.374 l.5), and Z″_k = (Ω^{∼5}_{k₀+1})^c ∩ Z ⊂ (Ω^{∼4}_{k₀+1})^c ∩ Z = Λ (LFR I (1.10), (1.73)). 𝐇_k takes −i log V′_k on Λ ((1.64)).
{#BLL.6-0} Since Ω^c_{j+1} ∩ Z ⊂ Ω_k^c ∩ Z for j < k, each argument is supported in Z^{(2)} (#BLL.2), or is 𝐆″_kb with b supported in Z^{(2)}, where 𝐆″_k has a walk expansion decaying as e^{−δ₀d} (LF #4.177, PBG Theorem 3.13).
{#BLL.7} For each component K of Z, E_K := the M-cubes at sup-distance < Mt_k from K ∩ Z^{(2)}. Set E := ⋃_KE_K, and let σ_t be the set of the M-cubes not in E. By #BLL.2, E_K ⊂ K at sup-distance ≥ 2MR_k − Mt_k − M from K^c, so E_K and its neighbours lie in K. Every source of #BLL.6 lies in cubes of E, and a cube of σ_t lies at sup-distance ≥ Mt_k from Z^{(2)}.
{#BLL.7-1} t_k := ⌈(32/(δ₀M))(2log α_{1,k}^{−1} + log(L⁴R_k²))⌉ is table #T4.53's. For g small, t_k ≤ 1 + (64/(δ₀M))R_k^{1/2}: 2log α_{1,k}^{−1} ≤ log g_k^{−2}, as α_{1,k} ≥ g_k (CRE (2.28), p.259 l.45–50), and log g_k^{−2} + 4log L + 2log R_k ≤ 2R_k^{1/2}, as R_k ≥ (log g_k^{−2})² (CRE (2.5), p.255 l.36–39, with r ≥ 2, p.245 l.33–34).
{#BLL.7-2} *The reference.* U⁰ := U(𝐁_k″(Y₁⁺(F)), d(𝐔)) is EP #39-0's configuration for the domain Y₁⁺(F) (#BLL.4-00), component by component. It is Bałaban's term with Y₂′ = Y₃′ = Y₄′ = ∅ (p.373 l.31–35, p.374 l.29–31): it has no left factors, the functions 𝐇_k″, 𝐇_{𝐁₁}, and 𝐇_k are 0 on its boundary layer, and it depends on 𝐔 on Y₁⁺(F) only. That layer lies within sup-distance M of Z^c (EP #39-5-2).
{#BLL.7-3} *Far bonds.* r_Z(x) is the sup-distance of x from Z^{(2)}. A fine bond is far when r_Z ≥ M(2R_k − 1) on it. A fine bond with a point within sup-distance 1 of an M-cube outside Z is far: its points lie within 2 of that cube, which lies in the closure of Z^c, at sup-distance ≥ 2MR_k from Z^{(2)} (#BLL.2), and 2MR_k − 2 ≥ M(2R_k − 1). A far bond lies in a cube of σ_t, since the points of a cube of E lie within Mt_k + M < M(2R_k − 1) of Z^{(2)} (#BLL.7, #BLL.10-1).
{#BLL.7-3-1} Set R_z := e^{(δ₀/128)MR_k}. Then e^{−(δ₀/128)r_Z} ≤ R_z^{−3/2} on a far bond, as 2R_k − 1 ≥ 3R_k/2, and R_z ≥ L^{(12d+1)R_k}, as δ₀M/128 ≥ δ₀M₁/64 ≥ (12d + 1)log L (M > L²M₁, CRE p.245 l.35–38; EP #99-2).
{#BLL.7-4} *The interpolation.* X_o is the set of the M-cubes of X(F)^{[1]} outside Z, so |X_o| ≤ 3^d|X(F)|. For an M-cube Δ, p_Δ is the convolution of Δ's indicator with a fixed C² probability density supported in the sup-ball of radius ½. So p_Δ ≥ 0, Σ_Δp_Δ = 1, p_Δ vanishes off the sup-neighbourhood of radius ½ of Δ, and Σ_Δ|∂p_Δ| ≤ C_∂ and Σ_Δ|∂²p_Δ| ≤ C_∂ for a constant C_∂ ≥ 1 of d. For z ∈ ℂ^{X_o} and a fine bond 𝔟 with midpoint x, z_𝔟 := 1 + Σ_{Δ∈X_o}(z(Δ) − 1)p_Δ(x). So z_𝔟 = 1 unless 𝔟 is far.
{#BLL.7-5} A fine bond 𝔟 with z_𝔟 ≠ 1 has its midpoint within sup-distance ½ of a cube of X_o ⊂ X(F)^{[1]}, so it lies in X(F)^{[2]} ⊂ Y₁⁺(F). Let V be a configuration with V_𝔟(U⁰_𝔟)^{−1} in the domain of the principal logarithm on the far bonds of X(F)^{[2]}, and b_𝔟 := (iη)^{−1}log(V_𝔟(U⁰_𝔟)^{−1}) there. V^z is the configuration with V^z_𝔟 := e^{iηz_𝔟b_𝔟}U⁰_𝔟 = e^{iη(z_𝔟−1)b_𝔟}V_𝔟 on these bonds and V^z_𝔟 := V_𝔟 on the others. It is entire in z, V^z = V at z ≡ 1, and V^z_𝔟 = U⁰_𝔟 where z_𝔟 = 0.
{#BLL.7-6} *The split.* For I ⊂ X_o, F_I(V) := ∫_{[0,1]^I}∂_z^IF(V^{(z_I, 0)})dz_I, where (z_I, 0) is z_I on I and 0 on X_o ∖ I. The fundamental theorem of calculus in each variable, as at RG2 (1.9), gives F(V) = Σ_{I⊂X_o}F_I(V). At z = (z_I, 0), z_𝔟 = Σ_{Δ∉X_o}p_Δ(x) + Σ_{Δ∈I}z(Δ)p_Δ(x). For x in X(F), the cubes Δ ∉ X_o with p_Δ(x) ≠ 0 lie in X(F)^{[1]}, hence in Z. So F_I depends on V only on Dep_I, the set of the fine bonds of Dep(F) whose midpoint lies within sup-distance ½ of a cube of I or of Z, and on U⁰ on Dep(F).
{#BLL.7-7} X(F_I) is the union of the M-cubes meeting Dep_I, and T̂_I := X(F_I) ∪ T̂_Z. An M-cube meeting a bond of Dep_I has a point within sup-distance 1 of a cube of I or of Z, so X(F_I) ⊂ X(F) ∩ (I^{[1]} ∪ Z^{[1]}). Hence T̂_I ⊂ T̂ and T̂_I ⊂ T̂_Z ∪ I^{[1]} (#BLL.5-0), and T̂_I contains every component it meets (#BLL.4-1). The units, 𝒰(F), and T̂_Z are F's, for every I.

## The Operations

{#BLL.8} *The four operations.* By #BLL.6, U^{old} depends on the components' data through four families of functions: for m = 0, the left factors of #BLL.3; for m = 1, 2, 3, the functions 𝐇_k″, 𝐇_{𝐁₁}, and 𝐇_k of (1.61)–(1.63) (p.373 l.15–p.374 l.31). The operation m is Bałaban's localisation operation for its family (p.373 l.31–35, p.374 l.8–11, l.26–29), taken with the set Q_m := σ_t ∖ T_m of cubes (T_m of #BLL.9).
{#BLL.8-00} The parameters s(Δ), Δ ∈ Q_m, multiply each walk term of the family's operators, and of 𝐆″_k in its arguments (#BLL.6-0), by the s(Δ) of the cubes it meets (RG2 p.003 l.27–32; #BFL.185), and s = 1 on the other cubes; at s = 1 the family is unchanged. RG2 (1.9), the fundamental theorem of calculus in these parameters, expands the function.
{#BLL.8-0} An operation is determined by its set of cubes (p.373 l.12). The operation 0 is the same construction for the left factors, which Bałaban's terms, taken at U_k^0, do not carry (EP #39-3).
{#BLL.8-1} *The decoupling.* Fix s. Each walk term that meets a cube with s = 0 vanishes. Take the cubes with s ≠ 0. Then a family's value on a set W depends only on the arguments supported in the components of these cubes that meet W, and on the s(Δ) of these components' cubes (RG2 p.004 l.17–30). This is #BFL.185-0, whose conditions hold: the kernels vanish across cubes with s = 0, and the equations, the sources, and the local functions are local.
{#BLL.8-2} A path leaving the closed union of one such component passes, just after leaving it, through a closed cube that meets the component and is not in it. That cube has s = 0.
{#BLL.9} *The targets.* Fix I ⊂ X_o. The operations expand F_I(U^{old}) in U^{old}, with U⁰ kept. T_0 := T̂_I. For m ≥ 1, G_{m−1} is the union of the components of T_{m−1} ∪ E ∪ S_c^{(m−1)} ∪ B^{(m−1)} (#BLL.14) that meet T_{m−1}. T_m is G_{m−1} together with the components of Z meeting it. Like every union of M-cubes, T_m, and the domain Y_P of #BLL.14-1-1, is the union of its wall-connected components, which are 𝐃_k domains (RG1 p.257 l.7–10).
{#BLL.9-1} Bałaban uses his representations (1.62)–(1.63) for the domains Y₂ and Y₃ that the expansion produces (p.373 l.35–36, p.374 l.13), localising the determining sets along each part of ∂Y (CRE p.257 l.7–26, EP #44-1-2). The localised determining set and its minimal configurations are built for every union of M₁-cubes (CRE p.256 l.57–63, p.257 l.7–26), and EP #44-1-2's widths use only the boundary of the domain. T_m is a union of M₁-cubes, the partitions being compatible (CRE p.245 l.38–40). So the operation m applies with Y_m := T_m.
{#BLL.10} *Reached units and collars.* 𝒦_m := the units contained in T_m, so 𝒦_0 = ∅. Set w_T := ⌈R_k/(1024√d)⌉ for a carried insertion and w_T := ⌈12d log R_k/(κ₁ − 1)⌉ for a letter or a home tensor, so that 1 ≤ w_T ≤ ⌈R_k/(1024√d)⌉ in both cases for g small; D_m := (m + 1)(w_T + 2), D′_m := (m + 1)(t_k + 2), λ_T := D_3 + 1 = 4w_T + 9, and λ_K := D′_3 + 1 = 4t_k + 9.
{#BLL.10-0} The collar A_m is the set of the cubes Δ of Q_m of either of two kinds. Δ is of the first kind when d_□(Δ, K) ≥ 2 for every K ∈ 𝒰(F) ∖ 𝒦_m, and either d_□(Δ, T̂_Z) ≤ D_m or d_□(Δ, K′) ≤ D′_m for some K′ ∈ 𝒦_m. Δ is in the inner collar of K when Δ ⊂ K ∈ 𝒰(F) ∖ 𝒦_m and d_□(Δ, E_K) ≤ t_k.
{#BLL.10-1} For g small, t_k + 2 ≤ R_k. So an inner-collar cube of K lies at sup-distance ≥ 2MR_k − 2Mt_k − M from K^c (#BLL.7), and it and its neighbours lie in K.
{#BLL.11} *The zones.* For m ≥ 0, T_m ⊂ T̂_Z^{[D_{m−1}+1]} ∪ T̂_I^{[1]} ∪ ⋃_{K∈𝒦_m}K^{[D′_{m−1}+1]} ∪ ⋃_cc^{[1]}, the last union being over the cores c of the stages < m (#BLL.14), with D_{−1} = D′_{−1} := −1. At m = 0 this holds, as T_0 = T̂_I. Assume it for m − 1.
{#BLL.11-0} A cube of T_m lies in one of five sets. It may lie in T_{m−1}, whose zone lies in that of the stage m, as D_{m−2} ≤ D_{m−1}, D′_{m−2} ≤ D′_{m−1}, and 𝒦_{m−1} ⊂ 𝒦_m. It may lie in E_K ⊂ K for a component K met by G_{m−1}. It may lie in a core of the stage m − 1. It may lie in A_{m−1}, whose first-kind cubes lie in T̂_Z^{[D_{m−1}]} or in K′^{[D′_{m−1}]} with K′ ∈ 𝒦_{m−1}, and whose inner-collar cubes lie in units met by G_{m−1}. Or it may lie in a component met by G_{m−1}. A component met by G_{m−1} lies in T_m, so it lies in T̂_Z or in 𝒦_m.
{#BLL.12} *Entering a unit.* (a) If a cube of T_m lies in a unit K, then K ∈ 𝒦_m. For m = 0 there is no such cube, as T_0 = T̂_I ⊂ T̂ (#BLL.4-1, #BLL.7-7). For m ≥ 1 the cube lies in G_{m−1}, or in a component meeting G_{m−1}, which is K; in both cases G_{m−1} meets K, so K ⊂ T_m.
{#BLL.12-1} (b) If G_m meets K ∈ 𝒰(F) ∖ 𝒦_m, then T_m or a core of the stage m meets K. By (a), T_m ∩ K = ∅. Take a chain of meeting cubes of G_m from T_m to a cube meeting K, and its first cube z meeting K. If z is not the first cube of the chain, its predecessor lies at d_□ ≥ 2 from K. A first-kind cube lies at d_□ ≥ 2 from K. A cube of E_K or of K's inner collar has its neighbours in K (#BLL.7, #BLL.10-1). E_{K″} and the inner collar of K″ ≠ K lie in K″, at d_□ ≥ R_k + 1 from K. So z lies in T_m or in a core.
{#BLL.12-2} (c) If T_m meets K ∈ 𝒰(F) ∖ 𝒦_m, then d_□(K, c) ≤ 2 for a core c of a stage < m. This follows from #BLL.11, since d_□(K, K′) ≥ R_k + 1 > D′_{m−1} + 2 for K′ ∈ 𝒦_m, and d_□(K, T̂) ≥ R_k − 2 > λ_T + 1 ≥ D_{m−1} + 2 (#BLL.4-1) for g small, with T̂_Z ∪ T̂_I ⊂ T̂.

## The Resummation

{#BLL.14} *Stage m.* Let P_{m−1} be a piece of the stages < m, as a function of the parameters of the stages ≥ m; P_{−1} := F_I(U^{old}). RG2 (1.9) gives P_{m−1} = Σ_{S⊂Q_m}∫_{[0,1]^S}∂^SP_{m−1}(s_S, 0 on Q_m ∖ S)ds_S. Write S = S_c ∪ D, with S_c the union of the components of S that meet Q_m ∖ A_m, the cores, and D the rest. {#BLL.16} *The kernel.* An s-point is the configuration U^{old}(s) that the four families build at parameters s on Q_0, …, Q_3 with |s(Δ)| ≤ e^{κ₁}. So 𝐆″_k (#BLL.6-0) keeps the decay e^{−(δ₀/2)d_{2.46}} in PRT2 (2.46)'s distance d_{2.46} (VAR's d, EP #44-2), with the factor e^{O(1)κ₁}, as #BFL.186-0–#BFL.186-0a count.

{#BLL.16-2} The change's kernel from a source on a j′-block y′ to a bond y of the layer j is at most O(1)B₀e^{O(1)κ₁}(L^jη)^{−1}(L^{j′}η)^{−d}e^{−(δ₀/16)ℓ}. ## The Cores

{#BLL.24} *Anchors.* Let c be a core of the stage m of a piece P ≠ 0. Its neighbours in Q_m ∖ c have s = 0: those in A_m lie in S_c^{[1]}, hence outside B^{(m)}, and the others lie outside S_c ∪ B^{(m)}. As c ⊂ G_m (#BLL.14-1) and c ∩ T_m = ∅, c is adjacent to a cube x of (T_m ∪ E) ∩ G_m, its anchor, through a cube r of c, its root. c also contains a cube outside A_m. An anchor outside T_m lies in E_K for some K ∈ 𝒰(F) ∖ 𝒦_m, since E_K ⊂ T_m for the other components K.
{#BLL.25} *Classes.* Each core gets the first class that applies, from five.
- (E) c has an anchor in E_K for a K ∈ 𝒰(F) ∖ 𝒦_m. Then |c| ≥ t_k.
- (T) c has an anchor in T̂_Z^{[D_{m−1}+1]}. Then |c| ≥ w_T.
- (X) c has an anchor in T̂_I^{[1]}. Then the anchor lies in I^{[2]} and the root in I^{[3]}, and |c| ≥ 1. Indeed, the anchor lies in y^{[1]} for a cube y of X(F_I) ⊂ X(F) ∩ (I^{[1]} ∪ Z^{[1]}) (#BLL.7-7): for m ≥ 1, as T̂_Z^{[1]} ⊂ T̂_Z^{[D_{m−1}+1]}; for m = 0, as the anchor lies in T_0 = T̂_I outside T̂_Z, an anchor outside T_0 having the class E (#BLL.24). And y ∉ Z^{[1]}, as otherwise y^{[1]} ⊂ T̂_Z (#BLL.5-0) and the class T applies.
- (B) c has an anchor in K′^{[D′_{m−1}+1]} with K′ ∈ 𝒦_m. Then |c| ≥ t_k.
- (Γ) None of these applies. Then, by #BLL.11, c has an anchor in c′^{[1]} for a core c′ of a stage < m, and |c| ≥ 1.
{#BLL.25-1} *The lengths.* Let y ∈ c lie outside A_m. In the class E, r is adjacent to E_K, so r lies in K's inner collar, and y does not. So y ⊄ K, at d_□ ≥ 2R_k − t_k − 2 from r (#BLL.7), or d_□(y, E_K) > t_k; in both cases |c| ≥ d_□(r, y) + 1 ≥ t_k.
{#BLL.25-2} In the class T, d_□(r, T̂_Z) ≤ D_{m−1} + 2 ≤ λ_T + 1. Suppose first that a cube of c lies at d_□ ≤ 1 from a unit K ∈ 𝒰(F) ∖ 𝒦_m. A chain of c from r to that cube shows |c| ≥ d_□(r, K) ≥ R_k − 3 − λ_T ≥ w_T for g small, as T̂_Z ⊂ T̂ (#BLL.4-1). Otherwise y lies at d_□ ≥ 2 from these units and outside their inner collars, which lie in them. As y is not of the first kind, d_□(y, T̂_Z) > D_m, and |c| ≥ d_□(r, y) + 1 ≥ D_m + 1 − (D_{m−1} + 2) + 1 = w_T + 2.
{#BLL.25-3} In the class B, d_□(r, K′) ≤ D′_{m−1} + 2 with K′ ∈ 𝒦_m. Either a cube of c lies at d_□ ≤ 1 from a unit K ∉ 𝒦_m, and |c| ≥ d_□(r, K) ≥ R_k − 1 − D′_{m−1} ≥ t_k for g small, as d_□(K, K′) ≥ R_k + 1. Or, as in the class T, d_□(y, K′) > D′_m, and |c| ≥ D′_m + 1 − (D′_{m−1} + 2) + 1 = t_k + 2.

## Connectivity and Length

{#BLL.26} *The graph.* For a piece P ≠ 0 with 𝒦(P) = 𝒦, 𝔊 is the graph on the vertex T̂_I, I = I(P), the units of 𝒦, and the cores of P. A core of the stage m is joined to T̂_I, to a unit K, or to an older core c′ when it has an anchor in T̂_Z^{[D_{m−1}+1]} ∪ T̂_I^{[1]}, in K^{[D′_{m−1}+1]} ∪ E_K, or in c′^{[1]}, respectively. A core is joined to every unit it meets. A unit K ∈ 𝒦_{m+1} ∖ 𝒦_m is joined to a core c′ of a stage < m when T_m meets K at a cube of c′^{[1]} (#BLL.12-2).
{#BLL.26-1} 𝔊 is connected. By induction on m, let T̂_I, the units of 𝒦_m, and the cores of the stages < m lie in one component 𝔊_m of 𝔊, as at m = 0, where 𝒦_0 = ∅.
