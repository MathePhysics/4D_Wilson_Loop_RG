# Bałaban Proof: PBG's Averaging Inputs for the Chain's Dressed Average

The properties of the averages that PBG's Sects. A–D take from CMP98 hold for the dressed average of BETA's §7 Setting (#B7.12-1–#B7.14), built from RG1's average (0.4) or (0.12) and RG1's variables (0.11); so PBG's results of Sects. A–D hold for it as PBG states them, on sequences with Ω₀ = T.

The tags {#BD.n} are this file's own. BETA = BETA_FUNCTION_WILSON_LOOPS.md, whose Lemma 7.3 and tags #B… are cited. PBG = CMP 99 (1985) 389–434, CMP96 = PBG's [4], CMP98 = PBG's [5], RG1 = CMP 109 (1987) 249–301; pins are to Balaban\tex\<paper>\parts\pNNN_corrected.tex.

## Statement

- {#BD.1} *Statement.* Let G be a compact Lie group, and take PBG's setting: a sequence {Ω_j} satisfying CMP96 (2.1)–(2.4) with Ω₀ = T (#BD.3a-1a), G-valued U of the class (3.35)–(3.36), and U′U of the class (3.37)–(3.38). In PBG's definitions (3.13)–(3.19), replace CMP98's averaging operations by the chain's dressed average. Q_j(U) is then the linear part of its j-fold composite, C_j(U; ·) the nonlinear part, and Q′_j(U) the composite of (3.19) with the variables (0.11).
  {#BD.1-1} Then: (a) Q_j and Q′_j are local and gauge covariant as in (3.32) and p.414 l.13–15; (b) the analogs of CMP98 (143) and (149) and of PBG (3.58)–(3.59) and (3.79)–(3.81) hold, with analyticity on (3.37), with constants depending on d, L, and G, and for Mα₀ and α₁ below thresholds depending on d, L, and G; (c) hence PBG's Theorems 3.1–3.13, (3.131)–(3.137), and (3.147)–(3.153) hold for these operators as PBG states them, with constants depending on d, L, and G.

## Conventions and Inputs

- {#BD.2} *Conventions.* Level l (0 ≤ l ≤ k) is the lattice of spacing L^lη with RG1's centred blocks (L odd, L ≥ 3). Level-l points are fine points, B^l(y) is the fine block of a level-l point y, and B^{l+1}(y) = ∪_{x∈B(y)}B^l(x) (RG1 (0.3)). For a level-j bond c, S_c := B^j(c₋) ∪ B^j(c₊).
  {#BD.2-1} A level-l configuration e^{iL^lηZ} is described by its normalised logarithm Z (PBG's A at level l; the normalisation of Lemma 7.3, #B7.175). |·| on 𝔤^c is an Ad(G)-invariant Hermitian norm, |Z| is the supremum over the bonds in question, and |Z| as a function means b ↦ |Z(b)|.
  {#BD.2-2} For a level-m bond c′ and level-l fields Y (l ≤ m), (Q″_{m←l}Y)(c′) := Σ L^{−(m−l)d}Y(b′) over the level-l bonds b′ ⊂ B^{m−l}(c′₋) ∪ B^{m−l}(c′₊), where B^{m−l} is the (m−l)-fold block of the level-l lattice. Q″_m := Q″_{m←0} is CMP98 (141), and Q″ := Q″_{l+1←l} is CMP98 (140).
  {#BD.2-3} Q^{(l+1)} is the straight one-step average from level l to level l + 1, Q_{m←l} := Q^{(m)}⋯Q^{(l+1)}, and Q_m := Q_{m←0}. For kernels, |K| ≤ K′ means |KY| ≤ K′|Y| for all Y, that is, a bound on the entries in operator norm.
- {#BD.3} *Inputs from BETA.* (I1) Lemma 7.3, step 1 (#B7.168–#B7.175): one step of the dressed average, from level l to level l + 1, at a background with level-l bond logarithms x and dressed fluctuation logarithms y on the n = n(d, L) bonds R_c of B(c₋) ∪ B(c₊), is (1/i)log Ū̄′_c = Ψ(x, y). Here Ψ is analytic on the complex polydisc P(r₀) = {|x_b|, |y_b| < r₀}, r₀ = r₀(d, L, G), with Ψ(x, 0) = 0, and Ψ does not depend on l, j, k, or c. Put K₀ := sup_{P(r₀)}|Ψ|.
  {#BD.3-1} In normalised logarithms, the linear part of the step at x = 0 is Q^{(l+1)}, an L^∞-contraction (step 4, #B7.185–#B7.187-1). The background of step l + 1 is the raw level-l average Ū^l of the base (step 5, #B7.191; CMP98 (91)).
  {#BD.3-2} (I2) The raw averages are point covariant, Ū^u_c = u(c₋)Ū_cu(c₊)^{−1}, and the variables (0.11) satisfy U(y, x) ↦ u(y)U(y, x)u(x)^{−1} (#B7.15–#B7.16, RG1 (0.6)). (I3) CMP98 (97) holds for the chain's average (#B9.40-3e). At base 1, where R̄ = 1, it says that the raw level-l average of W is (Ū̄^l)^{v_l}, with v_l(x) = w_0(x)⋯w_{l−1}(x) and w_i(x) the dressing factor of (3.113) at level i for the level-i dressed field, exp[iΣ_{x′∈B(x)}L^{−d}(1/i)log Ū̄^i(x, x′)].
  {#BD.3-3} (I4) RG1's mean M is analytic near 1 on G^c, with M(1, …, 1) = 1 and linear part the mean (RG1 (0.8), #B7.24); here it acts on the sets 𝐆(y, x), x ∈ B(y), of at most N ≤ d! contours, one per permutation of the axes (RG1 p.252 l.30–32), each of at most d(L − 1)/2 bonds (#B7.194–#B7.196). ψ(a, b) := (1/i)log(e^{ia}e^{−ib}) has ψ(b, b) = 0 and ψ(0, −b) = b; let r_ψ = r_ψ(G) > 0 and c_ψ = c_ψ(G) be such that ψ is analytic with |∂_aψ − I| ≤ 1 and |ψ(a, −b) − a − b| ≤ c_ψ|a||b| for |a|, |b| ≤ r_ψ (Cauchy's estimate, as ψ(a, −b) − a − b vanishes at a = 0 and at b = 0).
  {#BD.3-3a} So Z_m := ψ(Z_{m−1}, −z_m), Z₀ := 0, which is (1/i)log(e^{iz₁}⋯e^{iz_m}), satisfies 1 + c_ψ|Z_m| ≤ (1 + c_ψ|Z_{m−1}|)(1 + c_ψ|z_m|) ≤ e^{c_ψΣ_{i≤m}|z_i|} while |Z_{m−1}|, |z_m| ≤ r_ψ. Then, for every m, |log(e^{iz₁}⋯e^{iz_m})| ≤ (1 + δ)Σ|z_i| when Σ|z_i| ≤ ε_δ.
  {#BD.3-4} (I5) At U = 1 each one-step dressed average is the straight one-step average with centred blocks, by #B7.22–#B7.27-1, whose argument concerns one step and does not depend on the level. ## Proof

- {#BD.4} *Step 1 (one step).* Write the normalised step as (L^{l+1}η)^{−1}Ψ(L^lηa, L^lηY) = Q^{(l+1)}(a)Y + C^{(l+1)}(a, Y), with Q^{(l+1)}(a) linear in Y. Take L^lη|a|, L^lη|Y| ≤ r₀/4 and c₁ := 16nK₀L^d/r₀².
  {#BD.4-1} (O1) For p ∈ P(r₀/2) and h, k with all entries of norm ≤ 1, z, w ↦ Ψ(p + zh + wk) is analytic and bounded by K₀ on |z|, |w| < r₀/4, so Cauchy's estimate gives |D²Ψ(p)[h, k]| ≤ 16K₀/r₀². Taking h, k supported on one bond each, integrating along the segment from a′ to a, and summing over the n bonds of R_c, |Q^{(l+1)}(a) − Q^{(l+1)}(a′)| ≤ c₁L^{l−1}η|a − a′|Q″, since Q″ gives weight L^{−d} to every bond of R_c. In particular |Q^{(l+1)}(a) − Q^{(l+1)}| ≤ c₁L^{l−1}η|a|Q″, the analog of CMP98 (139).
  {#BD.4-2} (O2) f(z) := Ψ(x, zy) is analytic for |z| < R := r₀/(2|y|) ≥ 2, with |f| ≤ K₀ and f(0) = 0. So |f(1) − f′(0)| ≤ 2K₀R^{−2}, and |C^{(l+1)}(a, Y)| ≤ c₂L^{l−1}η|Y|² with c₂ := 8K₀/r₀².
  {#BD.4-3} (O3) In bond logarithms, dC^{(l+1)}(a, Y)[δY] corresponds to (∂_yΨ(x, y) − ∂_yΨ(x, 0))δy = ∫₀¹D²_yΨ(x, ty)[y, δy]dt. By (O1)'s bound on D²Ψ, |dC^{(l+1)}(a, Y)[δY]| ≤ c₁L^{l−1}η|Y|Q″|δY|, the analog of CMP98 (148).
- {#BD.5} *Step 2 (counting).* (K-a) Q″_{m←i}Q″_{i←l} ≤ 2dQ″_{m←l}. A level-l bond b′ lies in the support of Q″_{i←l}(c″, ·) only if its end points lie in the blocks of c″'s end points. If they lie in one block Y, there are at most 2d such c″ (those with an end point Y); if they lie in two adjacent blocks, there is only the bond joining them. The weights multiply to L^{−(m−l)d}, and the supports nest by #BD.2.
  {#BD.5-1} (K-b) Q^{(l+1)}Q″_{l←i} ≤ 2L^{−1}Q″_{l+1←i}. Q^{(l+1)} gives weight L^{−d−1} to each of the L^{d+1} level-l bonds c″ on the lines of c, all parallel to c. By (K-a)'s count, at most two of these c″ (the two with an end point at the block Y) have a given b′ in their support. So the entries are at most 2L^{−d−1}L^{−(l−i)d} = 2L^{−1}L^{−(l+1−i)d}.
  {#BD.5-2} With Q_{l←l} = Q″_{l←l} = I, (K-b) gives Q_{m←l} ≤ Q″_{m←l} by induction on m. Also Q″ has row sums at most 2d, since B(c₋) ∪ B(c₊) carries at most 2dL^d bonds.
- {#BD.6} *Step 3 (base 1).* Let X be 𝔤^c-valued on S_c with |X| ≤ ρ(L^jη)^{−1}, and W := e^{iηX}.
  {#BD.6-1} (i) With base 1 every background is 1, so by Step 1 the normalised logarithms X^{(l)} of the dressed average Ū̄^l(W; 1) satisfy |X^{(l+1)}| ≤ |X^{(l)}|(1 + c₂L^{l−1}η|X^{(l)}|). Inductively, |X^{(l)}| ≤ 2|X| for l ≤ j once exp(2c₂ρ/(L(L − 1))) ≤ 2 and 2ρ ≤ r₀/4, since Σ_{l<j}L^{l−1}η ≤ L^jη/(L(L − 1)).
  {#BD.6-2} (ii) By (I3), an identity of analytic functions and so valid also for complex W, each raw bond variable of Ū^l(W) is v_l(b₋)e^{iL^lηX^{(l)}_b}v_l(b₊)^{−1}. log w_i(x) is a mean of logarithms of M over contours of at most d(L − 1)/2 level-i bonds, each with logarithm at most 2L^iη|X| by (i). So by (I4), |log w_i| ≤ (1 + δ)²d(L − 1)L^iη|X|, and |log v_l| ≤ (1 + δ)³d(L − 1)Σ_{i<l}L^iη|X| ≤ (1 + δ)³dL^lη|X|.
  {#BD.6-3} Hence the raw bond logarithms are at most (1 + δ)(2 + 2(1 + δ)³d)L^lη|X| ≤ c_βL^lη|X|, with c_β := 3(1 + d), once c_βρ ≤ ε_δ: every sum to which (I4) is applied in (ii) and here is at most c_βρ, as L^iη|X| ≤ ρ/L for i < j and L^lη|X| ≤ ρ for l ≤ j. All of these quantities are analytic in X.
- {#BD.7} *Step 4 (linear part; CMP98 (143)).* Let W₀ = e^{iηX₀} with X₀ as in Step 3, and let a_l be the normalised logarithms of Ū^l(W₀), so |a_l| ≤ c_β|X₀|. Assume c_βρ ≤ r₀/4. By the chain rule and Ψ(x, 0) = 0, Q_{l+1}(W₀) = Q^{(l+1)}(a_l)Q_l(W₀), and similarly for Q_{m←l}(W₀).
  {#BD.7-1} Put κ_l := c₁c_βL^{l−1}η|X₀| and T := 4dc₁c_β/(L² − 2), and assume Tρ ≤ 1. We claim |Q_l(W₀)Z| ≤ Q_l|Z| + τ_lQ″_l|Z| with τ_l := TL^lη|X₀| ≤ 1; this holds for l = 0.
  {#BD.7-2} By (O1), (K-a), (K-b), and Q_l ≤ Q″_l, |Q_{l+1}(W₀)Z| ≤ (Q^{(l+1)} + κ_lQ″)(Q_l + τ_lQ″_l)|Z| ≤ Q_{l+1}|Z| + ((2/L)τ_l + 2dκ_l(1 + τ_l))Q″_{l+1}|Z|. The coefficient is at most L^{l+1}η|X₀|(2T + 4dc₁c_β)/L² = τ_{l+1}.
  {#BD.7-3} The same induction from level l gives |Q_{m←l}(W₀)| ≤ Q_{m←l} + TL^mη|X₀|Q″_{m←l} ≤ 2Q″_{m←l}.
- {#BD.8} *Step 5 (dependence on the base; PBG (3.79)–(3.81)).* Assume the conditions of Steps 3–4 with 2ρ in place of ρ, and let |X₀|, |X₀′| ≤ ρ(L^jη)^{−1}. Then a_l is analytic in X₀ on |X₀| < 2ρ(L^jη)^{−1}, with |a_l| ≤ 2c_βρ(L^jη)^{−1}. Cauchy's estimate on the balls of radius ρ(L^jη)^{−1} around the points of the segment from X₀′ to X₀ gives |a_l(X₀) − a_l(X₀′)| ≤ 2c_β|X₀ − X₀′|.
  {#BD.8-1} Telescoping as in (3.80), Q_j(W₀) − Q_j(W₀′) = Σ_{l<j}Q_{j←l+1}(W₀)[Q^{(l+1)}(a_l) − Q^{(l+1)}(a_l′)]Q_l(W₀′). By #BD.7-3, (O1), and (K-a) twice, the l-th term is at most 2·2·(2d)²·2c₁c_βL^{l−1}η|X₀ − X₀′|Q″_j.
  {#BD.8-2} Summing over l, |(Q_j(W₀) − Q_j(W₀′))Z| ≤ T′L^jη|X₀ − X₀′|Q″_j|Z|, with T′ := 32d²c₁c_β/(L(L − 1)). The transposed kernels obey the same bounds.
- {#BD.9} *Step 6 (nonlinear part; CMP98 (149)).* For X as in Step 3, put Φ_l(X) := X^{(l)} = Q_l(W₀)X + C_l(W₀; X), the normalised logarithms of the l-fold dressed average of e^{iηX}W₀ over the base W₀.
  {#BD.9-1} As in #BD.6-1, with (O1) and the row sums of #BD.5-2, |X^{(l+1)}| ≤ (1 + 2dκ_l)|X^{(l)}| + c₂L^{l−1}η|X^{(l)}|². So |X^{(l)}| ≤ 2|X| once exp((2dc₁c_β + 2c₂)ρ/(L(L − 1))) ≤ 2.
  {#BD.9-2} C_{l+1}(X) = Q^{(l+1)}(a_l)C_l(X) + C^{(l+1)}(a_l, X^{(l)}). Let S := 12dc₁/(L² − 3) and assume Sρ ≤ 1 and 2dc₁c_βρ ≤ 1. We claim |dC_l(X)[δX]| ≤ SL^lη|X|Q″_l|δX|; this holds for l = 0, where C_0 = 0.
  {#BD.9-3} Differentiating, and using (O1), (O3), (K-a), (K-b), Q_l ≤ Q″_l, and Step 4, |dC_{l+1}| ≤ (Q^{(l+1)} + κ_lQ″)SL^lη|X|Q″_l|δX| + c₁L^{l−1}η·2|X|·Q″(Q_l + τ_lQ″_l + SL^lη|X|Q″_l)|δX|. This is at most L^lη|X|[S(2/L + 2dκ_l) + 4dc₁L^{−1}(1 + τ_l + Sρ)]Q″_{l+1}|δX|.
  {#BD.9-4} Since 2dκ_l ≤ 1/L, the bracket is at most (3S + 12dc₁)/L ≤ SL. In CMP98's normalisation (150), where the argument is the field L^jηX on the unit level-j lattice (CMP98 p.040 l.43), this reads |dC_j[δX]| ≤ S|X|Q″_j|δX|, which is (149) with C₃ = S.
  {#BD.9-5} C_j is analytic in X. Its quadratic part C_j^{(2)}, the t² coefficient of C_j(tX), has dC_j^{(2)}(X)[δX] equal to the t coefficient of dC_j(tX)[δX]. Cauchy's formula on |t| = t₀ := ρ(L^jη)^{−1}/|X| bounds it by t₀^{−1}·SL^jηt₀|X|Q″_j|δX|, so C_j^{(2)} obeys the same bound. Integrating along tX, 0 ≤ t ≤ 1, gives |C_j(W₀; X)| ≤ 2dSL^jη|X|².
- {#BD.10} *Step 7 (the transports of Q′; PBG (3.58)–(3.59)).* For x ∈ B^j(y), let x_j = y, x_l ∈ B(x_{l+1}), x_0 = x. Then Q′_j(W)λ(y) = Σ_{x∈B^j(y)}L^{−jd}R(W^{(j)}(y, x))λ(x), with W^{(j)}(y, x) := Π_{l=j−1}^{0}Ū^l(W)(x_{l+1}, x_l), the composite of #B7.14, as in (3.55); R is multiplicative.
  {#BD.10-1} The level-l factor is M over contours of at most d(L − 1)/2 level-l bonds, whose raw logarithms are at most c_βL^lη|X| (#BD.6-3). So by (I4), |log W^{(j)}(y, x)| ≤ (1 + δ)³(d(L − 1)/2)c_βΣ_{l<j}L^lη|X| ≤ c_γL^jη|X|, with c_γ := dc_β, and W^{(j)} is analytic in X.
  {#BD.10-2} Let X and X′ be as in Step 5. Cauchy's estimate on the ball of half the radius gives |log W^{(j)}(X) − log W^{(j)}(X′)| ≤ 2c_γL^jη|X − X′|. As ψ(b, b) = 0 and |∂_aψ − I| ≤ 1 (I4), |log(W^{(j)}(X)(y, x)W^{(j)}(X′)(y, x)^{−1})| ≤ 4c_γL^jη|X − X′| once c_γρ ≤ r_ψ.
- {#BD.11} *Step 8 (covariance and locality).* Let u be G-valued, and transform (U, U′) ↦ (U^u, U′^u) with U′^u_b := u(b₋)U′_bu(b₋)^{−1}, so that (U′U)^u = U′^uU^u.
  {#BD.11-1} By (I2), in the one-step dressed average (PBG p.418 l.6 = CMP98 (89), with the dressing factor (3.113) = CMP98 (110)), written as Ū̄′_c = w₋^{−1}\overline{U′U}_cw₊Ū_c^{−1}, as R̄_c = R(Ū_c) (CMP98 p.027 l.35) acts on w₊ by conjugation (BETA #B7.174-1), the factors transform as w_± ↦ u(c_±)w_±u(c_±)^{−1}, \overline{U′U}_c ↦ u(c₋)\overline{U′U}_cu(c₊)^{−1}, and Ū_c^{−1} ↦ u(c₊)Ū_c^{−1}u(c₋)^{−1}. Hence Ū̄′_c ↦ u(c₋)Ū̄′_cu(c₋)^{−1}.
  {#BD.11-2} The next background Ū transforms as a configuration (I2). So by induction over (91), the composite at c is conjugated by u(c₋), and Q_j(U^u)(Ad(u)Z) = Ad(u(c₋))Q_j(U)Z and C_j(U^u; Ad(u)X) = Ad(u(c₋))C_j(U; X), with Ad(u) acting by Ad(u(b₋)) on fine bonds. This is (3.32) for Q_j. By (I2), W^{(j)}(y, x) ↦ u(y)W^{(j)}(y, x)u(x)^{−1}, which is (3.32) for Q′_j.
  {#BD.11-3} The step at a level-(l + 1) bond c uses the contours of 𝐆(c_±, x), which lie in B(c_±), and the lines [x, x′] joining B(c₋) to B(c₊) (#B7.204–#B7.205), so only level-l bonds b ⊂ B(c₋) ∪ B(c₊), each with S_b ⊂ S_c by the nesting of #BD.2.
