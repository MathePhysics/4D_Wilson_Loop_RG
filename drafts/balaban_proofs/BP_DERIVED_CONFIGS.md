# Bałaban Proof: The Derived Configurations at Critical Points

Bałaban proof: a rederivation of CRE (2.35) and (2.37) (p.261 l.17–21; LFR I p.190 l.20–31) at critical configurations, real and complex, from the configuration's own (2.34) and (2.36). Bałaban prints the conditions, not this identity. Used by the two-loops note (WILSON_IT1_TWO_LOOPS_UNIFORM_BOUND_2026_10_27.md) in its derivation of #6 (BP_TL_GC_TRANSFORMS.md in this folder, #6.16, #6.14). A cited #x not in this file is in that note or one of its BP_TL files, with its file in LABELS_TWO_LOOPS.md. Lengths are in scale-k units, L^kη = 1.

- {#BC.1} *The setting.*
  {#BC.1-0} *The group.* G ⊂ U(N) is a Lie subgroup with Lie algebra 𝔤 of Hermitian matrices, and G = e^{i𝔤} (CMP98 p.020 l.17, l.25–30), compact (#T12.27-1); 𝔤^c := 𝔤 + i𝔤 (#T12.27-1-2-0). |·| is the operator norm, Ad(G)-invariant (#T11.35), and R(g)Y := gYg^{−1} for invertible g (REG p.076 l.40; CMP98 (56), p.027 l.11). A gauge transformation u acts by U^u(b) = u(b₋)U(b)u(b₊)^{−1} (CMP98 p.027 l.2).
  {#BC.1-1} *Lattices and blocks.* For n ≥ 0 let T^{(n)} := L^nη(Z^d + (½, …, ½)) on the torus; then T^{(0)} = T_η, and T^{(n)} ⊂ T_η as L is odd (RG I (0.1), p.251 l.14–23). For y ∈ T^{(n)}, Δ(y), or Δ_n(y) where the scale must be shown, is the closed cube of side L^nη centred at y (p.251 l.23–24), and B^n(y) := Δ(y) ∩ T_η (RG I (0.3), p.252 l.21–27). For a set A ⊂ T_η, A^c := T_η∖A, and A^{(n)} is the set of points of T^{(n)} in A (RG I p.251 l.25–28). A set of points determines the bonds with at least one end-point in it and the plaquettes with at least one corner in it (VAR p.277 l.17).
  {#BC.1-1-1} *Bonds and plaquettes.* An n-bond is a bond ⟨b₋, b₊⟩ of nearest neighbours of T^{(n)}, and an n-plaquette a plaquette of T^{(n)} (RG I p.251 l.27–28); without an index they are those of T_η. A plaquette p_{μν}(x) = ⟨x, x + ηe_μ, x + ηe_μ + ηe_ν, x + ηe_ν⟩, μ < ν, has base point x (REG p.076 l.41–42), and U(∂p) = (∂U)(p) is the product of U around it (RG I p.252 l.8–12). For y ∈ T^{(n)}, n ≥ 1, B(y) := Δ(y) ∩ T^{(n−1)}, RG I's block of order 1, and for x ∈ B(y), 𝐆(y, x) is RG I's family of shortest contours from y to x (RG I (0.3), p.252 l.21–32).
  {#BC.1-2} *Boundaries and distances.* Let ∂A be, for every set A, the set of inner points of A (the points of A with a nearest neighbour in A^c), or for every A the set of outer points (the points of A^c with a nearest neighbour in A), or for every A the set of bonds with one end-point in A and one in A^c. Let dist be the sup, the Euclidean or the ℓ¹ distance.
  {#BC.1-3} *Domains.* A set of class 𝐃_n is the union of a finite connected family of ML^nη-cubes of a division (#BC.2-1, with M of #BC.2-1-3); connected means that any two cubes of the family are joined by a sequence of cubes of the family in which consecutive cubes share a (d − 1)-face (RG I p.257 l.2–11; #T11.27-1).
  {#BC.1-4} *Determining sets.* Let T_η = Ω₀ ⊃ Ω₁ ⊃ ⋯ ⊃ Ω_k with Ω_n ∈ 𝐃_n for n ≥ 1 (CRE p.254 l.46–51), the domains of the step-k determining set 𝐁_k at which BC is used (#BC.8-5). The determining set of this sequence is 𝐁 = ∪_{n=0}^k Γ_n, with Γ₀ = Ω₁^c, Γ_n = Ω_n^{(n)}∖Ω_{n+1}^{(n)} for 0 < n < k, and Γ_k = Ω_k^{(k)} (CRE (2.2), p.255 l.12–21). Its points of index n are those of Γ_n, the block of such a point y is B^n(y), and the layer of index n is Ω_n∖Ω_{n+1}, with Ω_{k+1} := ∅.
  {#BC.1-4-1} *Data and minimal configurations.* The average M_𝐁(U) of a configuration U on T_η is M^n(U) = Ū^n on Γ_n (CRE (2.11), p.256 l.36–41; RG I p.256 l.19), with Ū the chain's average, RG I (0.4) or (0.12) (p.253, p.254 l.2–6; CRE p.243 l.23–24), iterated over the levels as in CMP98 (43) (p.024 l.2–7). VAR's results, and the results of CMP98 and REG that they use, hold for these averages with constants depending on d, L and G (RG I p.254 l.7; BP_REG_DRESSED.md #BR.1-1, #BR.14-1).
  {#BC.1-4-1-0} At a G-valued U the averages are G-valued. In (0.4) (p.253 l.5–6) and (0.12) (p.254 l.4) each logarithm is that of a product near 1 of G-valued factors: transports of U, and in (0.12) contour variables, U(c₋, x) by (0.11) and (0.9) (p.253 l.46, l.30), and U(x′, c₊) as (0.11) for the reversed contours or as the inverse of U(c₊, x′).
  {#BC.1-4-1-0-1} G being a compact Lie subgroup of U(N) (#BC.1-0), A ↦ e^{iA} maps a neighbourhood of 0 in 𝔤 onto one of 1 in G, on which the matrix logarithm inverts it, so (1/i)log of the product lies in 𝔤. Then e^{i·} of a real weighted sum of these lies in G (CMP98 p.020 l.30), and so does its product with U(c). Iterating over the levels keeps this.
  {#BC.1-4-2} For a regular configuration V on 𝐁, U_𝐁(V) is an element of the unique minimal orbit of A^η, RG I (0.2)'s action, the sum over the plaquettes of T_η (p.252 l.7–10), on the regular U with M_𝐁(U) = V (CRE (2.12), p.256 l.43–51), and for complex V in VAR Proposition 9's ball about real regular data it is the analytic continuation (VAR p.305 l.31–36, p.309 l.8–14).
  {#BC.1-4-3} *Uniqueness.* Let 𝐁 meet VAR's standing conditions, V satisfy VAR (7) on 𝐁 with ε₁ ≤ a₁, and B₃ε₁ ≤ ε₀ ≤ a₀. A configuration critical for (5) on the space (6) of 𝐁, V and ε₀ (VAR's sense, p.290 l.39–47) has a critical orbit, as (5) and (6) are invariant under the transformations (4) (p.278 l.16–19, l.32–33) equal to 1 also at the end-points outside Ω₀ of the bonds leaving Ω₀ (#BC.3-1-3-1), and this is the minimal orbit, the unique critical orbit there (VAR Theorem 1, p.279 l.8–15).
  {#BC.1-4-3-1} So for every configuration F of the minimal orbit, U_𝐁(V) among them (#BC.1-4-2), it is F^w for such a transformation w.
  {#BC.1-5} *The domain X.* Let X ∈ 𝐃_m with X ⊂ Ω_{m+1}^c for some m (LFR I (1.63), p.189 l.46–47), and let 1 ≤ p ≤ k (p.190 l.31). 𝐁^X is the determining set of U_{p,X} = U(𝐁_p(X) ∪ {Γ_i}_{i<p}, ·) (CRE p.261 l.10; LFR I p.190 l.18).
- {#BC.2} *Proposition.* If y is a point of 𝐁^X of index n, then B^n(y) ⊂ Ω_n, and B^n(y) lies in a block of 𝐁 of index at least n.
  {#BC.2-1} *Divisions.* The cubes Δ(y), y ∈ T^{(n)}, form the division of the torus with corners at L^nηZ^d (RG I p.251 l.15), and these divisions are nested, as L^{n+1}ηZ^d ⊂ L^nηZ^d. All sets below are unions of cubes of these divisions.
  {#BC.2-1-1} *Lemma (C). Let n ≤ l, let A be a union of cubes of side L^lη of the divisions, and let Δ be a cube of side L^nη of its division. Then either Δ ⊂ A or no point of T_η in Δ lies in A; and Δ ⊂ A if the centre of Δ lies in A.* Proof: Δ lies in a cube Δ′ of side L^lη. A coordinate of a point of T^{(i)}, i ≥ 0, is an odd multiple of L^iη/2, while the faces of Δ′ lie at multiples of L^lη, so no such point lies on a face of Δ′. If Δ′ ⊂ A, then Δ ⊂ A. Otherwise each cube of A is distinct from Δ′ and meets it in faces only, so A contains neither a point of T_η in Δ nor the centre of Δ.
  {#BC.2-1-2} *The domains of 𝐁.* Each Ω_n, 1 ≤ n ≤ k, is a union of ML^nη-cubes, as Ω_n ∈ 𝐃_n (#BC.1-3, #BC.1-4), and so is Ω₀ = T_η.
  {#BC.2-1-3} *Cube sizes.* M and M₂ are powers of L with M₁ < M₂ < M (CRE p.245 l.32–35), and M₁ = K_{M′}M′ (table #T1.5) is a power of L (#T2.5, #T2.15), so M ≥ L²M₁. As M and M₁ are powers of L, the ML^nη-cubes and the M₁L^nη-cubes are the cubes of the divisions with corners at ML^nηZ^d and at M₁L^nηZ^d (#BC.2-1). Hence Ω_n and Ω_{n+1} are unions of M₁L^nη-cubes and of cubes of side L^nη, and by (C) a point y ∈ T^{(n)} lies in Γ_n if and only if B^n(y) ⊂ Ω_n∖Ω_{n+1}.
  {#BC.2-1-4} *Lemma (G). Every block b of side L^nη with b ⊂ Ω_n lies in a block of 𝐁 of index at least n.* Proof: let h ≥ n be the largest index with b ⊂ Ω_h, and let Δ″ be the cube of side L^hη containing b, with centre z. As Ω_h contains the points of b, Δ″ ⊂ Ω_h by (C). As Ω_{h+1} is a union of cubes of side L^hη and does not contain b, no point of T_η in Δ″ lies in Ω_{h+1}, by (C). So B^h(z) ⊂ Ω_h∖Ω_{h+1}: z is a point of 𝐁 of index h, and b ⊂ B^h(z).
  {#BC.2-2} *Localised sets.* Let q ≥ 0. The M₁-cubes of the lattice T_ξ, ξ = L^{−q} (CRE p.256 l.58), are the M₁L^qη-cubes, and the distance 2M₁ in T_ξ is 2M₁L^qη (#T11.27-1, #T11.27-2). For X a union of M₁L^qη-cubes, the localised set 𝐁_q(X) has the domains Ω₀(X) = X ⊃ Ω₁(X) ⊃ ⋯ ⊃ Ω_q(X), the maximal sequence with Ω_n(X) a union of M₁L^nη-cubes and dist(Ω_n(X), Ω_{n−1}(X)^c) ≥ M₁L^nη for 1 ≤ n ≤ q (p.256 l.58–61). The points of 𝐁_q(X) of index n < q are the points of T^{(n)} in Ω_n(X)∖Ω_{n+1}(X), and those of index q are the points of T^{(q)} in Ω_q(X).
  {#BC.2-2-1} *The strip.* Two cubes of a division touch if they are distinct and have a common point. For a union A of M₁L^qη-cubes, A^∼ is A with the M₁L^qη-cubes touching it (p.256 l.62–63, p.245 l.40–43), A^{∼−1} := ((A^c)^∼)^c (p.247 l.49) and A^{∼−2} := (A^{∼−1})^{∼−1}. The strip of X at the layer q is S^q_X := X∖X^{∼−2}.
  {#BC.2-2-2} *Lemma (S). If X is a union of M₁L^qη-cubes and x ∈ S^q_X, then |x − c|_∞ ≤ 2M₁L^qη for some c ∈ T_η∖X.* Proof: let Q be the M₁L^qη-cube containing x. By the definitions, Q ⊂ X, and Q touches a cube of X^c or a cube Q′ ⊂ X that touches a cube of X^c. Touching cubes contain points of T_η at ℓ∞-distance η, and two points of one cube are at ℓ∞-distance at most (M₁L^q − 1)η. So |x − c|_∞ ≤ 2(M₁L^q − 1)η + 2η = 2M₁L^qη.
  {#BC.2-3} *The set 𝐁^X.* The domains Ω_i∖Ω_{i+1}, i < p, of {Γ_i}_{i<p} partition Ω_p^c, and {Γ_i}_{i<p} has no points in Ω_p. The set 𝐁^X is formed by the joining (2.14) (CRE p.257 l.7–16; LFR I p.180 l.46–51), at the scale ξ = L^{−q} of the layer Ω_q∖Ω_{q+1} containing ∂X (CRE p.256 l.57–63, p.257 l.8–11; LFR II p.373 l.17, l.26–27).
  {#BC.2-3-1} *Description.* Let q < p and ∂X ⊂ Ω_q∖Ω_{q+1}. Then X is a union of M₁L^qη-cubes, the distance from ∂X to (∂Ω_q ∪ ∂Ω_{q+1}) ∩ X is at least 2M₁L^qη, and, outside Ω_p, the points of 𝐁^X are the points of {Γ_i}_{i<p} in X∖S^q_X and the points of 𝐁_q(X) in S^q_X, each with its index (CRE (2.14) in the units of #BC.2-2). If ∂X meets several of the domains Ω_i∖Ω_{i+1}, i < p, and Ω_p, the same holds on each of them, with both sets restricted to it (CRE p.257 l.22–26).
  {#BC.2-3-1-1} *Inside Ω_p.* Every point of 𝐁^X is a point of 𝐁_p(X) or of {Γ_i}_{i<p} (CRE p.261 l.10), with its index there, and {Γ_i}_{i<p} has no points in Ω_p (#BC.2-3). So the points of 𝐁^X in Ω_p are points of 𝐁_p(X), of index at most p. If ∂X ⊂ Ω_p, the distance from ∂X to ∂Ω_p ∩ X is at least 2M₁L^pη, (2.14)'s hypothesis at j = p (CRE p.257 l.7–11). By #BC.2-4, the points of 𝐁_q(X) in S^q_X are the points of 𝐁_p(X) there.
  {#BC.2-3-2} *Cases.* By #BC.2-3-1 and #BC.2-3-1-1, a point y of 𝐁^X of index n is (a) a point of Γ_i with i < p and n = i; (b) a point in Ω_p with n ≤ p; or (c) a point of 𝐁_q(X) in S^q_X∖Ω_p with q < p, where either ∂X ⊂ Ω_q∖Ω_{q+1} or y ∈ Ω_q∖Ω_{q+1}. In (c), q ≤ m by #BC.2-3-3, ∂X being non-empty, as (S) gives a point of T_η∖X near y ∈ X. When ∂X ⊂ Ω_p, S^p_X ⊂ Ω_p, by #BC.2-5 with p ≤ m (#BC.2-3-3) if ∂X is non-empty; if ∂X is empty, X = T_η, as X is non-empty and T_η connected, and S^p_X = ∅. So the points of the strip are in (b).
  {#BC.2-3-3} *Lemma. Let q ≤ p. If X meets Ω_q∖Ω_{q+1}, or if ∂X is non-empty, ∂X ⊂ Ω_q and the distance from ∂X to ∂Ω_q ∩ X is at least 2M₁L^qη, then q ≤ m.* Proof: if y ∈ X ∩ (Ω_q∖Ω_{q+1}), then q ≤ m, as X ∩ Ω_{m+1} = ∅.
  {#BC.2-3-3-1} Let ∂X ⊂ Ω_q and suppose q > m. An inner point of ∂X lies in X ∩ Ω_q ⊂ X ∩ Ω_{m+1} = ∅. An outer point a ∉ X has a nearest neighbour b ∈ X; a bond of ∂X is ⟨a, b⟩ with a ∉ X and b ∈ X. In both cases a ∈ Ω_q (for the bond because b ∉ Ω_q and a bond lies in Ω_q when an end-point does, VAR p.277 l.17), and b ∈ X∖Ω_q. So b, or ⟨a, b⟩, is an element of ∂Ω_q ∩ X within distance η of ∂X, contrary to the hypothesis.
  {#BC.2-4} *Lemma. For q ≤ min{p, m}, the points of 𝐁_q(X) and of 𝐁_p(X) in S^q_X coincide and have index at most q.*
  {#BC.2-4-1} *The maximal sequence.* By #BC.2-2, the condition on Ω_n(X) depends only on Ω_{n−1}(X) and n, it holds for a union of cubes when it holds for each cube, and a larger Ω_{n−1}(X) admits every cube that a smaller one admits. So the maximal sequence is unique: Ω₀(X) = X, and Ω_n(X) is the union of all M₁L^nη-cubes in Ω_{n−1}(X) at distance at least M₁L^nη from Ω_{n−1}(X)^c. This defines 𝐁_p(X) for every p ≤ k (LFR I p.190 l.31), and the domains of 𝐁_q(X) are its terms n ≤ q.
  {#BC.2-4-2} *The strip misses Ω_{q+1}(X).* dist(Ω_{q+1}(X), X^c) ≥ M₁L^{q+1}η, as X^c ⊂ Ω_q(X)^c. By (S), each x ∈ S^q_X is within ℓ∞-distance 2M₁L^qη of X^c, hence within distance 2dM₁L^qη, and 2d < L (#T1.3), so within distance below M₁L^{q+1}η. So S^q_X ∩ Ω_{q+1}(X) = ∅.
  {#BC.2-4-3} *Proof of the lemma.* By #BC.2-2 and (C), y ∈ T^{(n)} has index n < p in 𝐁_p(X) if and only if B^n(y) ⊂ Ω_n(X)∖Ω_{n+1}(X), and index p if and only if B^p(y) ⊂ Ω_p(X); the same holds for 𝐁_q(X) with q in place of p. A point has at most one index in each set: for n < n′, B^n(y) ⊂ B^{n′}(y) ⊂ Ω_{n′}(X) ⊂ Ω_{n+1}(X). Let y ∈ S^q_X. For n > q, y ∉ Ω_n(X) by #BC.2-4-2, so y has no index n > q in either set. For n = q, no point of B^q(y) lies in Ω_{q+1}(X), by (C) and #BC.2-4-2. So the two sets give y the same index, and it is at most q.
  {#BC.2-5} *Lemma. Let q ≤ m, ∂X ⊂ Ω_q, and let the distance from ∂X to ∂Ω_q ∩ X be at least 2M₁L^qη. Then S^q_X ⊂ Ω_q.*
  {#BC.2-5-1} *Two touching cubes.* Let x ∈ S^q_X and c be as in (S). Then |x − c|_∞ ≤ 2M₁L^qη < ML^qη, as M ≥ L²M₁ (#BC.2-1-3). X ∈ 𝐃_m is a union of ML^mη-cubes (#T11.27-1), hence of ML^qη-cubes as q ≤ m, and so is Ω_q (#BC.2-1-2). Let W and W_c be the ML^qη-cubes of x and c. Then W ⊂ X and W_c ⊂ X^c, and they touch, as x and c are closer than one side.
  {#BC.2-5-2} *A chain of cubes at a corner.* Let z be a corner of W lying in W_c. Changing one coordinate at a time the position of W relative to z into that of W_c gives cubes P₀ = W, P₁, …, P_t = W_c of side ML^qη, all with the corner z, each sharing a (d − 1)-face with the next. Let y_i be the point of T_η in P_i nearest to z, at ℓ∞-distance η/2 from z; then y_{i−1} and y_i are nearest neighbours. Each P_i lies in X or in X^c, and lies in Ω_q or has no point in Ω_q, by (C).
  {#BC.2-5-3} *The boundary of X.* Let e be least with P_e ⊂ X^c. Then y_{e−1} ∈ X and y_e ∉ X, so ∂X contains y_{e−1} (inner), y_e (outer) or ⟨y_{e−1}, y_e⟩ (bond). As ∂X ⊂ Ω_q, P_{e−1} ⊂ Ω_q (inner), P_e ⊂ Ω_q (outer), or one of them (bond). Suppose W ⊄ Ω_q, and let f ≥ 1 be least with P_f ⊂ Ω_q. Then f ≤ e − 1 (inner) or f ≤ e (outer, bond), so y_{f−1} ∈ X, and y_f ∈ X in the inner case.
  {#BC.2-5-4} *The boundary of Ω_q.* As y_{f−1} ∉ Ω_q and y_f ∈ Ω_q, ∂Ω_q ∩ X contains y_f (inner), y_{f−1} (outer) or ⟨y_{f−1}, y_f⟩ (bond). All y_i lie within ℓ∞-distance η/2 of z, so this element of ∂Ω_q ∩ X and that of ∂X are within ℓ¹-distance dη < 2M₁L^qη (M₁ = K_{M′}M′ ≥ K_{M′} ≥ 12d + 1, #T2.15), contrary to the hypothesis. So W ⊂ Ω_q, and x ∈ Ω_q.
  {#BC.2-6} *Proof of the proposition.* Let y be a point of 𝐁^X of index n, in a case of #BC.2-3-2. In (a), B^n(y) ⊂ Ω_n∖Ω_{n+1}. In (b), Ω_p is a union of cubes of side L^pη and contains y, so B^n(y) ⊂ Ω_p ⊂ Ω_n by (C). In (c), n ≤ q by #BC.2-4, and y ∈ Ω_q by #BC.2-6-1.
  {#BC.2-6-1} If y ∈ Ω_q∖Ω_{q+1}, then y ∈ Ω_q. If ∂X ⊂ Ω_q∖Ω_{q+1}, then q ≤ m (#BC.2-3-2) and the distance hypothesis of #BC.2-3-1 is that of #BC.2-5, so y ∈ S^q_X ⊂ Ω_q. Hence B^n(y) ⊂ Ω_q ⊂ Ω_n by (C), and in each case (G) gives a block of 𝐁 of index at least n containing B^n(y).
- {#BC.3} *Proposition.* Let U and U′ be configurations on T_η at which the averages of #BC.1-4-1 are defined (RG I p.253 l.15), such as regular ones, with U(b) = U′(b) at every bond b with no end-point in X, and M_{𝐁^X}(U) = M_{𝐁^X}(U′). Then M_𝐁(U) = M_𝐁(U′).
  {#BC.3-1} *Data.* A data bond of index n of a determining set is an n-bond with an end-point of index n whose other end-point has index n or n − 1, or, if n = 0, lies outside Ω₀ (#BC.3-1-3); its datum is Ū^n(c), with Ū^0 = U (#BC.1-4-1). Inside Ω₀ these are the two cases of REG (1.30)–(1.31) (p.081 l.33–35, p.082 l.4–10), and both end-points are points of the set, as REG's invariance of 𝔙 under the transformations (1.14), equal to 1 at those points, requires (p.078 l.14–18; #T11.19).
  {#BC.3-1-0} 𝐁^X is the determining set of a sequence X = Ω^X_0 ⊃ Ω^X_1 ⊃ ⋯ ⊃ Ω^X_{m^X}, with B^j(Λ^X_j) = Ω^X_j∖Ω^X_{j+1} for the set Λ^X_j of its points of index j, Ω^X_{m^X+1} := ∅, and m^X, its top index, the largest index of a point of 𝐁^X (VAR p.277 l.18–24), and VAR's results apply to it (CRE p.257 l.11–12, p.256 l.49–51).
  {#BC.3-1-1} That is, {Ω^X_j} meets VAR's standing conditions (p.277 l.11–17) at his block size M₁^{VAR} with his R₁ raised to M′/M₁^{VAR}, VAR's product being R₁M₁ = M′ (table #T2.7-1; BP_JOINS_BLOCK_SIZE.md #4.23-0).
