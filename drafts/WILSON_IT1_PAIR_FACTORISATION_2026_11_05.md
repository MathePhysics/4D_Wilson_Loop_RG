## 0. What the Objects Force {#PFS0}

{#PF0.1} *The quantity is a covariance.* Let K′ be the common stopping step (#307, #PC1.1), W^×_ren(ε) the renormalised pair expectation of #3-1, and W_ren(C_i; ε) := E_ε[W_{χ_i}(C_i)]/κ^{(K′)}_{C_i} the single-loop expectation normalised at K′, with κ^{(K′)}_{C_i} of #366. Then
{#PF0.1-1} W^×_ren(ε) − W_ren(C₁; ε)W_ren(C₂; ε) = Cov_ε(W_{χ₁}(C₁), W_{χ₂}(C₂))/(κ^{(K′)}_{C₁}κ^{(K′)}_{C₂}),
{#PF0.1-2} since both terms on the left have the denominator κ^{(K′)}_{C₁}κ^{(K′)}_{C₂}, and E_ε[XY] − E_ε[X]E_ε[Y] = Cov_ε(X, Y).
  {#PF0.1-2.1} The left side converges as K → ∞: the pair's limit by #PC7.1, the single limits by the Cauchy note run with the common stopping index j in place of j(C_i), as #PC5.1-1 runs it (#ND.6.7-3). So every bound on the right side that is uniform in K holds for the limit.
{#PF0.2} *Physical units.* ℓ_i = p_iL^{j−q_i} step-K′ units is the side ℓ_i^{ph} := p_iL^{−q_i}u (#945, #PC7.2), and D step-K′ units is the physical ℓ^∞ distance D^{ph} := DuL^{−j}. The period N_s is T_ph = N_suL^{−j} (#290). So ℓ_i/D = ℓ_i^{ph}/D^{ph} and ℓ_i/N_s = ℓ_i^{ph}/T_ph, independent of j.
{#PF0.3} *On a fixed torus, D is large only when the loops are small against their distance.* The physical torus is fixed, so D^{ph} ≤ T_ph/2 (#334, D ≤ N_s/2). A bound that decays in D at fixed ℓ_i^{ph} is therefore a bound that decays in D^{ph}/ℓ_i^{ph}. A bound that decays in D^{ph} uniformly in the torus size would be exponential clustering in infinite volume, a mass-gap statement. It is not the target here.
{#PF0.4} *The form of the target.* Nothing in the construction makes the covariance vanish at a fixed ratio ℓ_i^{ph}/D^{ph}. So the bound sought is small in the coupling, with the torus-scale factor ℓ₁ℓ₂/N_s² = ℓ₁^{ph}ℓ₂^{ph}/T_ph² (#PF0.2).
{#PF0.5} *The bound.* For C₁, C₂ as in #332–#332.1 with ℓ₁, ℓ₂ ≤ D, and ḡ ≤ ḡ_ty (#PF4.5), uniformly in K,
{#PF0.5-1} |W^×_ren − W_ren(C₁)W_ren(C₂)| ≤ 4|e^{E^{(1)}_{const,K′} + E^{(2)}_{const,K′}}|ρ_{cv}²ℓ₁ℓ₂/N_s² + 6δ_LFB^{(1)}_WB^{(2)}_W + (the right side of #PF3.2-9a),
{#PF0.5-2} with ρ_{cv} := sup_k(ρ_k + ε_k), ρ_k the activities' amplitude (map #MD2.0.3.35-2), ε_k CRE's small-field threshold (map #MD2.0.2.2), and E^{(i)}_{const,K′} the constant of C_i's dressing on ℓ₀ (#PF4.2-0-1). The first term is §4's bound on the covariance under ℓ₀ (#PF4.6). It does not depend on D, and it is small in the coupling relative to the product of the means (#PF4.6-2).
  {#PF0.5-3} The second term is the large-field labels' share (#PF4.2-0a), with δ_LF of #ND.7.1 and B^{(i)}_W #ND.8.1's B_W for C_i with its coefficient 8.48 and the O(1) of O(C_χρ_{K′}) multiplied by 1 + 2κ_s^{LF} (LF #3.99; #910); it does not decay in D. The third decays exponentially in D, except for the wraps' share, which decays exponentially in N_s (#PF3.2-9b).

## 3. The Cross Part: What Remains {#PFS3}

{#PF3.1} *(CL).* A group long on C_i whose network has no point within r₀ of C_{3−i} is the single-loop long group of C_i, with the same weight (#PF3.1-3e). - {#PF3.1-1} The obstruction (#431): long-ness and membership are properties of the whole term, chosen by the finest admissible partition.

- {#PF3.1-3} *Contour locality with the length.* For every group 𝒢 on C_i, short or long, Y_𝒢 and its weight depend on the contour only through the bonds of C_i in 𝒢's network and through C_i's cyclic set, length P_k(C_i), and base point x₀^{(i)}.

{#PF3.2} *(NF).* Given (CL), the pair's word and the product of the two words have the same far part (#PF3.2-5), and the terms with a near object are small in D (#PF3.2-6–#PF3.2-8). The bound is #PF3.2-9a.
{#PF3.2-3} *Near and far.* Let D ≥ 2r₀, and fix a labelled term ℓ; by (OB) it is the same for the pair and for each loop. An object of C_i at the step k, meaning an insertion with its lineage, a cluster of the step's reference gas, or a local term, is *near* if its network (#416: it contains the domains of the whole lineage) has a point within r₀ of C_{3−i} in step-k units (#348), and *far* otherwise.
  {#PF3.2-3.0} The words are read at K′, so their insertions and clusters are objects at k = K′. Such an object of C_i is also near if its support has a point at distance ≥ (D − 1)/2 from C_i in step-K′ units. Cross objects and the wrap terms of D(k)(ii) (#893) are near.
  {#PF3.2-3.1} A large-field component of scale k, a component of Λ_kᶜ (of Λᶜ at k = 1, D2.6 closure 6.2 *The components*), is *near* if its ℓ^∞ diameter is ≥ R^{nf}_k := (DL^{K′−k} − 2r₀)/2 in step-k units. One that is not near has a *far loop*, the loop farther from it (ties by a fixed order), and its members (#PF3.2-8d2-1) that lie at positions of its far loop are near.
  {#PF3.2-3.2} *Far objects of the two loops are compatible.* Groups are compatible when their arcs are disjoint, their constituent domains are compatible, and no occurrence of one hits a member of the other (#MD2.3.3.6), and a cluster hits an insertion through one of its groups. Arcs on different loops are disjoint, and compatible domains are disjoint with no common wall (#MD2.1.4.18(ii), #MD2.2.4.61, #MD2.2.5.30-3).
    {#PF3.2-3.2-1} The constituent domains and the occurrences' supports lie in the supports (map #MD2.3.4.46-2, #MD2.3.4.40-1). So a C₁-object and a C₂-object at K′ that are incompatible have support points x₁, x₂ with |x₁ − x₂|_∞ ≤ 1 in step-K′ units. Since dist(x₁, C₁) + dist(x₂, C₂) ≥ D − 1, one of them has a support point at distance ≥ (D − 1)/2 from its own loop, and it is near.
{#PF3.2-4} *Far objects coincide.* By (L×) (#413–#415), with (CL) for the long arcs (#PF3.1-3e), the far objects of the pair on C_i are the far objects of the single-loop class of C_i, with the same values, and conversely, since a far object's network has no point within r₀ of C_{3−i}.
{#PF3.2-5} *The far parts factorise.* Restrict each class to its far objects: the families 𝔅 of far insertions, Q over the far clusters, and the far local terms. A far family of the pair is 𝔅₁ ⊔ 𝔅₂ with 𝔅_i on C_i, and every such pair of families is compatible (#PF3.2-3.2), so the far families of the pair are those of the product. w(𝔅) = w₁(𝔅₁)w₂(𝔅₂) (#364).
  {#PF3.2-5.1} A far cluster of C_i hits no far insertion of C_{3−i} (#PF3.2-3.2). So Q^far(𝔅) = Q^{far,(1)}(𝔅₁)Q^{far,(2)}(𝔅₂), and the far local terms of the pair are the sums of the two loops' far local terms. Hence 𝒲̂^{×,far} = 𝒲̂^{(1),far}𝒲̂^{(2),far} on every labelled term.
{#PF3.2-6} *The near parts are small.* Let N^× and N^{(i)} be the masses of the near objects of the pair and of the class of C_i at K′, in the weights of the product forms (#923; the continuum note's (5.3)), with |φ| for the clusters and the local terms. Hence
