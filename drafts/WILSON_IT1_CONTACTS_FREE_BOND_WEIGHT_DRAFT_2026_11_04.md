## 1. The Weight (Definition)

- Let Λ be the large-field region of the T-step that evaluates at a point. Its layers Λ_j have the weights W_j and W_ms := max_jW_j of LF #A.11.42, with t the distance to the faces, edges, and vertices of Λ_{j+1} at the interface Λ_j/Λ_{j+1}, and t₃ that to the contacts of codimension ≥ 2 between the local components of Λ_j∖Λ_{j+1} that lie in ∂Λ_{j+1}, the pinches of Ω_j next to Λ_{j−1}, outside R_j, not counted, both in the units of L^j.
- A *free bond* is a bond of Λ's VAR problem that is not a data bond of Λ₀ (LF #A.10). At a free bond b, put w_j(b) := max(1, (L^{1/2}/t₃^{(j)}(b))^{1−κ}), with t₃^{(j)} the t₃ of W_j, and w_c(b) := max_jw_j(b), the contact weight. Here κ = max(1 − α/2, 1 − ν) < 1 is the exponent of note #5.4.
- *The stratum weight.* Let γ := α(d)/2 be the passage's exponent (the passage draft, §7), s̄ := max(s, ρ₀) with s the fine distance from b to the strata of codimension ≥ 3 of the interface Λ₀/Λ₁ (the passage's ∂C, the passage draft, §3 (b₀)), r_S := min(κ, 1 − γ), and a_S := max(½, ½(1 + (κ − γ)/r_S)). Then r_S > κ − γ, since γ > 0 and κ < 1, so a_S < 1. Put w_S(b) := max(1, (L^{a_S}/s̄(b))^{r_S}).
- Put w(b) := max(w_c(b), w_S(b)) at a free bond, and w := 1 at every other point. κ, γ, r_S, and a_S depend on d only.
- *The size of w.* Since t₃^{(j)}, s̄ ≥ ρ₀ ≥ 1, w_c ≤ L^{(1−κ)/2} and w_S ≤ L^{a_Sr_S}. So 1 ≤ w ≤ L^{c_w}, with c_w := max((1−κ)/2, a_Sr_S) < 1, since a_S < 1 and r_S ≤ κ < 1.
- On the layers n ≤ k₀, which contain Λ (note #5.7), the phase room of 𝒟^λ_k is λw(b) times its unit, in place of λ times it. On the layers n > k₀ the phase room is unchanged. The gradient and current rooms carry instead the factor μ^{(k)}_n of §4a, on every layer.
- *w varies slowly among the free bonds.* t₃^{(j)} is 1-Lipschitz (LF #A.11.42) and ≥ ρ₀ = C(d)log L ≥ 2 (note #5.4; LF #0.1.18), for L ≥ L₀(d). So for two free bonds b, b′ that share a site, t₃^{(j)}(b′) ≥ (1 − 1/ρ₀)t₃^{(j)}(b), and w_c(b′) ≤ (1 − 1/ρ₀)^{−(1−κ)}w_c(b) ≤ 2w_c(b). Likewise s̄ is 1-Lipschitz and ≥ ρ₀, so w_S(b′) ≤ (1 − 1/ρ₀)^{−r_S}w_S(b) ≤ 2w_S(b). Hence w(b′) ≤ 2w(b).
