## 0. Verdict

V3's main theorem, as stated, does not give an iterating bound.

- The theorem holds for every n under (H3), at fixed g₀. Its bounds are small only for n_C ≲ K·polylog(K) fine bonds when g₀ is matched as in §1 (log g₀⁻² = O(log K)).
- Each n_C-dependent bound is absolute, or of the form e^{n_C·rate} − 1 with rate ≥ e^{−C(log K)^{2p₀}}.
- A loop of fixed physical size has n_C ≍ L^K. At that length every error bound except those on the torus and corner–corner parts of E° exceeds |κ_C| already at the first step; the bounds on E_bdry and on the near-history part of E_def exceed 1 (§4). These are bounds, not the terms; the main term has no lower bound (Remark main-regime (3)).
- That sharpening V3's estimates cannot give an iterating bound is not shown. It would need a lower bound, for example that the history probability of A_covᶜ does not vanish at n_C ≍ L^K; §4(5) records only that it is not shown to be rare.
- The proposed route is a statement of a different kind: an expansion of the loop as a line defect at each scale, in which the divergent perimeter and corner parts are identified exactly rather than bounded. This is a design choice.
- Expectation, not shown: V3's flat-region calculations of δm^σ and z_c are the leading first-step coefficients of such an expansion. They depend on the cutoff prescription σ and the radius r (Thm (i)), and the sign of δm^σ is open (Remark main-regime (3)).
- The Cauchy form does not remove IT-1 to IT-3 (§6).

## 1. Regime and scales

- **Notation.** In this note ℓ is the physical side of the loop and K the number of RG steps. They are not V3's ℓ = LMR₁ and K = 256. V3's constant C_K = 4(4·256·LMR₁+9)⁴ (L122) is written C_V here.
- **Lattice and loop.** Take physical unit length and lattice spacing ε = L^{−K}, with a loop of fixed physical side ℓ. The first step sees n_C ≍ 4ℓL^K fine bonds. Step j sees n_{C,j} ≍ 4ℓL^{K−j+1} bonds of the lattice of spacing L^{j−1}ε.
- **Coupling.** RG I (0.18) (p. 255, read) is the first-step relation 1/g₁² = 1/g₀² − β₁(g₀). The general step 1/g_j² = 1/g_{j−1}² − β_j(g_{j−1}) is RG I (0.20) (p. 256, read: 1/g_k² = 1/g_{k+1}² + β_{k+1}(g_k), with k + 1 = j), which CRE Thm 1 cites as "(0.18), (0.20) [I]". The indexing β_j(g_{j−1}) agrees with CRE (2.43).
  - Summing needs three inputs: (i) g_K = g_phys (the matching, RG I Thm 2; accepted, not re-read); (ii) every g_{j−1} in the domain [0,γ] of β_j (p. 255), which is CRE Thm 1's hypothesis (I.0.33), so g_phys ∈ [0,γ] in particular; (iii) |β_j| ≤ B. Then 1/g₀² ≤ 1/g_phys² + BK, hence log g₀⁻² = O(log K).
  - The same inequality follows from the first inequality of CRE (2.6), g_n ≤ (1+g_n²β′(n−m))^{1/2}g_m, as quoted in V3 Remark next-step (3)(b),(d): m = 0, n = K gives 1/g₀² ≤ 1/g_K² + β′K.
  - For the counts in §4 this suffices as far as the displayed bounds go. Two caveats. (a) Applying V3's theorem at the matched coupling needs g₀ below V3's threshold (H2), an upper bound on g₀; this comes from asymptotic freedom or from the regime (I.0.33), not from |β| ≤ B. (b) The counts in §4(4), (6), (7) compare V3's upper bounds on δ^I_q, ρ and ε̄_ph with |κ_C|; they concern the displayed bounds, not lower bounds on the quantities themselves.
  - Asymptotic freedom (β_j ≥ β₀ > 0) is needed for g₀² ≍ 1/K, which gives τ² ≍ K⁻¹(log K)^{6p₀}.
- **V3 scales (L122).**
  - τ = C[g₀(1+r)³ + e^{−cr²}] with r = A₁(log g₀⁻²)^{p₀};
  - π_* = min(p₀(g₀), p₀(g₁)), so π_* ≤ p₀(g₀) = A₀(log g₀⁻²)^{p₀}; this upper bound is the one the counts in §4 use. The lower bound π_*² ≥ A₀²2^{−2p₀}(log g₀⁻²)^{2p₀} is from Remark main-regime (1), not L122;
  - ρ = O(g₀²(1+r)⁴) (an upper bound only);
  - ε₁ = g₁p₀(g₁).
- **The count that decides.** For fixed p₀, (log K)^{2p₀} = o(K). So L^K·e^{−c(log K)^{2p₀}} → ∞: per-bond weights that are only polylog-exponentially small lose against the bond count.

## 2. (i) Where V3 uses that the input is the Wilson action

- **Density.** The input is the Wilson density at g₀ on a torus of (H3). It enters through the bare expectation 𝔼_ε (L122) and the bare weight ρ₀ dU (L2192–2250); (H1)–(H4) (L124) fix the group, the threshold, the torus and Bałaban's inputs.
- **First-step structure.** (B1)–(B4), L309–313, are CRE's first-step statements (1.1)–(1.25), taken "as stated there, except where an item records a reading, a derivation or a proof" (L300–314). There is one small-field region Ω₁, one large-fluctuation region R₁ and the filled region Λ₁ they determine (L122): the large-field data of one step, and no history of earlier scales.
- **Coefficients.** The flat reference integrals N^σ, D^σ (eq:marginal-literal-ratio) and the perimeter and corner coefficients (L1174, L1517) use the first-step covariance on a flat background at g₀.
- **Observable.** The observable is the bare W_χ(C;U), a trace of a product of link variables, not a dressed loop.
- **Thresholds.** The threshold arithmetic (App. thresholds, L3965) uses CRE (2.6) for the first step only.
- **The general step in CRE (read).**
  - Theorem 1 (p. 262): if the coupling constants satisfy (I.0.33), the densities ρ_k generated by RT from ρ₀ = exp[−(1/g₀²)A − E] satisfy the inductive assumptions.
  - Theorem 2 (p. 263, (2.43)–(2.44)) gives localised bounds on the field-dependent, coupling-subtracted parts of E^{(j)} and R^{(j)}, for regular U_k only; (2.45)–(2.46) are stated after it as consequences. The sums run over the scales n = j, …, k, the sets Γ_n and the regions Ω_n and Λ_j^0. Γ_n is defined at CRE p. 255, (2.2): Γ₀ = Ω₁ᶜ, Γ_j = Ω_j^{(j)}∖Ω_{j+1}^{(j)} for j = 1, …, k−1, Γ_k = Ω_k^{(k)}; their union is the determining set of the domain sequence. For j < k, Γ_j is the part of the scale-j small-field domain that is not small-field at scale j+1; Γ_k is the last small-field domain.
  - So the step-k density is a multiscale object.
- **Differences that matter for the loop at step k.**
  - The loop sees the large-field regions of all earlier scales, not just the labels of one step.
  - Step k acts on ρ_{k−1}: the action is A at g_{k−1} plus the E^{(i)} and R^{(i)} terms for i ≤ k−1, and δm_k and z_k must be computed with them.
  - The observable is the output of step k−1 (§3), not a bare loop.
- **(B1)–(B4) at step k:** not located (IT-4). V3 Remark next-step (3)(b) records that CRE Sect. 2 leaves the T-operations undefined (p. 254) and defers them to Sect. 3 and later papers.

## 3. (ii) Is the output in the input class?

The output pair is (κ⁻¹N₁, ρ₁), with W₁(C;V) = O_V[J_ξ[1](L⁽⁰⁾_ξ + H_ξ)]/O_V[J_ξ[1]] and L⁽⁰⁾_ξ = κ_C e^{E°_ξ} L_ξ(C;V) on A_cov (L2192–2251). Its parts:

- **ρ₁.** V3's ρ₁ is the V-marginal of the pushforward of ρ₀ dU under V3's global average (L2192–2250). CRE Thm 1 (accepted) puts the densities generated by RT in Bałaban's class, and R preserves integrals only (LFR I p. 200). At step 1 the two coincide under the readings R-a and R-b of IT-1 note §3c. After step 1 this is not shown (IT-2(d)).
- **The leading loop L_ξ(C;V).**
  - It is the loop of the fine background field U₁ = U₁(V,V₀) of Thm (i), W_χ(C;U₁), not W_χ(C̄;V) (L2250: "The first step does not require replacing L_ξ(C;V) by W_χ(C̄;V)"). Its definition is (retained-loop-insertion).
  - Put X_{b'} := V_{b'}⁻¹·(the product of U₁ along the L fine bonds of the coarse bond b'). Then L_ξ = tr_χ ∏_{b'} V_{b'}X_{b'}: a coarse loop with group-valued insertions near 1 on A_cov. Locality would need decay of the dependence of U₁ on V, on V₀ and on the region labels away from b' (IT-2(a)).
  - The block constraint fixes a mean over several fine contours (RG I (0.4), p. 253; cf. Lemma H-2a, whose L on-axis contours each carry weight L^{1−d}). It does not force the straight contour to carry the mean, so X_{b'} = 1 is not implied.
  - An insertion that is central and within δ_G := min{‖z−1‖ : z ∈ Z(G)∖{1}} of 1 (in the fixed realisation G ⊂ U(N); vacuous for trivial centre) is 1, because Z(G) is finite for compact simple G.
  - The coarse-loop form "a coarse Wilson loop times the exponential of a sum over the coarse bonds" (V3 Remark next-step (3)(a)) would follow from X_{b'} = 1, or more generally from gauge-trivial insertions V_{b'}X_{b'} = g_xV_{b'}g_y⁻¹ with g near 1 at the coarse endpoints x, y of b', covariant in V. Then ∏V_{b'}X_{b'} = g_{x₀}(∏V_{b'})g_{x₀}⁻¹ and tr_χ gives W_χ(C̄;V) exactly. X = 1 is sufficient, not necessary.
  - Neither is known, so the class is taken with matrix-valued covariant insertions, and the step must map that class into itself. This is a safe choice, not a necessity.
- **E°_ξ.** It depends only on the discrete labels of the history (L2224), through the carrier I = Λ₁* (L122), and on C_n and T; the history ξ = (Ω₁,R₁,V,V₀,A_e) also contains continuous data, which E°_ξ does not see. It is a number for each label, not a local function of V. It can enter the next step only after it is localised and matched to the domain-sequence indexing of ρ₁ (IT-2(b)). Not done.
- **H_ξ (eq:retained-source-correction, L2224).** H_ξ = J_ξ[1]⁻¹∫[W_χ(C;U_ξ(a)) − L⁽⁰⁾_ξ(C;V)]…, the whole difference between the fluctuating loop and its leading term.
  - Pointwise, V3 asserts only |H_ξ| ≤ 1 + B_C. Its smallness is shown only after the history average, through E_def and E_bdry. That nothing better holds pointwise is not shown either.
  - The next step needs pointwise local bounds, so the output is not shown to be in any class that has them (IT-2).
- **Conclusion (ii): not shown.** A sufficient target class at step k (its necessity or minimality is not shown) is:
  - ρ_k in Bałaban's class, with the identification of IT-2(d);
  - times a loop on the step-k lattice with local covariant insertions along the contour (a coarse loop times scalar activities is a smaller class, not excluded);
  - times the exponential of contour-local scalar activities, including the large-field polymers that touch the contour (needed only if such polymers touch a contour of n_C ≍ L^K bonds with non-vanishing probability, which is the lower bound of §0);
  - analytic on the small-field chart and gauge covariant (V3 Remark next-step (3)(a));
  - indexed by CRE's domain sequences (p. 254);
  - with the action of the R-operation on the numerator included (V3 Remark next-step (3)(g));
  - with all bounds relative to the leading coefficient.
  
  V3's output is not shown to lie in this class, and no step is shown to preserve it.

## 4. (iii) The error terms along the flow

For each term: its form at step 1 (main theorem L126–158, L2849, L2966), then count × size at the first step for n_C ≍ 4ℓL^K, then its class.

- **(1) Perimeter factor e^{−δm^σP(C)}.**
  - Form: an exact number (eq:marginal-square-mass).
  - Count × size: |δm^σ|P(C) ≲ n_Cτ² ≲ L^K K⁻¹(log K)^{6p₀} (Remark main-regime (1): |δm^σ|P(C_n) ≤ 4Cnτ²). This is an upper bound only: the sign of δm^σ and a lower bound are open (Remark main-regime (3)).
  - Class: **absorb exactly.** Done at step 1; missing for j ≥ 2 (IT-3).
- **(2) Corner factor (z_c^σ)⁴.**
  - Form: an exact number (eq:corner-square-factor), with |ζ| ≤ Cτ².
  - Count × size: along the flow, Σ_j |ζ_j| can be as large as C·Σ_j τ_j² ≍ Σ_{i≤K} (log i)^{6p₀}/i ≍ (log K)^{6p₀+1}.
    - This count presupposes a step-j bound |ζ_j| ≤ Cτ_j², which is not proved for j ≥ 2.
    - It also presupposes g_j² ≍ 1/(K−j+1) at every step, i.e. asymptotic freedom along the whole flow. That is more than §1 uses.
    - The factor (log K)^{6p₀} comes from V3's τ, through r = A₁(log g₀⁻²)^{p₀}. It is not a known cusp scale.
  - Class: **absorb exactly at every step.** A corner remainder of order τ_j² per step that is not identified exactly is not summable. A remainder of O(τ_j²g_j^δ) with δ > 0 is summable.
- **(3) E°: torus and corner–corner parts.**
  - Form: 4n e^{−cι_T} and (n+1)e^{−cn}, times Cτ².
  - Count × size: at step j, n_j ≍ ℓL^{K−j}, so Σ_j τ_j²(n_j+1)e^{−cn_j} is dominated by the last O(1) steps. The torus part is dominated in the same way when the torus is large compared with the loop.
  - Class: **summable.** This covers the torus and corner–corner terms only. The boundary-distance part, the 4n terms e^{−cd_j}, is not in this class: d_j depends on the history through the carrier Λ₁*, not on n_j, and it belongs to (4).
- **(4) Carrier part of E° and the remainder e^{n_Cδ^I_q} − 1 (Thm (iii)).**
  - Form: the 4n boundary terms e^{−cd_j} of E°_ξ, each at most one, are perimeter-proportional and history-dependent. V3 L158: "in history average the exponential of |E°_ξ| carries the perimeter-rate factor e^{n_Cδ^I_q} of (iii)". The rate is δ^I_q ≤ exp[−c_qπ_*²/(2C_K)] ≍ exp[−c(log K)^{2p₀−4𝔯}], with 2p₀ − 4𝔯 ≥ 6𝔯 ≥ 12 (p₀ ≥ 5𝔯 ≥ 10).
  - Count × size: L^K·exp[−C(log K)^{2p₀−4𝔯}] → ∞, because (log K)^a = o(K) for fixed a. The exponent is two-sided, by C_K ≥ c(log g₁⁻²)^{4𝔯} and π_* ≤ p₀(g₀).
  - The same count holds for the rate δ^♯_q of Corollary main-factorisation, which V3 calls the factorisation remainder (Remark next-step (3)(e)).
  - Class: perimeter-proportional, so it must be absorbed exactly. **Fatal as a bound** (IT-1; the first-step construction is IT-1 note §3b).
- **(5) E_bdry and the near-history part of E_def.**
  - Form: absolute bounds C n_C e^{−cπ_*²}.
  - Count × size: L^K e^{−c(log K)^{2p₀}} → ∞. The bounds exceed 1 before any comparison with |κ|.
  - What they pay for: E_bdry is the exterior-response and uncovered term (L122); its uncovered part is the exclusion A_cov of histories with contour bonds outside Ω₁ (L2447). The near-history part of E_def is the cost of the near event N_b, a large-field region within b of Γ_C (L2842; Remark c5-5-11 (3)). At n_C ≍ L^K neither exclusion is shown to be rare.
  - Class: **fatal as stated.** The large-field polymers that touch the contour must be expanded (IT-1).
- **(6) Far-history part of E_def.**
  - Form: e^x − 1, relative to |κ_C| (Remark next-step (3)(c)), with x = C_χ n_C ρ(ε₁ + e^{−cb/(LM)}).
  - Count × size: V3's bound gives ρε₁ ≲ K^{−3/2}(log K)^{5p₀} per bond, so x ≲ L^K K^{−3/2}(log K)^{5p₀}. V3 (3)(c) calls the term "of order x"; that is all "fatal as a bound" needs.
  - The relative bound also carries the factor e^{Cτ²(2+1/c)+n_Cδ^I_q} (Remark main-regime (1)), which is covered by (4).
  - Class: perimeter-proportional, to be identified as a perimeter-local correction. **Fatal as a bound.**
- **(7) Family-sum factor e^{n_C ε̄_ph} (L2966).**
  - Form: ε̄_ph = O(g₀²(1+r)⁴) per fine bond. It multiplies the gains e^{−μ'(b−1)/4} and e^{−c_qϑ²π_*²/2} (L2849).
  - Count × size: the bound is small only while n_C ε̄_ph stays below those exponents, that is n_C ≲ (log K)^{𝔯}/ε̄_ph or (log K)^{2p₀}/ε̄_ph, both ≍ K·polylog(K).
  - The first-order prefactor C₂ε̄_t multiplies n_C N_lat(μ'/2) e^{−μ'(b−1)/2}, which has the same count problem as (5).
  - Class: **fatal as a bound at n_C ≍ L^K.** This is the place V3 itself flags at L2849.
- **(8) Endpoint.**
  - Form: the leading expectation of the one-plaquette loop at the last step, 1 + O(τ² + L²ε₁ + …) (Remark next-step (3)(f)).
  - Count: last step only, O(1).
  - Class: **not an iteration obstruction.** In the Cauchy form its size is not needed, only its Lipschitz dependence on the density (LIP-1).
- **(9) R-operation terms (Remark next-step (3)(g)).** Not counted, because they belong to the general step (IT-4).

**Range of validity.** By (4)–(7), V3's bounds are small only for n_C ≪ min{e^{cπ_*²}, e^{c_qπ_*²/(2C_K)}, (ρε₁)⁻¹, (log g₀⁻²)^{𝔯}/ε̄_ph}.

- Among these four the last binds, and it is of order K·polylog(K).
- V3 itself gives tighter ranges, also of order K·polylog(K):
  - n ≲ π_*²/τ² ≍ K(log K)^{−4p₀}, for the absolute terms relative to |κ| when δm^σ > 0 (Remark main-regime (1));
  - n ≲ τ^{−2} ≍ K(log K)^{−6p₀}, from the factor e^{Cτ²(9n+1)} of Corollary c5-7-4(a) (Remark main-regime (2)).
- In physical units such a loop has side ≲ K·polylog(K)·L^{−K} → 0.
- Conjecture conj:iteration (L3242) fits this. It is a fixed-k statement (g₀ < g_it(k), with τ and π_* at the bare coupling), so it is about loops of fixed lattice size.
- For k = K − O(1), its bound C(k+2)τ² ≍ (log K)^{6p₀} does not tend to 0. Its term n_Ce^{−cπ_*²} of R already diverges there, which is the stronger point.
- Its use of τ(g₀) at all steps fails at the last steps: there g_j is the coupling at the physical scale, while CRE (2.6) gives g_j² ≤ 2g₀² only as long as g_j²β'j ≤ 1.

## 5. Correction to my answer of 20:07

At 20:07 I said that V3's size-independent O(τ²) error per step sums along the flow to (log K)^{6p₀+1}, and I gave that as the main reason the bound does not iterate. That was wrong on two counts.

- **The τ² term is not size-independent.** The term τ²(2+1/c) in Theorem (iii) bounds the torus and corner–corner terms 4ne^{−cι_T} + (n+1)e^{−cn} (V3 L158). The bounds 4ne^{−cι_T} ≤ 1 and (n+1)e^{−cn} ≤ 1/c give 1 + 1/c; V3 states 2 + 1/c.
  - (n_j+1)e^{−cn_j} decays in n_j.
  - 4n_je^{−cι_{T,j}} decays because ι_{T,j} grows like L^{K−j} for a fixed physical torus; at a fixed torus it would grow with n.
  - So along the flow both are dominated by the last O(1) steps and are summable (§4(3)).
- **The (log K)^{6p₀+1} count has a different target.** It applies only to corner remainders of order τ_j² per step that are not identified exactly (§4(2)). The corner factor of the first step is exact.

The real obstruction is the perimeter count of §4(4)–(7): n_C ≍ L^K fine bonds against per-bond weights that are only polylog-exponentially small, which V3 never identifies exactly. It is stronger than what I said at 20:07, because it already bites at the first step, not only after many steps.

## 6. What the Cauchy form changes

The target is |E_{K+1}[W_ren(C)] − E_K[W_ren(C)]| ≤ s_K with Σ_K s_K < ∞, for the renormalised loop W_ren = W/(e^{−Σ_j δm_j P_j} ∏_j z_j⁴), at matched bare couplings (RG I Thm 2; accepted).

- **It removes** the size of the endpoint (§4(8)). That is replaced by Lipschitz dependence of the remaining flow on the density (LIP-1).
- **It does not remove IT-1 to IT-3.** The two expectations differ by the finest step, applied to a loop of n_C ≍ L^{K+1} bonds, so §4(4)–(7) enter the difference at full count.
- **Creutz-type ratios help only partly.** In W(R,T)W(R−1,T−1)/(W(R,T−1)W(R−1,T)) the perimeters agree, 4(R+T) − 4 on both sides, direction by direction (4R − 2 bonds along e₁ and 4T − 2 along e₂). Both sides have eight convex corners.
  - So exactly perimeter-local and corner-local factors cancel. What remains is the corner–corner terms of order e^{−c·min side}, and the torus terms 4ne^{−cι_T}, which differ between the rectangles.
  - V3 treats squares C_n only (L122), so rectangles need an extension of V3.
  - This removes the need to know δm_j and z_j.
  - It does not remove the need to prove that the divergent parts are exactly perimeter-local and corner-local, which is IT-1.

## 7. What an iterating statement needs, in order

- **D1 (first step, uniform in n).** Weakest form needed by D2 and D3. For the squares C_n of V3 (L122), and for the rectangles if the Creutz route of §6 is used, the step-1 output equals κ_C times a coarse loop with local covariant insertions, times exp(Σ_{b'} a_{b'}(V)) times a large-field part attached to C. These contours are unions of coarse-bond traversals at every scale, which is what "coarse loop" needs. The a_{b'} and the large-field activities must be bounded by local quantities, uniformly in n. Whether the large-field part is exponentiated or kept as a polymer sum is fixed by the IT-1 construction. This is IT-1 and IT-2 at the first step. D1 is necessary, and it fixes the class for D2.
- **D2.** The class of D1 is preserved by the general step, with the coefficients of IT-3, and the remainders after exact absorption are summable along the flow:
  - perimeter-proportional remainders are zero, because they are absorbed;
  - corner remainders are O(τ_j²g_j^δ);
  - the non-scalar parts of the insertions, of which there are about 4ℓL^{K−j+1} at step j, enter after the count: either their contour sum at step j is summable in j uniformly in K, or their perimeter-proportional effect is absorbed into δm_{j+1} and the rest is summable after the count.
- **D3.** The Cauchy comparison of §6, using LIP-1.

**Parked items.** H-2b (f′), the join R-S1(d), the LIP-1 R-terms and a review of Lemma H-2a are first-step Lipschitz items. They are needed for D3, not for D1, and stay parked.

**Rejected (21 Oct, 21:09): a loop of the block field instead of the bare loop.** This is not a route, for three reasons.
- It replaces the Wilson loop by a smeared observable. That sidesteps the perimeter and corner renormalisation, which is where §4 fails, without controlling it.
- It is not easier. Its expectation is an integral against the law of the block variable V_k, and ρ_k is not that law. LFR I (0.3)–(0.4), p.176: the operation R replaces large-field expressions "in such a way, that integrals of the densities are unchanged", and ∫(Rρ) = ∫ρ is the property claimed. LFR I p.200: the density obtained "may not be equal to ρ_k, but is equivalent to it, in the sense that they have equal integrals". So a block-field loop also has to be inserted into the integrand and carried through each R-operation, as the bare loop is (D1–D3); it cannot be read off from ρ_k.
- Without IT-1 there is no way back from it to the bare loop.
