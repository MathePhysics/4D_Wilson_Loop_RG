- The pair observable W_{χ₁}(C₁;U)W_{χ₂}(C₂;U) is the normalised trace of π₁(Hol_UC₁) ⊗ π₂(Hol_UC₂) on V_{π₁} ⊗ V_{π₂} (V4 eq:two-loop-observable). It is a product of two closed words, not the trace of one holonomy.
- The stopping step is common to both loops: K′ = K − j, with j the least j ≥ max(q₁, q₂, q₁₂) + q̄(j) (map l.193; that j exists and does not depend on ε, l.1093(i)).
  - So the corners of both loops are period points, and every step is commensurate for both.
  - At K′, C_i is a fixed contour C_i^{(K′)} with side ℓ_i = p_iL^{j−q_i} bonds.
  - D ≥ 1 is the ℓ^∞ distance between C₁ and C₂ in step-K′ units.
  - In step-k units the sides are ℓ_iL^{K′−k}, and every traversal of C_i lies at distance ≥ DL^{K′−k} from C_{3−i}.

## What That Forces

- **The normalisation factorises exactly.** The coefficients δm_{k+1} and ζ_{k+1} are ℤ⁴ quantities of a straight line and of a bent path, for one character (D2.3(5) *Independence*). Perimeters add, P_k(C₁ ⊔ C₂) = P_k(C₁) + P_k(C₂), and the pair has eight corners. So the pair's κ^{(K′)} is κ^{(K′)}_{C₁,χ₁}κ^{(K′)}_{C₂,χ₂}, with no cross factor ((5)). The cross constant E^× of (5) is the only cross term that the step map extracts. The loops stay coupled through the clusters of Q_k, which may hit insertions on both loops, through the cross insertions of (6), and through the common measure.
- **A cross group has no scalar flat value.** A group with arcs on both loops has a flat value in End(V_{π₁}) ⊗ End(V_{π₂}) (more tensor factors when its arc set on one loop has several intervals). This value commutes with π₁(u) ⊗ π₂(u) for u ∈ G, but it is not a scalar in general. So D2.3(3)'s subtraction c_𝒢T is not available for it. Cross groups get the flat value 0, as in V4 (a_W := 0, V4 L3590), and they are not contracted.
- **Cross groups are made with a doubly exponential decay in the distance to the stopping step.**
  - A cross group made at step k spans at least DL^{K′−k} step-k units.
  - The extent weight of (S-w) has the same rate at every step.
  - So the cross mass made at step k is ≤ C𝔞̄(ℓ₁+ℓ₂)L^{K′−k}e^{−ν_×DL^{K′−k}/M}, on every labelled term.
- **So the cross groups are carried, not taken out.**
  - They form their own species cross(j), last in D2.0(3)'s order. They are never contracted, so no step needs their flat value.
  - The D2.6 closure's carry lemma carries them without a bridge. Every later step can touch them, since the loops lie in the small-field region on most terms. So each step multiplies their mass by at most 1 + C_δc̄ ((6)).
  - The doubly exponential decay pays this geometric growth: the carried cross total A^× of (7) is uniform in K.
  - So the class carries everything to the stopping step, and the large-field terms are paid there, by B^×_LF, as for one loop. No step below K′ needs a large-field bound for the cross species. The attached species are carried by D2.6 as for one loop.
  - Taking the cross words out at the step that makes them doesn't work: on the large-field terms that step has no bound uniform in K.

### (4) Locality for the Pair (Derived by the Induction of D2.3(4), for Short Arcs)

(L×) Take any step k and a non-cross object of the pair on C_i: an insertion, a group with its occurrences, a cluster of the reference gas, or its weight, all of whose groups have short arcs on C_i (fewer than P_k(C_i)/2 traversals). Suppose its network has no point within r₀ of C_{3−i}.
- Then it is the object of the single-loop class of C_i with the same constituents, and it has the same value.
- Conversely, a single-loop object of C_i whose network has no point within r₀ of C_{3−i} is a non-cross object of the pair.
- The network of a group contains the domains of its old members and of the members of its merged occurrences (map l.1144: the supports, the arc hulls, and the merged occurrences' domains). Inductively, it therefore contains the domains of the group's whole lineage.

*Step 1.*
- V4 Lemma lem:two-loop-grouping(c): the C_i-groups of the pair are the single-square groups of C_i none of whose roots has a contact on C_{3−i}. They have the same interval, weight, footprint, and compatibility.
- a_W = a^{(i)}_W (V4 L3590).
- The paragraph *Cancellation* in the proof of Lemma lem:two-loop-cross: tuples of C_i-groups have the same Ursell coefficient and the same activities.
- A constituent with a contact on C_{3−i} lies within r₀ of C_{3−i}.
- At the retreated carrier D(Λ) these transfer: by the proof of Lemma lem:two-loop-retreated, part (i), the grouping lemma does not involve the carrier (V4 L3737).

*Step k → k+1.*
- A step-(k+1) object is built from its constituents by the composition, the joint cluster expansion, the merging rules, the flat value, and the hard core.
- For short arcs these use the contour only through the slots, arcs, and contact sets inside the object's network (D2.3(4) *Contour locality*, map l.1083; its proof is per object). A group with a short arc has only members with short arcs, since its arc contains theirs.
- The constituents are step-k objects whose networks lie inside the new network, so the hypothesis applies to them. The vacuum activities and occurrences do not involve the contour.
- Consider an old occurrence with arcs on both loops, that is, a cluster containing a C₁-group and a C₂-group. It puts arcs on both loops into the group it merges into, since the third merging rule takes the hull over J_Γ. That group is then a cross group.
- So no non-cross object contains such an occurrence or a cross insertion. Cross groups have activity 0 in the reference gas; in the words they are the cross insertions of (6).
- Groups long on C_i are not covered. D2.3(4)'s contour locality is for short arcs (map l.1083, l.1086), and the long-ness and the membership of a long group are properties of the whole term. The step-(k+1) analogue of V4 Lemma lem:two-loop-grouping(a) and (c) for long groups is not derived. (5) pays the clusters with long groups by (T′) instead.

### (5) The Factorisation of the Reference Gas (Derived)

Work on ℓ₀ at the map k → k+1, k ≥ 1. The first step is V4's (*The first step* below).
- S°(ℓ₀) is the pair's reference group gas: C₁- and C₂-groups with their activities c_𝒢, cross activities 0, and D2.3(3)'s hard core.

Put E^×_{k+1} := log S°(ℓ₀) − log S°_{C₁}(ℓ₀) − log S°_{C₂}(ℓ₀).

- *Allocation.* In each gas put a(b) := Σ_{Γ: b∈J_Γ}Φ°(Γ)/|J_Γ| (D2.3(6)(a)).
  - Then log S° = Σ_ba(b) over the step-k traversals of C, and log S°_{C_i} = Σ_{b∈C_i}a_{C_i}(b).
  - So E^×_{k+1} = Σ_iΣ_{b∈C_i}(a(b) − a_{C_i}(b)). The rearrangement uses absolute convergence, from the pair gas's Kotecký–Preiss condition ((6A)(v)).
- *Cancellation.* Let b ∈ C_i, and let Γ ∋ b be a cluster of either gas whose network has diameter < δ_b^{(k)} − r₀ and which contains no group long on C_i.
  - Its network has no point within r₀ of C_{3−i}.
  - By (L×) it is a cluster of both gases, with the same weight and the same J_Γ; the Ursell function depends only on the incompatibility graph, which is the same in both.
  - So a(b) − a_{C_i}(b) contains only clusters of network diameter ≥ δ_b^{(k)} − r₀ and clusters with a group long on C_i.
  - The latter have network diameter ≥ ℓ_iL^{K′−k} = P_k(C_i)/4: the arc of a long group has at least 2ℓ_iL^{K′−k} consecutive traversals of C_i, so it contains a whole side, as for the whole-contour groups of map l.1086.

  |E^×_{k+1}| ≤ 8C_S′e^{c_dr₀}𝔞_{k+1}(ℓ₁+ℓ₂)L^{K′−k}e^{−c_dDL^{K′−k}} + 8C_S′𝔞_{k+1}Σ_iℓ_iL^{K′−k}e^{−c_dℓ_iL^{K′−k}}.

- *The first step.* E^×_1 is V4's cross exponent at the retreated carrier, E^×_Λ of Lemma lem:two-loop-retreated(a), with the bound of Lemma lem:two-loop-cross(b): |E^×_1| ≤ C_×ρ_VΔ_× ≤ 4C_×ρ_V(ℓ₁+ℓ₂)L^{K′}e^{−c_×DL^{K′−1}/M}. There are 4(ℓ₁+ℓ₂)L^{K′} fine traversals, each with δ_a ≥ DL^{K′} fine units, and V4's ρ_V (at g₀) is ≤ C_V(1+β₀)⁶ρ₁ (map l.298), with C_V depending on χ₁ and χ₂ through V4's constants (V4 L3614).

### (6) The Cross Groups: Production and Carry (Derived Given the Inherited Inputs)

- *The step.* Apply the map k → k+1 to N^×_k. After the interactions are expanded (D2.3(3)), the family sum runs over the compatible families of step-(k+1) groups of the pair.
  - D2.3(3)'s multilinear expansion Y_𝒢 = c_𝒢T + Y^d_𝒢 and its resummation apply as for one loop. Each factor of the two-trace word is expanded along its own loop, as in V4 Lemma lem:two-loop-schur(b)–(c) at step 1.
  - For a cross group c_𝒢 = 0, so Y^d_𝒢 = Y_𝒢: the group enters the new word as an uncontracted insertion.
  - The result is 𝒲^×_{k+1} of (2), with insertions of every species, Q^{out}_{k+1}(D) = S°^{[D]}(ℓ)/S°(ℓ), and the factor κ_{k+1} of (5).

### (9) What the Bound Does Not Give

- Convergence along the full sequence. The obstruction is the continuum note's (3.5) and (9.3), for the pair as for one loop.
- Factorisation. That W^×_ren − W_ren(C₁)W_ren(C₂) is small for large D, with both single-loop expectations normalised at the common stopping step, is not derived.
  - A^×, C^{R×}_E, and the first two terms of C^×_E decay in D. The long-group term of C^×_E does not ((5)).
  - μ^{[K]}_{ℓ₀} couples the two coarse loops, and its decay of correlations at the stopping scale is not addressed.
- Smallness of the cross terms at fixed coupling. A^cr and C^×_E are bounded for every D ≥ 1. They tend to 0 in the limit γ → 0 with γ/g_min bounded:
  - with q := max(q₁, q₂, q₁₂), ℓ_i = p_iL^{j−q_i} ≤ p_iL^{q−q_i}L^{q̄(j)+1};
  - 𝔞̄ ≤ 𝔟̄, and 𝔟̄ is O(g_k(1+2r_k)⁴pl_k) maximised over k (ρ_k, ρ_{ph,k}, and ρ_{t,k} at map l.295; b_k ≤ Cρ_{t,k+1}, D2.6 closure §2.1);

  - C^{R×}_E and A^{cr,R} carry the factor e^{−p₀(ḡ_R)/2} ((6B)), so they are small when ḡ_R is.
