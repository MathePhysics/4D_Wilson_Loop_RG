## 9. (ii): A Real Base U₀ ≠ 1 (Derived)

- *Assumption.* In the note's uses the real base U₀ is a minimal configuration of VAR's problem on Λ: the response of U(𝐁″_k(Y₁), ·) is taken at real data V, at U₀ = U(𝐁″_k(Y₁), V) (note #5.3, #5.6). VAR's ε₁ of (172) and (190), which sets r_B (note #45), stays fixed. The base's regularity is measured separately: V obeys VAR (7) with ε₁′ := O(sup_nα_{0,n} + B₅M⁶L²R_kε_k) in place of ε₁ (the contacts draft, §4a, *The bases are near pure gauges*; VAR p.278 l.46; note #6.16), so ε₁′ → 0 as g → 0 for L and M fixed. VAR Theorem 1 at ε₁′ describes the same minimiser, the unique critical orbit (p.279 l.15). So U₀ lies in 𝔘_k({Ω_j}, B₃ε₁′) (VAR (8), p.279 l.11–13), and REG (1.33) (p.82 l.33) holds with α₀ := B₃ε₁′. From here on ε₁ in this section means ε₁′. REG's restrictions on α₀ (p.87 l.14; p.98 l.34–35) hold for g small given L. (REG pins are raw-file line − 1.)
- *Gauge covariance.* The Landau condition R(U₀)D*_{U₀}A = 0, the averages Q(U₀), and the contour averages K(U₀) are covariant under (U₀, A) ↦ (U₀^v, vAv^{−1}). The bounds read abs(∂ω) and abs(A_N) in the adjoint-invariant norm, and 𝒜_y through averaged loop integrals with U₀'s transport (#5.10.4). So every bound may be proved in any gauge of U₀, chosen locally.
- *The regular gauge.* By VAR Theorem 1 (p.279 l.17–27), on every cube □ of its class at the layer j_c, of size 2M′L^{j_c}η with M′ a multiple of R₁M₁ and M′ ≤ M(ε₁) = R₁M₁a₁/ε₁, there is a gauge on a neighbourhood of □ in which U₀ = e^{iηA₀} on □, with abs(A₀) < B₃M′ε₁(L^{j_c}η)^{−1}, abs(∇A₀) < B₃M′ε₁(L^{j_c}η)^{−2}, and ‖A₀‖_{1,1} < B₄(1)M′ε₁(L^{j_c}η)^{−3} ((9) at β = β₀ = 1). In fine units the bond angle a₀ := ηA₀ and its first and second differences per fine step are at most B₃M′ε₁L^{−j_c}, B₃M′ε₁L^{−2j_c}, and B₄(1)M′ε₁L^{−3j_c}. B₃ depends on d and L only, and B₄(1) on d, L, and β₀ = 1 (p.279 l.28).
  - *The cubes.* About each fine site x of the layer j′, take the cube of fine side ℓ(x) := C₀(d)ñ(x)log L, with ñ the scale function of §2 (Lemma A′), as a cube of VAR's class j_c := j′, or j_c := j′ − 1 within the fine distance 2ℓ(x) of the interface with the layer j′ − 1. Its M′ is the least multiple of R₁M₁ with 2M′L^{j_c} ≥ ℓ(x), so M′ ≤ C₀(d)L²log L + R₁M₁. VAR's class asks that the concentric cube of side (2M′ + 4R₁M₁)L^{j_c} lie in the layers j_c and j_c + 1 and meet the layer j_c (p.279 l.1–4). The layers have widths > RML^{j′} (§2, *Distances*), so this holds once RM ≥ C(d)L³log L + 8R₁M₁, which R and M, chosen after L, satisfy. M′ ≤ M(ε₁) for g small given L.
  - *The size.* Since ℓ ≤ 2M′L^{j_c}, at fine scales r ≤ ℓ the relative sizes abs(a₀)r, abs(∂a₀)r², and abs(∂²a₀)r³ are at most ε₀ := C(d)(B₃ + B₄(1))M′⁴ε₁, which tends to 0 as g → 0 for L, M, and R fixed. The passage needs ε₀ ≤ ε(d), which holds for g small given L, M, and R.
- *Perturbation.* In the regular gauge D_{U₀} = ∂ + cτ, with τ the unit shift and c the multiplication by Ad U₀(b) − 1. So abs(c) ≤ 2abs(a₀), abs(∂c) ≤ C(d)(abs(∂a₀) + abs(a₀)²), and abs(∂²c) ≤ C(d)(abs(∂²a₀) + abs(a₀)abs(∂a₀) + abs(a₀)³). Every operator of the passage at U₀ (A(U₀) = M(U₀)*M(U₀), Q′(U₀), E(U₀), Δ_{U₀}) is a composition of such differences, parallel transports, and the flat kernels of Lemma M. Its difference from the flat operator is kept in the composition form V = Σ X₁cX₂, with X₁, X₂ flat, and X₁ is moved onto the Green function or the test function by summation by parts. Each term has one difference fewer than the flat operator, so on fields varying at the fine scale r ≤ ℓ its relative size is ≤ C(d)ε₀. Where a difference falls on c, abs(∂c)r² and abs(∂²c)r³ are ≤ C(d)ε₀ as well. The parallel transports in Q(U₀) and K(U₀) change the masses by factors 1 + C(d)ε₀.
- *Lemma R at U₀.* Lemma R is local: its ball has fine radius c(d)n′ ≤ c(d)L^{j+1} < ℓ/4, so it is proved in one regular gauge, with no tails. There Δ_{U₀} = Δ + W, with W := Σ_μ(∂_μ*cτ_μ + τ_μ*c*∂_μ + τ_μ*c*cτ_μ). Run (1)–(4) with v := Δ_{U₀}f, so that Δv = g − Wv and Δf = v − Wf.
  - (1) applies to g only, with the bump ψ_B transported by the transports of Q′(U₀). In the regular gauge Δ_{U₀}ψ_B − Δψ_B consists of terms with c or ∂c, so ‖Δ_{U₀}ψ_B‖ ≤ (1 + C(d)ε₀)C(d)n_B^{−2−d/2}.
  - In (3), the extra part of v_p is G*(ζWv), ζ a smooth cutoff at the scale n′ in place of 1_{B_{8n′}} (its differences add terms of the same form). For the term ∂_μ*cτ_μv, ∂_μ* is moved onto G, and the term τ_μ*c*∂_μv keeps its difference on v. So abs(G*(ζWv)) ≤ C(d)sup abs(c)·(n′sup abs(v) + n′²sup abs(∇v)) + C(d)n′²sup abs(c)²sup abs(v) ≤ C(d)ε₀(sup abs(v) + n′sup abs(∇v)), the sups over B_{8n′}, since Σ_{abs(s)≤Cn′}abs(∇G(s)) ≤ C(d)n′ (Lawler–Limic Corollary 4.3.3) and sup abs(c)·n′ ≤ ε₀.
  - In (4), at the point x where ∇f is bounded, write c = c(x) + c̃ with abs(c̃(x + s)) ≤ sup abs(∂c)·abs(s). The constant c(x) commutes with the flat kernels, so its part of ∇G*(ζ∂_μ*(cτ_μf)) is c(x)∇G*(ζ∂_μ*τ_μf), with the difference left on f: it is ≤ C(d)abs(c(x))n′sup abs(∇f) ≤ C(d)ε₀sup abs(∇f). The part of c̃ is ∇∂_μG*(ζc̃τ_μf), and Σ_{abs(s)≤Cn′}abs(∇²G(s))abs(s) ≤ C(d)n′, so it is ≤ C(d)sup abs(∂c)·n′sup abs(f) ≤ C(d)ε₀n′^{−1}sup abs(f), since sup abs(∂c)·n′² ≤ C(d)ε₀. The other terms of W carry no difference on c and are bounded as in (3). So abs(∇G*(ζWf)) ≤ C(d)ε₀(sup abs(∇f) + n′^{−1}sup abs(f)) on B_{4n′}, with no Calderón–Zygmund logarithm, and ∇v is bounded in the same way.
  - The harmonic parts are flat-harmonic in the regular gauge up to these sources, and (2) and Theorem 6.3.8 apply to them.
  - With S(r) := sup_{B_r}abs(∇f) + n′^{−1}sup_{B_r}abs(f) + n′sup_{B_r}abs(v) + n′²sup_{B_r}abs(∇v), this gives S(r) ≤ C(d)[(flat right side of Lemma R) + n′^{−1−d/2}‖f‖_{B_{c(d)n′}}] + C(d)ε₀(n′/(r′ − r))^{C(d)}S(r′) for n′ ≤ r < r′ ≤ 2n′. The iteration over radii, as in Lemma B (2′), absorbs the last term for ε₀ ≤ ε(d).
  - The added term n′^{−1−d/2}‖f‖ bounds n′^{−1}sup abs(f) through the mean value bound for the subsolution abs(f) (Kato's inequality on the lattice: abs(Δ_{U₀}f) ≥ Δabs(f) pointwise, so Δabs(f) ≤ abs(v)). - *Γ(U₀), H′(U₀), and P(U₀).* Let Γ(U₀) := Π(ΠΔ_{U₀}²Π)^{−1}Π, Π the orthogonal projection onto N(Q′(U₀)), on the torus and on the complement of the constants as in #5.10.1.   - *The weighted energy of Γ(U₀).* For a source s supported in a block B(y) and its fine neighbours, Γ(U₀)s minimises ‖Δ_{U₀}φ‖² − 2⟨s, φ⟩ over N(Q′(U₀)). It is covariant: ‖D_{U₀}φ‖² = ⟨φ, Δ_{U₀}φ⟩ exactly, and Poincaré on a block of side n holds in the regular gauge with C(d) doubled. - *Localisation.* The objects with exponential decay at the fine scale L^{j+1} or below are H′(U₀), P(U₀) (above), Q′(U₀), K(U₀) (#5.10.4, with its transports), T(U₀) (below), and G_S beyond the distance L in scale-j units (Lemma A, whose proof at U₀ is the first local step below). The cut-off tails are ≤ e^{−δ(d)C₀(d)log L/8} ≤ L^{−10d} times the uncut bound, for C₀(d) large, and so are negligible against the polynomial counts of §§3, 7. The cut objects live in one cube of the regular gauge, and the gauge covariance turns their bounds into bounds in any gauge. So no patching of local gauges is needed for them.
- *τ_y without polynomials (derived).* #5.10.3 controls (I − P)τ_y through the cancellation (I − P)q = 0 for quadratic q, which has no covariant form. It is not needed. Let Γ := (I − P)Δ^{−2}, on the torus and on the complement of the constants as in #5.10.1. Since Δ^{−1} = Δ^{−2}Δ there, (I − P)τ = ΓΔEh and T = ∂ΓΔE = ∂Γ∂*∂E.
  - *The energy of f_y := (I − P)τ_y = ΓΔEδ_y.* P is the a-orthogonal projection, a(φ, φ) := ‖Δφ‖², onto the range of H′, so ‖Δf_y‖ ≤ ‖Δτ_y‖ = ‖Eδ_y‖ = n^{d/2}. f_y ∈ N(Q′), and for φ ∈ N(Q′), ‖φ‖ ≤ C(d)n‖∂φ‖ (Poincaré on each block) and ‖∂φ‖² = ⟨φ, Δφ⟩ ≤ ‖φ‖‖Δφ‖, so ‖φ‖ ≤ C(d)n²‖Δφ‖ and ‖∂φ‖ ≤ C(d)n‖Δφ‖.
