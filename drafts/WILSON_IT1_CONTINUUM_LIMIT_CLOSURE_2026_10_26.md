## What the Objects Are

- **The stopping lattice is fixed.** ε = uL^{−K}, u fixed, C a square of physical side pL^{−q}u, torus periods T_ph fixed multiples of u. The stopping scale is a := uL^{−j(C)} with j(C) the least j ≥ q + q̄(j), q̄(j) := ⌈log_L(L³M(1+γ²β′(j+1))^{β₀}(log g_min^{−2})^r)⌉ (map l.193, l.1093; g_min := inf_K g_K > 0, γ := sup_K g_K). Under the coupling prescription j(C) depends on q, L, M, β₀, β′, r, g_min, γ only, so for every K the step K′ := K − j(C) integrates over the field V_{K′} on the lattice T^{(K′)} of spacing a with N_s := (T_ph/u)L^{j(C)} sites per side, |T^{(K′)}| = N_s⁴ sites, and C is the fixed closed contour C^{(K′)} of P_{K′}(C) = 4ℓ_C bonds, ℓ_C = pL^{j(C)−q}. None of a, N_s, ℓ_C, or C^{(K′)} depends on K.
- **The run depends on K.** The density ρ_{K′} = ρ^{[K]}_{K′} on T^{(K′)} is the output of K′ = K − j(C) renormalisation steps from the ε-lattice, with the bare coupling g_0(ε, g) of RG I Theorem 2. ## What That Forces

- On ℓ₀ the integration variable is K-independent and the leading function can be made K-independent: by the background closure §3.4, on the terms whose scale-K′ blocks of C lie in Ω_{K′}, which on ℓ₀ is every term, f_{K′} = W_χ(C;U_{K′}(V_{K′})) = W_χ(C^{(K′)};V_{K′}) + β^{(K′)}_{K′}(V_{K′}) with |β^{(K′)}_{K′}| ≤ Lip_χ(e^{8a_dc_hB₃ℓ_Cε_{K′}} − 1) pointwise (the map's (7)(b) at k = K′, with the constraint M^{K′}U_{K′}(V_{K′}) = V_{K′} on every bond of ℓ₀, CRE (2.12)). W_χ(C^{(K′)};·) is one fixed continuous function on the fixed compact space G^{bonds(T^{(K′)})}. So the objects whose convergence is in question are the measures μ^{[K]}_ℓ := ρ^{[K]}_ℓdV/Z^{[K]} (sub-probability measures, total mass w_ℓ), the numbers E_const,K′, and the K-dependent bounded functions β^{(K′)}_{K′}, I_{K′}, E^∂_{K′}(ℓ) and the large-field dressings, integrated against those measures. A limit of W_ren(ε) is a statement about the whole sum (6.3): Bałaban's effective measure at a fixed physical scale tested against the fixed coarse loop, together with the remainder r_K, whose parts are K-dependent functions (through channels (i)–(ii)) integrated against K-dependent measures; weak convergence of the measures alone would not give it, since the test functions in r_K move with K.
- Every printed result of Bałaban's at the stopping scale is a bound uniform in K (CRE Theorem 2's E₁ and R₁ "independent of j, k, Ω, {Ω_j}, {Λ_j}, T", p.263; Corollary 3's E_± "independent of η and T", p.264, but dependent on g_k; LFR II (0.1)'s E_± "independent of k, T_η, U_k", p.356), and the family of all these K-dependent functions is uniformly bounded on the fixed compact space. Uniform bounds give compactness (every sequence of sub-probability measures on a compact metric space has a weakly convergent subsequence; the bounded sequence W_ren(ε_K) has convergent subsequences), never convergence of the full sequence; convergence needs a Cauchy statement across K, which compares two different runs and which neither Bałaban's step (one run, k → k+1) nor D2's step map (the same) contains.
- The large-field terms are not small by counting (CRE p.244, LFR1 p.175: the per-plaquette factor "does not give any positive power of ε", against ≍ (T_ph/ε)⁴ fine plaquettes); for K′ ≤ N, where no R-operation has acted, the weight w_{ℓ₀} of the small-field term is not shown to be bounded below as K → ∞ by any available estimate (item (4.1); a lower bound of the same order as the diverging count is expected and not proved, map l.376), so on the available bounds the ℓ₀-term alone is not the limit object. With the R-operation (K′ > N, i.e. for every large K) the surviving large-field regions carry their fundamental factors and the renormalised regions created at step K′ leave functions of V_{K′} only (LFR2 pp.391–392), and the total relative weight of the terms other than ℓ₀ is reduced in item (7) to the exact quotient identity (7.1) and to one residual quotient Q_ℓ, the small-field integral over Z^∼(ℓ) of the term's completed form against the ℓ₀ term's, which no printed statement bounds ((7.3)); the existence of the limit does not need it, since Σ_ℓw_ℓ = 1 ((8)). The cancellation of the far large-field terms against the partition function is performed by the R-operation itself, conditionally on D2.7 (item (4.4)); the near ones are D2.7's observable part.
- κ^{(K′)} is a number that is not shown to converge: its perimeter part is (P_phys/ε) times a running mass that is at most O(1/log(1/ε)) in modulus, and its corner part is bounded by exp(O((log log(1/ε))^{1+6p₀})) (item (6)). Dividing it out is the exact form of the perimeter and corner renormalisation, and W_ren(ε) is K-uniformly bounded given D(K′) on every labelled term, since Σ_ℓw_ℓ = 1 ((8.1)).

### (4) Item (b): the large-field terms on ε-dependent lattices and their normalisation against the partition function

- *(4.1) Counting does not control them (checked against the print).* CRE p.244 and LFR1 p.175 (parallel passages, the displays differ): for d = 4 "the bare coupling constant behaves asymptotically as (a + b log ε^{−1})^{−1/2}, for ε → 0, … hence the bound does not give any positive power of ε.
