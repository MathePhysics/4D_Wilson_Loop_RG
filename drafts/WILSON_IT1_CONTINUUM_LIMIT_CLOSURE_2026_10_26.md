# The Continuum Limit at the Stopping Scale

{#NP.1} For a loop C on the fixed torus, the renormalised Wilson-loop expectation W_ren(ε) := E_ε[W(C)]/κ^{(K′)} is an exact convex combination over Bałaban's labelled terms at the fixed stopping lattice ((2)). It is bounded uniformly in K ((8.1)), every sequence of lattice spacings has a subsequence along which it converges for every admissible loop ((8.2)–(8.3)), and it converges along the full sequence ((8.4)).
{#NP.2} D(K′) holds on every labelled term: by the map's D2.0–D2.6 with the D2.6 closure, and at the 𝐑-steps K′ > N by the D2.7 closure's 15.8, at the complex evaluation points of its #7 block.

## What the Objects Are {#NSWTOA}

- {#NWTOA.1} **The stopping lattice is fixed.** ε = uL^{−K}, u fixed, C a square of physical side pL^{−q}u, torus periods T_ph fixed multiples of u.
  {#NWTOA.1-1} The stopping scale is a := uL^{−j(C)} with j(C) the least j ≥ q + q̄(j), q̄(j) := ⌈log_L(L³M(1+γ²β′(j+1))^{β₀}(log g_min^{−2})^r)⌉ (map #MOMGD.30, #MD2.3.4.15; g_min := inf_K g_K > 0, γ := sup_K g_K).
  {#NWTOA.1-2} Under the coupling prescription j(C) depends on q, L, M, β₀, β′, r, g_min, γ only, so for every K the step K′ := K − j(C) integrates over the field V_{K′} on the lattice T^{(K′)} of spacing a with N_s := (T_ph/u)L^{j(C)} sites per side, |T^{(K′)}| = N_s⁴ sites, and C is the fixed closed contour C^{(K′)} of P_{K′}(C) = 4ℓ_C bonds, ℓ_C = pL^{j(C)−q}.
  {#NWTOA.1-3} None of a, N_s, ℓ_C, or C^{(K′)} depends on K.
- {#NWTOA.2} **The run depends on K.** The density ρ_{K′} = ρ^{[K]}_{K′} on T^{(K′)} is the output of K′ = K − j(C) renormalisation steps from the ε-lattice, with the bare coupling g_0(ε, g) of RG I Theorem 2.
  {#NWTOA.2-1} Its K-dependence passes through four channels and no other: (i) the effective coupling g_{K′} and the sequence g_1, …, g_{K′} (CRE (2.24), p.259, with RG I Theorem 2's g_0(ε, g));
  {#NWTOA.2-2} (ii) the ε-lattice minimiser U_{K′}(V_{K′}) (CRE (2.12), p.256: the minimal orbit of A^η on {U regular, M_𝔹(U) = V}, a field on T_η with η = L^{−K′} in step-K′ units, i.e. on the ε-lattice), through which every function of V_{K′} in the action is defined (CRE p.258: A_k "depends on the gauge field variable V … through the background field U_k(V)"), and the fluctuation covariances built from it (RG1 (1.4), p.260: Z^{(j)}(U_k) = ∫dB δ(Q̃B)exp[−½⟨B,Δ^{(j)}(U_k)B⟩]);
  {#NWTOA.2-3} (iii) the number of steps summed, Σ_{j=1}^{K′}, in 𝐄_{K′} (CRE (2.25), p.259), 𝐑_{K′} (CRE (2.30), p.260), 𝐁_{K′} (CRE (2.40), p.261), E_{K′} (p.262), and in the dressing's κ^{(K′)}, 𝓔_{K′}, and insertions (D2.3(8), map #MSD2.3.8–#MD2.3.8.8);
  {#NWTOA.2-4} (iv) the label set of the terms, the sequences {Ω_j}, {Λ_j}, {S_j} of K′ steps (CRE (2.18), p.257), with the pending 𝐓-integrations over the old variables on the lattices of spacing uL^{j−K} inside the surviving large-field regions (CRE (2.19)–(2.22)) and, for K′ > N, the 𝐑-histories of the renormalised regions (LFR II p.390: "The new large field region is equal to Z_k ∪ ⋃Y_i", so a kept Y_i persists as a large-field region with its pending V_k).
- {#NWTOA.3} **The identity is a convex combination.** On every labelled term ℓ of ρ_{K′} (D(K′) holds on each, #NP.2), N^C_ℓ := ∫𝒲_ℓρ_ℓ dV (the numerator identity, map #MD2.0.3.66, D2.2(2)), Z_ℓ := ∫ρ_ℓ dV ≥ 0 (LFR1 p.176: "We will prove that the densities are positive"), Z := Σ_ℓZ_ℓ, w_ℓ := Z_ℓ/Z ∈ [0,1], Σ_ℓw_ℓ = 1, and E_ε[W(C)] = Σ_ℓw_ℓ⟨𝒲_ℓ⟩_ℓ with ⟨F⟩_ℓ := ∫Fρ_ℓdV/Z_ℓ a probability expectation on the term's variables (for Z_ℓ > 0; a term with Z_ℓ = 0 contributes nothing).
  {#NWTOA.3-1} The dressing is 𝒲_ℓ = κ^{(K′)}e^{𝓔_{K′}(ℓ)}[f_{K′} + I_{K′}(ℓ)] (D(K′)(i), (v); map #MD2.0.3.2), with the one number κ^{(K′)} in front of every term.

## What That Forces {#NSWTF}

- {#NWTF.1} On ℓ₀ the integration variable is K-independent and the leading function can be made K-independent: by the background closure §3.4, on the terms whose scale-K′ blocks of C lie in Ω_{K′}, which on ℓ₀ is every term, f_{K′} = W_χ(C;U_{K′}(V_{K′})) = W_χ(C^{(K′)};V_{K′}) + β^{(K′)}_{K′}(V_{K′}) with |β^{(K′)}_{K′}| ≤ Lip_χ(e^{8a_dc_hB₃ℓ_Cε_{K′}} − 1) pointwise (the map's (7)(b) at k = K′, with the constraint M^{K′}U_{K′}(V_{K′}) = V_{K′} on every bond of ℓ₀, CRE (2.12)).
  {#NWTF.1-1} W_χ(C^{(K′)};·) is one fixed continuous function on the fixed compact space G^{bonds(T^{(K′)})}.
  {#NWTF.1-2} So the objects whose convergence is in question are the measures μ^{[K]}_ℓ := ρ^{[K]}_ℓdV/Z^{[K]} (sub-probability measures, total mass w_ℓ), the numbers E_const,K′, and the K-dependent bounded functions β^{(K′)}_{K′}, I_{K′}, E^∂_{K′}(ℓ) and the large-field dressings, integrated against those measures.
  {#NWTF.1-3} A limit of W_ren(ε) is a statement about the whole sum #ND.2.6: Bałaban's effective measure at a fixed physical scale tested against the fixed coarse loop, together with the remainder r_K, whose parts are K-dependent functions (through channels (i)–(ii)) integrated against K-dependent measures; weak convergence of the measures alone would not give it, since the test functions in r_K move with K.
- {#NWTF.2} Every printed result of Bałaban's at the stopping scale is a bound uniform in K (CRE Theorem 2's E₁ and R₁ "independent of j, k, Ω, {Ω_j}, {Λ_j}, T", p.263; Corollary 3's E_± "independent of η and T", p.264, but dependent on g_k; LFR II (0.1)'s E_± "independent of k, T_η, U_k", p.356), and the family of all these K-dependent functions is uniformly bounded on the fixed compact space.
  {#NWTF.2-1} Uniform bounds give compactness (every sequence of sub-probability measures on a compact metric space has a weakly convergent subsequence; the bounded sequence W_ren(ε_K) has convergent subsequences). Convergence of the full sequence needs a Cauchy statement across K, which compares two runs; that is the Cauchy note's.
- {#NWTF.3} The large-field terms are not small by counting (CRE p.244, LFR1 p.175: the per-plaquette factor "does not give any positive power of ε", against ≍ (T_ph/ε)⁴ fine plaquettes). They are controlled through the 𝐑-operation, which acts at the stopping scale for every large K ((4.1)).
  {#NWTF.3-1} There the surviving large-field regions carry their fundamental factors, the renormalised regions created at step K′ leave functions of V_{K′} only (LFR2 pp.391–392), and the total relative weight of the terms other than ℓ₀ is bounded uniformly in K by comparing whole integrals on the fixed stopping lattice ((7)).
  {#NWTF.3-2} The existence of subsequential limits needs only Σ_ℓw_ℓ = 1 ((8)).
- {#NWTF.4} κ^{(K′)} is a K-dependent number: its perimeter part is (P_phys/ε) times a running mass that is at most O(1/log(1/ε)) in modulus, and its corner part is bounded by exp(O((log log(1/ε))^{1+6p₀})) (item (6)).
  {#NWTF.4-1} Dividing it out is the exact form of the perimeter and corner renormalisation, and W_ren(ε) is K-uniformly bounded, since Σ_ℓw_ℓ = 1 ((8.1)).

## Derivation {#NSD}

### (1) The Stopping Lattice and the Coupling Window {#NSD.1}

- {#ND.1.1} By the map's #MD2.3.4.15–#MD2.3.4.16, with ε = uL^{−K} the torus of physical period T_ph has T_ph/ε = (T_ph/u)L^K fine sites per side and N_s = (T_ph/u)L^{K−K′} = (T_ph/u)L^{j(C)} sites per side at step K′; C has side ℓ_C = pL^{−q}u/a = pL^{j(C)−q} step-K′ bonds.
  {#ND.1.1-1} Both are fixed once j(C) is, and j(C) is fixed by #MD2.3.4.15 under the prescription.
  {#ND.1.1-2} The torus condition (TC) and the admissible-sides condition hold at K′ by #MD2.3.4.16-5.
- {#ND.1.2} *The coupling at the stopping scale.* CRE (2.6), p.255, with n = K, m = K′ (n − m = j(C)): g_{K′} ≤ (1+β₀)g_K and g_K ≤ (1+g_K²β′j(C))^{1/2}g_{K′}, so g_{K′} ∈ [g_K(1+γ²β′j(C))^{−1/2}, (1+β₀)g_K], a window fixed by the prescription; CRE (2.8), p.256: ε_{K′} ≤ (1+β₀)(1+g_K²β′j(C))^{β₀}ε_K; CRE (2.9): R_{K′} ≤ L(1+g_K²β′j(C))^{β₀}R_K.
  {#ND.1.2-2} The coupling prescription itself (g_0(ε, g) with g_K = g and all g_k ∈ ]0, γ]) is RG I Theorem 2's first assertion, p.259 (printed for SU(2); general G by p.260's "restriction to the group SU(2) is superficial", Bałaban's, accepted; for simple G Corollary 8.2: "a bare g_0(ε, g), independent of the torus; g_K = g, and all g_k in ]0, γ]").

### (2) The Identity at the Stopping Scale {#NSD.2}

- {#ND.2.1} By D2.2(2), D2.3(8), the background closure §1.1 and §3.4, and the positivity of the densities (LFR1 p.176), with the notation of *What the Objects Are*, on every labelled term (D(K′) on each, #NP.2),
  {#ND.2.2} E_ε[W(C)] = κ^{(K′)}·Σ_ℓw_ℓ⟨e^{𝓔_{K′}(ℓ)}[f_{K′} + I_{K′}(ℓ)]⟩_ℓ,
  {#ND.2.3} and on ℓ₀, where 𝓔_{K′}(ℓ₀) = E_const,K′ := Σ_{j≤K′}E°_j is a number (D(k)(v), map #MD2.0.3.64; D2.3(8)) and f_{K′} = W_χ(C^{(K′)};V_{K′}) + β^{(K′)}_{K′},
  {#ND.2.4} w_{ℓ₀}⟨𝒲̂⟩_{ℓ₀} = e^{E_const,K′}·[μ^{[K]}_{ℓ₀}(W_χ(C^{(K′)};·)) + μ^{[K]}_{ℓ₀}(β^{(K′)}_{K′} + I_{K′})],  𝒲̂ := 𝒲/κ^{(K′)},  μ^{[K]}_{ℓ₀}(F) := ∫F ρ^{[K]}_{ℓ₀}dV/Z^{[K]}.
- {#ND.2.5} *The renormalised expectation.* Put W_ren(ε) := E_ε[W(C)]/κ^{(K′)}.
  {#ND.2.5-1} Then, exactly,
  {#ND.2.6} W_ren(ε) = e^{E_const,K′}μ^{[K]}_{ℓ₀}(W_χ(C^{(K′)};·)) + r_K,  r_K := e^{E_const,K′}μ^{[K]}_{ℓ₀}(β^{(K′)}_{K′} + I_{K′}) + Σ_{ℓ≠ℓ₀}w_ℓ⟨e^{E^∂_{K′}(ℓ)}[f_{K′} + I_{K′}(ℓ)]⟩_ℓe^{E_const,K′},
  {#ND.2.7} using 𝓔_{K′}(ℓ) = E_const,K′ + E^∂_{K′}(ℓ) with E^∂_{K′}(ℓ) := Σ_{j≤K′}E^∂_j(ℓ) the attached local terms (D2.3(8), map #MD2.3.8.3–4; the D2.6 closure §7.4(a)).
  {#ND.2.7-2} The factor e^{E_const,K′} lies in [e^{−B_E}, e^{B_E}] for the admissible sides, B_E independent of K (#ND.5.3).

### (3) The K-Dependence on ℓ₀ {#NSD.3}

- {#ND.3.1} *(3.1) What varies with K on ℓ₀.* The step-K′ small-field term is ρ^{[K]}_{ℓ₀}(V) = χ_{K′}(T^{(K′)})exp A_{K′}(1/g_{K′}², U_{K′}(V)) (CRE (2.17)–(2.18), p.257, with Ω_j = Λ_j = T for every j), A_{K′} = −(1/g_{K′}²)A(U_{K′}) + 𝐄_{K′}(U_{K′}) + 𝐑_{K′}(U_{K′}) + 𝐁_{K′} − E_{K′} (CRE (2.23), p.258).
  {#ND.3.1-1} Term by term:
  - {#ND.3.2} the characteristic function χ_{K′}(T^{(K′)}) tests sup_{p⊂□^∼}|U_{K′,□}(V,∂p) − 1| < ε_{K′}η² over the LM₂R_{K′}-cubes □ of T_η (CRE (2.17)), with U_{K′,□}(V) = U(𝔹_{K′}(□^{∼4}), M^·(Q^{s*}_{K′}V)) (CRE (2.16)) a minimiser on the ε-lattice: a K-dependent set in the fixed space G^{bonds(T^{(K′)})}, through channel (ii) and through ε_{K′}, R_{K′} (channel (i));
  - {#ND.3.3} the Wilson term (1/g_{K′}²)A(U_{K′}(V)) is the minimal ε-lattice action under the block constraint, divided by g_{K′}²: channels (i) and (ii);
  - {#ND.3.4} 𝐄_{K′}(U_{K′}) = Σ_{j=1}^{K′}[𝐄^{(j)}(T,g_{j−1},U_{K′}) − 𝐄^{(j)}(T,g_{j−1},1) − β_j(g_{j−1})A(φ_j,U_{K′})] (CRE (2.25) with Λ_j = T): channels (i)–(iii).
    {#ND.3.4-1} By CRE Theorem 2 (2.43), p.263, on ℓ₀ (Γ_n = ∅ for n < K′, Γ_{K′} = T^{(K′)}, Ω = T) the step-j summand is bounded by E₁(L^{j−K′})^β|T^{(K′)}| = E₁L^{−β(K′−j)}N_s⁴, for regular U_{K′}, so the contribution of the steps j ≤ K′ − m is at most E₁N_s⁴L^{−βm}/(1 − L^{−β}) uniformly in K: the early steps enter 𝐄_{K′} on the fixed lattice through a geometrically small tail, and (2.45) bounds the whole by E₁(1 − L^{−β})^{−1}N_s⁴;
  - {#ND.3.5} 𝐑_{K′}(U_{K′}): on a term that never had a large-field region, 𝐑 = 0 (the 𝐑-terms arise from 𝐑-operations, CRE p.259, LFR2 (1.98)–(1.101)); on the terms of ℓ₀ that had regions later renormalised (LFR1 (0.6), p.176: the term is relabelled by the surviving components Z″ and acquires exp Σ_X𝐑(X,V)), 𝐑_{K′} is bounded by CRE (2.46), p.263: |𝐑_{K′}| ≤ R₁N_s⁴Σ_{j≤K′}g_j^{κ₀} < N_s⁴ for κ₀ ≥ 7 and g small, uniformly in K, each localised term by (2.31), |𝐑^{(j)}(X)| ≤ g_j^{κ₀}e^{−κd_j(X)};
  - {#ND.3.6} 𝐁_{K′}: its terms need X ∩ Z_j^∼ ≠ ∅ (CRE (2.41), p.261), so on a term without surviving large-field regions only the boundary terms left by renormalised components can occur (LFR2 (1.104), p.391, with p.392's "boundary terms with localization domains containing one of the domains Y_i", and p.392: these and the functions 𝐓′_k(Y_i)1 "depend only on the new field variables V_k restricted to Y_i"); CRE (2.48), p.264, bounds the total by 2B₁Σ_n|Γ_n| ≤ 2B₁N_s⁴;
  - {#ND.3.7} E_{K′}: the vacuum-energy constant, "depending on {Ω_j}, {Λ_j} also" (CRE p.262), of the order of the volume |T_η| ≍ (T_ph/ε)⁴ (Corollary 3's E_±|T_η|); the initial constant E is the same for every term and cancels in w_ℓ = Z_ℓ/Z exactly, while the label-dependent one-step subtractions differ between terms and survive in the relative weights (p.262: "obtained by subtracting one-step vacuum energy expressions, generated in small field regions, from the initial constant E").
  {#ND.3.8} The dressing's constants on ℓ₀: κ^{(K′)} (item (6)), E_const,K′ (bounded by B_E, each E°_j a K-dependent number through channels (i)–(ii), the sum over j absolutely convergent uniformly in K, #ND.5.3), and the insertion sum I_{K′} (item (5)).
- {#ND.3.9} *(3.2) What is K-independent on ℓ₀.* The space G^{bonds(T^{(K′)})}, the Haar measure dV, the contour C^{(K′)}, and the function W_χ(C^{(K′)};V) = d_π^{−1}tr π(∏_{c∈C^{(K′)}}V(c)).
  {#ND.3.9-1} Nothing else on ℓ₀ is K-independent.
- {#ND.3.10} *(3.3) Uniform bounds.* Bałaban's bounds hold at every k with constants independent of k and T: CRE Theorem 1 (p.262: ρ_k "satisfies all the inductive assumptions"), Theorem 2 ((2.43)–(2.46), p.263), Corollary 3 ((2.50), p.264), and LFR II Theorem 1 (p.355). So do the constants of D(K′), which are uniform in k, K, the contour size, the torus, and the labelled term (D(k)(iv), map #MD2.0.3.63; D2.3(5)–(7); D2.4(12)(f) with the SW closure's (S-w)). So every term of the identity of (2) is bounded uniformly in K.
- {#ND.3.12} *(3.4) The limit object.* By (2), W_ren(ε) = e^{E_const,K′}μ^{[K]}_{ℓ₀}(W_χ(C^{(K′)};·)) + r_K.
  {#ND.3.12-1} The sequence of sub-probability measures μ^{[K]}_{ℓ₀} on the compact space G^{bonds(T^{(K′)})} has weakly convergent subsequences (the space of sub-probability measures on a compact metric space is weakly compact), the sequence E_const,K′ is bounded, and |r_K| is bounded uniformly in K (items (4)–(5); the large-field part by (1 − w_{ℓ₀})B_LF ≤ B_LF); so along a subsequence K_i → ∞ every term converges, and lim_iW_ren(ε_{K_i}) = e^{Ē}μ̄(W_χ(C^{(K′)};·)) + r̄ exists, with μ̄ a sub-probability measure on the fixed space.
  {#ND.3.12-2} So the limit object is a stopping-scale effective measure μ̄ on G^{bonds(T^{(K′)})} integrated against the fixed coarse loop, plus the limit of the bounded remainders. Along the full sequence the limit exists by (8.4).

### (4) The Large-Field Terms {#NSD.4}

- {#ND.4.1} *(4.1) The range of the 𝐑-operation.* For every large K, K′ > N, so the stopping scale lies in the range of D2 where the 𝐑-operation acts (LFR I p.177 (ii); the D2.7 closure's item 1(b)–(c); map #MD2.3.4.16).
- {#ND.4.2} *(4.2) The mechanism in Z.* LFR1 (0.2)–(0.6), p.176: ρ(V) = Σ_Zρ(Z,V); Z = Z′ ∪ Z″ with Z″ the components "for which the small factors connected with large field control some number of next steps" and Z′ the rest; (ℝρ)(V) = Σ_Zρ(Z″,V)·∫dV|_{Z′}ρ(Z,V)/∫dV|_{Z′}ρ(Z″,V), with the normalisation (0.4), ∫dV(ℝρ)(V) = ∫dVρ(V);
  {#ND.4.2-1} the ratios, summed over Z′ ⊂ Z″ᶜ, are exponentiated, (0.5)–(0.6): (ℝρ)(V) = Σ_{Z″}ρ(Z″,V)exp Σ_X𝐑(X,V). p.177: the basic step acts on the components satisfying (i) (contained in a cube of size 100MR_k) and (ii) ("in the preceding N renormalization steps no new large field regions were created inside this component"); p.177 also states that the denominators are in fact "the parts localized in neighborhoods of the domains Z′, so they do not depend on the large field regions Z″, and they are determined by small field effective actions only".
  {#ND.4.2-2} LFR2 p.384's statement with (1.80): the factor exp(−κ_j(Z)) of a component "controls K renormalization steps" until S^K(Z) satisfies (i), (ii) with N = R_j; p.385, line 5: "doing at most R_j further steps we obtain a domain satisfying both conditions (i), (ii)"; p.387: "κ_k(X) ≥ 0, and we have the fundamental inequality (1.89)", 𝐓′_k(X)1 ≤ exp(−2(1+β₀)^{−1}p₀(g_k)); (1.90)–(1.98), pp.387–390: the exponentiated polymer expansion of the ratios with |𝐑′^{(k)}(X)| ≤ exp(−p₀(g_k))exp(−κd_k(X)) for the terms away from the large-field regions ((1.100));
  {#ND.4.2-3} (1.104), p.391: after the 𝐑-operation the renormalised domains Y_i enter as the characteristic functions and the functions 𝐓′_k(Y_i)1 multiplying the action density, which (p.392) "depend only on the new field variables V_k restricted to Y_i, therefore they do not participate in operations connected with next renormalization transformations"; "The contributions from the previous large field regions is isolated in these boundary terms, and in the functions 𝐓′_k(Y_i)1" (p.392, so printed).
- {#ND.4.3} *(4.3) The large-field terms at the stopping scale, K′ > N.* Three classes of labelled terms of ρ_{K′}:
  - {#ND.4.4} ℓ₀: no surviving component; a function of V_{K′} on the fixed lattice, with the 𝐑_{K′} and boundary terms left by renormalised components ((3.1));
  - {#ND.4.5} terms whose large-field content is renormalised domains Y_i created at step K′ only: by (1.104) and p.392 these are functions of V_{K′} on the fixed lattice too, each Y_i a union of MR_{K′}-cubes of T^{(K′)} (CRE p.256) carrying the factor 𝐓′_{K′}(Y_i)1 ≤ exp(−2(1+β₀)^{−1}p₀(g_{K′})) ((1.89)) and the characteristic function χ^c_{K′}(Y_i^{∼−6}); a Y_i created at an earlier step K″ < K′ is a large-field region of the later steps ("The new large field region is equal to Z_k ∪ ⋃Y_i", p.390) with pending V_{K″} on the lattice of spacing aL^{−(K′−K″)}, and belongs to the third class;
  - {#ND.4.6} terms with a surviving unrenormalised component Z of Z_{K′}: its variables are V_j|_{Γ_j} and A_j on the lattices of spacing aL^{−(K′−j)} for the steps j of its history (CRE (2.2), (2.20)–(2.21)), lattices that depend on ε through K′ − j only, i.e. on the component's age; its factor is exp(−κ_{K′}(Z) − 2p₀(g_{j(Z)})) with κ_{K′}(Z) ≥ 0 inside its lifetime (p.384's statement, p.387's argument), and p₀(g_{j(Z)}) ≥ (1+β₀)^{−1}p₀(g_{K′}) by CRE (2.7)'s first line (p.255; m = j(Z) < n = K′), so the factor is ≤ exp(−2(1+β₀)^{−1}p₀(g_{K′})) as well;
    {#ND.4.6-1} its current support Z ⊂ T^{(K′)} is a union of MR_{K′}-cubes of the fixed lattice.
  {#ND.4.7} Every surviving or renormalised component therefore sits at one of at most N_s⁴(MR_{K′})^{−4} positions of the fixed lattice and carries its fundamental factor ≤ exp(−2(1+β₀)^{−1}p₀(g_{K′})) inside its own term.
