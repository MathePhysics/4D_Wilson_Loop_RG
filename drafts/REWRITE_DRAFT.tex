\subsection{The Expansion of the Words in the Localisation Parameters}\label{sec:localisation-expansion}

After the change of variables of the step the dressed loop is a function of the fluctuation field, and the first task is to see on which part of that field each of its words depends. The difficulty is that the dependence is not local. By \cite[(3.6), p.~266]{BalabanCRE}, in the form in which \cite[p.~267]{BalabanCRE} transfers the formulae of \cite[Sect.~2]{BalabanRG1} to the operators of a sequence of domains, the background of the step $k$ at the block field $e^{iB'}V^{(k)}$ is a gauge transform of $e^{i\eta\mathbf H_k(B')}U_{k+1}$, where $B'$ is the fluctuation variable \cite[(1.19), p.~6]{BalabanRG2} and $\mathbf H_k$ is the function \cite[(1.17), p.~6]{BalabanRG2}, built from the propagators $H$, $\tilde G$, and $H_0$ of the step. These propagators have kernels which decay exponentially and do not vanish at any distance, so that the transport of the new configuration along a single bond of a loop depends on the fluctuation field on the whole lattice. Ba{\l}aban meets the same difficulty for the terms of the effective action, and he resolves it by introducing parameters which switch off the propagators across the walls of a partition \cite[pp.~3--7]{BalabanRG2}. We apply his device to the words of the dressed loop.

\emph{The parameters.} Let $\sigma_k$ be the partition of the torus into the cubes $\Delta$ of side $M$ of the scale $k$, which is the family that \cite[p.~3]{BalabanRG2} takes in its example, here with no cube exempt. To each cube we assign a parameter $s(\Delta)$, and we define the propagators $H(s)$, $\tilde G(s)$, $H_0(s)$ by the rule of \cite[p.~3]{BalabanRG2}: the term of a walk $\omega$ in the expansion \cite[(1.6)]{BalabanRG2} of a propagator is multiplied by $s(\Delta_1) \cdots s(\Delta_m)$, where $\Delta_1, \dots, \Delta_m$ are the cubes of $\sigma_k$ which meet the localisation domain of $\omega$. Let $\mathbf H(s, B')$ be the function \cite[(1.17)]{BalabanRG2} with these propagators; by \cite[(1.5), p.~3]{BalabanRG2} the solutions which enter it are functionals of the propagators with the properties of the original ones. For a set $\sigma \subset \sigma_k$ let $Y(\sigma)$ be the union of its cubes, and call a union of cubes \emph{connected} if any two of its cubes are joined by a chain of its cubes in which consecutive ones share a wall of dimension $d - 1$ \cite[p.~4]{BalabanRG2}. We use three properties.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item $\mathbf H(1, B') = \mathbf H_k(B')$, since the propagators at $s = 1$ are the original ones \cite[p.~3]{BalabanRG2}, and $\mathbf H(0, B') = 0$, since the localisation domain of every walk meets a cube, so that all three propagators vanish at $s = 0$.
\item If $s(\Delta) = 0$ for every $\Delta \notin \sigma$, then $\mathbf H(s, B')$ vanishes on a neighbourhood of $Y(\sigma)^c$, and on a component $Y$ of $Y(\sigma)$ it depends only on the restrictions of $s$, of $B'$, and of the configuration to $Y$; we write $\mathbf H_Y(s, B')$ for it. This is the argument of \cite[p.~4]{BalabanRG2}: the kernels of the propagators vanish unless both arguments lie in the interior of one component, the equations \cite[(1.3), (1.4)]{BalabanRG2} separate into equations in the components, and $B'$ is a local function of the fluctuation field.
\item For complex parameters with $\abs{s(\Delta)} \leq e^{\kappa_1}$, where $\kappa_1$ is the constant of \cite[p.~5]{BalabanRG2} with $\delta_1M \geq \kappa_1$, and for $\abs{B'} < \varepsilon_3$, the function $\mathbf H_Y(s, B')$ is analytic in $s$ and $B'$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:localised-h-bound}
    \abs{\mathbf H_Y(s, B')} \leq 4B_0e^{16\kappa_1}\abs{B'}_Y\;,
\end{equation}
with $\abs{B'}_Y$ the supremum over $Y$, by \cite[(1.18), p.~6]{BalabanRG2} applied to $B'$ restricted to $Y$; the constants $B_0$, $\varepsilon_2$, $\varepsilon_3$ are those of \cite[pp.~3, 5, 6]{BalabanRG2}, with $4C_2B_0e^{16\kappa_1}\varepsilon_2 \leq 1$, $4C_4B_0^2e^{32\kappa_1}\varepsilon_3 \leq 1$, and $2B_0e^{16\kappa_1}\varepsilon_3 \leq \varepsilon_2$, and ``all the bounds above are uniform on the domain'' of the configurations \cite[p.~6]{BalabanRG2}. None of the estimates \cite[(1.11)--(1.18)]{BalabanRG2} uses the cube which Ba{\l}aban exempts, so that they hold with every cube carrying a parameter.
\end{enumerate}

\emph{The expansion of a word.} Consider first a word of the dressed loop none of whose factors depends on an outer fluctuation field; on the trivial term these are all the words. By the covariance of its factors (Definition~\ref{def:dressed-loop}(iii)) the word is invariant under gauge transformations of the configuration, so that at the block field $e^{iB'}V^{(k)}$ it is the same word evaluated at the configuration $e^{i\eta\mathbf H_k(B')}U_{k+1}$. For a family $\mathfrak B$ of the step $k$, the empty family included, let $\Phi_{\mathfrak B}(s)$ be its word evaluated at $e^{i\eta\mathbf H(s, B')}U_{k+1}$. For $k \geq 2$ the factors are functions of a configuration and of the variable $\mathbf J$ of \cite[(2.36), p.~261]{BalabanCRE}, and they are evaluated at the argument which \cite[(3.10)--(3.13), p.~272]{BalabanRG1} substitutes for $\mathbf J$ when the configuration is $e^{i\xi\mathbf A}U_{k+1}$: by \cite[(3.11), (3.12)]{BalabanRG1} the current of that configuration is $(L^{j-1}\eta)^3J_{k+1} + D^{\xi*}_{U_{k+1}}D^\xi_{U_{k+1}}\mathbf A + \mathbf F(U_{k+1}, \mathbf A)$, in the normalisation of the step $j$ at which the factor was made, and we take this expression at $\mathbf A = \mathbf H(s, B')$. By (a), $\Phi_{\mathfrak B}(1)$ is the word at the configuration of integration and $\Phi_{\mathfrak B}(0)$ is the word at $U_{k+1}$. The torus has finitely many cubes, and the fundamental theorem of calculus in each parameter gives, as in \cite[(1.9), p.~4]{BalabanRG2},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:parameter-expansion}
    \Phi_{\mathfrak B}(1) = \sum_{\sigma \subset \sigma_k}\prod_{\Delta \in \sigma}\int_0^1\partial_{s(\Delta)}\Phi_{\mathfrak B}(s)\big|_{s(\sigma^c) = 0}\,\dd s(\Delta)\;.
\end{equation}
The term $\sigma = \emptyset$ is $\Phi_{\mathfrak B}(0)$. In the term of $\sigma$, by (b), the configuration on a fine bond is $e^{i\eta\mathbf H_Y}U_{k+1}$ if the bond lies in a component $Y$ of $Y(\sigma)$ and $U_{k+1}$ otherwise. A factor of the word therefore has its value at $U_{k+1}$ unless a component meets the set on which it depends: for a transport, the bonds of its arc, and for an insertion $Y_W$ of $\mathfrak B$, its domain $X_W$. We call a component \emph{active} for $\mathfrak B$ if it contains a fine bond of a loop or meets the domain of a member of $\mathfrak B$. If a component of $Y(\sigma)$ is not active, the word does not depend on its parameters and the term vanishes, so that only the sets $\sigma$ all of whose components are active remain.

\emph{Arcs and groups.} Let $Y$ be an active component and $C_i$ a loop. Let $S'_i(Y)$ be the set of the fine bonds of $C_i$ in $Y$ together with the intervals on $C_i$ of the members $W$ of $\mathfrak B$ whose domains $Y$ meets. If $S'_i(Y)$ is not empty, let $I_i(Y)$ be the arc of $C_i$ obtained by removing the longest maximal run of bonds which are not in $S'_i(Y)$, the first such run from the base point of $C_i$ if several are longest, and then enlarged at each end to the end of any interval of a member of $\mathfrak B$ which it cuts; $I_i(Y) = C_i$ if $S'_i(Y) = C_i$. The intervals of the members of a family are pairwise disjoint, so that the enlargement adds at most one interval at each end, and every interval of a member lies in $I_i(Y)$ or is disjoint from it. Two active components are \emph{linked} if for some $i$ their arcs on $C_i$ share a bond, and the \emph{groups} $G$ of the term are the classes of the equivalence relation which this generates; the arc set of a group is $I(G) \coloneqq \bigcup_i\bigcup_{Y \in G}I_i(Y)$, a union of arcs of the loops, and the arc sets of distinct groups are disjoint. A group whose arc set meets two loops contains a component with bonds or touched domains on both, because arcs on different loops share no bond.

For a group $G$ let $Y_G$ be the expression obtained by applying the operations $\prod_{Y \in G}\prod_{\Delta \subset Y}\int_0^1\dd s(\Delta)\,\partial_{s(\Delta)}$ to the ordered product, along each arc of $I(G)$, of the insertions of the members of $\mathfrak B$ with intervals in that arc and of the transports of $e^{i\eta\mathbf H(s, B')}U_{k+1}$ along the pieces of the arc between them, at $s(\Delta) = 0$ for every cube outside the components of $G$. If $I(G)$ lies on one loop, $Y_G$ is a linear map between the fibres at the two ends of its arc; otherwise it is a tensor with one such factor for each of its arcs. The parameters of the components of another group do not enter $Y_G$, since by (b) the factors on the arcs of $G$ depend only on the parameters of the components which meet the sets on which they depend, and these are components of $G$. A member of $\mathfrak B$ whose interval lies in no arc of a group, and whose domain no component meets, keeps its value at $U_{k+1}$; we call it \emph{untouched}.

\begin{lemma}[The Expansion of a Word]\label{lem:word-expansion}
Let $\mathfrak B$ be a family of the step $k$ none of whose insertions depends on an outer fluctuation field. For real fluctuation fields in the support of the test functions of the step, the word of $\mathfrak B$ at the configuration of integration equals
\begin{equation}\label{eq:word-expansion}
    \sum_{\mathfrak G}w(\mathfrak B, \mathfrak G)\;,
\end{equation}
where the sum runs over the families $\mathfrak G$ of groups which are compatible with $\mathfrak B$, the empty family included, and $w(\mathfrak B, \mathfrak G)$ is the word formed, in the cyclic order of each loop, from the tensors $Y_G$, $G \in \mathfrak G$, the insertions at $U_{k+1}$ of the untouched members of $\mathfrak B$, and the transports of $U_{k+1}$ between them. A family $\mathfrak G$ is \emph{compatible with} $\mathfrak B$ if each of its components is a connected union of cubes of $\sigma_k$ which is active for $\mathfrak B$, its components are pairwise disjoint and share no wall, the arc sets of its groups are pairwise disjoint, and each of its groups is a class of the relation of linked components. The term $\mathfrak G = \emptyset$ is the word of $\mathfrak B$ at $U_{k+1}$; for $\mathfrak B = \emptyset$ it is the product of the leading words of the step $k + 1$. Each $Y_G$ depends on the fluctuation field and on the configuration only on the components of $G$, and on $U_{k+1}$ along $I(G)$ and on the domains of the members of $\mathfrak B$ which $G$ contains.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:parameter-expansion} and the remarks which follow it, the word is the sum over the sets $\sigma$ all of whose components are active. For such a set the components of $Y(\sigma)$, with their arcs, determine the groups, and the integrand of the term of $\sigma$ is the word in which the factors on the arcs of the groups are evaluated at the configurations $e^{i\eta\mathbf H_Y}U_{k+1}$ and all other factors at $U_{k+1}$. The operations $\int_0^1\dd s(\Delta)\,\partial_{s(\Delta)}$ of the cubes of a component act only on the factors of its group, by (b), and the word is multilinear in its factors, so that the term of $\sigma$ is the word $w(\mathfrak B, \mathfrak G)$ of the family $\mathfrak G$ of these groups. The map $\sigma \mapsto \mathfrak G$ is a bijection onto the compatible families: given $\mathfrak G$, the set $\sigma$ consists of the cubes of its components, and the components of $Y(\sigma)$ are exactly these, because they are connected and no two of them share a wall. The locality of $Y_G$ is (b) with the locality of the factors of the word.
\end{proof}

At the first order in the fluctuation field the expansion has a symmetry which the count of the next subsections uses. A group with two or more components has no part which is linear in $B'$, because the factor of each of its components vanishes with $B'$ on that component, by \eqref{eq:localised-h-bound}. For a group $G = \{Y\}$ of one component which contains no member of $\mathfrak B$, the part of $Y_G$ linear in $B'$ is, by the transport identity $\mathrm{Hol}(e^{iX}U) = \prod_i\exp(i\operatorname{Ad}(\Gamma_i)X(b_i))\,\mathrm{Hol}(U)$ along an arc with the bonds $b_i$ and the partial transports $\Gamma_i$ of $U$, the operation of the cubes of $Y$ applied to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:first-order-part}
    i\eta\sum_{b \in S'(Y)}\mathrm{Hol}_{<b}(U_{k+1})\,\pi_*\bigl((H_0(s)B')(b)\bigr)\,\mathrm{Hol}_{\geq b}(U_{k+1})\;,
\end{equation}
which is odd in $B'$.
