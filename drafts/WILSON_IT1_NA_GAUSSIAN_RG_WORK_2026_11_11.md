## 3 The Heat Flow and Duhamel's Formula

{#NAG3.1} *Scales.* For t ≥ 0 let B_{<t} and B_{>t} be independent centred Gaussian 1-forms, as in #NAG1.3, of covariances C_{<t} := C_ε − C_{e^tε} and C_{>t} := C_{e^tε}, so B = B_{<t} + B_{>t} in law; Ċ_t := ∂_tC_{<t} = Ċ^{(e^tε)} ≥ 0 in Fourier space.
{#NAG3.2} *Generator.* For a function F of smooth 1-forms with continuous bounded second derivative put 𝓛_tF(φ) := ½𝔼D²F(φ)[Y_t, Y_t], Y_t centred Gaussian with covariance Ċ_t.
{#NAG3.3} *Lemma HF.* 𝔉_t(φ) := 𝔼χ(Hol_𝗀(φ + B_{<t})) is C¹ in t ≥ 0 with ∂_t𝔉_t = 𝓛_t𝔉_t.
{#NAG3.3-1} *Proof.* 𝔉_{t+h}(φ) = 𝔼𝔉_t(φ + Z_h) with Z_h independent of B_{<t}, centred, of covariance ∫_t^{t+h}Ċ_udu. {#NAG3.3-2} By Sobolev's embedding H³ ⊂ C⁰ on a neighbourhood of γ, sup_γ|Z̃_h| is at most a sum of L²-norms of ∂^αZ_h, |α| ≤ 3, whose second moments are O(h), the Fourier support of Ċ_u being bounded; Gaussian vectors in a Hilbert space have 𝔼‖·‖³ ≤ c(𝔼‖·‖²)^{3/2}, so 𝔼R = O(h^{3/2}). u ↦ 𝓛_u𝔉_t(φ) is continuous, so the right derivative is 𝓛_t𝔉_t(φ), and the same computation from t − h gives the left one. ∎

## 6 The Perimeter: a Polymer Gas of Chords

{#NAG6.1} *Grid.* Fix η ≤ r₀ := min(s, t)/4, put z := 𝗀² ∈ ℂ, 𝒢 := 𝒢_η (#NAG2.4-1), 𝔏 := 1 + ln(P(𝖱₄)/η), C_c := d_𝔤c_π² and C_× := 2c_Cc_𝖱^{−2}, c_𝖱 a constant of #NAG2.4-2 valid for 𝖱₁, …, 𝖱₄, necessarily ≤ 1. s and t lie in aℕ, a := uL^{−q} (#NG1.1). For k ∈ ℕ put h := a/(4k), parametrise 𝖱_n by arclength from o, so that its corners and r₀ lie in hℤ, and call hℤ ∩ [0, P(𝖱_n)) its grid, with γ′ the right derivative at corners. We take k so large that h ≤ η²/P(𝖱₄); then h ≤ η/2 and h ≤ r₀.
{#NAG6.1-1} *Chord sets.* A grid chord is a chord (#NAG2.3) with distinct grid endpoints, and a grid chord set S is a finite set of grid chords with pairwise distinct endpoints. Its colour factor is c(S) := Σ_aχ(T^{a_1}⋯T^{a_{2|S|}}), one factor per endpoint in the order of [0, P), later on the left, the two endpoints of a chord carrying the same index, summed over 1, …, d_𝔤; |c(S)| ≤ C_c^{|S|}, as ‖T^a‖ ≤ c_π. Put W_k(γ) := Σ_Sc(S)∏_{c∈S}(−z𝒢(c)h²).
{#NAG6.1-2} *Convergence.* By #NAG1.4-1 at φ = 0 and Isserlis, as in #NAG2.1-1, W(γ) = Σ_mβ_mz^m, β_m := ∫_{P>s_1>⋯>s_{2m}>0}Σ_pc(p)∏_{c∈p}(−𝒢(c))ds, p running over the pairings of the 2m points, each a chord set. The m-chord part of W_k is the Riemann sum of this integrand over distinct grid points; the integrand is bounded and continuous off the null set where two points coincide or one is a corner, and both are at most (C_cc_CP²/(2η²))^m/m! (#NAG2.4-2). So W_k → W uniformly on compact sets of z as k → ∞.
{#NAG6.2} *Factorisation.* For a grid chord set S = S₁ ⊔ S₂ with no chord of S₁ crossing one of S₂, c(S) = c(S₁)c(S₂). By induction over the crossing components of S₂ it suffices to take S₂ with connected crossing graph. A chord c₁ ∈ S₁ crosses no chord of S₂, so each chord of S₂ has both endpoints in one of the two open arcs cut out by c₁, crossing chords in the same one; so all of S₂ lies in one of them, and the other, joining the endpoints of c₁, contains no endpoint of S₂. Thus both endpoints of c₁ lie in one gap of S₂, an arc between cyclically consecutive endpoints of S₂.
{#NAG6.2-1} χ is cyclic, so the word of c(S) may be read around the loop from an endpoint of S₂: the S₂-letters with, in each gap j, the word of the S₁-chords there, which pair among themselves. Σ_aπ(g)T^aπ(g)^{−1} ⊗ π(g)T^aπ(g)^{−1} = Σ_aT^a ⊗ T^a, Ad_g being orthogonal (#NAG4.2), so each gap word, summed over its indices, commutes with π(G) and equals σ_j·1 by Schur's lemma. So c(S) = (∏_jσ_j)c(S₂); read around the loop, the word of c(S₁) is the product of the gap words in the same cyclic order, so c(S₁) = ∏_jσ_j. ∎
{#NAG6.3} *Polymers.* A polymer is a nonempty grid chord set with connected crossing graph, of weight w(γ) := c(γ)∏_{c∈γ}(−z𝒢(c)h²). Polymers γ, γ′ are incompatible, γ ≁ γ′, if a chord of one crosses or shares an endpoint with a chord of the other; so γ ≁ γ. The crossing components of a grid chord set are pairwise compatible polymers, and pairwise compatible polymers are the crossing components of their union, a grid chord set. By #NAG6.2, W_k is the polymer partition function of FV Definition 5.2 (p.221) with δ := 1 on compatible and 0 on incompatible pairs, which satisfies (5.1) and (5.2).
{#NAG6.4} *Crossing sums.* Let c₀ be a grid chord. A grid chord c crossing c₀ has one endpoint u inside arc(c₀) and the other, u ± ℓ(c), outside its closure, so the arc of length ℓ(c) from u to it passes the endpoint e of c₀ lying ahead of u in that direction, and u = e ∓ jh with 1 ≤ j < ℓ(c)/h. So for each ℓ ∈ hℕ at most 2ℓ/h such c have ℓ(c) = ℓ, and by #NAG2.4-2, Σ_{c crosses c₀}h²|𝒢(c)| ≤ 2Σ_{ℓ∈hℕ}hg(ℓ), g(ℓ) := c_Cℓmin(η^{−2}, (c_𝖱ℓ)^{−2}) for ℓ ≤ P/2 and g := 0 beyond.
{#NAG6.4-1} g increases on [0, η/c_𝖱] and decreases beyond, so Σ_ℓhg(ℓ) ≤ ∫g + h max g ≤ c_Cc_𝖱^{−2}(1/2 + ln(P/η)) + hc_C/(c_𝖱η), using c_𝖱 ≤ 1 ≤ P/η; with h ≤ η/2 and P ≤ P(𝖱₄) the crossing sum is at most C_×𝔏. The grid chords sharing an endpoint with c₀ give at most 2(P/h)h²c_Cη^{−2} ≤ 2c_C ≤ C_×𝔏, as h ≤ η²/P(𝖱₄). At most P/h grid chords have ℓ(c) = ℓ, and ℓ^{−2}e^{−r₀/ℓ} has integral 1/r₀ and maximum 4e^{−2}r₀^{−2}, so with h ≤ r₀, Σ_ch²|𝒢(c)|e^{−r₀/ℓ(c)} ≤ 2Pc_Cc_𝖱^{−2}/r₀. Finally Σ_ch²|𝒢(c)| ≤ c_CP²/(2η²).
{#NAG6.5} *Lemma NAG5.* Let δ₀ := (8e⁴C_cC_×)^{−1}, 𝒟 := {z: |z| < δ₀/𝔏}, γ one of 𝖱₁, …, 𝖱₄, h as in #NAG6.1, and w̄(γ′) := sup_𝒟|w(γ′)|. (i) W_k ≠ 0 on 𝒟, and log W_k = Σ_{m≥1}Σ_{γ_1,…,γ_m}φ(γ_1, …, γ_m)∏_jw(γ_j), φ the Ursell function of FV p.222, converges absolutely and is analytic on 𝒟. (ii) For every polymer γ₁, 1 + Σ_{m≥2}mΣ_{γ_2,…,γ_m}|φ(γ_1, …, γ_m)|∏_{j≥2}w̄(γ_j)e^{|γ_j|} ≤ e^{2|γ₁|}. (iii) Σ_{γ₁}w̄(γ₁)e^{3|γ₁|} ≤ K := e⁴C_cδ₀c_CP²/(𝔏η²).
{#NAG6.5-1} *Proof.* Let c₀ be a grid chord and m ≥ 1. A polymer of m chords incompatible with c₀ comes from m! ordered m-tuples of distinct chords with connected crossing graph. Choose an entry i ≁ c₀ (m ways) and a spanning tree of the crossing graph (m^{m−2} labelled trees, Cayley), and sum the entries outward from i: entry i gives at most 2C_×𝔏 and every other, crossing its parent, at most C_×𝔏 (#NAG6.4-1). {#NAG6.5-2} So for every polymer γ, Σ_{γ′≁γ}w̄(γ′)e^{|γ′|}e^{2|γ′|} ≤ Σ_{c₀∈γ}Σ_{m≥1}2·8^{−m} ≤ 2|γ|: FV (5.10) holds with a(γ) := 2|γ| for the weights w̄e^{|·|}, hence for w̄. The w(γ′) are polynomials in z bounded by w̄ on 𝒟, so FV Theorem 5.8 (p.228) gives (i), and FV Theorem 5.4 (p.224) for the weights w̄e^{|·|} gives (ii). {#NAG6.5-3} *Limit.* By (ii) and (iii), |log W_k| ≤ Σ_{γ₁}w̄(γ₁)e^{2|γ₁|} ≤ K on 𝒟 for all k. Along any subsequence, Montel's theorem gives a further one on which log W_k converges locally uniformly on 𝒟 to an analytic L with e^L = W (#NAG6.1-2) and L(0) = 0. A continuous logarithm of W on 𝒟 vanishing at 0 is unique, so log W_k → L =: log W on 𝒟; W ≠ 0 on 𝒟, and near 0, log W is the principal logarithm.
{#NAG6.6} *Span.* The span of a cluster (γ_1, …, γ_m) is the length of the shortest closed arc containing all endpoints of its chords. φ ≠ 0 only if the incompatibility graph of the γ_j is connected (FV p.222). Incompatible chords have intersecting closed arcs, so the union of the closed arcs of the cluster's chords is connected and contains all endpoints: it is an arc of length at least the span, or the loop. With N_X := Σ_j|γ_j| and L_X the largest ℓ(c), N_XL_X ≥ span.
{#NAG6.7} *Lemma NAG6.* On 𝒟, G(z) := Σ_nn_S(𝖱_n)log W(𝖱_n) satisfies |G(z)| ≤ C_G|z|, C_G := 24e⁴C_cc_CP(𝖱₄)/(c_𝖱²r₀).

Σn_S = 0 and Σn_SP(𝖱_n) = 0 (#NG1.3-1, P(𝖱_n) = 2nt + 2s) remove (A) and (B) from G_k := Σn_Slog W_k(𝖱_n).

{#NAG6.8} *Lemma NAG7.* For 𝗀²𝔏 ≤ δ₀/2, 𝔶_S(W; λ) = 𝗀²G₁ + 𝗀⁴G₂ + R, G₁ := −(C₂/2)Σn_SQ(𝖱_n; η), G₂ := −(C₂C_A/2)j(η), |R| ≤ 2C_Gδ₀^{−2}𝗀⁶𝔏².

For a spanning tree 𝒯 of g let R(𝒯) be the set of edges e ∉ 𝒯 of g that come after every edge of the path in 𝒯 joining the ends of e. We show that a spanning tree 𝒯 ⊆ G is 𝒯(G) if and only if G ∖ 𝒯 ⊆ R(𝒯).
