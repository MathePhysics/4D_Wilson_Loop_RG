## Setting

- {#RP.1} Points are x = (x₀, x₁, x₂, x₃), and e_μ is the unit vector of the μ-th axis. The torus has physical period T_ph in every direction, a fixed multiple of u (#NWTOA.1), and T_ph/u is even. The fine lattice has spacing ε = uL^{−K} and N_ε := (T_ph/u)L^K sites per direction, so N_ε is even and N_ε ≥ 12 (L ≥ 6, #T1.3).
  {#RP.1-1} The bare expectation is E_ε[F] := ∫Fρ₀dU/∫ρ₀dU, with ρ₀ = exp(−g₀^{−2}S(U) − E_ε), S(U) = Σ_p(1 − Re tr_N U(∂p)) over the unoriented plaquettes, E_ε a field-independent scalar, dU the product normalised Haar measure on G^E, E the positive bonds, and G ⊂ U(N) compact (V4 (eq:bare-action), L95–100). U(b^{−1}) = U(b)^{−1} (V4 L92). Cov_ε(X, Y) := E_ε[XY] − E_ε[X]E_ε[Y], bilinear (#PF0.1-2).
- {#RP.2} *The reflection.* θ(x) := (−x₀, x₁, x₂, x₃) mod N_ε is a bijection of the lattice. It fixes pointwise the hyperplanes P₀ := {x₀ ≡ 0} and P₁ := {x₀ ≡ N_ε/2}, and it maps oriented bonds to oriented bonds and plaquettes to plaquettes. For a configuration U let (θU)(b) := U(θb), θb the image of the oriented bond b. Then the holonomy of θU along a path γ is the holonomy of U along θγ.
  {#RP.2-1} Let Λ̄₊ := {x₀ ≡ m, 0 ≤ m ≤ N_ε/2} and Λ̄₋ := θΛ̄₊ = {x₀ ≡ m, N_ε/2 ≤ m ≤ N_ε}, so Λ̄₊ ∩ Λ̄₋ = P₀ ∪ P₁. A bond has x₀-coordinates {m} or {m, m + 1}, so both its endpoints lie in Λ̄₊ or both in Λ̄₋, and both lie in Λ̄₊ ∩ Λ̄₋ exactly when both lie in P₀ or both in P₁ (N_ε ≥ 4).
  {#RP.2-2} E₀ is the set of bonds with both endpoints in P₀ or both in P₁, E₊ the set of the other bonds with both endpoints in Λ̄₊, and E₋ := θE₊, so E = E₊ ⊔ E₀ ⊔ E₋. θ fixes every bond of E₀ with its orientation, since such a bond is orthogonal to e₀ and its endpoints are fixed.
  {#RP.2-3} 𝒜₊ is the set of bounded measurable F: G^E → ℂ that depend only on U restricted to E₊ ∪ E₀. For any bounded measurable F let (ΘF)(U) := conj F(θU); for F ∈ 𝒜₊, ΘF depends only on U restricted to E₋ ∪ E₀. Θ is antilinear, Θ(FG) = ΘF·ΘG, and Θc = conj c for constants.

## Reflection Positivity on the Lattice

- {#RP.3} *Lemma.* For F ∈ 𝒜₊, E_ε[F·ΘF] ≥ 0.
  {#RP.3-1} *The action splits.* As for bonds (#RP.2-1), a plaquette lies in Λ̄₊ or in Λ̄₋, and in both exactly when it lies in P₀ or in P₁. Let B(U) := Σ_{p⊂Λ̄₊, p⊄P₀∪P₁}(1 − Re tr_N U(∂p)) + ½Σ_{p⊂P₀∪P₁}(1 − Re tr_N U(∂p)). B ∈ 𝒜₊ is real.
  {#RP.3-2} By #RP.2, (θU)(∂p) is the holonomy of U around θp from one of its corners, in one of its two orientations. Re tr_N is invariant under conjugation, and Re tr_N(h^{−1}) = Re tr_N(h) for unitary h, so 1 − Re tr_N (θU)(∂p) = 1 − Re tr_N U(∂(θp)). θ maps the plaquettes in Λ̄₊ not in P₀ ∪ P₁ bijectively onto those in Λ̄₋ not in P₀ ∪ P₁, and maps each plaquette in P₀ ∪ P₁ to itself. So ΘB(U) = B(θU) = Σ_{p⊂Λ̄₋, p⊄P₀∪P₁}(1 − Re tr_N U(∂p)) + ½Σ_{p⊂P₀∪P₁}(1 − Re tr_N U(∂p)), and S = B + ΘB.
  {#RP.3-3} *Integration.* Write U = (U₊, U₀, U₋) by restriction to E₊, E₀, E₋; dU = dU₊dU₀dU₋. Let f := Fe^{−g₀^{−2}B} ∈ 𝒜₊. As B is real, F·ΘF·e^{−g₀^{−2}S} = f(U)·conj f(θU). The restriction of θU to E₊ ∪ E₀ is (φ(U₋), U₀), with φ: G^{E₋} → G^{E₊} given by φ(U₋)(b) := U₋(θb), read with U(b^{−1}) = U(b)^{−1}. Each coordinate of φ(U₋) is a coordinate of U₋, or its inverse for the bonds along e₀, whose images are reversed. Haar measure is invariant under inversion, so φ maps product Haar measure to product Haar measure.
  {#RP.3-4} By Fubini, ∫F·ΘF·e^{−g₀^{−2}S}dU = ∫dU₀(∫f(U₊, U₀)dU₊)·conj(∫f(φ(U₋), U₀)dU₋) = ∫dU₀|∫f(U₊, U₀)dU₊|² ≥ 0, and E_ε[F·ΘF] is this times e^{−E_ε}/∫ρ₀dU > 0.
- {#RP.4} *Consequences at fixed ε.*
  {#RP.4-1} θ is an involution of G^E preserving S (#RP.3-2) and the product Haar measure (#RP.3-3), so E_ε[ΘF] = conj E_ε[F] for every bounded measurable F.
  {#RP.4-2} ⟨F, G⟩ := E_ε[F·ΘG] is sesquilinear on 𝒜₊ and positive semidefinite by #RP.3, hence Hermitian, with |⟨F, G⟩|² ≤ ⟨F, F⟩⟨G, G⟩. For F, G ∈ 𝒜₊ with means c_F, c_G, constants lie in 𝒜₊, and #RP.4-1 gives ⟨F − c_F, G − c_G⟩ = E_ε[F·ΘG] − c_F·conj c_G = Cov_ε(F, ΘG). So Cov_ε(F, ΘF) ≥ 0, and |Cov_ε(F, ΘG)|² ≤ Cov_ε(F, ΘF)Cov_ε(G, ΘG).
  {#RP.4-3} *Translations.* For s ∈ ℤ let (T_sU)(b) := U(b + se₀) and τ_sF := F∘T_s. The measure is T_s-invariant. From θ(x − se₀) = θx + se₀, θ∘T_s = T_{−s}∘θ on configurations, so Θτ_sG = τ_{−s}ΘG and E_ε[τ_sF·Θτ_tG] = E_ε[τ_{s+t}F·ΘG] for all s, t ∈ ℤ.
  {#RP.4-4} Fix F ∈ 𝒜₊ with mean c_F, and integers s₁, …, s_n with every τ_{s_i}F ∈ 𝒜₊. The means of τ_{s_i}F and Θτ_{s_j}F are c_F and conj c_F (#RP.4-1), so ⟨τ_{s_i}F − c_F, τ_{s_j}F − c_F⟩ = Cov_ε(τ_{s_i}F, Θτ_{s_j}F) = Cov_ε(τ_{s_i+s_j}F, ΘF) by #RP.4-2–#RP.4-3. This Gram matrix is Hermitian positive semidefinite, and its entries depend on i, j only through s_i + s_j.

## Wilson Loops under the Reflection

- {#RP.5} Let C be a counterclockwise square (#332) in a plane parallel to a coordinate plane, with V(C) ⊂ Λ̄₊, and χ = dim(π)^{−1}tr π with π irreducible and unitary. Then W_χ(C) ∈ 𝒜₊ and ΘW_χ(C)(U) = conj χ(Hol_{θC}U) (V4 (eq:wilson-definition), #RP.2), θC the image of C with its image orientation. For unitary π, conj χ(h) = χ̄(h) = χ(h^{−1}), and χ is a class function.
  {#RP.5-1} (A) If the plane of C does not contain e₀, θ acts on it as the translation by −2x₀(C)e₀, so θC is a counterclockwise square and ΘW_χ(C) = W_χ̄(θC).
  {#RP.5-2} (B) If the plane of C contains e₀, θ reflects it, so θC is clockwise. With θ̂C the counterclockwise traversal of θC, Hol_{θC}U is conjugate to (Hol_{θ̂C}U)^{−1}, and ΘW_χ(C) = W_χ(θ̂C).
  {#RP.5-3} Write (θ̂C, χ′) := (θC, χ̄) in case (A) and (θ̂C, χ) in case (B). Then ΘW_χ(C) = W_{χ′}(θ̂C).

## The Continuum Inequalities

- {#RP.6} *The family.* Fix q ∈ ℕ. 𝒞_q is the set of counterclockwise squares (#332), each with a character as in #RP.5, of physical side pL^{−q}u ≤ T_ph/2 with p ∈ ℕ, each in a plane parallel to a coordinate plane, with corners in L^{−q}uℤ⁴, and contained in the open half-torus H₊ := {0 < x₀ < T_ph/2} (physical coordinates mod T_ph).
  {#RP.6-1} θ(y) := (−y₀, y₁, y₂, y₃) mod T_ph maps L^{−q}uℤ⁴ to itself and H₊ onto H₋ := {T_ph/2 < x₀ < T_ph}. So for C ∈ 𝒞_q, θ̂C (with χ′, #RP.5-3) is a counterclockwise square of the same side, in a plane parallel to the same coordinate plane, with corners in L^{−q}uℤ⁴, contained in H₋.
  {#RP.6-2} *Distance.* Let C₁, C₂ ∈ 𝒞_q. Every point of C₁ or θ̂C₂ lies on an edge along some e_μ with endpoints in L^{−q}uℤ⁴, so its coordinates other than the μ-th lie in L^{−q}uℤ. Take a on an edge along e_μ of C₁ and b on an edge along e_ν of θ̂C₂. The edges are disjoint, since H₊ ∩ H₋ = ∅. If a and b differ in a coordinate other than the μ-th and the ν-th, they differ in it by at least L^{−q}u on the circle ℝ/T_phℤ. Otherwise, if μ = ν, the two edges are disjoint closed arcs of one coordinate circle with endpoints in L^{−q}uℤ, so |a_μ − b_μ| ≥ L^{−q}u.
  {#RP.6-2.1} If μ ≠ ν, the edges are {a_ν fixed, a_μ ∈ I} and {b_μ fixed, b_ν ∈ J} with arcs I, J with endpoints in L^{−q}uℤ. They meet exactly when b_μ ∈ I and a_ν ∈ J, so one fails, say b_μ ∉ I. Then the distance from b_μ to a_μ ∈ I is at least L^{−q}u, as b_μ and the endpoints of I lie in L^{−q}uℤ; likewise if a_ν ∉ J. Hence the ℓ^∞ distance between C₁ and θ̂C₂ is at least L^{−q}u.
  {#RP.6-3} *Admissibility.* For C₁, C₂ ∈ 𝒞_q the pair (C₁, θ̂C₂) has q₁ = q₂ = q, and q₁₂ ≤ q since the displacement between corners lies in L^{−q}uℤ⁴. So the common stopping index of #MD2.3.4.15-11 (#PC1.1) is j, the least j ≥ q + q̄(j), which is also the stopping index j(C) of each single loop C of side pL^{−q}u (#MOMGD.30). A step-K′ unit is εL^{K′} = uL^{−j} ≤ L^{−q}u, so by #RP.6-2 the pair is at ℓ^∞ distance D ≥ 1 in step-K′ units. The squares are vertex-disjoint, have sides at most T_ph/2, and lie in #ND.8.5's family, so #PC7.2 applies.
  {#RP.6-4} *The limits.* For a loop C of 𝒞_q or its image θ̂C, with character χ, let κ_{C,χ} := κ^{(K′)}_{C,χ} (#366) at K′ = K − j. It is nonzero, as log κ_{C,χ} is defined (#ND.6.1), and it depends on C only through its side, the coordinate plane C is parallel to, and χ: δm_k and ζ_k are quantities of a straight line and a bent path through a period point in the orientations and corners of that plane, independent of the position of C, and may depend on the plane (#ND.6.1-1, #MD2.3.5.4–#MD2.3.5.12-3).
    {#RP.6-4-1} Let W(C) := lim_K E_ε[W_χ(C)]/κ_{C,χ}, which exists by #PF0.1-2.1, and W^×(C₁, θ̂C₂) := lim_K E_ε[W_{χ₁}(C₁)W_{χ₂′}(θ̂C₂)]/(κ_{C₁,χ₁}κ_{θ̂C₂,χ₂′}), which exists by #PC7.1–#PC7.2 and #RP.6-3. Let B(C) := W(C)W(θ̂C).
- {#RP.7} *Theorem.* (i) Let C ∈ 𝒞_q and s₁, …, s_n ∈ L^{−q}uℤ with every C_i := C + s_ie₀ in 𝒞_q, with C's character. Then B(C_i) = B(C), the matrix ([W^×(C_i, θ̂C_j) − B(C)]·conj B(C))_{i,j} is Hermitian positive semidefinite, and W^×(C_i, θ̂C_j) depends on i, j only through s_i + s_j. In particular [W^×(C, θ̂C) − B(C)]·conj B(C) ≥ 0.
  {#RP.7-1} (ii) Let C₁, C₂ ∈ 𝒞_q, with characters χ₁, χ₂, satisfy κ_{θ̂C_i,χ_i′} = κ_{C_i,χ_i} for i = 1, 2. This holds in case (B) of #RP.5, and in case (A) when χ_i is real. Let X := W^×(C₁, θ̂C₂) − W(C₁)W(θ̂C₂) and Y_i := W^×(C_i, θ̂C_i) − B(C_i). Then |X|²|B(C₁)||B(C₂)| ≤ (Y₁·conj B(C₁))(Y₂·conj B(C₂)).
  {#RP.7-2} (iii) If B(C) ≠ 0, then u_{ij} := W^×(C_i, θ̂C_j)/B(C) − 1 forms a Hermitian positive semidefinite matrix. In particular u_{ii} ≥ 0 and |u_{ij}|² ≤ u_{ii}u_{jj}. B(C) ≠ 0 holds for ḡ below the single-loop thresholds of C and θ̂C (#NV.6).
- {#RP.8} *Proof.* Fix K with K ≥ q and K′ = K − j ≥ 1. The physical point y is the fine site y/ε, L^{−q}uℤ⁴ ⊂ εℤ⁴, θ of #RP.6-1 is θ of #RP.2, and H₊ is {0 < x₀ < N_ε/2}. So for C ∈ 𝒞_q, V(C) ⊂ Λ̄₊ and W_χ(C) ∈ 𝒜₊. In #RP.7(i) let F := W_χ(C) and t_i := s_i/ε ∈ ℤ, so τ_{t_i}F = W_χ(C_i) ∈ 𝒜₊, and ΘW_χ(C_j) = W_{χ′}(θ̂C_j) by #RP.5-3.
  {#RP.8-1} κ_{C_i,χ} = κ_{C,χ} =: κ and κ_{θ̂C_i,χ′} = κ_{θ̂C,χ′} =: κ′, all sides and planes being equal (#RP.6-1, #RP.6-4). By translation invariance of E_ε, a := E_ε[W_χ(C)] = E_ε[W_χ(C_i)], and E_ε[W_{χ′}(θ̂C_j)] = E_ε[ΘW_χ(C_j)] = conj a by #RP.4-1. So with W_ren(·; ε) the quotients of #RP.6-4-1 at ε, B_ε(C_i) := W_ren(C_i; ε)W_ren(θ̂C_i; ε) = |a|²/(κκ′) for every i, and B(C_i) = B(C) in the limit.
  {#RP.8-2} At ε, W^×_ren(C_i, θ̂C_j; ε) − B_ε(C) = (E_ε[τ_{t_i}F·Θτ_{t_j}F] − |a|²)/(κκ′) = Cov_ε(τ_{t_i}F, Θτ_{t_j}F)/(κκ′), and conj B_ε(C) = |a|²/conj(κκ′). So the matrix of (i) at ε is the Gram matrix of #RP.4-4 times |a|²/|κκ′|² ≥ 0. It is Hermitian positive semidefinite, and its W^×-entries depend on i, j only through t_i + t_j. Its entries converge as K → ∞ (#RP.6-4-1), and a limit of Hermitian positive semidefinite matrices is Hermitian positive semidefinite. This proves (i).
  {#RP.8-3} For (ii) let F_i := W_{χ_i}(C_i), a_i := E_ε[F_i] and κ_i := κ_{C_i,χ_i} = κ_{θ̂C_i,χ_i′}. As in #RP.8-1–#RP.8-2, X_ε = Cov_ε(F₁, ΘF₂)/(κ₁κ₂), Y_{i,ε} = Cov_ε(F_i, ΘF_i)/κ_i², and B_ε(C_i) = |a_i|²/κ_i². So |X_ε|²|B_ε(C₁)||B_ε(C₂)| = |Cov_ε(F₁, ΘF₂)|²|a₁a₂|²/|κ₁κ₂|⁴, and (Y_{1,ε}conj B_ε(C₁))(Y_{2,ε}conj B_ε(C₂)) = Cov_ε(F₁, ΘF₁)Cov_ε(F₂, ΘF₂)|a₁a₂|²/|κ₁κ₂|⁴. #RP.4-2 gives the inequality at ε, and both sides converge.
  {#RP.8-4} (iii) is (i) times |B(C)|^{−2} > 0, as (W^× − B)conj B/|B|² = W^×/B − 1, with the 1 × 1 and 2 × 2 principal minors for the last claims.

## Finite Products

Then W(𝔇_a ∪ θ̂𝔇_b) := lim_K W_ren(𝔇_a ∪ θ̂𝔇_b; ε_K), with W_ren of #NV.9, exists (#RP.10-2), and:

  {#RP.9-2} (ii) W(θ̂𝔇_a) = conj W(𝔇_a), and the matrix (W(𝔇_a ∪ θ̂𝔇_b) − W(𝔇_a)·conj W(𝔇_b))_{a,b} is Hermitian positive semidefinite.
  {#RP.9-3} (iii) If W(𝔇_a) ≠ 0 and W(θ̂𝔇_a) ≠ 0 for every a, the matrix (W(𝔇_a ∪ θ̂𝔇_b)/(W(𝔇_a)W(θ̂𝔇_b)) − 1)_{a,b} is Hermitian positive semidefinite. The nonvanishing holds for ḡ below the thresholds of #NV.4 (one square) and #NV.5 (several) for the 𝔇_a and θ̂𝔇_a.
- {#RP.10} *Proof.*
  {#RP.10-1} *The configurations.* For 𝔇, 𝔇′ ∈ 𝔊_q, 𝔇 ∪ θ̂𝔇′ is a configuration: θ preserves L^{−q}uℤ⁴, sides and plane directions (#RP.6-1), the squares of θ̂𝔇′ are pairwise vertex-disjoint as θ is a bijection, and H₊ ∩ H₋ = ∅.
  {#RP.10-1.1} Two vertex-disjoint squares with corners in L^{−q}uℤ⁴ have disjoint edges: a common point of edges along e_μ and e_ν, μ ≠ ν, has all its coordinates in L^{−q}uℤ, and two intersecting edges along e_μ lie on one coordinate circle, where one contains an endpoint of the other; either way the squares would share a point of L^{−q}uℤ⁴ ⊂ εℤ⁴ (#RP.8), a common vertex.
  {#RP.10-2} #RP.6-2's argument uses only that the edges are disjoint with endpoints in L^{−q}uℤ⁴, so any two squares of 𝔇 ∪ θ̂𝔇′ are at ℓ^∞ distance ≥ L^{−q}u, at least 1 in step-K′ units (#RP.6-3). The stopping index is j (#NV.9, as in #RP.6-3), sides ≤ T_ph/2 give N_s ≥ 2max_iℓ_i, and the squares lie in #ND.8.5's family. So the limit exists by #NL10.7 (#PC7.1–#PC7.2 for two squares, #PF0.1-2.1 for one), and W_ren(∅; ε) = 1.
  {#RP.10-3} *At fixed ε.* Fix K with K′ = K − j ≥ 1. For 𝔇 ∈ 𝔊_q, F_𝔇 ∈ 𝒜₊, as each factor is (#RP.8) and 𝒜₊ is closed under products; ΘF_𝔇 = F_{θ̂𝔇} (#NV.9-1); and κ_𝔇 := Π_{D∈𝔇}κ_{D,χ_D} ≠ 0 (#RP.6-4), with F_∅ = κ_∅ = 1. κ-products multiply over unions of configurations, so E_ε[F_{𝔇_a}·ΘF_{𝔇_b}] = E_ε[F_{𝔇_a∪θ̂𝔇_b}] = κ_{𝔇_a}κ_{θ̂𝔇_b}W_ren(𝔇_a ∪ θ̂𝔇_b; ε) (#NV.9).
  {#RP.10-4} *(i).* By (★) (#RP.11), κ_{θ̂𝔇_b} = conj κ_{𝔇_b}. With G_a := F_{𝔇_a}/κ_{𝔇_a} ∈ 𝒜₊ and Θ antilinear, W_ren(𝔇_a ∪ θ̂𝔇_b; ε) = E_ε[G_a·ΘG_b] = ⟨G_a, G_b⟩ (#RP.4-2). So Σ_{a,b}c_a·conj c_b·W_ren(𝔇_a ∪ θ̂𝔇_b; ε) = ⟨Σ_ac_aG_a, Σ_bc_bG_b⟩ ≥ 0, and the matrix is Hermitian (#RP.4-2). Each entry converges as K → ∞ (#RP.10-2), so the limit matrix is Hermitian positive semidefinite.
  {#RP.10-5} *(ii).* By #RP.4-1 and (★), W_ren(θ̂𝔇; ε) = E_ε[ΘF_𝔇]/κ_{θ̂𝔇} = conj(E_ε[F_𝔇]/κ_𝔇) = conj W_ren(𝔇; ε). The mean of G_a is W_ren(𝔇_a; ε), so #RP.4-2 gives ⟨G_a − W_ren(𝔇_a; ε), G_b − W_ren(𝔇_b; ε)⟩ = W_ren(𝔇_a ∪ θ̂𝔇_b; ε) − W_ren(𝔇_a; ε)·conj W_ren(𝔇_b; ε), and the limit is taken as in #RP.10-4.
  {#RP.10-6} *(iii), without (★).* For large K, W_ren(𝔇_a; ε) and W_ren(θ̂𝔇_a; ε) are nonzero, so E_ε[F_{𝔇_a}] ≠ 0 and H_a := F_{𝔇_a}/E_ε[F_{𝔇_a}] ∈ 𝒜₊ has mean 1. As conj E_ε[F_{𝔇_b}] = E_ε[F_{θ̂𝔇_b}] (#RP.4-1), #RP.4-2 gives ⟨H_a − 1, H_b − 1⟩ = E_ε[F_{𝔇_a∪θ̂𝔇_b}]/(E_ε[F_{𝔇_a}]E_ε[F_{θ̂𝔇_b}]) − 1 = W_ren(𝔇_a ∪ θ̂𝔇_b; ε)/(W_ren(𝔇_a; ε)W_ren(θ̂𝔇_b; ε)) − 1, the κ-products cancelling. The denominators converge to W(𝔇_a)W(θ̂𝔇_b) ≠ 0, and the limit is taken as in #RP.10-4.

## Covariance of the Normalisations

- {#RP.11} *Lemma.* (C1)–(C3) of #RP.12 at every step k ≤ K′ imply, for every C ∈ 𝒞_q with character χ, (★) κ_{θ̂C,χ′} = conj κ_{C,χ}. In case (A) of #RP.5 only (C1) is used.
  {#RP.11-1} *Planes.* For the coordinate plane π spanned by e_μ, e_ν, μ < ν, let 𝒱_π := {±e_μ, ±e_ν}, and let 𝒞_π be the incoming and outgoing pairs at the corners of a counterclockwise square in π (V4 eq:corner-square-pairs). Consecutive sides of a square traversed once turn the same way at every corner, so 𝒞_π = {(v, w) ∈ 𝒱_π²: v ⊥ w, det(v, w) = s_π} for a sign s_π, the determinant taken in the basis (e_μ, e_ν). Let 𝒞_π^− be the set with −s_π.
  {#RP.11-2} *Coefficients.* By #ND.6.1 and #MD2.0.3.4, κ_{C,χ} = exp(−Σ_{k≤K′}δm_kP_{k−1}(C) + 4Σ_{k≤K′}ζ_k) with P_{k−1}(C) > 0, and, π being the plane of C, δm_k = −c_kΣ_{v∈𝒱_π}a^{(k)}_v(χ) and ζ_k = ¼Σ_{(v,w)∈𝒞_π}b^{(k)}_{v,w}(χ), with c_k > 0 independent of χ and π.
  {#RP.11-2.1} At k = 1, a^{(1)}_v = κ^σ_v, b^{(1)}_{v,w} = ζ^σ_{v,w} and c₁ = P(C_n)/(4LεP_0(C_n)) (V4 L1698 and eq:corner-square-factor; #MD2.0.3.4-1). At k ≥ 2, a^{(k)}_v = κ^{(k)}_v, b^{(k)}_{v,w} = ζ^{(k)}_{v,w} and c_k = (4Π_k)^{−1} (#MD2.3.5.5, #MD2.3.5.9); at an 𝐑-step, a^{(k)}_v also contains (Π_k/(LΠ^R_k))κ^R_v, and b^{(k)}_{v,w} the 𝐑-gas's corner coefficient of (v, w) (#MD2.3.5.5-1; #RP.12-6(c)).
  {#RP.11-3} *Case (A).* θ̂C has the side and plane of C (#RP.6-1) and χ′ = χ̄, so κ_{θ̂C,χ̄} = κ_{C,χ̄} (#RP.6-4). By (C1), δm_k and ζ_k at χ̄ are the conjugates of their values at χ, since c_k is real. So the exponent of #RP.11-2 at χ̄ is the conjugate of that at χ, P_{k−1}(C) being real, and κ_{C,χ̄} = conj κ_{C,χ}.
  {#RP.11-4} *Case (B).* χ′ = χ and κ_{θ̂C,χ} = κ_{C,χ} (#RP.6-4), so (★) says that κ_{C,χ} is real. It is if Σ_{v∈𝒱_π}a^{(k)}_v(χ) and Σ_{(v,w)∈𝒞_π}b^{(k)}_{v,w}(χ) are real for every k. By (C1), and (C3) at χ̄, conj a_v(χ) = a_v(χ̄) = a_{−v}(χ); as −𝒱_π = 𝒱_π, the first sum is real.
  {#RP.11-4.1} Likewise conj b_{v,w}(χ) = b_{v,w}(χ̄) = b_{−w,−v}(χ). The map (v, w) ↦ (−w, −v) takes 𝒞_π onto 𝒞_π^−, as det(−w, −v) = −det(v, w), so conj Σ_{𝒞_π}b_{v,w}(χ) = Σ_{𝒞_π^−}b_{v,w}(χ). As e₀ ∈ π, θ restricted to π has determinant −1, so (v, w) ↦ (θv, θw) takes 𝒞_π^− onto 𝒞_π, and (C2) gives Σ_{𝒞_π^−}b_{v,w}(χ) = Σ_{𝒞_π^−}b_{θv,θw}(χ) = Σ_{𝒞_π}b_{v,w}(χ). So the second sum is real.
  {#RP.11-5} *Necessity.* #RP.9(i) for 𝔇₁ = ∅ and 𝔇₂ = {C} is Hermitian, so W(θ̂C) = conj W(C), while W_ren(θ̂C; ε) = conj W_ren(C; ε)·conj κ_{C,χ}/κ_{θ̂C,χ′} by #RP.4-1. So if W(C) ≠ 0, then κ_{θ̂C,χ′}/conj κ_{C,χ} → 1: the stated normalisations satisfy #RP.9(i) only if (★) holds asymptotically.
