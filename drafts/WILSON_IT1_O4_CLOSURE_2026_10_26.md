## What the Objects Are and What That Forces

*The species are terms of one expansion of one integral.* On a labelled term with final region Λ, V4 integrates the young variable a ∈ 𝔤^{D(Λ)} against K^Λ_{ξ;t}(a) = 𝟏_{𝒟^Λ}(a)exp[−½⟨a,Ha⟩ − t⟨a,ℓ_Λ⟩ + P^Λ_t(a)] (V4 sec:c5-4), with H = H_{D(Λ)D(Λ)}, ℓ_Λ = H_{D(Λ)J(Λ)}A^{Λ,Ψ}, and the cutoff 𝟏_{𝒟^Λ}(a) = Π_{b∈D(Λ)}𝟏{|a_b|_σ < r} on the support of the exterior cutoffs (V4 sec:c5-4, the paragraph before eq:c5-4-1; the norm is |·|_op or |·|_HS, V4 eq:marginal-two-norms and eq:literal-cutoff). Every cutoff is an indicator of one bond's a_b (V4 l.2754, property (i): "each χ_b a Borel function of a_b alone with values in [0,1]"). The translation B := a − tm (V4 sec:c5-4-2) centres the Gaussian: by eq:c5-4-2, ∫F(a)K^Λ_{ξ;t}(a) da = e^{½t²⟨m,Hm⟩}∫F(B+tm)Θ^t(B)exp[−½⟨B,HB⟩ + Σ_Y𝐖_t(Y,B)] dB with χ^t_b(B) = 𝟏{|B_b + tm_b|_σ < r}, Θ^t = Π_bχ^t_b, 𝐖_t(Y,B) = 𝐕^Λ_{ξ,t}(Y,B+tm), and the constant e^{½t²⟨m,Hm⟩} "is the same in the numerator and the denominator of every conditional expectation" (eq:c5-4-3) "and is never estimated". The species are the terms of the polymer and source-insertion expansions of these normalised integrals (V4 lem:c5-4-1, lem:c5-4-2, sec:c5-5): cluster integrals of products of Mayer factors, cutoffs, cutoff differences (V4 lem:c5-5-6, *Marked Differences*), and the source, against the conditioned Gaussian density V4 eq:fixed-kernel-density. The shift m is a parameter of the expansion, not of the integral: any real vector m with |m_b|_σ ≤ r/4 (eq:c5-4-1 for V4's m = −H^{−1}ℓ_Λ; IT-1 §3j(1) for the local m̂) gives a valid expansion of the same integral, and different shifts give different species. This is the fact behind closure §6.4's remark that "the split between the reference Gaussian and the host tilts depends on the centre".

*What a complex background does.* At a complex 𝐔 in the ρ₁-polydisc around a real base point U₀ (‖a′‖_∞ < ρ₁ := α_{1,1}/L in V3's fine units, IT-1 D1(iii)′ l.902 and §3e(4) l.1131–1133; CRE (2.39) at j = n = 1), the data H_𝐔, ℓ_𝐔, and the primitives are analytic in 𝐔 (IT-1 §3e(3.1), (3.6)), and the local shift m̂(𝐔) = −λP_𝐔Σ_{n≤N}K_𝐔ⁿℓ_𝐔 is analytic too (closure 6.3 *Complex bound*, 6.4 *Shift*). The real integration variable cannot be translated by the complex vector tm̂(𝐔) (IT-1 (3.12) *The problem*). Two representations are available at 𝐔: (F) the frozen-centre representation, the translation by the real tm̂₀, m̂₀ := m̂(U₀), with the residual linear term −t⟨B,δℓ⟩, δℓ := H_𝐔m̂₀ + ℓ_𝐔, put into the host potentials as a tilt (IT-1 (3.12), §3j(6) l.1757); (M) the analytic continuation in 𝐔 of the real function U ↦ (species built at U with the centre tm̂(U)), which closure §6.4 takes as the carried factor. (F) is analytic in 𝐔 on the polydisc, since nothing but analytic data enters it (§2 below). (M) exists only if the real function is analytic in the background.

*Structural consequence 1: (M) does not exist for the marked species.* The source-insertion expansion telescopes the cutoffs and Mayer factors between t = 0 and t = 1 (V4 lem:c5-5-6; IT-1 §3j(4)(e), l.1730: "its measure is the t = 0 measure plus marked cutoff differences"). A marked term carries, for one bond b, the bond's own cutoff at one centre and a host's differenced cutoff at the other: V4 l.2974 bounds exactly such a product, "(1 − χ⁰_b)χ¹_b ≤ 𝟏_{S_b}(B_b)", S_b the shell r − |m_b|_σ ≤ |x|_σ < r + |m_b|_σ (V4 lem:c5-5-5). As a function of the shift m_b, the integral of an analytic integrand over {|B_b|_σ ≥ r, |B_b + m_b|_σ < r} is not analytic at m_b = 0 (§3 below: in one dimension its one-sided derivatives at 0 are −g(r) and g(−r)), and for the operator norm it is only piecewise analytic in the direction of m_b. The real species therefore fail to be real-analytic in the background at every background where a marked bond has m̂_b = 0, and the ρ₁-polydisc around a base point contains such backgrounds for the marked bonds b with |m̂_b(U₀)|_σ below the variation c_hC_Δρ₁ϑ_Vr C_μ of m̂_b over the polydisc (closure 6.3(d); §1 below): the far marks, whose weight ϖ_b ∝ |m̂_b|_σ is tiny but not zero. So there is no analytic continuation of the marked species with the centre tm̂(𝐔), and O4 as posed ("the carried species' activities, as analytic functions of 𝐔") can only be answered for (F).

*Structural consequence 2: (F) at the base point of each polydisc is the carried factor.* (F) expands the same integral at every real point of the polydisc (the translation identity eq:c5-4-2 holds for every real vector; IT-1 (3.12) *The tilt in the host potentials*: "the identity is exact"), is analytic on the polydisc, and is bounded there by IT-1 §3j(6) (with §3e(4)) at the frozen centre, with the complex tilt marks and the factor τ̄ (§2). At the base point itself it is the real step-1 species of D(1)(ii). The later steps' consumers use the carried factors on polydiscs around real base points (map D2.2(6)(e); closure §5.0 "the att bounds over the ω-polydiscs"), through their values at real points, their bounds, and Cauchy estimates on the polydisc (closure §5.1–5.2); none uses a single analytic function on Bałaban's whole space U^c_j(X, α₀, α₁) of RG1 p.262 (Definition, (1.11)–(1.14)). So the carried t > 0 species of a term are defined, on each polydisc, as the (F)-species at that polydisc's base point: re-frozen at every step. Their labels, dependence sets, and bounds are those of §3j(6) at every base point, with constants independent of the base point (§2(e)), which is all the carry lemma (closure §5) reads.

*Structural consequence 3: one centre per term.* The leading word and the lw slots are the expansion of the source f_ξ at B = 0 (IT-1 §3j(4) *Identity*; closure 6.5), so they are terms of the same expansion as the t > 0 species and must use its centre. Closure §6.4 gives them the moving centre m̂(𝐔) and the t > 0 species the open bound O4; with (F) the leading word on the polydisc is f_ξ(m̂₀) and the lw slots are Y_i at m̂₀, as IT-1 (3.12) *What else uses m* had it before the amendment of closure 6.4. Closure §6.1–6.4's bounds hold a fortiori (the complex sup bound 6.3(c) covers the real m̂₀; the Λ-dependence and the dependence sets are those of §3j(2) at U₀; the touching acts through the stencil background only). The amendment is for the user's review (§4(e)).

*Structural consequence 4: no new booking.* The per-bond costs of the complex background in (F) are the host tilt τ̄ (inside ω̂_t) and the complex tilt marks 𝔪^δ_Y ≤ C^δ_bdρ₁ϑ_Vr²Σ_{b:Y_b=Y}Σ_{e∈J_col(Λ)}e^{−μ_xd(b,e)} (IT-1 (3.12) *New host marks*, §3j(6) l.1757), which closure 7.1 *Host marks* already carries inside C_h and hence inside C_{B,1} ⊂ C_A(1), with the remark "if O4 closes by its stated route (a host tilt at the centre tm̂(𝐔)), this term of C_h and K₁'s condition (7.2) are to be re-checked". O4 closes by (F), so C_h, C_A(1), §6.5's share 3·2^dlog 2, and K₁'s condition stand as written; §5 below checks the count. The reserve |Re m̂(𝐔)_b|_σ ≤ r/3 of the planned route is not needed: the cutoffs stay at the real m̂₀ with (c5-4-1)'s r/4.

## 2. The Frozen-Centre Representation on a Polydisc

*(a) Definition.* Let U₀ be a real background in the support of a term's indicators, 𝒫(U₀) := {𝐔 = e^{iηa′}U₀ : ‖a′‖_∞ < ρ₁} its polydisc (IT-1 D1(iii)′ l.902), and m̂₀ := m̂(U₀) the real local shift of IT-1 §3j(1) at U₀, with |m̂₀,b|_σ ≤ r/4 (the clipping λ) and h(m̂₀,b) ≤ 2C_mϑ_VrΣ_{e∈J₁(Λ)}e^{−μ_xd(b,e)} (§3j(5)). For 𝐔 ∈ 𝒫(U₀) and B := a − tm̂₀ (a real translation),
  −½⟨a,H_𝐔a⟩ − t⟨a,ℓ_𝐔⟩ = −½⟨B,H_𝐔B⟩ − t⟨B,δℓ_𝐔⟩ − t²(½⟨m̂₀,H_𝐔m̂₀⟩ + ⟨m̂₀,ℓ_𝐔⟩),  δℓ_𝐔 := H_𝐔m̂₀ + ℓ_𝐔,
the identity of IT-1 (3.12) with m̂₀ in place of m₀ (§3j(6) l.1757: "At complex backgrounds (3.12)'s real shift becomes m̂₀ := m̂(U₁) … and the two tilts add"). The tilt splits as δℓ_𝐔 = τ̂_N(U₀) + (H_𝐔 − H_{U₀})m̂₀ + (H_𝐔 − H_{U₀})_{DJ}A^{Λ,Ψ}: the real tilt τ̂_N := H_{U₀}m̂₀ + ℓ_{U₀} of §3j(4) (zero for the exact shift, of size C_shε_Nϑ_Vr for the local one) and (3.12)'s complex tilt δℓ, now with m̂₀ for m₀, bounded entrywise by h_ℂ(δℓ(b)) ≤ C_δρ₁ϑ_VrΣ_{e∈J₁(Λ)}e^{−μ_Hd(b,e)}, C_δ := C_ee^{μ_Hρ_L}(4(2ρ_L+1)⁴C_m + 1) (IT-1 l.1107, with §3j(5)'s 2C_m in place of C_m where the entry bound is used; the closure's §6.4 *Shift* already records this). The (F)-species at 𝐔 are the terms of V4's expansions (lem:c5-4-1, lem:c5-4-2, sec:c5-5 with IT-1 §3j(4)(a)–(e)) for the data (H_𝐔, the fixed real cutoffs χ^t_b(B) = 𝟏{|B_b + tm̂₀,b|_σ < r}, the hosts 𝐖_t(Y,B;𝐔) = Θ^t_{E(Y)}(B)𝐏_t(Y,B;𝐔) with 𝐏_t(Y,B;𝐔) = Σ_{𝔭 near, Y_𝔭=Y}𝔭_𝐔(B + tm̂₀ + tA^{Λ,Ψ}) (V4 proof of lem:c5-5-6, l.2958, and eq:c-potential-definition l.701), and the tilt put into the hosts, W̃_t(Y,B;𝐔) := 𝐖_t(Y,B;𝐔) − tΘ^t_{E(Y)}(B)Σ_{b:Y_b=Y}⟨B_b,δℓ_𝐔(b)⟩ (IT-1 (3.12) *The tilt in the host potentials*, with the host assignment b ↦ Y_b of §3j(4)). The constant e^{−t²(…)} is common to the numerator and the denominator of every ratio and is never estimated (V4 eq:c5-4-3; IT-1 (3.12)).

*(b) Exactness at real points.* At every real 𝐔 = U ∈ 𝒫(U₀) ∩ {real}, (a) is V4's identity eq:c5-4-2 for the integral at U with the real shift vector m̂₀ in place of m: eq:c5-4-2 is the change of variables a = B + tm for an arbitrary real vector m (its proof completes the square and translates), so it holds for m̂₀, and the residual linear term −t⟨B,δℓ_U⟩ is exactly what the completion of the square leaves when m̂₀ ≠ −H_U⁻¹ℓ_U. IT-1 (3.12) *The tilt in the host potentials*: "the identity is exact" on Θ^t's support, where Θ^t_{E(Y)} = 1. So the (F)-species at a real U expand the same integral as the moving-centre species at U, with a different split between the reference Gaussian and the hosts; the integral, the conditional expectations (eq:c5-4-3), and Ξ^{f,t} are the same functions of U. The (F)-species at the base point itself are the real species of D(1)(ii) at U₀ in IT-1 §3j(4)'s form.

## 3. Why the Moving-Centre Continuation Does Not Exist

*(a) The host-mark terms carry cutoffs at two centres.* V4's hosts are defined with their own local-box cutoff: 𝐕_ξ(Y,a) = Σ_{𝔭 near, Y_𝔭=Y}𝔭(a) if |a(b)| < r for all b ∈ E(Y), and 0 otherwise (eq:c-potential-definition, l.701; l.990: "every selected host Y comes with the factors χ_b, b ∈ E(Y) ⊆ E(Y₀)"). Translated, 𝐖_t(Y,B) = Θ^t_{E(Y)}(B)𝐏_t(Y,B), Θ^t_{E(Y)} = Π_{b∈E(Y)}χ^t_b (proof of lem:c5-5-6, l.2958). The source-insertion telescoping between t = 0 and t = 1 ("Order these factors (bonds first, then hosts) and telescope … Each term has exactly one differenced factor, the mark") produces, at a host mark Y, a term whose bond factors are at t = 1 and whose differenced Mayer factor is
  M¹_Y − M⁰_Y = ∫₀¹[𝐏₁e^{t_Y𝐏₁} − 𝐏₀e^{t_Y𝐏₀}] dt_Y + (1 − Θ⁰_{E(Y)})∫₀¹𝐏₀e^{t_Y𝐏₀} dt_Y
(l.2962). The "cutoff part" (1 − Θ⁰_{E(Y)}(B))Π_{b∈E(Y₀)}χ¹_b(B) contains, for each b ∈ E(Y), the product χ¹_b(B_b)(1 − χ⁰_b(B_b)) = 𝟏{|B_b + m_b|_σ < r}𝟏{|B_b|_σ ≥ r}: cutoffs of the same bond at the two centres. V4 bounds it by 𝟏_{S_b}(B_b) (l.2964, "on the support of χ¹_b, (1 − χ⁰_b)χ¹_b ≤ 𝟏_{S_b}(B_b)") and pays it with ϖ_b ≤ C_Vr^{d_G−1}|m_b|_σ (lem:c5-5-5); the second ϖ_b in 𝔪_b = 2ϖ_b is exactly this host-generated bond mark ("one for the cutoffs of the hosts containing b, paid by (c5-5-T)(iii)"). These terms are a genuine part of the expansion, not of its bound: the source-insertion bound (prop:c5-5-9) is the sum over them.

*(b) The one-sided lens is not analytic in the shift.* Fix such a term and the bond b, and substitute B_{b′} = y_{b′} − m_{b′} for the other bonds b′ ∈ E(Y₀) (their factors are at t = 1 and become fixed; the rest of the integrand, G say, is analytic in (B, m), since the Gaussian, the density, the undifferenced Mayer factors, and the source are analytic in B on the enlarged box, l.2958). The term's dependence on m_b is through
  I(m_b) := ∫_{|x|_σ ≥ r, |x + m_b|_σ < r} G(x; m) dx = ∫_{|y|_σ<r}𝟏{|y − m_b|_σ ≥ r}G(y − m_b; m) dy.
In one variable (d_G = 1, |·| the absolute value, G > 0 analytic and m-independent for the model): for 0 < m < 2r, I(m) = ∫_{−r−m}^{−r}G(x) dx, so I′(0⁺) = G(−r); for −2r < m < 0, I(m) = ∫_r^{r−m}G(x) dx, so I′(0⁻) = −G(r). The one-sided derivatives differ by G(−r) + G(r) > 0: I has a kink at m = 0 and is not analytic there. In general: I(0) = 0 and I(m) ≥ (min_{shell}G)·Leb{|x|_σ ≥ r, |x + m|_σ < r} ≥ c_r|m|_σ for small m (V4 lem:c5-5-5's ϖ_b is this volume's upper bound, of the same order), so I(m) + I(−m) ≥ 2c_r|m|_σ, whereas an analytic I with I(0) = 0 has I(m) + I(−m) = O(|m|²). For the operator norm the ball {|x|_op < r} has a non-smooth boundary (d_G ≥ 2), so I is only piecewise analytic in the direction of m_b even away from 0.

*(c) Consequence for the moving-centre species.* The real moving-centre species at the real background U is a sum of such terms composed with U ↦ m̂_b(U). Its real-analytic continuation in U, bond by bond, has a radius bounded by the distance of m̂_b(U₀) to the singular set {m_b = 0} of I (and, for σ = op, to the non-smooth set). The inductive hypothesis bounds |m̂_b(U)|_σ from above (closure 6.3(c), IT-1 §3j(5)) and never from below; the displacement of m̂_b over 𝒫(U₀) is of the same order e^{−μ_xd(b,J_col(Λ))}ρ₁ϑ_Vr as the far bonds' |m̂_b(U₀)|_σ itself (§1), and a bond with m̂_b(U₀) = 0 (a symmetric background, or by accident) has radius 0 at that term. So no analytic continuation of the moving-centre species to 𝒫(U₀) exists uniformly in U₀, and the object named in closure §6.4 *The step's shift convention* ("the analytic continuation of a carried t > 0 species has the centre tm̂(𝐔)") and in O4 is not available. What is available is (F) (§2), which is what IT-1 §3e(4) l.1131–1133 and §3j(6) l.1757 bound, and what §3e(4) names: "Υ¹_W, the words U_j and f_ξ(m₀) are analytic in a′", the leading word at the frozen m₀.

*(d) Where the obstruction would disappear (not derived here).* With hosts defined without their own cutoff (𝐏_t in place of 𝐖_t = Θ^t_{E(Y)}𝐏_t, bounded on the enlarged box {|a_b|_σ ≤ 2r} where V4 already has the primitives analytic with |𝔭| ≤ C_pg₀s̄³, l.2958 and rem:c5-4-5) every term of the telescoping is, after the substitution of (b), an integral of an analytic integrand over fixed sets or a plain difference χ¹_b − χ⁰_b, which integrated against an analytic G is ∫_{|y|<r}[G(y − m_b) − G(y)] dy, analytic in m_b. The moving-centre species would then be analytic in the background and O4 would close in its posed form. This is a change of V4's host definition (eq:c-potential-definition) with a re-check of every host bound that uses the local box (l.990: "only the values on the local boxes enter"); it is not needed, since (F) suffices for the consumers (§4), and it is recorded for the V3/V4 overhaul only.

## 4. Analyticity Domains, Radii, and the Amendment

*(a) What the consumers read.* The later steps use the carried factors of D(1)(iii) through (i) their values at real backgrounds (the real step), (ii) sup bounds on ω-polydiscs around real base points (map D2.2(6)(e); closure §5.0: "the att bounds over the ω-polydiscs"; closure §5.4: on 𝒟^{sub}_{k+1} "the complex part of the background is bounded by the top-layer radius α_{1,k+1}L^{−(k+1)} per fine bond on every layer"), and (iii) Cauchy estimates for the touching (closure §5.1–5.2: the derivative of a carried factor in the stencil background is bounded by its sup over the polydisc divided by the radius). Bałaban's own analyticity spaces are of the same kind: U^c_j(X, α₀, α₁, γ₀) of RG1 p.262 (Definition, (1.11)–(1.14): 𝐔 = U′U with U′ = exp iξA′, |A′|, |∇A′| < α₁, a tube around the real configurations), and his bounds are stated as sup bounds there and used through Cauchy's formula on circles of radius α₂ (RG2 p.7 (1.22): 1/|t_□| = 8B₀C₁e^{16κ₁}α₂⁻¹g_k|B|; CRE p.277: "we have to use a part of the analyticity domains … powers of 1/2 … much greater than bounds on this function"). No consumer uses a single analytic function on the union of all polydiscs.

*(b) Radii.* On 𝒫(U₀) the (F)-species are analytic (§2(c)) with the sup bound §2(d). The consumer at step k + 1 evaluates on backgrounds whose complex part is ≤ α_{1,k+1}L^{−(k+1)} per fine bond (closure §5.4), and α_{1,k+1}L^{−(k+1)} ≤ (1 + β₀)²k^{1/2}α_{1,1}L^{−(k+1)} = (1 + β₀)²k^{1/2}L^{−k}ρ₁ (CRE (2.6)–(2.7) at j = 1, as closure §5.4 uses them). Since k^{1/2} ≤ L^{k−1} for L ≥ 2 and k ≥ 1, this is ≤ (1 + β₀)²ρ₁/L ≤ ρ₁/2 once L ≥ 2(1 + β₀)², a choice of L before g₀. So the consumer's complex backgrounds lie in the polydisc of radius ρ₁/2 around their real part, and the Cauchy radius left for the touching inside 𝒫(U₀) is ρ₁/2 per fine bond. This is the margin closure §5.2's Cauchy estimates have: if §5.2's touching constant C_X was defined with the full radius ρ₁, it is to be doubled, and the ledger K₁ ≥ 4(1 + C_X)B₁ + 12·2^dlog 2 (closure §7.2) absorbs a doubling of C_X by a choice of constants before g₀ (flagged for the user's check of §5.2's definition; this note edits no other file).

*(c) Re-freezing.* At step k + 1 the polydisc is 𝒫_{k+1}(U₀^{(k+1)}) around the real base point of that step (map D2.2(6)(e)); the carried species on it are the (F)-species at m̂₀^{(k+1)} := m̂(U₀^{(k+1)}). At a real point U common to two polydiscs the two (F)-representations expand the same integral (§2(b)) and agree as functions; the species differ term by term (a different split), but every consumer reads a bound or a value of the whole factor (the sum of the species of the term), which is the same. The label structure, the dependence sets R_dep = R_t + 6L + 2 of closure §6.4, and the decay rate μ_x are those of §3j(2) and (5), which hold at every real base point with the same constants. So the carry lemma (closure §5) is applied with the base-point-independent constants of §2(d).

*(d) The factor's continuation is unique.* The complex extension of a term at 𝐔 ∈ 𝒫(U₀) is defined by evaluating the step's construction at the complex data (H_𝐔, ℓ_𝐔, 𝔭_𝐔) with the real cutoffs (IT-1 §3e(4): "The indicators stay outside as factors"), which is (F); it is analytic on 𝒫(U₀) and agrees with the real factor on the real section, which is a totally real submanifold of maximal dimension of the connected 𝒫(U₀), so by the identity theorem it is the unique analytic function on 𝒫(U₀) with this property. "The analytic continuation of the carried factor to 𝒫(U₀)" is therefore well defined and equals the sum of the (F)-species, whatever centre is used to expand it: the continuation exists for the factor (the sum), not for the individual moving-centre species. O4 asked for the bound of the species as analytic functions; the species to be carried are (F)'s, whose sum is that continuation.

*(e) The amendment to closure §6.4 (for the user's review).* Closure §6.4 *The step's shift convention* continues every carried factor "with m̂(𝐔)" and leaves the t > 0 species open. With (F): (i) the carried t > 0 species on 𝒫(U₀) are the (F)-species at m̂₀ = m̂(U₀); (ii) on the same term the leading word is f_ξ(m̂₀) and the lw slots Y_i are evaluated at m̂₀ (IT-1 §3e(4) l.1133 and (3.12) *What else uses m*, as before closure 6.4's change); (iii) the lw-slot bounds of closure §6.1–6.4 hold a fortiori: the sup bound 6.3(c) h_ℂ(m̂(𝐔)_b) ≤ C_m^ℂϑ_Vr covers the real m̂₀ (the 𝐔 = U₀ case), the Λ-dependence and the dependence set R_dep come from §3j(2) at U₀, and the touching of the lw slots acts through the stencil background U_s only (the m̂-dependence on 𝐔 drops out), which removes the term "the touching through m̂" from 6.4's slot derivative bound and shrinks its constant; (iv) closure 6.3(d)'s displacement bound and 6.3(a)–(c) remain in use for the lw slots' analyticity and for §3(c)'s far-mark remark, and nothing else. The amendment changes no constant of the ledger (§5).
