## What the objects are

- *The density.* ρ_k = Σ_{{Ω_j},{Λ_j}} χ_k(Ω_k)𝐓_k exp A_k (CRE (2.18), p.257). Here 𝐓_k = Π_j𝐓^{(j)} is the product of the integrals over the old fields V_j in the large-field regions Z_j = Λ_j^c ((2.3), (2.19)–(2.22), p.258), and A_k = −A + 𝐄_k + 𝐑_k + 𝐁_k − E_k ((2.23)).
  A term of ρ_k is the summand of one sequence {Ω_j, Λ_j}. Its pending variables are V_k everywhere and the old fields V_j inside Z_j.
- *The numerator.* N_k^C = Σ_{{Ω_j},{Λ_j}} χ_k(Ω_k)𝐓_k[𝒲_k exp A_k] (map #MD2.0.3.66), with 𝒲_k the dressed loop of class D(k) (map D2.0(3)). 𝒲_k consists of the leading word, the slot insertions, and the attached species of D2.6. The leading word is a function of V_k on Ũ_k^c and of the old fields in the large-field regions the loop crosses. The labels κ, 𝓔, and Q are numbers of the term (D(k)(v)).
  E[W(C)] = ∫N_k^C dV_k / ∫ρ_k dV_k at every k, since both sides are the same integral over the original field (map #MD2.0.3.66).
- *The class.* The 𝐑-operation (LFR I p.177 ff.; LFR II (1.72), p.379) acts on the components of Z_k = Λ_k^c such that
  (i) the component lies in a cube of side 100MR_k, and
  (ii) no new large-field region was created inside it in the preceding N steps, and the previous regions satisfy (i) on their scales (p.177).
  Let Z be the union of these class components. Then (1.1) reads 𝐓_k(Z_k) = 𝐓_k(Z_k ∩ Z^c)𝐓_k(Z), and "we do not make any changes in the operation 𝐓_k(Z_k ∩ Z^c)" (p.177).
- *The number N.* LFR I (1.94), p.198, requires N ≤ O(1)(log g_k^{−2})^ν with ν/2 ≤ p₀ − p₁ − 1, and N "sufficiently large". LFR I p.192 has N₀ = O(log g_k^{−2}), and LFR II (1.80), p.384, is applied "with N = R_j". So N = N_k is a polylog number depending on the step.
- *The operation.* It consists of rewritings of 𝐓_k(Z)exp A_k that preserve the density, followed by one operation that changes it. LFR I p.193 l.37–41: "All the above transformations preserve the k-th density ρ_k, they change only the representation". In the last operation "the equality sign is replaced by the equivalence sign, the equivalence means that both sides have equal integrals over the space of fields V_k" (same lines).
  The result is R′ρ_k of (1.100), normalised by ∫dV_k R′ρ_k = ∫dV_k ρ_k ((1.102)). By p.200 l.45–46 the output is "a density, which may not be equal to ρ_k, but is equivalent to it, in the sense that they have equal integrals".

## What that forces

- *R averages.* R acts on 𝐓_k, the integrals over the term's old variables inside the class components. It also acts on V_k on Λ ⊂ X: "The next operation is an integration of this term with respect to the field variables V_k over the domain Λ. We obtain a new expression, which we consider as a function of all field variables V_k, but independent of V_k⌈_Λ" (LFR I p.193 l.41–p.194 l.3). The result is then multiplied by the normalised fraction of (1.100) in a fresh V_k⌈_Λ. So whatever multiplies exp A_k under these integrals is averaged by a positive normalised operation.
- *The loop elsewhere.* The leading word is a function of the multi-scale minimiser U_k(V) (map #MOMST.85; CRE (2.12), p.266), which depends on the old variables inside X and on V_k⌈_Λ. After the 𝐑-step the background is recomputed on the new determining set (LFR II p.379 l.23). Bałaban's expansion localises this dependence into the polymers of the class components, as he localises the action's terms (pp.376–377, p.379 l.33–39). This is 15.5.
- *Integrals, not laws.* The equivalence of p.193 is an equality of integrals, not of densities. So every statement about the loop after an 𝐑-step is a statement about the pair (∫R′N_k^C, ∫R′ρ_k). The identity of item 3 needs only that the 𝐑-step preserves both integrals. The bounds need the form of the post-R integrand, the ratio of gases of 15.3.
- *The geometry.* The class and every exclusion in the construction are characteristic functions of the fields alone. So the 𝐑-step has the same geometry on a term of N_k^C as on the same term of ρ_k: the loop changes which function is averaged, never where. This is item 2.
- *The size.* By positivity the averaged block is at most sup‖w_X‖ at real fields. With D26's attached species this is up to exp(2Σ_jA_j(Z)) (D26 §5.0, §7.1), not 1. (1.75) extends the bound to the analytic continuation, with the loss e^{3sup|σ|}.

## Derivation

### 1. Where R Acts, and the Identity Regime k ≤ N_k

(a) *An empty class.* 𝐓_k(Z_k ∩ Z^c) is not changed ((1.1), p.177). Every rewriting of LFR I Sect. 1 acts on 𝐓_k(Z) and on the factors of exp A_k localised in Z (p.187 ff.). So a term with an empty class is not changed by any of them, and in (1.72) it appears with n = m = 0:
- the product over i is empty;
- the curly bracket is exp Σ_YV(Y, U_k), and the sum over Y does "not includ[e] terms with localization domains equal to one of the components of Z^∼" (p.379 l.25–26);
- V(Y, U_k) is defined only when Y ∩ Z^∼ is a nonempty union of components of Z^∼ (p.377 l.3–9), so for Z = ∅ there is no V-term.
The admissible domains of 𝐑′^{(k)} "contain at least one large field region connected with one of the 𝐓′_k-operations" (p.390 l.10–11), so 𝐑′^{(k)} = 0 for such a term. Hence Rρ_k = ρ_k term by term when the class is empty, and likewise for N_k^C.

(b) *The steps k ≤ N_k.* Condition (ii) requires N preceding steps without new large-field regions inside the component. For k ≤ N_k there are fewer than N_k preceding steps. So no component satisfies (ii), the class is empty, and R is the identity on every term. This is the map's case k ≤ N, in which no 𝐑-operation has acted.
Here N is taken at the step: the 𝐑-operation at scale k has the depth N_k := N̄(K − k), the Cauchy note's choice of LFR I's N at that step (#CP.13). Bałaban states his conditions on N through g_k:
- N is a power of log g_k^{−2} (LFR I p.198 l.54–57); #CP.14 checks this, with (½log g_k^{−2})^ν ≤ N_k ≤ (2log g_k^{−2})^ν + 1;
- N ≥ (ν/log L)log log g_k^{−2} (LFR II p.363 l.39–40), which follows from the lower bound for g_k small.
RC Sect. 3's derivation of 𝐑^{(j)} = 0 for j ≤ N holds with N_k in place of N.

(c) *Growth with K.* The map says at #MD2.3.4.16 that N does not grow with K. This holds in the sense that N_k is a function of g_k, which does not depend on K at fixed k. For k < K′, CRE (2.6) at n = K′ and m = k gives g_{K′}² ≤ (1 + g_{K′}²β′_{2.6}(K′ − k))g_k² (p.255 l.44); dividing by g_{K′}²g_k², g_k^{−2} ≤ g_{K′}^{−2} + β′_{2.6}(K′ − k), which holds trivially at k = K′. So N_k ≤ (2log(g_{K′}^{−2} + β′_{2.6}(K′ − k)))^ν + 1 (#CP.14). For the stopping scale K′ (map #MOMGD.30, #MD2.3.4.16–19) this gives:
- K′ > N_{K′} once K′ > (2log g_{K′}^{−2})^ν + 1, a condition on K′ alone, since g_{K′} is the coupling at the stopping scale;
- the 𝐑-free initial segment {k ≤ K′ : k ≤ N_k} has at most (2log(g_{K′}^{−2} + β′_{2.6}K′))^ν + 1 steps, and the other steps up to K′ are D2.7 steps.
Nothing in D26 depends on the length of this segment. D26 §1 states its bounds "for k+1 ≤ N" and uses them "for the components R does not touch", which item 2 makes precise.

### 3. The Numerator Identity through the 𝐑-Step

The rewritings of LFR I Sect. 1 up to p.193 l.37 are identities of functions of V_k: changes of variables, insertions of decompositions of unity, and Fubini on the old variables (pp.190–193). On a term of N_k^C, with the extra factor 𝒲_k under 𝐓_k, they remain identities. Indeed, 𝒲_k is a bounded measurable function of the same variables, and every step is an identity of integrals of integrable functions. Two operations change the density, and both preserve its integral over V_k.

- *The basic part R′.* The term is integrated over V_k⌈_Λ (p.193 l.44–p.194 l.3) and multiplied by the fraction of (1.100), whose integral over V_k⌈_{Λ_i} is 1. Bałaban calls (1.100) "not a complete 𝐑-operation yet, but … a basic part of it", and (1.102) is "the fundamental normalization property" (p.201).
  (1.102) holds for any integrable integrand f. Indeed, R′f is the normalised fraction in V_k⌈_Λ times ∫dV_k⌈_Λ f, so ∫dV_k R′f = ∫dV_k f, and no property of exp A_k is used. Bałaban prints no proof. Ours is the Haar invariance under V_k ↦ V_kV_{Λ_i}^{−1}, where V_{Λ_i} depends only on V_k on X_i ∩ Λ_iᶜ (Prop. 1, p.194), together with the normalised averages over G_i and T_i. So ∫dV_k R′N_k^C = ∫dV_k N_k^C.
- *The removal of δ_{G_0∩X_i}(V_k′) on the first-class components.* This is LFR II p.378 l.17–20's reversed Faddeev–Popov procedure: "the density without the δ-functions connected with these components is equivalent, but not equal, to the density with these functions". It comes after (1.100) and completes the 𝐑-operation (p.378 l.21–23). It preserves the V_k-integral if the integrand is invariant under the gauge transformations of V_k on X_i that the δ-function fixes.

Gauge invariance enters at three further places, inside the integral (1.76), after the V_k⌈_Λ integration and before the multiplication by the fraction:
- the Faddeev–Popov insertion of δ_{T_0} (p.195 l.39–p.196 l.36), which needs the whole integrand invariant under the multi-scale gauge transformations on 𝔹_0;
- the axial-gauge change (p.197 l.40–43);
- its compensating transformations, of V_h on ∂⁺(Z_h′^∼)^{(h)} and of V′ in the adjoint.

So on a term of N_k^C the four steps need 𝒲_k invariant under these transformations of the multi-scale variables. This follows from Cov(k) (map #MD2.2.4.1–4) and the covariance of the minimiser:
- *The averaging.* M_𝔹 of CRE (2.11), p.256, is covariant: M_𝔹(U^u) = (M_𝔹U)^{u⌈} for a fine gauge transformation u, with u⌈ the restriction of u to the sites of the lattices of 𝔹. This holds for each M^j by the form of RG1 (0.4), p.253: the averaged loops are based at the initial point of the bond, and the principal logarithm is conjugation-equivariant (as in the coarse-chain note's (D2)).
- *The minimiser.* Let v be a transformation of the multi-scale variables V on 𝔹, and u a fine transformation with u⌈ = v. Regularity is a condition on plaquettes, hence gauge invariant. So U ↦ U^u maps the regular U with M_𝔹(U) = V bijectively onto the regular U with M_𝔹(U) = V^v, and A^η(U^u) = A^η(U). Hence the minimal orbit of (2.12) for V^v is the u-image of the minimal orbit for V, and U_k(V^v) = U_k(V)^{u′} for a fine u′ with u′⌈ = v.
- *The fluctuation fields* transform by the adjoint (CRE (2.41)(iii), p.261), which is R(u′) in Cov(k).
- *The word.* By Cov(k), every insertion transforms by π(u′) at the endpoints of its interval. So the closed word 𝒲_k is invariant, and its value depends neither on the choice of u′ nor on the point of the minimal orbit.

With (1.102) and p.378's equivalence for the denominator,

Numerator and denominator are sums over the terms of (1.72), which are terms of Rρ_k. Nothing is claimed, or needed, about R′N_k^C as a function of V_k being a loop expectation under a block law. Every later statement in this note is a statement about these two integrals.

### 15. The Loop through the 𝐑-Step: the Flat Scalar into κ^{(K)}, the Ratio of Gases, the Deviation Remainder

*15.0 Notation.* Fix a step k > N_k and a term of (1.72), with first-class components {X_j}, second-class components {Y_i}, and the unchanged components.
- *Widening.* For a component K of Z, of either class, K⁺ := K^{∼2}, that is, K^∼ widened by one layer of MR_k-cubes.
- *Gauge-fixed sites.* For a first-class X, 𝐓′_k(X)'s construction fixes a gauge at two kinds of sites. The first are the sites of 𝔹_0 ⊂ Λ (LFR I p.195 l.37–p.196 l.33: "they are invariant with respect to the gauge transformations defined on 𝔹_0", the tree T_0 "fixing completely a gauge in this set"). The second are the sites of the shells of (1.53)–(1.72), all in X^∼. Every gauge-fixed site lies in the interior of X⁺.
- *Arcs.* C ∩ X⁺ is a union of arcs, several when C re-enters X⁺. Each arc has its endpoints a_X, b_X ∈ ∂X⁺. w_X is the ordered set of the dressed loop's words on these arcs, one slot per arc in loop order. The statements below are written for one arc and hold for each.
- *Homes.* The homes are the classes of the components of Z under the equivalence generated by the overlap of their sets K⁺. The arcs of a home h are those of C ∩ ⋃_{K∈h}K⁺, with their endpoints on its boundary. Its words on these arcs are averaged jointly (15.1's last sentence), and its first-class components belong to one polymer of 15.3. The gauge-fixed sites of their constructions lie in ⋃_{K∈h}K^∼, so no endpoint of an arc is one of them. Below, X⁺ and "the component" stand for ⋃_{K∈h}K⁺ and h where components overlap.
- *Configurations.* U^{old} is the term's configuration before the 𝐑-step, a function of V_k and of the old fields inside the class components (CRE (2.12)). U^R is the configuration of the post-R term, the minimiser on the new determining set. Its domain Ω_k is "the new k-th domain defined by the determining set of the configuration U_k" (p.379 l.22). U^R is a function of the post-R term's V_k, which on Λ ⊂ X is the fresh variable of (1.100).
- *The operation.* 𝒯_X := (𝐓′_k(X)1)^{−1}𝐓′_k(X) is a positive normalised operation on functions of the integration variables (p.380 l.3, p.391 l.13–14, (1.73)). Let F depend also on the external variables and on (𝐔, 𝐉). Then (1.73) and (1.75) hold with the sup over the integration variables taken at the given external data, since the triangle inequality for the positive measure 𝐓′_k(X,(U,0)) does not see parameters.
- *The flat reference.* The flat reference of the external data of X is the identity configuration V_k ≡ 1 on X⁺ ∖ Λ and on the adjacent small-field region, with 𝐉 = 0. This is the convention of D2.3's U₂ = 1 (map #MOMST.52).
- *Letters.* The loop is taken term by term in D(k)'s multilinear expansion of its insertions (map #MD2.0.3.19, #MD2.0.3.30). On a fixed term the dressed word is an ordered product of letters. The letters are the link transports on single bonds and the term's insertions, each insertion an ordered factor of the word on its interval.
- *Slots.* An insertion of the term whose interval meets an arc of C ∩ X⁺ is a slot insertion of 15.5(i). It belongs to that arc's slot with its whole interval, and arcs so enlarged are merged when they meet. So every slot is a union of whole letters of the term's word. The enlarged arcs' endpoints lie outside X⁺, away from the gauge-fixed sites.

*15.1 The averaged arc at the flat reference is a scalar.* Let M_X := 𝒯_X[w_X] at the flat reference.
Let u₀ ∈ G be a constant gauge transformation. It acts on every variable of the term by conjugation: V_b ↦ u₀V_bu₀^{−1} for G-valued bonds, and Ad(u₀) for the Lie-algebra-valued B_j, A_j, B, A′. It fixes the flat external data. It leaves each factor of 𝐓′_k(X)'s construction invariant:
- the Haar measures;
- the Wilson action A(U), and the terms 𝐄, 𝐑, 𝐁 of A_k′, which are gauge-invariant functions of the fields (CRE (2.23), (2.30));
- the Gaussian form ⟨DH″B, ζDH″B⟩ and σ(g_kB) of (1.70), since the inner product on 𝔤 is Ad-invariant;
- every characteristic function, since each is a function of |∂U − 1|, |B|, |A|, which are invariant under conjugation;
- the gauge-fixing δ-functions δ_{T_0}, δ_{rT}, δ_{G_0}, since they put bond variables equal to 1 on tree bonds (p.196 l.24–25) and u₀·1·u₀^{−1} = 1;
- the minimiser V_Λ, which is covariant by Prop. 1 (p.194) and fixed at flat data;
- the 𝐇-functions and the configurations (1.61)–(1.63), which are covariant.

So the measure 𝒯_X at the flat reference is invariant under the constant conjugation of its integration variables. The word transforms as w_X(u₀·V) = π(u₀)w_X(V)π(u₀)^{−1}: every link factor is conjugated, the insertions on the arc are covariant by D(k)(ii), and both endpoints a_X, b_X carry u₀.
Hence π(u₀)M_Xπ(u₀)^{−1} = M_X for every u₀ ∈ G. For an irreducible χ, Schur's lemma gives M_X = c_X·1 with c_X := dim(π)^{−1}tr M_X.
The number c_X depends on k, on the shape of X and of the arc in X⁺, and on the internal sequence {Ω_j ∩ X, Λ_j ∩ X} of X, which is summed inside (1.72)'s bracket. By item 5(a), |c_X| ≤ sup‖w_X‖ ≤ e^{2ΣA}.
The same holds for a joint average over a set of class components, when no other component's word stands between them along C. The averaged object is the ordered product, along C, of their arc words and of the far insertions of 15.5 attached to them. This product is covariant at its two end sites, so its joint average at the flat reference is G-invariant, hence a scalar.

*15.2 Gauge covariance of the averaged arc at every external configuration.*

(α) *The fixed gauge.* w_X is invariant under every gauge transformation supported at the sites where the construction fixes a gauge. Such a transformation acts on the arc's link factors by conjugation at interior sites, so the product telescopes. The endpoints a_X, b_X ∈ ∂X⁺ (∂ of the union for overlapping components, 15.0) are not among those sites. The insertions on the arc are covariant at their own endpoints and telescope with the links.
So three operations are legitimate for the integrand with w_X exactly as for the integrand without it:
- the Faddeev–Popov insertion of δ_{T_0} (p.196 l.31–33);
- its reversal for the first-class components on p.378 l.16–20 ("we can remove the gauge fixing δ-functions δ_{G_0∩X_i}(V_k′) by a reversed Faddeev–Popov procedure. This is possible only if we integrate the density with respect to the gauge field variables V_k, at least on the components X_i");
- the averages over the trees rT.

That is, let F be any function of the term's variables invariant under those transformations. Then the gauge-fixed functional 𝐓′_k(X)[w_X F] equals the unfixed integral.

(β) *External gauge transformations.* Let u be an external gauge transformation, supported outside the fixed sites, in particular on X⁺ ∖ Λ and outside X⁺. It acts on the unfixed integral by the change of integration variables that u induces:
- V_b ↦ V_bu(y)^{−1} for a bond b ∈ 𝔹_0 crossing ∂Λ with outer endpoint y (p.195 l.35–37);
- Ad(u) on the Lie-algebra variables at the sites where u ≠ 1.

The Haar and Gaussian measures are invariant under this change. Every unfixed factor is covariant, by 15.1's list, now for a non-constant u; in particular V_Λ(u·V) = u·V_Λ(V) by Prop. 1.
Hence 𝐓′_k(X)[w_X F](u·V_ext) = π(u(a_X))𝐓′_k(X)[w_X F](V_ext)π(u(b_X))^{−1} for every gauge-invariant F.
At complex external data the identity holds for real u, by the same change of variables. Indeed, Bałaban's extension continues the external configuration (p.379 l.27–29), and the integration variables stay real.

*Consequence.* On a pure-gauge external configuration V_ext = u·1 the averaged arc is π(u(a_X))M_Xπ(u(b_X))^{−1}. So the closed words built from it below take the same values as at the identity configuration.

*15.3 The loop-linked part of (1.72)'s bracket as a ratio of gases, an identity given (1.90)–(1.97).* *Bałaban's gas.* Bałaban's bracket is the polymer gas (1.90), Ξ := 1 + Σ_{r≥1}Σ_{{X′_1,…,X′_r}}Π_pF(X′_p). The sum runs over families of polymers X′ with disjoint localisation domains, and the activities are (1.91). The logarithm of Ξ is Σ_X𝐑′^{(k)}(X). The series converges by (1.92)–(1.97), with c₁ = e^{−p₀(g_k)} away from ∪Y_i and c₁ = α^{1/3} at ∪Y_i (pp.389–390; Bałaban's, accepted).

*Polymers with the loop.* With the loop present, every object of the word is first expanded into its pieces, each joining the components of h(F) ∪ 𝒦(P) (15.5(i)–(i-2)).
- A polymer is a union of class components, localisation domains of V-terms, and the domains Y_P^{[1]} of the pieces, any two of these constituents being joined by a chain of constituents, consecutive ones meeting. A piece's domain need not be connected (15.4(i)). A Bałaban polymer meeting a piece's domain is merged with it. So Ξ_{¬C}(𝔉) below is a subgas of Ξ, and F(P) is the product of the Bałaban activities of P's constituents.
- A polymer's activity depends on the renormalised configuration on the polymer only (p.379 l.12–13).
- A polymer *meets C* when its activity carries a slot of the loop, which is a piece of 15.5(i-3). The slot stands inside the operations 𝐓′_k(X) of the polymer's first-class components, at their own data.
- For a polymer P meeting C, its loop-linked activity T_P is (1.91)'s expression with the slots of P in their loop order. It is a tensor with one pair of indices per slot.
- For a polymer not meeting C the activity is F(P).
- The words of C in no slot stand outside all operations. On the term of 15.0 they are the far letters' π(T_i^R), the members of 15.5(i-5), and the second-class material of 15.5(i-3).

*The two gases.* Write Ξ^C for the bracket with the loop. For a family 𝔉, write Ξ_{¬C}(𝔉) for the gas of the polymers that do not meet C and are compatible with every member of 𝔉. So Ξ_{¬C}(∅) is the gas of all the polymers not meeting C.
Every configuration of the gas is, uniquely, the compatible family 𝔉 of its polymers meeting C together with a configuration of Ξ_{¬C}(𝔉). Hence, with 𝔉 running over the compatible families of polymers meeting C, 𝔉 = ∅ included,
- Ξ = Σ_𝔉Π_{P∈𝔉}F(P)·Ξ_{¬C}(𝔉);
- Ξ^C = Σ_𝔉Ξ_{¬C}(𝔉)·A^C(𝔉).

Here A^C(𝔉) is the trace of the path-ordered product along C of the tensors T_P, P ∈ 𝔉, contracted in loop order, and of the words at U^R on the bonds in no slot. A^C(∅) is the trace of 𝒲^{sf}(U^R) with the term's insertions at U^R. Dividing,

Ξ^C/Ξ = Σ_𝔉 e^{−Φ(𝔉)}A^C(𝔉),  Φ(𝔉) := log Ξ − log Ξ_{¬C}(𝔉) = Σ φ^T(𝒞) over the clusters 𝒞 that contain a polymer meeting C or a polymer incompatible with 𝔉,

with the incompatibility factor e^{−Φ(𝔉)}.

*The bound on Φ(∅).* Φ(∅) is the sum over the clusters containing a polymer meeting C. It is not 0. *15.4 The flat part and its subtraction into κ_k, from 15.1–15.3 and translation covariance.* *Spans.* Let P be a polymer with slots on C. span(P) is the shortest arc of the closed loop C containing all the slots of P:
- span(P) := C when the slots of P cover C;
- otherwise span(P) is the complement of a longest arc of C that runs between two consecutive slots of P and shares no bond with a slot of P. When several are longest, which happens only for t ≥ s_k below, take the one whose first bond is reached first from a fixed base bond of C in C's orientation.

So the two end bonds of span(P) are bonds of slots of P.

*Groups.* Let 𝔉 be a compatible family. P, P′ ∈ 𝔉 interleave if a slot of P shares a bond with span(P′), or a slot of P′ shares a bond with span(P). The interleaving groups are the classes of the equivalence relation generated by interleaving. A group g has the span span(g) := ∪_{P∈g}span(P).
Two members whose spans share a bond interleave. If one span is C, a slot of the other shares a bond with it. Otherwise walk along span(P′) from the shared bond to an end of span(P′). If the walk stays in span(P), that end is a bond of a slot of P′ lying in span(P). If not, the walk leaves span(P) through an end of span(P), which is a bond of a slot of P lying in span(P′).
So the spans of distinct groups share no bond, and span(g) shares no bond with a slot of another group.
Each slot lies in its polymer's span, so interleaving members have intersecting spans. Hence span(g) is an arc of C whose two ends are bonds of slots of g, or C itself.

(i) *The flat scalar.* Take the flat reference of all the external data along C: the term's small-field region near C is flat, and the far large-field data of the term are unchanged. Then the link transports at U^R are 1. By 15.1, the contraction of a group's slots in loop order, with these transports on the bonds of span(g) in no slot, is a scalar. Write it as c_g·Π_{P∈g}F(P)|_flat·1.
- c_g is a number depending only on the shapes, the internal sequences, and the relative positions of the group's polymers and of C's bonds.
- For a one-slot group {X}, c_g = c_X of 15.1.
- For a group one of whose polymers meets a second-class or unchanged component, put c_g := 0. Its whole contraction is then D_g of 15.6, an att insertion by 15.6(e). Here and below, a polymer *meets* a component when one of its localisation domains X′ of (1.90) does, as in (1.97)'s criterion for c₁ (p.390 l.2: "c₁ = exp(−p₀(g_k)) if X′ does not intersect ⋃Y_i, and c₁ = α^{1/3} in the remaining cases"); meeting it through a set K⁺, a link's set X̂, a piece's domain, or a carried X_D does not count (table #T12.2-17; two-loops #800).

So, on the term with no insertion at U^R off the slots, A^C(𝔉)|_flat = dim π·Π_gc_g·Π_{P∈𝔉}F(P)|_flat. The term's insertions at U^R are treated in 15.6(f). Hence Ξ^C/Ξ|_flat = dim π·Σ_𝔉e^{−Φ(𝔉)}Π_gc_gΠ_PF(P) =: dim π·exp E_k^R(C), where E_k^R(C) = log(Ξ^c/Ξ) and Ξ^c := Σ_𝔉Π_gc_gΠ_{P∈𝔉}F(P)·Ξ_{¬C}(𝔉).

*The gas Ξ^c.* Its polymers are the polymers not meeting C and the groups.
- A group's activity is c_gΠ_{P∈g}F(P), bounded by (ĉ₁)^{#components}e^{−κ(size)}.
- Two groups are compatible when their domains are disjoint and they do not interleave; interleaving groups are one group.

*The size of a polymer.* For an insertion D with a piece in P, T_D is table #T12.2-13's closed-connected set, which contains Dep(D) ∖ Z′ (two-loops #693-2), and ℓ_D := dist_∞(T_D, Z′) for an att(j, Z′) with Z′ ⊂ Z, ℓ_D := 0 otherwise (15.5(i-0-1)). |P| is the number of MR_k-cubes meeting dom P, the pieces' domains included, or a set K⁺ with K a component of P, plus τ_D := ⌈(diam_∞T_D + ℓ_D)/(MR_k)⌉ for every such D. A polymer's span lies within |P| layers of MR_k-cubes of its domain (shown below). And |P| ≤ size(P) + |P|_𝔏 + |P|_w, where:
- size(P) is the number of MR_k-cubes of dom P that κ(size) counts; a tree of (1.67) through all the M-cubes gives κ per MR_k-cube (two-loops #637-0);
- |P|_𝔏 is the number of MR_k-cubes meeting P's links, whose own decay pays their share of the factors below (15.8, *The links in the gas*);
- |P|_w is the number of the other cubes counted in |P|, those meeting the pieces' domains or the sets K⁺, plus Σ_Dτ_D.

By 15.5(ii)(β)–(β-0), |P|_w is at most the sum of five shares, each paid as follows:
- 107^d per component K of P, the MR_k-cubes meeting (K⁺)^{[λ̂]}, paid by the component's e^{−p₀(g_k)}; at a second-class component this is within 15.8(c)'s costs, and such a component lies in at most one polymer of a family;

*Why a span lies within |P| layers.*
- C is the square loop of side s_k = ℓ_CL^{K′−k} in step-k units (map #MD2.0.1.6, #MD2.3.4.9).
- The torus periods are at least 2s_k. This is the quantified part N^{(k)}_min ≥ 2s_k of map (5)(g)'s T_ph ≫ ℓ_C (#MD2.3.6.3: "N^{(k)}_min ≫ s_k"). So the torus ∞-distance between points of C is the planar one.

The set dom^♯P := dom P ∪ ⋃_KK⁺ ∪ ⋃_D(T_D ∪ s_D), over the components K of P and the insertions D with a piece in P, is connected (next bullet).
- Its constituents are joined by chains of meeting constituents (15.3), each replaced by a connected superset. A component K becomes K⁺, a V-term's domain is a 𝐃_k domain, and a piece of an object F becomes Y_P^{[1]} ∪ B(F), with B(F) := Dep(F) ∪ ⋃_{K∈h(F)}K⁺ ∪ T_F ∪ s_F (T_F ∪ s_F := ∅ unless F is an insertion). - If t ≥ s_k, every bond of C lies within ∞-distance s_k ≤ t of each slot.

*The entropy of the groups through a cube.* Split e^{−κ(size)} into thirds: one for the Kotecký–Preiss margin, one for the diameters of 15.7, and one for the entropy, which consists of the following factors.
- A polymer whose span meets a given cube has its domain within |P| layers of that cube. So dom P meets one of (2|P|+1)^d MR_k-cubes Q′.

So the groups whose hulls meet a given MR_k-cube sum, with their Kotecký–Preiss weights, to at most e^{−p₀(g_k)/4}. Both cluster expansions converge under the assumption of 15.3.

(ii) *Periodicity.* Bałaban's construction is covariant under the translations of the lattice T^{(k)} that preserve the partition into MR_k-cubes and the partitions of the scales j ≤ k entering 𝐓′_k(X). Indeed, the cubes, the trees rT averaged over r, the characteristic functions, and the operations are defined from these partitions. For the terms 𝐑′^{(k)}(X), p.390 l.27 states it: "By their construction it is also clear that they are Euclidean covariant, i.e., they have the property (2.32) [III]".
So at the flat reference the group activities are periodic along each straight side of C, with period Π^R_k := MR_k in step-k units. They decay exponentially in the group's size.

*The period is common to all the partitions.* Π^R_k is a power of L, since M = L^m (CRE p.245 l.34) and R_j is the smallest power of L with R_j ≥ (log g_j^{−2})^r ((2.5) p.255). The partitions of the scales j < k have side MR_jL^{j−k} in step-k units, and they refine the MR_k-partition:
- by CRE (2.9), R_jL^j ≤ (L+1)(k−j)^{β₀}L^{−(k−j)}R_kL^k < 2R_kL^k;
- R_jL^j is a power of L below 2R_kL^k, so it divides the power R_kL^k.

So Π^R_k is a common period of all the partitions entering step k, for every K at which the run stops.

*The cluster series.* The cluster series of this gas on a rectangle whose sides are multiples of Π^R_k is computed exactly as in D2.3(4)–(8) (map #MOMST.53-1: log S°(ℓ₀) = P₁(C)μ + 4ζ + E°₂(C,T), "what vanishes at the flat reference are the deviation words of the bracket, not part of the series"). Here:
- μ_k^R := (4LΠ^R_k)^{−1}Σ_vκ^{R}_v is the per-length form of the period sums κ^R_v of the cluster terms rooted in one period, averaged over the orientations v of C's sides. It is the map's κ^{(k)}_v with its division by 4Π_{k+1} (#MD2.3.5.5), with the 𝐑-gas's cluster terms. The extra L appears because P_{k−1}(C) is in step-(k−1) units and Π^R_k in step-k units. - E°_k^R is the corner and torus remainder. With c in MR_k-cube units, |E°_k^R| ≤ C·MR_k·e^{−p₀(g_k)/4}[(s_k+1)e^{−cs_k} + s_ke^{−cι_{T,k}}].
