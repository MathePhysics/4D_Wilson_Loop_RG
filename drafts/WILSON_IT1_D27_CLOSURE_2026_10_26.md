## What the objects are

- The k-th density is ρ_k = Σ_{{Ω_j},{Λ_j}} χ_k(Ω_k) 𝐓_k exp A_k (CRE (2.18) p.257), 𝐓_k = Π_j 𝐓^{(j)} the product of the integrals over the old fields V_j in the large-field regions Z_j = Λ_j^c ((2.3), (2.19)–(2.22) p.258), A_k = −A + 𝐄_k + 𝐑_k + 𝐁_k − E_k (2.23). A term of ρ_k is a summand with a fixed sequence {Ω_j, Λ_j}; its pending variables are V_k everywhere and the old fields V_j inside Z_j.
- The numerator is N_k^C = Σ_{{Ω_j},{Λ_j}} χ_k(Ω_k) 𝐓_k[𝒲_k exp A_k] (map l.326), 𝒲_k the dressed loop of class D(k) (D26 §0): the leading word, a function of V_k on Ũ_k^c and of the old fields in the large-field regions the loop crosses, the slot insertions, and the attached species of D2.6, with the labels κ, 𝓔, Q as numbers of the term (D(k)(v)). The identity E[W(C)] = ∫N_k^C dV_k / ∫ρ_k dV_k holds at every k because both sides are the same integral over the original field (map l.326, derived there).
- The R-operation (LFR I p.177 ff., LFR II (1.72) p.379) acts on the class of components of Z_k = Λ_k^c that satisfy (i) the component lies in a cube of side 100MR_k and (ii) no new large-field region was created inside it in the preceding N steps and the previous regions satisfy (i) on their scales (p.177). With Z the union of the class components, (1.1) reads 𝐓_k(Z_k) = 𝐓_k(Z_k ∩ Z^c) 𝐓_k(Z) and "we do not make any changes in the operation 𝐓_k(Z_k ∩ Z^c)" (p.177). N is the number in (ii); p.198 (1.94) requires N ≤ O(1)(log g_k^{−2})^ν with (1/2)ν ≤ p₀ − p₁ − 1 and N "sufficiently large", p.192 has N₀ = O(log g_k^{−2}), and LFR II p.384 (1.80) is applied "with N = R_j". So N = N_k is a step-dependent polylog number.
- The operation is a sequence of rewritings of 𝐓_k(Z) exp A_k that preserve the density (LFR I p.193 l.37–41: "All the above transformations preserve the k-th density ρ_k, they change only the representation"), followed by one operation that "changes the density", where "the equality sign is replaced by the equivalence sign, the equivalence means that both sides have equal integrals over the space of fields V_k" (same lines), with the result R′ρ_k of (1.100) normalised by ∫dV_k (R′ρ_k) = ∫dV_k ρ_k (1.102) and the statement p.200 l.45–46 that the output is "a density, which may not be equal to ρ_k, but is equivalent to it, in the sense that they have equal integrals". LFR II (1.70)–(1.72) pp.377–379 write the final form: for each class component X a localised integral operation 𝐓′_k(X), the composition of the preliminary integrations (1.53)–(1.72) of LFR I (boundary variables B_j, A_j of the last N steps), the T_h-operation of the step h = k − N inside X, the fluctuation integral over V″ on 𝔹_0 around the minimiser V_Λ (LFR I (1.78)–(1.81)), and the average (1/2^d d!) Σ_r over axial-gauge trees rT (p.379 l.10–12), with all expressions "localized in the domain X" (p.379 l.13). The output (1.72) has three kinds of components of Z_k: the first class {X_j}, whose operations are summed into exp Σ_X 𝐑′^{(k)}(X) by (1.90)–(1.98); the second class {Y_i}, which carry χ_k^c(Y_i^{∼−6}) χ_{k,Λ_i} χ_i δ_{G_i}(V_k′) 𝐓′_k(Y_i) and remain large-field regions; and the components of Z_k ∩ Z^c, unchanged. "Ω_k is the new k-th domain defined by the determining set of the configuration U_k" (p.379 l.23).
- 𝐓′_k(X) is positive and normalisable: p.380 l.3 "exponential density in the integral is positive", (1.73)–(1.75), and p.391 l.13–14 "the 𝐓′_k-operations are normalized, i.e., if such an operation is applied to a function which does not depend on the integration variables connected with the operation, then it is equal to 1".

## What that forces

- R acts on 𝐓_k, the integrals over the term's old variables inside the class components, and also on the field V_k itself on Λ ⊂ X: "The next operation is an integration of this term with respect to the field variables V_k over the domain Λ. We obtain a new expression, which we consider as a function of all field variables V_k, but independent of V_k⌈_Λ" (LFR I p.193 l.41–p.194 l.3), followed by the multiplication by the normalised fraction of (1.100) in a fresh V_k⌈_Λ. Whatever multiplies exp A_k under those integrals is averaged by a positive normalised operation. The loop's factors elsewhere are not untouched: the leading word is a function of the multi-scale minimiser U_k(V) (map l.153; CRE (2.12), p.266), which depends on the old variables inside X and on V_k⌈_Λ, and after the R-step the background is recomputed on the new determining set (LFR II p.379 l.23). That dependence must be localised into the class component's activity as Bałaban localises the action's (pp.376–377, p.379 l.33–39); this is not done below (item 4).
- Since ρ_k give Z and not block laws (the equivalence of p.193 is an equality of integrals, not of densities), every statement about the loop after an R-step is a statement about the pair (∫R′N_k^C, ∫R′ρ_k). The identity of item 3 needs only that the R-step preserves both integrals; the bounds of items 4–9 need the form of the post-R integrand, which items 4–9 did not supply and item 15 does (the ratio of gases, 15.3).
- The class and every exclusion in the construction are defined by characteristic functions of the fields alone, so the geometry of the R-step for a term of N_k^C is the geometry of the R-step for the same term of ρ_k. The loop changes which function is averaged, never where the averaging takes place. That is item 2.
- Positivity bounds the averaged block by sup‖w_X‖ at real fields, which with D26's attached species is up to exp(2Σ_jA_j(Z)) (D26 §5.0, §7.1), not 1, and (1.75) extends this to the analytic continuation with the loss e^{3 sup|σ|}. The smallness of the R-shed correction comes from the weight 𝐓′_k(X)1 ≤ e^{−2(1+β₀)^{−1}p₀(g_k)} (1.89) of any term that has a class component at all. The per-block multiplicative accounting gives an exponent that is additive over the blocks of C at every scale, Σ_k|C|L^{K−k}·(per-block mass), and that sum is not bounded in K (item 9): the per-block scalar at flat configurations belongs in κ^{(K)}, and only a remainder vanishing at flat backgrounds and contracting under D2.0(5)(c) can be carried as a species. Items 5, 6 and 9 do not deliver this; item 15 does (15.4, 15.6).

## Derivation

### 1. Where R acts, and the identity regime k ≤ N_k. Status: derived.

(a) By (1.1) and p.177, 𝐓_k(Z_k ∩ Z^c) is not changed, so a term whose class is empty is not changed by any of the rewritings of LFR I Sect. 1 (all of them act on 𝐓_k(Z) and on the factors of exp A_k localised in Z, p.187 ff.), and in (1.72) the term appears with n = m = 0: the product over i is empty, the curly bracket is exp Σ_Y V(Y, U_k) with the sum over Y "not includ[ing] terms with localization domains equal to one of the components of Z^∼" (p.379 l.25–26), and V(Y, U_k) is defined on p.377 l.3–9 for Y such that Y ∩ Z^∼ is a union of components of Z^∼ containing at least one, so for Z = ∅ there is no V-term. The admissible domains of 𝐑′^{(k)} "contain at least one large field region connected with one of the 𝐓′_k-operations" (p.390 l.10–11), so 𝐑′^{(k)} = 0 for such a term. Hence Rρ_k = ρ_k term by term when the class is empty, and the same for N_k^C.

(b) Condition (ii) needs N preceding steps without new large-field regions inside the component. For k ≤ N_k there are fewer than N_k preceding steps, so no component satisfies (ii), the class is empty, and R is the identity on every term. This is R-h (map l.327) with N read as N_k; the derivation of RC Sect. 3 (𝐑^{(j)} = 0 for j ≤ N) is unchanged by this reading. The map's gloss at l.1094 that N does not grow with K is correct in the sense that N_k is a function of g_k, which does not depend on K at fixed k; it is not correct as a statement that N is a constant, because g_k^{−2} grows with K − k (CRE (2.4)–(2.9), the running coupling), so N_k ≤ O(1)(log g_k^{−2})^ν ≈ O(1)(log(g^{−2} + c(K − k)))^ν grows like (log K)^ν at fixed k. Consequences for the stopping scale K′ (map l.193, l.1094–1097): K′ > N_{K′} holds as soon as K′ ≥ C (log g_{K′}^{−2})^ν, a condition on K′ alone, since g_{K′} is the coupling at the stopping scale; and the R-free initial segment {k : k ≤ N_k} has length O((log K)^ν), so the large majority of the K′ steps are D2.7 steps. Nothing in D26 depends on the length of the initial segment (D26 §1 states its bounds "for k+1 ≤ N" and uses them "for the components R does not touch", which item 2 below makes precise).

### 2. The class and the exclusions are observable-independent. Status: derived.

The class is defined by the geometry of the term's sequence {Ω_j, Λ_j} (conditions (i), (ii)), the exclusions along the construction are decompositions of unity in the fields alone — 1 − χ′^{(j)} (1.55), 1 − χ_{k,Λ} (1.75), 1 − χ′ (1.82), P ≠ ∅ (1.88), the completion (1.28) of p.183, and the large-field functions χ_k^c(Y_i^{∼−6}) of (1.72) — and the minimiser V_Λ, the configuration U_0 = U_{k,Z}(V_Λ) (1.79), the boundary terms 𝐁_k^{(n)} = Σ 𝐂_k^{(i)} (1.61)–(1.63), and the spaces Ũ_k^{(n)c} (1.64)–(1.69) are functions of the fields V_k, V_j of the term. None of them involves the factor multiplying exp A_k under 𝐓_k. So for a term of N_k^C, with the extra factor 𝒲_k under the integrals, every decomposition of unity is inserted at the same place, every excluded component is excluded on the same event, the same Z, Λ, V_Λ, 𝔹_0 arise, and the output has the same three kinds of components with the same characteristic functions as the corresponding term of ρ_k. This is RC Sect. 4's statement ("components never acted on") extended to the whole construction, and it is what D26 §1's "used by D2.7 unchanged for the components R does not touch" needs: on Z_k ∩ Z^c the operation 𝐓_k is literally the same operator, so every D26 bound stated for a component of Z_k that is not in the class holds after the R-step as it held before.

### 3. The numerator identity through the R-step. Status: derived.

The rewritings of LFR I Sect. 1 up to p.193 l.37 are identities of functions of V_k (changes of variables, insertions of decompositions of unity, Fubini on the old variables, p.190–193); applied to a term of N_k^C with the extra factor 𝒲_k under 𝐓_k they remain identities, because 𝒲_k is a bounded measurable function of the same variables and every step is an identity of integrals of integrable functions. The one operation that changes the density is the integration of the term over V_k⌈_Λ (p.193 l.41–p.194 l.3), followed by the multiplication by the fraction of (1.100), whose integral over V_k⌈_{Λ_i} is 1; this is an equivalence in Bałaban's sense, equal integrals over V_k, and (1.102) is its normalisation, "the fundamental normalization property" (p.201). It holds for any integrable integrand: R′ maps a term f to (the normalised fraction in V_k⌈_Λ) × ∫dV_k⌈_Λ f, whose V_k-integral is ∫dV_k f, with no property of exp A_k used; the proof is the Haar invariance under V_k ↦ V_kV_{Λ_i}^{−1}, V_{Λ_i} depending only on V_k on X_i ∩ Λ_iᶜ (Prop. 1, p.194), with the normalised averages over G_i and T_i. So ∫dV_k (R′N_k^C) = ∫dV_k N_k^C. Gauge invariance enters the equalities that precede this operation: the Faddeev–Popov insertion of δ_{T_0} (p.195 l.39–p.196 l.36), which needs the invariance of the whole integrand under the multi-scale gauge transformations on 𝔹_0; the axial-gauge change (p.197 l.40–43), with the compensating transformations of V_h on ∂⁺(Z_h′^∼)^{(h)} and of V′; and LFR II p.378's reversed Faddeev–Popov step. For N_k^C these equalities need 𝒲_k to be invariant under exactly those transformations, which is D(k)(ii)'s gauge invariance of the dressed loop as a closed word with covariant insertions (D26 §0; the loop is a product of link variables and of covariant insertions along C, D2.1–D2.6), applied to the multi-scale transformations: its closed-word invariance under transformations of the fields at the scales the insertions are built from is D(k)(ii)'s content and is used here as such. With (1.102) for the denominator,

E[W(C)] = ∫dV_k N_k^C / ∫dV_k ρ_k = ∫dV_k (R′N_k^C) / ∫dV_k (R′ρ_k),

and both numerator and denominator are now sums over the terms of (1.72). No statement is made, or needed, about R′N_k^C as a function of V_k being a loop expectation under a block law. This closes the structural point of the brief: every later statement in this note is a statement about the two integrals.

### 4. The per-term action of R on the loop. Status: not derived.

*Where the argument below fails, and what closes it.* (a) "V_k is not integrated" is false: R′ integrates V_k on Λ ⊂ X (LFR I p.194 l.1–3; in (1.76) the variable V″ on 𝔹_k″ ∩ Λ includes the level-k variables on Γ_k″), so the averaged block 𝒯_X[w_X] is independent of the new V_k on Λ and depends on V_k only on X ∖ Λ, through V_Λ (Prop. 1), U_0 (1.79), χ_{k,Λ} and the boundary terms. (b) "For a block b outside every X^∼ and Y_i^∼, w_b does not depend on the variables integrated by 𝒯_X" is false in general: the leading word depends on U_k(V), which depends on the old V_j in X and on the old V_k⌈_Λ through the multi-scale minimiser (CRE (2.12), p.266; map l.153), and after R the background is the new U_k on the new determining set. The display below holds only after the loop's dependence on the variables inside X is localised into the class component's activity, as Bałaban localises the action's terms by the expansion in the 𝐇-functions (LFR II p.379 l.33–39, p.376) and moves the differences into V(Y) (p.377 l.15–21): (term of R′N_k^C) = (term of R′ρ_k) × Π_b w_b(new U_k) × [the loop-attached differences near each class component, with the 𝐇-decay, inside that component's activity] × [the averaged blocks]. That localisation is the first piece of the open work.

Fix a term of (1.72) for N_k^C with class components X (first class) and Y_i (second class). Let 𝒯_X denote the normalised operation (𝐓′_k(X)1)^{−1} 𝐓′_k(X), and let 𝒯_X also stand for the composition with the preliminary integrations of (1.70), all of which are integrals over the old variables of the term localised in X together with the boundary variables B_j, A_j in X^∼ ∖ Z of the last N steps (LFR I (1.53)–(1.72)). The variables integrated by 𝒯_X are exactly the pending variables of the term in X^∼ other than V_k; V_k is not integrated, and nothing outside X^∼ is integrated (p.379 l.13). The dressed loop factorises along C into the product of its blocks, 𝒲_k = Π_b w_b with w_b the word of 𝒲_k on the arc C ∩ b^∼ of the block b (a block being a component X^∼, a component Y_i^∼, or a connected piece of the complement), with the slot insertions and attached species inside the arc as factors of w_b (D(k)(iv), D26 §0). The labels κ, 𝓔, Q are numbers of the term, so they pass through 𝒯_X unchanged. For a block b outside every X^∼ and Y_i^∼, w_b does not depend on the variables integrated by 𝒯_X or 𝒯_{Y_i}, so by p.391 l.13–14 the operations act on it as the identity. For the block in X^∼, 𝒯_X acts on w_b and produces a function of the external variables, V_k on X^∼ and the old variables of the adjacent non-class components, through the minimiser V_Λ (Prop. 1, (1.78)), the configuration U_0 (1.79), and the boundary terms (1.61)–(1.63). The output term is therefore

(term of R′N_k^C) = (term of R′ρ_k) × Π_{b outside} w_b × Π_{X} 𝒯_X[w_X] × Π_i 𝒯_{Y_i}[w_{Y_i}],

with the understanding that for the first-class components the factor (𝐓′_k(X)1) and the V-terms are then summed as in item 6, and for the second-class components the factor (𝐓′_k(Y_i)1) stays in front as in (1.103)–(1.104). This is the observable part of the R-operation: a product over blocks in which only the blocks inside class components change.
